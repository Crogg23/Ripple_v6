Chris (2026-08-22, after the fix-session "where am I, 0-100" exchange): "please
always communicate it this way — this is very helpful in me not getting lost in
the chaos of the project. I need you to bias yourself toward that specifically."

The pattern he locked in (from the two messages he approved):

1. **Anchor number first.** One blended 0-100 score for "where am I," stated
   with conviction ("you're at about a 62"), then the honest split when the
   question is really two questions (e.g. trust-in-what's-loaded ~70 vs
   build-completeness ~50, one line each).
2. **Dual register in the same message.** Portfolio view (asset/inventory
   terms: what changed, what I now hold) AND a bar version (one plain-speech
   blockquote a stranger would follow: "the rooms you live in are clean, two
   big rooms are known-broken, a whole wing isn't built").
3. **What moves the number** — ranked levers with rough points-per-dollar
   ("drug-side-effects reload +4-5, ~$10").
4. **Drags stated as drags**, up front and quantified ("the two broken marquee
   datasets cost ~10 points alone") — never buried as trailing caveats.

**Why:** the project is huge and chaotic; deltas without a fixed frame get him
lost. A stable scoreboard + what-buys-points turns any status into orientation.

**How to apply:** whenever reporting state, progress, or the meaning of a work
session (boot briefs, closes, "where are we" asks, after big fixes), bias
toward this shape: anchor score (or the relevant fixed frame) → what moved it →
what still drags it → ranked next levers, with the bar-version blockquote as
the one takeaway. Keeps [[interaction-contract]] rules (no table names in chat,
bad news first) and [[feedback-two-knobs-not-one-blanket-brief]] intact — this
is the delivery frame, not a license to shorten substance.
