Chris's goal (stated 2026-08-05): get Ripple to ~$200k/year so he can quit
his day job and be his own boss. Explored three monetization lanes in one
session:

1. **Compliance-screening SaaS** (banned-provider/OIG exclusion checks) —
   real mandated market (42 CFR 1001.1901), proven data (8,503 exact NPI
   matches), but crowded (Verisys, ProviderTrust, Exclusion Screening LLC
   already there); needs ~100-200 small-clinic subscribers at $100-200/mo.
   Fastest to an MVP, least differentiated.
2. **Entity-dossier / due-diligence-as-a-service** — sell the wired
   cross-source graph (money/courts/ownership/geography/sanctions) to law
   firms, PE, banks, insurers as automated background investigations,
   comping against Kroll/LexisNexis/Sayari-style manual reports ($500-5k
   each today). Bigger price point, but real structural barriers for a solo
   unknown: incumbent pricing doesn't transfer (unbranded seller realistically
   starts ~$100-300/report), regulated buyers (bank KYC/AML) need
   SOC2/indemnification a solo can't offer yet, known data coverage gaps
   (energy/ag/insurance/SSA domains missing) become liability once someone's
   paying. **Distribution/sales is the actual bottleneck, not the data or
   tech** — Chris has no existing sales network into law firms/banks/PE.
3. **"Amateur" independent investigative journalism** (newsletter/
   subscription, accountability-pattern journalism) — closest fit to the
   mission already in CLAUDE.md. Real proven comp: Judd Legum's *Popular
   Information*, solo FEC/lobbying-pattern journalism built to real
   subscription revenue. Needs ~2,000-4,000 paying subscribers at $5-8/mo
   for $200k, built over 1-3 years via consistent publishing + audience-
   building (a different skill than data engineering). Upside: makes the
   journalism-only-licensed sources (Senate/House trades data, see
   [[senate-trades-reality]]) usable instead of dead weight; lowest trust/
   compliance barrier to entry; the existing human-sign-off/no-auto-publish
   rule in CLAUDE.md already fits this model's governance needs.

**Why:** none of these is obviously "the" answer yet — Chris hasn't picked a
lane. Revisit before assuming a direction.
**How to apply:** when this comes up again, don't re-derive the market comps
from scratch — start from this list and ask which lane, or whether
reality-testing (a real sales attempt / a real published piece) has happened
since.

**Competitive landscape gut-check (2026-08-05):** Chris asked point-blank if
he's rebuilding someone else's wheel. Honest answer — on the infrastructure
side, yes: **Aleph** (OCCRP, open source, free, built specifically for
investigative journalists, does cross-source entity matching — closest and
most important comp to check against), **LittleSis** (free power-mapping),
**OpenCorporates**, **OpenSecrets/FollowTheMoney.org** already cover pieces
of the "wired public record" vision. Enterprise side is dominated by
well-funded incumbents: Palantir, Quantexa, LexisNexis Risk, Thomson Reuters
CLEAR, Kroll, Sayari. This is why the B2B/due-diligence lane is weak (caught
between free nonprofit tools and billion-dollar players); it's why the
journalism lane is comparatively strong (that business sells voice/trust on
top of the data, not the data infrastructure itself — nobody out-competes
Palantir by writing a better newsletter, because the newsletter isn't
competing with Palantir). Open, unverified question: whether the Hunch
Engine's no-predefined-question anomaly discovery is actually differentiated
vs. what Palantir/Quantexa already do internally — not confirmed either way,
worth checking before leaning on it as an edge.
