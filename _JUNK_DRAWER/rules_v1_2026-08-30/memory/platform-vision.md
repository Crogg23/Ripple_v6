Chris confirmed (2026-06-24, emphatically — "exactly the vision I have") this as the platform's true ceiling:

**Hold a meaningful chunk of the public record** — federal, state, local, international registries, courts, money, property, vessels, corporate filings — all in ONE place, all wired together by shared IDs (NPI, EIN, FIPS, CIK, lat/lon, name+place). The thing almost nobody has as a single queryable system; newsrooms rebuild it by hand per-investigation, data brokers sell only pieces.

What it enables:
- **Instant cross-domain dossier** — type a name/company/place → where they got money, who they're tied to, where they operate, what they've been sued for.
- **Stories raise their own hand** — stop hunting; the connection graph surfaces anomalies ("200 entities got federal money AND are foreign agents").
- **Publishing layer** — findings become beautiful interactive data-viz stories.

The honest frontier (where it gets hard): can't hold *literally* everything (paywalls, PDFs/leaks need extraction); the real ceiling is **entity resolution** — proving the same human/org across sources with no shared ID. That's exactly what the corroboration/fuzzy-match work creeps toward; cracking it levels the whole thing up.

Why it matters to Chris: this isn't a portfolio that *looks* professional — it's the actual toolkit of a digital investigative journalist, the thing that could replace the day job. Floor = "this guy can build." Ceiling = "this guy found a story nobody else could." Ties directly to the dual goal in [[CLAUDE.md]]. The spine already runs (638 tables, 12k+ connections, banned-providers story proven) — the question isn't *whether*, it's *how far you pour*.
