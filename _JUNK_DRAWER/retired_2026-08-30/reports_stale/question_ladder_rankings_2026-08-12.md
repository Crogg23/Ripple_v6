## A. Domain power rankings (all tiers combined)

1. **Health & medicine** — deepest bench in the file. ~15 SOLID tier-2 chains on the provider/facility
   ID spine, ~10 SOLID tier-3 mechanisms, 4 SOLID tier-4 dossiers. Direct body-count harm
   (exclusions, chemical restraint, nursing-home neglect, opioids). Weak spot: the entire opioid
   shipment family (178M rows) is FUZZY until a DEA→NPI bridge exists.
2. **Environment & pollution** — second strongest. Facility-ID family at 97–100% match rates plus
   the corporate parent roll-up gives 5+ SOLID corporate-accountability chains ready to run.
   Enforcement-vacuum shaped: "where did the regulator stop regulating."
3. **Labor & workplace** — best SOLID ratio in tier 2. Mine-ID and pension/EIN chains are complete-record;
   OSHA injury data has a real hours-worked denominator (rare). Thin spot: OSHA↔SEC overlap is
   only ~191 employers.
4. **Federal money** — strong UEI-spine chains (debarred-but-paid, bad-audit grantees keep money)
   but every count is a floor: contracts capped at exactly 20M rows, exclusions sampled.
5. **Courts & justice** — highest untapped harm density, ZERO SOLID chains. 118 strong tier-1
   questions (trial penalty, Chapter 13 trap) run fine single-source; everything cross-source waits
   on registering the court data's internal IDs into the connection map.
6. **Finance & markets** — one excellent SOLID chain (insider selling before pension failure); the rest
   waits on a securities-ID↔SEC-filer bridge. Much of tier 1 here is disclosure-mechanics trivia.
7. **Money in politics** — bifurcated. FEC-internal chains SOLID (dark-money operators); every chain
   that touches the real economy or Congress is name-match FUZZY. One cheap column check
   (FEC candidate ID in the legislator roster) flips the whole money→votes lane.
8. **Housing** — highest harm per question (modern redlining, algorithmic denial) but flagship
   mortgage data is a 17k-row sample; solid plumbing, no water until the full re-pull.
9. **Immigration** — small, harm-dense (citizens held on detainers, bond-priced freedom), half-blocked:
   the immigration-court table is a 12.6M-row husk with one real column.
10. **Corporate shells** — the platform's thesis (opacity as mechanism); best as fuel for higher tiers;
    UK ownership data truncated and the one probabilistic edge in the graph.
11. **Sanctions & enforcement** — conceptually strong, data-thin, zero verified joins; pure name-match
    territory until the alias-aware screening spine is built.
12. **Disasters / Transport / Energy** — pockets of sharp harm (FEMA's own recorded unmet need,
    recall-lag, grade crossings, high-hazard dams) amid truncated loads and unverified utility keys.
13. **Politics & government** — bulk is civic-metrics padding; sharp minority (rejected ballots,
    Wayback edit-tracking of the DOJ Epstein library — 1.54M captures, genuinely novel).
14. **Nonprofits / Food & ag / Science-education** — thin or blocked; nonprofits capped at 500k-row
    loads and a 200-row financials mart with an unwired 5.5M-row sibling.
15. **Crosscutting catalog (59 q)** — platform auditing itself; internal QA value, zero external harm;
    inflates the tier-1 headline count ~8–10%.

## D. The honest bad news (from the file itself)

- ~A quarter of tier 1 isn't currently buildable — caveats double as a defect registry.
- Question count ≠ askable count: 1,832 headline, but husks, caps, and samples gate a large share.
- The harm-heaviest sources are systematically the worst-loaded (opioids, courts, sanctions,
  immigration, device injuries, lobbying) — the influence/harm record is the least complete record.
- Politics has zero verified joins to the entity map; courts have zero verified joins to anything.
- ~16 broken-model bugs (HTML captured as data, header-eating loaders, number casts nulling
  country/county columns, leading-zero loss) — several silently kill joins.
- Only 57 of 475 tier-2 and 50 of 185 tier-3/4 questions are SOLID today. The SOLID core is
  health + environment + labor + federal money; everything else is one-to-three fixes away.
