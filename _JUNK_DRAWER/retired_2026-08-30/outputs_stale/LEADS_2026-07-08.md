## LEAD 1 — ICE started detaining the long-lawyered

**The hook.** For three straight years, ICE almost never detained immigrants whose court case
had a lawyer on record for 2+ years — 135 to 307 cases a quarter, nationwide, 0.5–0.9% of all
detentions. The quarter of the January 2025 inauguration, that number jumped to 731. It then
climbed every single quarter to 2,770 by Q4-2025. Since 2025-01-20: **~10,769 cases detained
whose counsel appeared a median 3.6 years earlier** (4,114 of them 5+ years earlier).

**Why it's surprising.** Everyone assumes the 2025 enforcement surge hit recent arrivals and
no-shows. This is the opposite population: people years into represented proceedings, showing
up to hearings — the group that was de-facto safe under every prior quarter in the data.

**What's actually going on.** The overall detention volume rose ~1.7×, but this category's
per-eligible rate rose ~6× (0.95 → 6.01 per 1,000 eligible cases/quarter vs the 2023-24
baseline) — so it's a targeting change, not just more enforcement. Mechanically this is what
courthouse/check-in arrests look like in case data: the person was findable precisely because
they were complying.

**Receipts.**
```sql
WITH f AS (SELECT TRY_TO_TIMESTAMP(SPLIT_PART(CASE_TYPE,'\t',11),'YYYY-MM-DD HH24:MI:SS.FF3') e28, TRY_TO_TIMESTAMP(SPLIT_PART(CASE_TYPE,'\t',32),'YYYY-MM-DD HH24:MI:SS.FF3') det, SPLIT_PART(CASE_TYPE,'\t',9) cust FROM LIBRARY_RAW.LANDING.FED_EOIR_CASE_DATA) SELECT DATE_TRUNC('quarter',det)::date qtr, COUNT(*) all_dets, SUM(IFF(e28 IS NOT NULL AND DATEDIFF('day',e28,det)>=730,1,0)) det_2yr_after_atty, ROUND(100.0*SUM(IFF(e28 IS NOT NULL AND DATEDIFF('day',e28,det)>=730,1,0))/COUNT(*),1) pct, SUM(IFF(det>='2025-01-20' AND e28 IS NOT NULL AND DATEDIFF('day',e28,det)>=730,1,0)) qtr_part_of_cumulative FROM f WHERE det >= '2022-01-01' AND det < '2026-06-01' GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1
```
- Key numbers (re-run 2026-07-08): quarterly counts 135/172/221/207 (2022) → 276/273/242/244
  (2023) → 260/280/297/307 (2024) → **731 / 1,292 / 1,883 / 2,770 (2025)** → 2,683 / 1,484
  (2026, Q2 partial). Share of all detentions: 0.5% → 4.8%.
- Baseline: 12 pre-2025 quarters, tight band 135–307. Sample: 12.6M EOIR cases.
- Note on the query: the EOIR landing load packed the source's tab-delimited row into
  `CASE_TYPE`; field 11 = attorney (E-28) entry timestamp, 32 = latest detention date,
  9 = custody code. The verifier validated the mapping against EOIR's FOIA schema.
- Confidence: **high** — single source, internal case-key join, no name matching. Fan-out
  clean (one row per case). 9 rows with impossible future detention dates excluded
  (10,778 → 10,769).
- Caveat: unit is *cases* (IDNCASE), not people — one respondent can hold multiple cases.

**Where to dig.** Split the cumulative cohort by court city and custody code (9,490 'D' vs
1,276 'R' at snapshot); top nationalities are MX 2,221 / GT 1,384 / HN 1,125. Cross against
news reports of courthouse arrests in the top venues. FOIA ICE for arrest-method (courthouse /
check-in / at-large) on this cohort. Kill test: if the E-28 field turns out to be re-stamped on
representation changes, the "years of counsel" median shrinks — verify on a 20-case sample via
EOIR's public case-status line.

## LEAD 2 — Doctors who eat on pharma's dime 200+ days a year

**The hook.** The median US prescriber who takes any industry meal takes them ~3 days a year.
But 427 doctor-years in 2022–2024 show a clinician taking industry meals on **200+ distinct
calendar days in a single year** — the record is 249 days, essentially every working day — and
the champions run 700+ meal-days over three years from up to 62 different manufacturers in one
year.

**Why it's surprising.** People know doctors get pharma lunches. They don't know there's a
cohort for whom the free meal is a *daily subscription* funded by dozens of direct competitors
simultaneously — and that it's visible, per-doctor, in a public file.

**What's actually going on.** These are reps' favorite offices — high-prescribing targets where
every manufacturer in a class buys the same access. And it correlates with behavior: the 2024
200+-day cohort (112 in Part D with ≥50 claims) has a median brand-name share of 17.8% vs 11.5%
for their own specialty's median — 85 of 112 sit above their specialty median. Psychiatry
dominates the top of the list.

**Receipts.**
```sql
WITH meals AS (SELECT NPI, PROGRAM_YEAR AS py, TO_DATE(DATE_OF_PAYMENT,'MM/DD/YYYY') AS pay_date FROM LIBRARY_RAW.LANDING.FED_CMS_OPEN_PAYMENTS WHERE NPI <> '' AND NATURE_OF_PAYMENT_OR_TRANSFER_OF_VALUE = 'Food and Beverage' UNION ALL SELECT NPI, PROGRAM_YEAR, TO_DATE(DATE_OF_PAYMENT,'MM/DD/YYYY') FROM LIBRARY_RAW.LANDING.FED_CMS_OPEN_PAYMENTS_2023 WHERE NPI <> '' AND NATURE_OF_PAYMENT_OR_TRANSFER_OF_VALUE = 'Food and Beverage' UNION ALL SELECT NPI, PROGRAM_YEAR, TO_DATE(DATE_OF_PAYMENT,'MM/DD/YYYY') FROM LIBRARY_RAW.LANDING.FED_CMS_OPEN_PAYMENTS_2022 WHERE NPI <> '' AND NATURE_OF_PAYMENT_OR_TRANSFER_OF_VALUE = 'Food and Beverage'), per AS (SELECT NPI, py, COUNT(DISTINCT pay_date) AS meal_days FROM meals GROUP BY 1,2), pd AS (SELECT NPI, PRSCRBR_TYPE, TRY_TO_NUMBER(TOT_CLMS) AS tot, TRY_TO_NUMBER(BRND_TOT_CLMS) AS brnd FROM LIBRARY_RAW.LANDING.FED_CMS_PART_D_PRESCRIBERS WHERE TRY_TO_NUMBER(TOT_CLMS) >= 50 AND TRY_TO_NUMBER(BRND_TOT_CLMS) IS NOT NULL), spec_base AS (SELECT PRSCRBR_TYPE, MEDIAN(100*brnd/tot) AS spec_median_brand FROM pd GROUP BY 1), cohort24 AS (SELECT pd.NPI, 100*pd.brnd/pd.tot AS brand_pct, sb.spec_median_brand FROM pd JOIN per p ON pd.NPI = p.NPI AND p.py = '2024' AND p.meal_days >= 200 JOIN spec_base sb ON pd.PRSCRBR_TYPE = sb.PRSCRBR_TYPE) SELECT (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM per) AS doctor_years, (SELECT MEDIAN(meal_days) FROM per) AS median_meal_days, (SELECT SUM(CASE WHEN meal_days >= 100 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) FROM per) AS dy_100plus, (SELECT SUM(CASE WHEN meal_days >= 200 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) FROM per) AS dy_200plus, (SELECT MAX(meal_days) FROM per) AS max_meal_days_one_year, (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM cohort24) AS cohort24_in_partd, (SELECT ROUND(MEDIAN(brand_pct),1) FROM cohort24) AS cohort_median_brand_pct, (SELECT ROUND(MEDIAN(spec_median_brand),1) FROM cohort24) AS matched_specialty_median_pct, (SELECT SUM(CASE WHEN brand_pct > spec_median_brand THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) FROM cohort24) AS cohort_above_spec_median
```
- Key numbers (re-run): 2,684,974 doctor-years · median 3 meal-days · 46,139 at 100+ ·
  **427 at 200+** · max 249 · cohort 17.8% vs 11.5% brand share, 85/112 above specialty median.
- Faces (from the drill-down, verifier-corrected): Vadim Baram (psychiatry, MO) — 721 distinct
  meal-days over 3 years, 40 manufacturers, $44,812. Richard Khalil (family practice, NY) — 714
  days, 71 manufacturers. Gustavo Alva (psychiatry, CA) — 712 days, $76,669.
- Baseline honesty: "median 3 days" is among prescribers with ≥1 meal; most prescribers take
  zero. Brand-share comparison uses the 660,655 Part D 2024 prescribers with ≥50 claims and
  unsuppressed brand counts (suppression makes the gap conservative, per sensitivity check).
- Confidence: **high** — NPI hard joins throughout; Open Payments RECORD_ID unique per year,
  year-tables disjoint.

**Where to dig.** These are named, licensed people — pull their top prescribed brands (needs
the Part D by-provider-and-drug file, not yet landed) and match against which manufacturers fed
them. The story writes itself as "a day in the calendar of America's most-fed doctor."
Correlation ≠ causation on brand share — say so; the meal-days number alone carries the piece.

## LEAD 3 — The $60,000.00 wage, priced to the penny of a 1998 statute

**The hook.** Outsourcing firms pay H-1B workers **exactly $60,000.00, thousands of times
over** — because $60k is the never-indexed 1998 threshold that lets an "H-1B-dependent"
employer skip attesting it tried to recruit US workers first. Certified filings by dependent
employers fall off a cliff at the line: 317 in the $55–60k band vs 10,774 in the $60–65k band
(34×). Non-dependent employers: 1.4×.

**Why it's surprising.** A wage floor set when Google was a Stanford dorm project still steers
a market to the penny in 2024-25 — and firms *voluntarily overpay* the legal prevailing-wage
minimum by $5–11k to buy their way out of the recruitment requirement. Where the prevailing
wage is $40–58k, dependent employers pay exactly $60,000 on 35.6% of filings vs 5.1% for
everyone else.

**What's actually going on.** ACWIA 1998 created the exemption ($60k salary OR a master's
degree) and Congress never indexed it; inflation quietly turned it into a bargain. The
premium-over-floor is the tell that this is deliberate statute-shopping, not wage-setting.

**Receipts.**
```sql
WITH w AS (SELECT H_1B_DEPENDENT dep, TRY_TO_DOUBLE(REPLACE(REPLACE(WAGE_RATE_OF_PAY_FROM_1,'$',''),',','')) wage, TRY_TO_DOUBLE(REPLACE(REPLACE(PREVAILING_WAGE_1,'$',''),',','')) pw FROM LIBRARY_RAW.LANDING.FED_DOL_OFLC WHERE VISA_CLASS='H-1B' AND WAGE_UNIT_OF_PAY_1='Year' AND CASE_STATUS='CERTIFIED') SELECT dep, COUNT(*) certified_filings, SUM(CASE WHEN wage>=55000 AND wage<60000 THEN 1 END) band_55_60, SUM(CASE WHEN wage>=60000 AND wage<65000 THEN 1 END) band_60_65, ROUND(band_60_65/NULLIF(band_55_60,0),1) cliff_ratio, SUM(CASE WHEN wage=60000 THEN 1 END) exact_60000, SUM(CASE WHEN pw>=40000 AND pw<58000 AND wage=60000 THEN 1 END) exact_60k_where_legal_min_under_58k, SUM(CASE WHEN pw>=40000 AND pw<58000 THEN 1 END) all_where_legal_min_under_58k FROM w WHERE dep IN ('Y','N') GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1
```
- Key numbers (re-run): dep=Y — 201,905 certified filings, 317 vs 10,774 (**34.0×**), 2,497 at
  exactly $60,000, 1,904/5,354 = 35.6% in the $40–58k-prevailing-wage group. dep=N — 337,915
  filings, 1.4×, 5.1%.
- Top exact-$60k filers, certified-only (verifier-corrected): Cognizant 770, Mphasis 269,
  HCL America 230, Capgemini 180, Wipro 167, L&T Tech 125. (An earlier "Marvell 310" was a
  same-named staffing shop with zero certified filings — dropped.)
- Confidence: **high** — single source (DOL OFLC disclosure), no cross-source join. The
  self-declared `H_1B_DEPENDENT` flag is the employer's own attestation.

**Where to dig.** Trend the cliff by filing year (is it growing as inflation makes $60k
cheaper?); the policy peg is that House/Senate bills to raise the threshold died repeatedly —
this is the dataset that shows what non-indexing costs. Clean legislative-accountability story.

## LEAD 4 — Judges time their retirements for the team — and 2020 is a smoking gun

**The hook.** In 2020, **zero of 86 eligible Democratic-appointed judges** took senior status —
0.0% — while Republican appointees took it at 14%. The moment Biden was sworn in, 36 of 110
eligible Democratic appointees took it (32.7%), and the Dem rate stayed at 31–37% for all four
Biden years while the GOP rate collapsed to 5.7%. Under Trump it runs the other way (GOP 26–28%
in 2017-18; 18.7% in 2025).

**Why it's surprising.** Senior status is semi-retirement at full pay — a personal, financial,
health-driven choice in theory. The data says it's a partisan act: judges sit on the decision,
sometimes for years, until their party controls the appointment.

**What's actually going on.** Life-tenured judges control the timing of their own replacement,
and they use it. The placebo makes it clean: senior-status events match the sitting president's
party 57.8% of the time; **deaths in active service — timing nobody chooses — match 45.0%**,
right at coin-flip.

**Receipts.**
```sql
WITH svc AS (SELECT s.NID, s.PARTY_OF_APPOINTING_PRESIDENT AS party, TO_DATE(NULLIF(s.COMMISSION_DATE,'')) AS comm_date, TO_DATE(NULLIF(s.SENIOR_STATUS_DATE,'')) AS senior_date, TO_DATE(NULLIF(s.TERMINATION_DATE,'')) AS term_date, TRY_TO_NUMBER(j.BIRTH_YEAR) AS birth_year, MIN(TO_DATE(NULLIF(s.COMMISSION_DATE,''))) OVER (PARTITION BY s.NID) AS first_comm FROM LIBRARY_RAW.LANDING.FED_FJC_SERVICE s JOIN LIBRARY_RAW.LANDING.FED_FJC_JUDGES j ON j.NID = s.NID WHERE s.PARTY_OF_APPOINTING_PRESIDENT IN ('Democratic','Republican') AND s.COURT_TYPE IN ('U.S. District Court','U.S. Court of Appeals') AND NULLIF(s.COMMISSION_DATE,'') IS NOT NULL), yrs AS (SELECT 2014+SEQ4() AS yr FROM TABLE(GENERATOR(ROWCOUNT=>13))), pool AS (SELECT y.yr, v.NID, v.party, IFF(v.senior_date IS NOT NULL AND YEAR(v.senior_date)=y.yr,1,0) AS took_senior FROM svc v CROSS JOIN yrs y WHERE v.comm_date < DATEADD(year, y.yr-2000, '2000-01-01') AND (v.term_date IS NULL OR v.term_date >= DATEADD(year, y.yr-2000, '2000-01-01')) AND (v.senior_date IS NULL OR YEAR(v.senior_date) >= y.yr) AND v.birth_year IS NOT NULL AND (y.yr - v.birth_year) >= 65 AND (y.yr - v.birth_year) + (y.yr - YEAR(v.first_comm)) >= 80 AND (y.yr - YEAR(v.first_comm)) >= 10 QUALIFY ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY y.yr, v.NID ORDER BY v.comm_date DESC) = 1) SELECT yr, party, COUNT(*) AS eligible, SUM(took_senior) AS took, ROUND(100.0*SUM(took_senior)/COUNT(*),1) AS hazard_pct FROM pool GROUP BY 1,2 ORDER BY 1,2
```
- Key numbers (re-run, full 2014–2026 table): 2020 Dem **0/86**; 2021 Dem 36/110; Dem 30.9–36.8%
  through 2024; 2023 GOP 5.7%; 2025 GOP 18.7% vs Dem 9.5%. Eligibility = Rule-of-80 + age 65 +
  10 yrs service (the statutory test).
- Honesty caveat (from the verify pass): the clean asymmetry runs **2017–2026**; 2015-16 invert
  (Obama-II is mixed). Print the whole table, not a cherry-picked window.
- Confidence: **high** — FJC's own biographical directory, hard dates, no joins across sources.
- Known-territory note: political scientists have papered strategic retirement; the 0/86 pandemic
  year and the death-placebo framing are the fresh, publishable quantification.

**Where to dig.** Name the 2021 wave (all 36 are individually identifiable); interview retired
judges — several have said this out loud. Follow-on: compute how many 2025-26 GOP senior-status
moves hand Trump II seats, live, as they happen.

## LEAD 5 — A sitting federal judge appears to have been a serial political donor

**The hook.** FEC itemized records show a donor "STAPLETON, WALTER" — occupation **"FEDERAL
JUDGE"**, employer **"US COURT OF APPEALS FOR THE THIRD CIRC"** — making 31 contributions
totaling $9,350 to the DNC, DSCC, DCCC, Emily's List, the Harris Victory Fund, Fetterman,
Casey, and Giffords across 2023–24. Walter K. Stapleton, the only Walter Stapleton ever to sit
on the Third Circuit, was alive and holding office as a senior judge the entire time (he died
2024-11-23). The Code of Conduct (Canon 5) flatly prohibits federal judges — including senior
judges — from making political contributions.

**Why it's surprising.** The ban is absolute and famous; judicial nominees get grilled over
$250 gifts from decades earlier. A sitting circuit judge writing his court's name on FEC forms
31 times, undetected, means **no one — not the FEC, not the judiciary — screens for this.**

**What's actually going on (baseline).** Across 84M itemized contributions, self-described
federal judges essentially never appear: the only other hits are three one-off small donations
by non-Article-III judicial officers (two magistrates, one CAAF judge). This is not a common
sloppy-form thing — it's one sustained donor.

**Receipts.**

  $1,750 · Harris Victory Fund $1,700 · DCCC $1,600 · DSCC $1,250 · Emily's List $1,100 ·
  Fetterman $750 · SMP $500 · Casey $400 · Giffords $300.
- Identity evidence: 22 of 31 rows carry the judge occupation/employer strings; 9 (the Victory

## LEAD 6 — Contracting offices price to $249,999 — and split buys to stay there

**The hook.** FY2025 federal contract awards pile up 6.1:1 in the $1,000 just *under* the
$250,000 simplified-acquisition threshold (1,172 vs 192) — 32 awards land at exactly $249,999 —
and some offices split one requirement into same-day pieces: an Army office issued **12
purchase orders to one vendor (Veterans Electrical Group) in a single day, $2.09M total, every
one under $250k**, spanning 12 of 13 consecutive solicitation numbers.

**Why it's surprising.** Under the threshold, purchases skip full competition and review. The
bunching shows the line is being *managed to*; the same-day split is the part with teeth —
FAR 13.003(c) explicitly prohibits splitting requirements to avoid the threshold.

**What's actually going on.** Simplified acquisition is faster for everyone — COs close files,
vendors quote to the line. The data can't distinguish officer-driven parking from
vendor-driven pricing (say "awards pile up," not "officers park"), but a 12-PO same-day split
with sequential solicitations is a discrete, named, FOIA-able event.

**Receipts.**
```sql
WITH awards AS (SELECT contract_award_unique_key k, MIN(period_of_performance_start_date) pop, SUM(TRY_TO_DOUBLE(federal_action_obligation)) amt FROM LIBRARY_RAW.LANDING.FED_USASPENDING_CONTRACTS GROUP BY 1), fy AS (SELECT amt FROM awards WHERE pop BETWEEN '2024-10-01' AND '2025-09-30') SELECT 200000 line, COUNT_IF(amt BETWEEN 199000 AND 199999.99) under_1k, COUNT_IF(amt BETWEEN 200000.01 AND 201000) over_1k FROM fy UNION ALL SELECT 250000, COUNT_IF(amt BETWEEN 249000 AND 249999.99), COUNT_IF(amt BETWEEN 250000.01 AND 251000) FROM fy UNION ALL SELECT 300000, COUNT_IF(amt BETWEEN 299000 AND 299999.99), COUNT_IF(amt BETWEEN 300000.01 AND 301000) FROM fy UNION ALL SELECT 500000, COUNT_IF(amt BETWEEN 499000 AND 499999.99), COUNT_IF(amt BETWEEN 500000.01 AND 501000) FROM fy
```
- Key numbers (re-run): $250k line **1,172 under vs 192 over (6.1:1)**; controls: $200k 1.6:1,
  $300k 1.6:1, $500k 2.9:1. Drill-downs (verified): 467 awards in $249,900–999.99 vs 18 just
  over (26:1); top bunchers Army 291 / DLA 266 / Navy 149; the split case = PIIDs
  W911S225PB054–PB101, solicitations U1695–U1707 (12 of 13), $2,093,099.01, max single PO
  $247,403.09.
- Grain honesty: award grain (transactions summed per `CONTRACT_AWARD_UNIQUE_KEY`), the known
  USASpending trap handled.
- Confidence: **high** — single source, internal aggregation.

**Where to dig.** FOIA the 12 Veterans Electrical POs and the missing 13th solicitation
(U1700); ask the issuing Army office (W911S2 = Fort Eustis/MICC) for the requirement docs. Then
generalize: the same-vendor-same-day-multi-PO-under-threshold pattern is one GROUP BY away from
a league table — that's the systemic story.

## LEAD 7 — Washington's Russia lobbying is now a sanctioned-oligarch delisting market

**The hook.** FARA has 248 Russia relationships in its entire history. Exactly **5 are active
today — and every one is an individual oligarch or executive, zero state agencies or
companies.** Four of the five are sanctioned somewhere; one is Rostec CEO Sergei Chemezov
(SDN-listed under the 2014 Ukraine EO and again 2022), whose Washington firm registered in
**May 2025** — eleven years after his first designation. The newest signing is April 2026.

**Why it's surprising.** The assumption is that sanctions ended Russia's Washington presence.
What actually happened: the state clients (banks, Sputnik-adjacent media) terminated within
days of the invasion — and a new market grew in their place, where the client is the sanctioned
individual and the product is getting *off* the list.

**What's actually going on.** Registering under FARA (rather than claiming the legal-services
exemption) is consistent with activity beyond pure litigation — delisting petitions, PR,
government outreach. Firms take these engagements because OFAC licenses make the fees legal.

**Receipts.**
```sql
WITH active AS (SELECT REGISTRANT_NAME, FOREIGN_PRINCIPAL, TRY_TO_DATE(FOREIGN_PRINCIPAL_REGISTRATION_DATE,'MM/DD/YYYY') reg_date, UPPER(SPLIT_PART(FOREIGN_PRINCIPAL,' ',1)) first_tok, UPPER(SPLIT_PART(FOREIGN_PRINCIPAL,' ',-1)) last_tok FROM LIBRARY_RAW.LANDING.FED_FARA_BULK WHERE SOURCE_FILE='FARA_All_ForeignPrincipals.csv' AND COUNTRY_LOCATION_REPRESENTED='RUSSIA' AND NULLIF(TRIM(FOREIGN_PRINCIPAL_TERMINATION_DATE),'') IS NULL) SELECT a.REGISTRANT_NAME, a.FOREIGN_PRINCIPAL, a.reg_date, MAX(o.SDN_NAME||' ['||o.PROGRAM||']') ofac_sdn_match, COUNT(DISTINCT o.SDN_NAME) sdn_fanout, MAX(SUBSTR(s.DATASET,1,60)) other_sanctions_match, COUNT(DISTINCT s.ID) os_fanout FROM active a LEFT JOIN LIBRARY_RAW.LANDING.FED_OFAC_SDN o ON TRIM(o.SDN_TYPE)='individual' AND UPPER(o.SDN_NAME) LIKE '%'||a.last_tok||'%' AND UPPER(o.SDN_NAME) LIKE '%'||a.first_tok||'%' LEFT JOIN LIBRARY_RAW.LANDING.INTL_OPENSANCTIONS s ON s.SCHEMA='Person' AND UPPER(s.NAME) LIKE '%'||a.last_tok||'%' AND UPPER(s.NAME) LIKE '%'||a.first_tok||'%' GROUP BY 1,2,3 ORDER BY 3 DESC
```
- Key numbers (re-run): Sher Tremonte → Alexey Kuzovkin (2026-04-02); Rathmell Short → **Sergei
  Chemezov + Vasily Brovko** (2025-05-20, both OFAC SDN, fan-out exactly 1); Vermillion
  Advisors → Eduard Khudainatov (2023-10-22, EU/Belgian/Canadian-sanctioned); Kobre & Kim →
  Roman Abramovich (2022-07-05, EU/UK-sphere sanctioned, not US). Baseline: 8 active on
  invasion eve; the state entities terminated Feb-Mar 2022; USSR-era 302 all terminated.
- Confidence: **high** on the FARA records (registrant's own filings); SDN name-matches
  verified 1:1 fan-out; cite Chemezov's exact designation dates from Treasury's site, not the

  (state clients → 100% sanctioned-individual clients) is the new frame.

**Where to dig.** Pull the actual FARA registration statements (Exhibit AB shows scope + fees)

## LEAD 8 — The IRS's two public answer-books disagree about 25,903 charities and $34B

**The hook.** The IRS publishes two files that answer "is this charity tax-exempt?": the
auto-revocation list and the Business Master File. They flatly contradict each other on
**25,903 organizations holding $34.2B in assets** — the revocation file says
revoked-and-never-reinstated, the BMF simultaneously carries them as status-01 exempt, and
22,194 of them have current (2024+) filing periods. Among them: UMSJ Health System (University
of Maryland, $605.6M revenue), the Ben and Catherine Ivy Foundation ($315.4M — one of the
largest private brain-cancer funders), and University of Minnesota Health Clinics.

**Why it's surprising.** Donors, grantmakers, and compliance software treat these files as
ground truth — foundations screen grantees against the revocation list. For ~18% of all
reinstatements, the IRS never updates the public record: 25,903/144,510 orgs that are exempt in
the BMF still read "revoked, never reinstated" in the other book.

**What's actually going on.** Most of these orgs really were revoked (usually for three years
of non-filing), then quietly fixed it — 18,171 have BMF ruling dates *after* their revocation,
i.e., they were re-approved — but the revocation file's reinstatement column was never
populated. The IRS's own FAQ concedes listed orgs "may have been reinstated"; the story is the
*scale* of the lag and who's caught in it.

**Receipts.**
```sql
WITH rev AS (SELECT EIN, MAX(TO_DATE(REVOCATION_DATE,'DD-MON-YYYY')) rev_dt, MAX(TO_DATE(REVOCATION_POSTING_DATE,'DD-MON-YYYY')) post_dt, MAX(COALESCE(EXEMPTION_REINSTATEMENT_DATE,'')) reinst FROM LIBRARY_RAW.LANDING.FED_IRS_REVOCATION GROUP BY EIN) SELECT CASE WHEN rev.reinst='' THEN 'never_reinstated' ELSE 'reinstated_recorded' END grp, COUNT(*) n_orgs, SUM(CASE WHEN b.EIN IS NOT NULL AND b.STATUS='01' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) in_bmf_status01, ROUND(100*in_bmf_status01/n_orgs,2) pct_in_bmf, ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN b.STATUS='01' THEN TRY_TO_DOUBLE(b.ASSET_AMT) END)/1e9,2) bmf01_assets_bn, SUM(CASE WHEN b.STATUS='01' AND b.TAX_PERIOD>='202401' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) filing_2024plus, SUM(CASE WHEN b.STATUS='01' AND TRY_TO_NUMBER(b.RULING)>=TRY_TO_NUMBER(TO_CHAR(rev.rev_dt,'YYYYMM')) THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) ruled_after_revocation FROM rev LEFT JOIN LIBRARY_RAW.LANDING.FED_IRS_BMF b ON rev.EIN=b.EIN WHERE rev.post_dt < '2024-01-01' GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1
```
- Key numbers (re-run): never_reinstated 888,228 → **25,903 in BMF status-01 (2.92%), $34.19B
  assets, 22,194 filing 2024+, 18,171 ruled after revocation**. Contrast group
  (reinstatement recorded): 72.93% in BMF. Strict slice (verified separately): 13,388
  independent, donor-deductible 501(c)(3)s, $9.25B.
- Confidence: **high** — EIN-to-EIN join between two IRS files; pre-2024 posting filter kills
  the "it just hasn't posted yet" excuse.
- Framing discipline: don't say "these charities aren't really exempt" — say the *public
  record* is wrong at scale, in the file people actually check.

## Second shelf — verified, one notch below the bar

- **SBA's $150k sweet spot** — 40,787 7(a) loans at exactly $150,000 (16× neighbors) where the
  85% guarantee maxes and the fee waives; when the waiver moved to $125k in FY2018, the pile
  moved with it (401 → 2,410) and snapped back. The default-cliff half of the story only holds
  for 3 specialist lenders (industry-wide +1.8pt ≈ round-number control) — ship the bunching +
  natural experiment, not "double defaults."
- **PPP's $2M audit cliff** — after Treasury announced audits for loans over $2M (Apr 2020),
  under/over density at $2M jumped 1.21× → 2.19× (4.47× in the 2021 reopening) while six
  placebo thresholds stayed flat; exact-$2,000,000.00 loans ("in excess of" spares them)
  doubled as a share. The "914 borrowers walked loans back" clause died in verification.
- **Pharma logs meals for deactivated NPIs** — 6,129 in-person meals recorded to 2,709 NPIs
  *after* NPPES deactivation (median 3 years after; 293 manufacturers), while CMS's claims side
  blocks the same IDs almost perfectly (4 of 1.4M). ~40-45% may be stale-ID misattribution to
  living doctors — which is itself a data-integrity story about the COI system of record.
- **The Predator spyware gap** — OFAC SDN remarks carry Irish registry numbers for 5 companies;
  the 3 Russia-program ones are in liquidation, but Intellexa Ltd + Thalestris Ltd (Predator
  network) are still status "Normal" in Ireland 28 months post-designation — and the EU has
  never sanctioned the network at all (0 of 5,994 EU-listed entities). Both look like abandoned
  shells (no returns since 2022) — that nuance must ship with it.
- **LEIE's structural blind spot** — people excluded by OIG get *fresh NPIs* the exclusion list
  can't see (LEIE carries no NPI for them). Verification melted the headline count to ~18
  middle-name-corroborated cases and exactly one corroborated 2024 Part D biller ($8.7k) — but
  the mechanism (every NPI-based screen is blind to re-enumeration) is real and checkable.
- **"Russia's UN isolation is over" — killed, instructively** — the raw agreement index did
  flip in session 79 (median country now votes with Russia more than pre-invasion), but ideal
  points are flat: it's the *agenda* that changed (Gaza-heavy votes isolate the US), not
  countries moving toward Moscow. Kept here as a caution — the index alone would have been a
  false story.
