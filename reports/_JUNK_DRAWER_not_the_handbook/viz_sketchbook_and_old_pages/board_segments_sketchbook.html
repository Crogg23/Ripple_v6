Sketchbook — not shipped

Thirteen more shapes, sizes, and concepts for Board Segments.

The generator ships eight scenes. This is the next round to pick from — seven variants on shapes already live (same color as the original), and six shapes that don't exist yet (teal, new family). Nothing here is wired into the generator. Look through it, mark which ones earn a build, and that becomes the next work — not a guess at what "more" meant.

Shape logic and vocabulary come from Deadspace & Counterweight. The eight shapes these build on are live in Board Segments.

Variants on the shipped eight

SAME MECHANISM, DIFFERENT HAND, ANGLE, OR SCALE — CHEAPEST TO BUILD, LOWEST RISK

V1 / CIRCLE IN A BOX — MIRRORED

Circle in a Box — Mirrored

Same geometry, opposite hand. The shipped scene always puts its hero on the left — this puts it on the right, pocket bottom-left.

Changeswhich side the eye lands on first
Use it whenthe scene before it already put its hero on the left — alternating hands stops a sequence from reading as a repeated template.
V2 / FULL-BLEED BAND — STACKED PAIR

Full-Bleed Band — Stacked Pair

Two full-bleed rows back to back usually reads as a mistake — the field guide says so. This is the exception: a tall hero band over a visibly thinner echo band reads as one composed unit, not a stutter, because the weights are clearly unequal.

Changeslets two full-width rows sit together on purpose
Use it whena hero finding needs a one-line footnote or source directly under it, not off to the side.
V3 / RADIAL STARBURST — EVEN SPOKES

Radial Starburst — Even Spokes

The shipped scene has 3 spokes at uneven angles, which reads as a specific found relationship. Six spokes at exactly 60° apart reads as a taxonomy — every branch weighted the same, on purpose.

Changesirregular → regular spacing, 3 → 6 spokes
Use it whenthe hub really does have equal-weight categories hanging off it, not one dominant branch and some minor ones.
V4 / TALL COLUMN — REVERSED GUTTER

Tall Column — Reversed Gutter

The main column moves to the right edge, gutters trail off to the left. Mirrors V1 — pairs the two so a sequence can alternate which edge carries the weight.

Changeswhich edge the tall element anchors to
Use it whenthe previous scene already anchored left — same reasoning as V1.
V5 / DENSE FIELD — REAL SMALL MULTIPLES

Dense Field — Real Small Multiples

The shipped scene fakes density with a decorative texture. When the content actually IS a small-multiples set, draw the real 8-cell grid — a reader can tell a stand-in from the real thing.

Changesdecorative texture → 8 real mini-chart cells
Use it whenthere's an actual repeated small chart to place, not just a "this feels dense" moment.
V6 / DIAGONAL SWEEP — STEEP CUT

Diagonal Sweep — Steep Cut

A shallow-angle cut (shipped) reads as "mostly one thing, small aside." A near-vertical cut reads as two co-equal halves — same mechanism, opposite weight balance.

Changesthe cut angle — shallow vs. near-vertical
Use it whenthe two things on either side of the line are genuinely equal in importance, not main-path-plus-aside.
V7 / CIRCLE IN A BOX — TWO SCALES

Circle in a Box — Two Scales

This is the "sizes" gap directly: the same shape family at hero scale and at support scale, in one frame, so the size relationship is a real choice — not every circle defaulting to the shipped 190px.

Changesa size ladder, not just a new shape
Use it whena scene needs a hero AND a smaller echo of the same shape, not a hero plus an unrelated stat box.

New concepts

NOT DERIVED FROM THE SHIPPED EIGHT — TEAL THROUGHOUT SO THEY READ AS A DISTINCT FAMILY

N1 / STAIRCASE CASCADE

Staircase Cascade

A ranked list — top costs, worst violators, biggest jumps — IS a staircase. Draw the actual rank order as the shape instead of hiding it inside a bar chart, then float the caption in the triangular void the steps leave above them.

Big shape4-6 descending bars, left to right, bottom-aligned
Fits the pocketa short caption naming what's ranked, sitting in the empty triangle over the shortest bars.
N2 / NESTED FRAME

Nested Frame

Every other shape here pushes its content to an edge to make a pocket. This does the opposite: center it, leave an even margin on all four sides. The margin is the whole point — it says "nothing competes with this."

Big shapeone shape, centered, equal gap on every side
Fits the pocketnothing — reserve it for the one number or one quote that needs zero competition, not a supporting fact.
N3 / SPLIT SPINE

Split Spine

Diagonal Sweep implies a main path and an aside. A straight, full-height spine implies two things read against each other — a claim and a rebuttal, before and after, two agencies compared. The spine is the argument, not a divider — hence the two different colors.

Big shapetwo equal columns, one thin dotted rule between them
Fits the pocketa matched pair of things — the same chart type twice, once per side.
N4 / ORBIT RING

Orbit Ring

Radial Starburst has a hub because one thing IS the center of the story. Orbit Ring has none — six satellites evenly spaced, center left deliberately empty. Use it when the finding is that six things are peers with no single controlling one.

Big shape6 equal-size satellites in a ring, hollow middle
Fits the pocketnothing in the center — the hollow middle is the finding (no controlling node), not free space to fill.
N5 / ANCHOR ROW + RAIL

Anchor Row + Rail

Not a corner pocket — the rail runs the full remaining height below the header row, so it fits something that applies to everything below it: a running total, a persistent legend, a source list.

Big shapea full-width header row + two stacked blocks + a narrow full-height rail
Fits the pocketa legend, a running count, a source note — anything that's true for the whole scene, not just one block.
N6 / TERRACE

Terrace

Full-width bands, alternately inset from the left and right edge, leave a zig-zag margin down both sides — not one corner pocket, but a run of small alternating pockets.

Big shape3-4 full-height bands, alternating which edge they're inset from
Fits the pocketshort alternating annotations — left note, right note, left note — one per zig-zag gap.
