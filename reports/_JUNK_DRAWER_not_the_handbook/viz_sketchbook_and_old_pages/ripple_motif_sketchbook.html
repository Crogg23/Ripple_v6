Four ways a ripple can mean consequence

Same motif, four different physics. Each one says something different about cause and effect. These are feel-checks — squint at the motion, ignore the polish. All four run on fake data.

Sketch A

The single drop

One event hits the water. Rings spread outward, and each ring it crosses is a layer of consequence — the violation, the penalty, the payout, the people. Clean, calm, legible.

one cause → ordered consequences
Where it winsInstantly readable. The rust dot is the sun of the page with zero explanation.
Where it breaksIt's orderly — says "consequence," not "chaos." Feels like a diagram, not a system.
Sketch B

Interference

Many events, dropping at random. Their rings collide and the surface becomes a pattern nobody designed. This is the chaos read: no single cause, only the weather of thousands.

many causes → emergent pattern
Where it winsBeautiful, alive, ambient. Says "the system is bigger than any one story."
Where it breaksNo protagonist. Nothing to point at. Weak as a portfolio "I built this" signal on its own.
Sketch C

The cascade

Not water — a web. One node fires and the pulse travels the connections, lighting up everything downstream. This is cause-and-effect as your warehouse actually stores it: entities joined to entities.

one node fires → the network answers
Where it winsTruest to the mission — "connecting the dots" made literal. Strongest craft signal.
Where it breaksNode graphs are a portfolio cliché if drawn lazily. Needs restraint to not look like every "big data" stock image.
Sketch D

The struck surface

A calm grid of dots — the public record at rest. One strike displaces it and a real wave rolls through the field, bending everything it passes. Order and disruption in one frame.

the record at rest → disturbed by one event
Where it winsBest of both: the calm grid says scale and rigor, the wave says consequence. Very "intentional first visual."
Where it breaksSubtle — on a fast skim it can read as decoration before the wave hits.

How to choose: A is the clearest, B is the most atmospheric, C is the most on-mission, D is the most composed. They also combine — a struck surface (D) whose wave travels along real connections (C) is probably the ceiling. Pick the feel; the real data wiring comes after.
