# Skin substitutes: $1.13B to 20 billers, $77K in reported maker payments

Lead F-033, widened from one Bakersfield surgeon to the national top 20.
Every name below is a **data match, not verified against primary records.**
Data years: Part B is 2024 (the only year in the warehouse). Open Payments is 2022, 2023 and 2024.

---

## The one-line story

In 2024, 20 clinicians billed Medicare **$1.13B** for skin substitutes (cell and tissue sheets laid on wounds, billed per square centimeter).
Over three years, drug and device makers reported paying those same 20 people **$76,937**. That's about $1 reported for every $14,600 billed.
**$64,529 of it went to one man**, Bakersfield general surgeon Ravi Kapadia, and it came from Organogenesis. The products he actually billed (Helicoll, Derm-maxx) aren't Organogenesis products.
The one maker the warehouse can tie to a top product is EnColl Corp., which makes Helicoll. **EnColl filed zero Open Payments reports in 2022-24.**
Two of the 20 are Phoenix-area nurse practitioners who billed only one product, Impax. Both now sit on the federal exclusion list as "proceedings pending." The federal court index shows a one-count health-fraud conspiracy case filed in Phoenix in June 2025 under each surname, one day apart.

**Short version:** the biggest skin-substitute money doesn't show up in the government's payments-to-doctors ledger. Where it can be traced, it goes to court instead.

## Chain

### 1. Part B service file → the biller list
- **Checked:** Q4100-Q4399 rows. Allowed = average allowed × services, summed per NPI. Product = the code's own description.
- **Result:** 1,153 billers, $4.62B. Five products take 59%: Membrane Wrap $849M, Complete FT $619M, Esano ACA $572M, Restorigin $389M, Helicoll $318M. Big-company products (Apligraf, EpiFix, Grafix) aren't in the top 30.
- **Kapadia (NPI 1669736427):** Q4164 Helicoll, 28 patients, 55,394 sq cm, $86.5M. Q4238 Derm-maxx, 11 patients, $5.3M. The application codes (15271-15274) add up to roughly the same area, so the product units and the procedures agree with each other.
- **Hit means:** a handful of people bill brand-new products at a scale no peer comes close to.
- **Miss would have meant:** big wound centers with big patient counts. Kapadia's are the opposite: 28 patients.
- **Trap applied:** the service file deletes rows under 11 patients. Kapadia's visible rows cover 94.9% of his payment, so little is hidden. Patient counts are floors: the largest single code's count, or 35 from the provider file.

### 2. Part B provider file → totals
- **Key:** NPI, 30 of 30 matched.
- **Kapadia:** $97.07M allowed in total, $96.75M of it drugs and biologicals, 35 drug patients, 113 patients overall.
- **Per patient on the provider-file count:** $2.76M. On the largest code's count: $3.28M. Either way he's still #1.

### 3. Open Payments → the portfolio table
- **Key:** NPI, across all three years.
- **Checked:** 218 payment groups to the top 30. For the 9 surnames with thin or zero results, a fallback on name + state found **no payments hiding under another NPI**, so the NPI join is complete.
- **Result:** 4 of the top 20 have zero payments on record. 10 got anything from a wound company. Without Kapadia, the other 19 got $12,408 in total over three years, almost all of it meals.
- **Kapadia:** Organogenesis paid him $62,861 in 2023-24 ($63,088 counting 2022). That's $48,675 in speaker fees, $11,168 travel, $3,018 food. **The address on those payments is Huntington, WV.** His Medicare billing and NPPES record are in Bakersfield, CA. Same NPI and same Open Payments profile (809127), so it's one person with two addresses. Why WV isn't known.
- **Maker check:** the names of skin-substitute firms were searched across all makers in all three years:
  - EnColl: **0 rows.** It's the one maker the warehouse can link, since FDA registration ties Helicoll to it.
  - Legacy Medical Consultants: **0 rows**, but 8+ federal contract suits against providers in 2025-26.
  - BioLab Sciences: $200K to 6 NPIs, 2022 only.
  - Organogenesis paid $2.02M to 9,084 NPIs in 2024, 10% of it to skin-substitute billers.
- **Hit would have meant:** the top billers take money from the makers whose products they bill.
- **Miss means:** they don't, **or the money moves through someone who doesn't have to report to Open Payments.** This data can't tell those two apart.

## Weak points a hostile editor would hit

1. **Open Payments only covers "applicable manufacturers."** A distributor or sales firm selling someone else's graft may not have to report. The $77K could be a legal blind spot, not hidden money. That's the story either way, but say it plainly.
2. **Product-to-maker is proven for one product only**, Helicoll → EnColl, via FDA registration. Membrane Wrap, Esano, Complete FT, Impax and Orion aren't in GUDID or 510(k), since tissue products usually aren't. Their makers come from outside sources, not this warehouse.
3. **Only one year of Part B (2024).** "When did it start" rests on PECOS enrollment dates (Kapadia CA 2022-02), not billing history.
4. **Patient counts are floors, and per-patient math depends on them.** $2.76M to $3.28M per patient, depending on the count used.
5. **The court links are name joins.** Denny and Palacios are common surnames. Only the docket-to-FJC step uses a real key. The case-to-person step still needs the complaint or information.
6. **"Allowed" isn't "paid."** Kapadia's actual Medicare payment was $77.3M.
7. **Kapadia's Organogenesis money is legal on its face.** Speaker fees get reported. The oddity is only that he bills other companies' products, and the Huntington WV address.
8. **The Open Payments tables are general payments only.** Ownership stakes and research payments sit in separate CMS files not checked here. A doctor who owns part of a distributor would show up there, not here.
