# Texas hospice farms: one official, one building, many hospices

Every person or company named here is a **data match, not verified against primary records.**
SQL: `j5.sql` (16 statements, 3 of them failed or broken and marked). Raw results: `j5/*.json`.

**The one-line story:** In San Antonio, 36 of 282 active hospice provider IDs (12.8%) belong to "farms": one authorized official listing 4+ differently named hospices at one street address. Nationwide outside California, Houston and San Antonio it's 53 of 11,472 (0.5%). Four Texas clusters, one official or one promoter each, span 68 hospice IDs. Five of them are the Houston "Suite 610, Rooms A to E" hospices, and Medicare's provider registry names who runs them: Benjamin Arise signs for four, Adebayo Oshinuga for the fifth.

## Chain

**1. Roster to enrollment, on CCN** (statements 2, 6)
- Checked: 358 Harris + Bexar hospices; 299 have an enrollment row, 59 don't (Harris 48, Bexar 11).
- Sample: 971602 Santa Rita Home Health → Dos Hermanas Ortiz Inc; A91712 Limitless Hospice LLC; 971545 Anchorage Hospice Inc; 971535 Magnolia Hospice Company → Nosotros Hospice Inc; 741740 Changing Seasons Hospice → AIP Hospice LLC.
- A hit gives the legal company behind the brand. A miss means never enrolled, revoked, or not yet posted. The skeptic already showed that missing enrollment rows are a four-state pattern (22% in CA/NV/AZ/TX, 3% elsewhere), not a Houston one.

**2. NPPES to roster, on NPI or name + city** (statements 3, 5, 6)
- Checked: 68 cluster IDs. 30 tie to a certified CCN: 18 by NPI through the enrollment file, 12 by exact name (INC/LLC stripped) **and** the same city.
- Sample: 1639894066 Origins Hospice Inc → A91592 (name+city); 1518633973 Distinguished Hospice Inc → A91574 (NPI); 1316539273 Birch Hospice LLC → 971787 (NPI); 1245902899 CFHC NO4 Inc → A91631 Ariel Hospice (NPI); 1366038143 New Dawn Hospice Inc → 971743 (NPI).
- **Caught and fixed:** my first name matcher had no city check. It tied Arise's "Harmony Hospice, Inc." (Houston) to CFHC NO13's "Harmony Hospice Care" (San Antonio). With the city check that match is gone.
- A hit ties a registry official to a certified hospice. A miss means the ID never reached certification, or it did under a brand name I can't see.

**3. The farm rate, national peer group** (statement 5)
- Checked: 18,159 active hospice organization IDs nationally. A "farm" = one official (first + last name), one street address (suite stripped), 4+ distinct legal names. 62 farms, 308 IDs.

- Some farms are real chains filing one entity per state: ProHealth (AL), Great Plains Hospice (NE), Superior Hospice's numbered LLCs (San Antonio). The 0.5% already includes them.
- The Texas farms are single-city and mostly same-season: the tree LLCs over 5 months, the CFHC corps over 3 weeks, Suite 610 in one week.

## Weak points a hostile editor would hit

- **No money.** The warehouse has no hospice claims, payments, live-discharge or quality data. I can't say any of these hospices billed Medicare a dollar. That is the biggest hole.
- **Consultant, not criminal.** Jennifer Roy lists herself as "administrator" on 10 hospices and is the official of J Roy Consulting Inc on the same phone. That reads like a **licensing/credentialing consultant** who registers hospices for clients. The brand swaps (New Dawn → Compassion Hospice of Texas) fit that too. The same boring story may explain Gleason/Lozano and the CFHC corps: a formation shop selling ready-to-license hospice corporations. That business can be legal. Buying a new Medicare hospice ID to skip the wait is exactly what CMS's 2023 hospice rules target, but that's context, not proof.
- **Name matching.** 13 of the 30 certified ties are exact name + same city, not an ID. Generic names like "Specialty Hospice" could in principle collide. Each was eyeballed; the suite/address also agrees for the Houston ones.
- **The "authorized official" is not the owner.** NPPES lists who signed the ID application. The owner file isn't landed.
- **Farm rule is my rule.** 4+ distinct names, one official, one address. Legit multi-state chains also trip it (ProHealth, Great Plains). They're in the 0.5% baseline, which makes the gap conservative.
- **Enrollment gaps are statewide.** "Not in the enrollment file" is a CA/NV/AZ/TX pattern (22% vs 3%), so it isn't evidence against these five in particular.
- **Old news risk.** Houston and San Antonio hospice fraud has been prosecuted and CMS put Texas under enhanced hospice oversight. I checked federal dockets for these names and found nothing, but I can't search news from here.
