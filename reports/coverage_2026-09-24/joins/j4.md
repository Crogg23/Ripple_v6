# Pills 2006-12, deaths 2019-24: the staircase, Knox County, and who shipped to the Food City cluster

j4, 2026-09-24. Python door, tag `joins-2026-09-24`. **7 statements** (1 failed on a column name), all SELECT/WITH, in `j4.sql`.
Raw outputs and local math: `j4/` (q01-q07 TSVs, `an3.py` staircase + controls, `an4.py` distributors, `an5.py` Knox).
Every pharmacy, distributor or company named here is a data match, not verified against primary records.

---

**The one-line story:** US counties that got the most opioid pills per person in 2006-12 buried **2.0x** as many people per capita to overdose in 2019-24 as the fewest-pill counties (40.8 vs 20.2 per 100k). Knox County TN sits in that top group at **74.5**, 3.3x the median US county. Four Knoxville Food City pharmacies took **42% of the county's strongest oxycodone**. One distributor, AmerisourceBergen, shipped **87%** of the six-store cluster's 30mg+ oxycodone, at a **61%** strong mix. The same distributor sent other Tennessee Food City stores a **12%** mix.

⚠ **Two corrections to the ledger line (F-002):**
- **"2.7x" is the county-average number.** It reproduces as 2.6x when you take the median county in each group and keep only counties with all six years unsuppressed. Counted per person (all deaths ÷ all people in the group), it's **2.0x**. Use 2.0x in the chart.
- **Most of the gap is counties that were already dying in 2006-12.** Controlling for the 2006-12 overdose baseline shrinks the pill slope by about 70%. The part left is real (t = 6.5) but small. Details in Chain 4.

## Chain

### 1. ARCOS → county (pills per person, 2006-12)
- **Checked:** summed `DOSAGE_UNITS`, code `S` only, by buyer first, then by the buyer's county. 76.6B pills total. 453 buyers with no county hold 101M pills (0.13%), dropped.
- **Mail-order trap, fixed.** The top buyers are not local pharmacies. VA mail-order in North Charleston SC (512M pills) and in Leavenworth KS (105M). Kaiser central fill in Livermore and Downey CA (354M, 176M). OptumRx, Walgreens Mail, Humana, Express Scripts-style mailers.
  - Removed: any buyer over 40M pills, plus name patterns MAIL, OPTUMRX, HUMANA PHARMACY, EXPRESS SCRIPTS, MEDCO, CAREMARK, INJURED WORKERS, ENCLARA, PMSI.
  - Charleston County drops from 245 to 34 pills per person per year. Leavenworth drops from 224 to 24. Knox is untouched.
- **Denominator:** 2006-12 average population from the NCHS file. ÷ 7 years = pills per person per year.
- **Hit means:** the county's pharmacies and doctors got that many pills. **Miss means:** 115 tiny counties (Alaska boroughs, Kalawao HI) show no shipments. Most have no pharmacy. Left out, not counted as zero.
- **Eyeballed 5 rows:** Kent MI 603K people, 128M pills, 6 clean years, 14.7/100k. Allen KY, Fannin GA, Green WI, Buffalo NE all look sane.

### 2. CDC injury county (deaths 2019-24)
- Traps applied: `COUNT_SUP` **is** the count, null means CDC suppressed 1-9 deaths. `RATE` = -999 is a placeholder. Kept rows with a count and `RATE >= 0`.
- **The suppression problem.** Only 862 of 3,026 counties have all six years unsuppressed. Dropping the rest drops small rural counties, and those are the pill-heavy ones.
- **Fix:** group rate = (known deaths + 5 for each suppressed year) ÷ (2020 population × 6). The bounds put 1 or 9 in place of the 5. At 20K people and up, the bounds move the top/bottom ratio only 1.83-2.23x.
- **Hit means:** real deaths, 2019-24. **Miss means:** 6 CDC counties are not in NCHS (Alaska re-codes, Oglala Lakota 46102). Out.

### 4. The control: the 2006-12 baseline
- **Baseline:** the NCHS modeled age-adjusted drug-poisoning rate, averaged over 2006-12. It comes as bands like 12.1-14; I used band midpoints, and ">30" = 31.
  - ⚠ Top-coded at 30. Knox is not capped (19.6). Some Appalachian counties are.
- Pills and baseline move together: correlation 0.61 across 20K+ counties. **The pill-heavy counties were already the dying counties.**
- **Population-weighted regression**, log(2019-24 rate) on log(pills per person):

| Model | Pill slope | t |
|---|---|---|
| Pills alone | 0.457 | 21.0 |
| Pills + 2006-12 baseline | **0.133** | **6.5** |

  - Read it: doubling pills goes with about 37% more deaths alone, and about 10% more once baseline is held fixed. Baseline t = 31.5.
- **Same-baseline groups:** split counties into 5 baseline groups. In each, compare the top fifth by pills to the bottom fifth (per-person rates).

| Baseline group (2006-12 rate) | Low-pill rate | High-pill rate | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.6-6.8 | 12.7 | 24.6 | **1.94** |
| 6.8-8.8 | 17.3 | 24.0 | 1.39 |
| 9.1-11.1 | 21.6 | 24.9 | 1.15 |
| 11.1-14.5 | 32.6 | 31.1 | 0.95 |
| 14.5-31.0 | 40.6 | 50.6 | 1.25 |

- **What it means:** the pill effect is real but not the 2x headline. It's clearest where deaths started low: those counties got flooded and caught up. Where deaths were already high, pills add little on top.
- ⚠ **Hostile-editor footnote:** the 2006-12 deaths are partly caused by the same 2006-12 pills. So controlling for them undercounts the pill effect. The truth sits between 0.13 and 0.46.

### 5. Knox County TN and its neighbors
- **Knox:** 71.0 pills per person per year, #139 of 1,832 (top group). Its death rate is 74.5, **#27 of 1,832**. Deaths outrank pills, so Knox dies at 1.8x its own pill group's rate (40.8).
- Knox's 2006-12 baseline (19.6) was already top-tier, and it rose to 27 by 2015.
- TN median county at 20K+: 60.8 pills, 35.2 deaths. US median: 37.9 pills, 22.5 deaths.

### 6. Who shipped to the Knoxville Food City stores (ARCOS reporter)
- **Name trap handled.** Filtered `BUYER_NAME ILIKE 'FOOD CITY%'` to TN/VA/KY/GA only, so the 16 unrelated Arizona "Food City" stores are out. That leaves 77 stores.
- **The cluster** is the six TN stores at 39%+ strong oxycodone: #616, #674, #694, #682 (Knoxville), #644 (Seymour), #632 (Loudon). It's a slope, not a wall: #687 Knoxville runs 38.3% and #650 Lenoir City 35.6%.
- Six stores: 31.9M pills, **19.8M oxycodone 30mg+ (62%)**.

| Distributor (ARCOS reporter) | DEA no. | Strong oxy to cluster | Share of cluster's strong oxy | Strong share of what it shipped | Years |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AmerisourceBergen Drug, Pelham AL | RA0223432 | 17,194,460 | **86.9%** | 61.1% | 2006-12 |
| Masters Pharmaceutical (family: River City Pharma), Cincinnati OH | RD0277409 | 2,321,600 | 11.7% | **72.1%** | 2008-11 only |
| McKesson, Duluth GA | PR0040357 | 257,700 | 1.3% | 59.4% | 2012 only |
| Harvard Drug Group, Livonia MI | RG0131499 | 9,600 | 0.0% | 8.6% | 2006-11 |

- **Peer check, same distributor, same chain:** AmerisourceBergen shipped the other **50 TN Food City stores** 34.6M pills at a **12.0%** strong mix. The cluster got a **61%** mix. **Same wholesaler, same chain, five times the strong share.**
- **#674 alone:** 95.8% of its strong oxycodone came from AmerisourceBergen Pelham, 2006-12.
- **#644 Seymour is the odd one:** Masters shipped it more strong oxycodone (567K) than AmerisourceBergen did (413K). 83% of what Masters sent there was 30mg+.
- **Masters is the add-on supplier chain-wide:** 2008-11, 45 of the 50 other TN stores, at a 23.7% mix. Its share was far heavier in the cluster.
- **By year:** the cluster's AmerisourceBergen strong pills ran 1.7M (2006) → 2.9M (2008) → 2.8M (2011) → 2.6M (2012). Masters rose from 10K (2008) to 958K (2011), then stopped. McKesson appears in 2012 at all six stores.
- **Knox County context:** the four Knox cluster stores took 18.75M of the county's 44.2M strong oxycodone pills (**42%**). #674 alone took 31%. The other 125 Knox pharmacies ran a 13.5% strong mix, served mostly by Cardinal Health Knoxville, AmerisourceBergen and Walgreens.
- **Hit means:** these distributors filed DEA reports of shipping these pills to these DEA numbers. **What it can't say:** who wrote the prescriptions. ARCOS has no prescriber.

## Weak points a hostile editor would hit

- **"Correlation, not cause", and it's mostly confounded.** About 70% of the slope goes away with the 2006-12 baseline. Don't headline 2x as a pill effect. Headline it as "the flooded counties are still the dying counties."
- **Different rate types.** The 2019-24 rate is crude (deaths ÷ people). The baseline is modeled, age-adjusted and banded. Fine for a control. Not fine for a "rates tripled" line.
- **Imputed suppressed years.** Put 5 into 4,154 suppressed county-years in the 20K+ set. The bounds are shown, and the ratio stays 1.83-2.23x.
- **Buyer county ≠ where people live.** Pills cross county lines. Knox is the regional hub, and its pharmacies serve Campbell, Claiborne and Union. That helps explain Knox's high pill count and the neighbors' high deaths.
- **Mail-order filter is a rule I chose** (over 40M pills, or a mail-order name). Without it, Charleston SC and Leavenworth KS land in the top group with no local flood.
- **2019-24 deaths are mostly fentanyl.** Pills 2006-12 to fentanyl deaths 2019-24 is a 7-17 year chain through heroin. The data can't show the chain.
- **Food City:** the 39% cutoff that makes "six stores" is mine, and the next two stores run 38% and 36%. AmerisourceBergen being the chain's main wholesaler is normal. The point is the **mix** it shipped, not that it shipped.
- **ARCOS has no prescriber.** A high strong-oxy share could be local pain clinics sending patients to the nearest counter, not the pharmacy's own doing.
