---

**The one-line story:**
One St. Louis owner's 31 Missouri nursing homes give residents **35% fewer nurse hours** than other Missouri for-profit homes (2.17 vs 3.35 hours per resident per day), even though their residents are sicker. They draw **3.9x the harm citations** and **4.4x the fine dollars per bed**. They took about **$12.2M in Covid relief**, and the owner gave about **$160K to federal candidates of both parties**.

## Weak points a hostile editor would hit

1. **The before/after is small.** 8 → 15 citations is not significant. Don't lead with it. Most homes were Reliant's long before any data in the file, so a true before/after exists for only 11 homes.
2. **Chain label disagreement.** CMS puts Cassville (MO) and Holton (KS) in chain 446. NursingHome411 labels them Prime Healthcare Management and Anew Healthcare. Cassville has no Reliant owner row: drop it and MO is 30 homes. Holton became Reliant's on 2025-01-25, and its bad record is pre-Reliant.
3. **Complaint-driven counts.** Most harm came from complaint surveys, and Reliant homes get about twice the survey visits. Per survey visit the gap is still 2.0x (skeptic pass), but a defender will say "more complaints, more citations."
4. **Staffing is one snapshot**, the current CMS provider file. It is not a time series, so "understaffed since takeover" cannot be shown.
5. **PRF is a name match with no payment date.** Amounts are floors: Bernard and Milan have no line. There is no date field, so "money after the takeover date" cannot be checked. It only matters for the 2020 homes (Edgewood, Nathan Richard).
6. **FEC is a surname + state + employer match.** Conduit rows (WinRed, ActBlue, victory funds) may double count a few thousand dollars. "About $160K" is itemized gifts, not a verified total.
7. **"Both parties" can read as a smear angle.** Giving is legal. Present it as context: who the owner supports while the homes draw fines. Don't imply a link.
8. **Court disposition codes.** 13 is FJC's code for "settled". I have not checked it against the codebook.
9. **The 300A injury rate** comes from 4 to 10 self-filed logs a year. 2024 is at peer level.
10. **Known story risk.** CMS's own star rating already says 1.19 stars and 5 special-focus candidates. The new part is the owner join, the staffing gap and the money, not "bad homes."
