## POLITICS__WHO_WON: live (modest)

**The lead**
- Georgia had 69 U.S. House general elections in 2010-2018. **20 had no runner-up (29%).**
- Nationally it was 86 of 2,175 (4.0%). The median state had 0.
- Alabama came next with 9 of 35 (26%). Oklahoma had 3 of 25 (12%).
- **Time:** in 1976-1984 Georgia was at 34% (17 of 50). The nation was at 7.6% (165 of 2,175).
  - The nation's rate halved. Georgia's barely moved.

**Checked so it isn't a few units or bad rows**
- The 20 races spread across 11 of Georgia's 14 districts. Republicans won 15, Democrats 5.
- In 15 of the 20, total votes equal the winner's votes (share 1.0). The other 5 are 99.5%+, where write-ins take the rest.
- Georgia has no filler "1" vote rows. Florida has 90 and Louisiana 70, so those two states' counts mean something else (see traps).

**Chain**
- Checked: seat-elections with no runner-up (or a vote share of 100%), per state, 2010-2018 against 1976-1984.
- Hit means: Georgia voters had no choice on the House ballot about seven times as often as the national rate (29% vs 4.0%), and the rate was just as high in 1976-1984. The years in between weren't tested.
- Miss would have meant Georgia was near the 4% national rate. It isn't.

**Dull explanation**
- One-party districts. But every state has those, and the median state still had zero.
- The likely mechanism is Georgia's petition rule for independent and third-party House candidates. It's widely reported as the strictest in the country and has been fought in federal court.
- **Not tested.** The warehouse has no ballot-access table. Alabama, in second place, also has a hard petition rule, which fits.

**Why it's only modest**
- It's a known pattern. And the table **stops at 2018 for the House**, so a story needs 2020-2024 from `FED_MEDSL_HOUSE_RETURNS` first.

**Traps (for the ledger)**
- **The series stops.** House runs 1976-2018, President 1976-2016, Senate 1976-2024. The catalog calls it "every federal election."
- **House special elections are missing.** All 29 IS_SPECIAL rows are Senate races.
  - Texas is short 10 seats in 1996 (20 of 30) and 4 in 2006 (28 of 32).
  - Likely cause: those were the court-redrawn districts that Texas ran as November specials. Not checked.
- **BIOGUIDE is matched on surname + state + chamber + term span, and it's wrong on at least 77 rows.**
  - 154 rows (23 IDs) put one member ID on two different seats in the same year.
  - Phillip Burton's wins carry John Burton's ID. Dan Daniel carries Robert Daniel's. Philip Crane carries Daniel Crane's. Sam B. Hall carries Ralph Hall's. Bob Smith (OR-2) carries Denny Smith's.
  - The winner's first initial disagrees with the matched member's on 576 rows. Most are nicknames (Bill/William). Some are family handoffs:
    - Susan Molinari's rows carry Guy Molinari's ID.
    - Gillis Long's carry Catherine Long's.
    - William Coyne's carry James Coyne's.
    - Michael Myers's carry Gary Myers's.
  - So the true bad count sits between 77 and 576. Any join to votes or money through BIOGUIDE or ICPSR inherits it.
- 683 House winners (7%) have no BIOGUIDE at all.
- WINNER_VOTES = 1 is filler on 185 rows (181 House, 4 Senate). Florida doesn't print unopposed races, and Louisiana's open primary decided races in October.
- The winner's name isn't a key: one name wins two House seats in every year from 2002 to 2012. That's two people who share a name (which name wasn't checked).
- WINNER_PARTY case isn't cleaned: "democrat" and "DEMOCRAT" both appear.
- **Tooling trap:** the catalog profile cache said Lloyd Doggett won 59 rows. The real count is 11. Approximate top-k counts on near-unique columns are fiction.

## POLITICS__CA_LOBBY_CONTRIBUTIONS: probed

**Shape:** 6,505 rows, $12.9M. Filing periods run 2000 Q1 to 2001 Q2 ($11.86M in 2000, $1.03M in 2001).

**Join: who the filers are**
- FILER_ID was matched against the CA lobby lists (employers, firms, lobbyists).
- **96% of the money lands** on one of them. Employers alone carry 89.5%.
- `CA_LOBBY_COVER` is no use as a classifier here: 5,512 of 5,644 rows from 2000 have no filer there.

**Gray Davis**
- Recipient ID 1069390 took $1.60M from 88 filers, under 15 spellings. That's 12.4% of all the money.
- Matching on his name instead gives $1.75M.

**Time test: did Prop 34's lobbyist ban show up?**
- Prop 34 barred registered lobbyists from giving to the state officers they lobby, starting Jan 1, 2001 (date not verified).
- Q1 to Q1, lobbyist-matched filers fell from $405K to $15,775 (-96%). Employers, who aren't covered by the ban, fell 78%.
- **But one filer is $365K of that $405K.** Take it out and lobbyists fell 61%, which is less than employers.
- So there's no ban effect you can defend. 2000 was an election year, and that drop hits everyone.

**What kept coming after the ban (the lead to chase)**
- Lobbyist-matched IDs reported $16,425 in 2001:
  - John Kimbrough (GlaxoSmithKline, per the 1999 CA lobbyist list): $5,000 to "The Governor Gray Davis Committee," plus about $5,100 to seven legislative candidates.
  - Mark Battaglini (Schering-Plough): $3,250 to four candidates.
  - Karen Bowman (AstraZeneca): $1,000 to two.
- Dull explanation: the lobbyist report (Form 615) asked for contributions the lobbyist **made or delivered**. An in-house lobbyist handing over the company PAC's check reports it too.
- **The table has no made/delivered field.** This is a records check on the original Form 615, not a finding.

**Traps (for the ledger)**
- **Identical rows can't be told from a duplicate load.** Filer 1149615 has 13 identical $25,000 rows to Davis in Q1 2000, plus $15,000 twice and $10,000 once: $365,000 in total.
  - The 1999 CA list names that filer as Thomas R. McMorrow of Manatt, Phelps & Phillips.
  - There's no transaction ID and no contributor field. These are most likely checks he delivered for others, not his own money.
- 157 rows repeat filer + recipient name + amount within one period.
- **RECIPIENT_ID isn't clean.** ID 1077687 carries 32 spellings, including a Republican senator's committee and the Assembly Republican PAC under an Assembly Democratic leadership ID. 177 rows ($310K) have no recipient ID.
- Typo'd period ends: 2031-03-01 (5 rows, $150K) and 0200-06-30 (1 row). CONTRIBUTION_DT is empty on every row.
- The lobbyist lists cover the 1999 session only, so anyone who registered in 2001 shows up as "unmatched." The 2001 lobbyist figure is a floor.

## POLITICS__CA_LOBBY_EMPLOYER: probed

**Shape**
- One session only (1999-2000). 1,730 rows, 1,726 employer IDs (4 published doubles).

**Trap: the session total is really one year**
- SESSION_TOTAL_AMT = YR_1 + YR_2 on every row.
- But YR_1 (1999) and QTR_1-4 are non-zero on **1 row**. $102.0M of the $104.0M total is year two.
- So "WSPA spent $3.8M in the session" really means "WSPA reported $3.8M, almost all of it in 2000." 1999 is missing.
- CONTRIBUTOR_ID is "0" or blank on 1,716 of 1,730 rows. 154 rows total $0. 3 rows have no industry.

**Peer: share of the industry's money**
- Western States Petroleum Association: 51% of oil and gas ($3.80M of $7.48M, 21 employers). That's 70x the industry median of $54K.
- Pacific Telesis: 36% of utilities. California Teachers Association: 28% of education.
- These are known big spenders. Nothing new, and with one session there's no time test.

**Join: lobbying spend vs campaign gifts the same employer reported (FILER_ID = EMPLOYER_ID, 2000)**
- 322 of 1,726 employers (19%) reported giving: $10.62M in gifts against $103.5M in lobbying.
- Among givers, the median employer gave $0.19 per lobbying dollar.
- Outliers that gave more than they lobbied:
  - Morongo Band's casino: $431,000 given vs $65,903 lobbying (6.5x)
  - E&J Gallo: 3.1x
  - California Public Securities Association: 3.0x
  - Mercury General: 2.2x ($477,800 vs $216,070)
  - Zenith Insurance: 2.0x
- Dull explanation: the 2000 ballot had tribal-gaming and insurance measures, and this table counts gifts to ballot-measure committees too (the Prop 39 committee took $135K). Not tested recipient by recipient.

**Pointer on the full landing copy (not the mart)**

  - That's 3,151 to 4,135 people, while all named lobbyists went 12,849 to 13,903 (+8%).
