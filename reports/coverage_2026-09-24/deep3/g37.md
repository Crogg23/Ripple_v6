## 1. ENVIRONMENT__FED_EPA_NPDES_ICIS_FACILITIES: **live**

**What it is:** 1,213,737 rows, one per water-discharge permit (NPDES_ID). 67% are stormwater general-permit coverages (3rd character `R`). **72,025 are individual permits** (3rd character `0`), the ones every state must send discharge reports for [q01].
**What it adds:** state, facility type (MWD = municipal), impaired-water flag, name, FRS ID. The violation and enforcement facts come from joins on NPDES_ID.

### The chain
1. Quarterly effluent-violation counts (`NPDES_QNCR_HISTORY.NUME90_Q`) for each individual permit.
2. "Chronic" = effluent violations in 8+ of the 12 quarters 2023Q3-2026Q2. "Six-year chronic" = that, *and* 8+ of the 12 quarters before (2020Q3-2023Q2).
3. Join formal actions (`NPDES_FORMAL_ENFORCEMENT_ACTIONS`) on the permit, then on any permit sharing its FRS ID.
4. Compare states.

### National picture [q12, q17]
- 35,806 individual permits reported in 2023Q3-2026Q2.
- 4,072 chronic. **2,164 six-year chronic.**
- **433 of the 2,164 have no formal action on file, ever.** 429 have none on any permit at the same FRS ID either.

### Peer comparison: by state [q24]
Six-year chronic individual permits, share with no formal action ever:

| Threshold (quarters of 12, both windows) | Missouri | Ohio | Rest of US |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6+ | 178 of 317 (56%) | 167 of 295 (57%) | 632 of 3,410 (18.5%) |
| **8+** | **97 of 194 (50%)** | **68 of 130 (52%)** | **268 of 1,840 (14.6%)** |
| 10+ | 50 of 104 (48%) | 22 of 49 (45%) | 89 of 883 (10%) |
| all 12 | 11 of 36 | 4 of 10 | 21 of 257 (8%) |

- It holds at every threshold. MO vs rest at 8+: one-sided Fisher p ≈ 2e-27.
- Missouri + Ohio: **165 of the 433 (38%)**, from 14.8% of individual permits (5,297 of 35,806).
- Other states with 40+ six-year chronic permits [q17]: TX 19 of 182, LA 5 of 175, KY 2 of 108, OK 0 of 56, PA 42 of 145, NY 24 of 120, WV 29 of 136.
- Not a few big units: the 97 are 97 different permits. Median **68 effluent violations** each over six years. 49 are municipal sewage plants (MWD), 52 are some kind of government.

### Second table agrees: ECHO [q28]
- All 433 land in ECHO by FRS ID. ECHO shows **any** formal action, in any program, on only 9 of them (MO 4, OH 0). 7 of the 9 are sites that also have air, drinking-water or hazardous-waste programs.
- 383 of 433 show 8+ of 12 quarters out of compliance in ECHO too.

### Time: is Missouri still sending data? [q21]
Actions on individual permits entered in ICIS, per year:

| Year | MO formal | MO informal | OH formal | OH informal | Rest formal | Rest informal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | 12 | 0 | 24 | 229 | 1,879 | 8,333 |
| 2020 | 20 | **236** | 12 | 113 | 1,778 | 10,779 |
| 2022 | 35 | 176 | 35 | 280 | 1,949 | 10,962 |
| 2024 | 26 | 66 | 25 | 331 | 1,907 | 10,878 |
| 2025 | 36 | **39** | 29 | 371 | 1,771 | 10,930 |

- Missouri's formal actions keep flowing: 20-54 a year since 2018. So the pipe isn't dead.
- Missouri's **informal** actions (warning letters, violation notices) fell **83%**, 236 → 39, from 2020 to 2025. The rest of the country stayed flat.
- Missouri sent almost **no** informal actions to ICIS before 2017 (2 in 2012-2016). So the informal series is young and may reflect how MO reports, not only what it does.

### Eyeballed: Missouri's top no-action permits [q17, q27]
| Permit | Place | Type | Quarters in violation (2020-23 + 2023-26) | Effluent violations | Last informal action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MO0098752 Madison Mine | Fredericktown | lead-zinc ore mine (SIC 1031), ECHO "major" | 12 + 12 | 341 + 295 | 2002 |
| MO0104256 Leadwood WWTP | Leadwood | sewage | 12 + 12 | 72 + 266 | 2006 |
| MO0107719 Center WWTF | Center | sewage | 12 + 11 | 76 + 183 | 2017 |
| MO0035742 Lake Forest Clean Water District | Ste. Genevieve | subdivision sewer | 11 + 12 | 47 + 147 | none |
| MO0055182 Benton WWTF | Benton | sewage | 12 + 12 | 117 + 67 | 2020 |

- ECHO marks the four checked (Madison, Leadwood, Center, Lake Forest) "Significant Violation", out of compliance in 11-12 of the last 12 quarters. Benton was not checked.
- ECHO shows Madison Mine with one formal action in 2019 and Leadwood with one in 2025. Neither is in the NPDES formal table, on the permit or at the same FRS ID. Leadwood's FRS ID is its *drinking-water plant* too ("LEADWOOD PWS-TREATMENT PLANT"). ECHO doesn't say which program took the action (known trap).
- Ohio's top example: Bloomville WWTP (OH0021032), violations in all 24 quarters, 208 in the last three years, no formal action, 303(d)-listed water.

### The dull explanations, and what was tested
- **"Missouri just doesn't send formal actions to EPA."** Tested: it has sent 20-54 a year for individual permits since 2018. The gap is real in the data. Whether MO handles cases off-system is not testable here.
- **"They're on compliance schedules."** 87 of the 97 MO permits have compliance- or permit-schedule violation records, meaning schedule events flagged as missed or late [q17]. A schedule written into a permit isn't an enforcement action, and they're missing it anyway.
- **"Small towns can't pay for upgrades."** Probably true for many. That's a policy story, not a data artifact. Missouri's small-town lagoon problem may already have press coverage (not checked).
- **"Effluent counts inflate with many limits."** The median permit had 68 violations in six years. The ranking uses *quarters* in violation, not counts.

**Hit means:** in EPA's own records, Missouri and Ohio let chronic dischargers run for six years with no formal action, at 3-4x the national rate. Missouri's informal actions also fell 83% in five years.
**Miss means:** if MO DNR and Ohio EPA keep consent orders in state systems that never reach ICIS, the gap is a reporting gap. That itself matters, because EPA and the public can't see it.
**Next join:** state enforcement dockets (MO DNR, Ohio EPA orders); DMR limit-level detail (which pollutant: E. coli, ammonia, metals) if landed; ACS population for the towns.

## 2. ENVIRONMENT__FED_EPA_ICIS_AIR_ICIS_AIR_PROGRAMS: **live (small)**

**What it is:** 457,581 rows = one facility in one Clean Air Act program. 267,166 facilities (PGM_SYS_ID) [q03].
**What it adds:** the denominator (13,905 operating Title V sources) and each program's operating status. The violations come from `ICIS_AIR_VIOLATION_HISTORY`, joined on PGM_SYS_ID.

### Peer comparison: old, open, never-actioned HPVs [q11, q25]
HPV = "high-priority violation," EPA's top air-violation tier. EPA's policy expects a formal action within 180 days (outside knowledge).

Facilities with an HPV that started before 2024-07-01, still unresolved, at an operating facility, with **no formal action on file at any date** [q25]:

| State | Operating Title V sources | Such facilities |
|---|---|---|
| **Nebraska** | **120** | **24** |
| California | 903 | 10 |
| Texas | 2,790 | 8 |
| Indiana | 555 | 4 |
| Wisconsin | 338 | 3 |
| US total | 13,905 | 66 |

- Nebraska: **36% of the national count**, from 0.9% of major sources.
- A second cut, HPVs that began in 2019-2022, gives the same picture [q11]. Nebraska has 25 facilities; 24 are still open, all 24 with no formal action since day zero, $0 in penalties. Nationally: 1,426 facilities, 279 open, 74 open with no action.
- EPA's own status flag agrees [q20]: Nebraska has **41 facilities "Unaddressed-State" and 0 "Addressed"**. For comparison: Michigan 6 vs 61, Colorado 4 vs 62, Texas 31 vs 33.

### Inside Nebraska: time [q11, q16]
- HPVs that began 2015-2018: 3 facilities, 2 resolved.
- HPVs that began 2019-2022: 25 facilities, 1 resolved.
- Nebraska formal air actions entered in ICIS, 2015-2026: **15 in total**, 0-3 a year. So some data flows, just very little.
- So something changed around late 2019: Nebraska started designating HPVs and stopped closing them.

### Eyeballed [q16, q27]
All "State" found, most still open (data match, not verified):
- **ADM Corn Processing, Columbus**: 4 HPVs (SO2 2020 and 2021, VOC 2023, PM10 2023). Title V. ECHO: significant violation 12 of 12 quarters, 0 formal actions, last formal 2015.
- **Ethanol plants:** KAAPA Aurora (NOx 2023), E Energy Adams (VOC 2021), Green Plains Wood River (VOC 2022), Siouxland Ethanol (NOx 2022), POET Fairmont (PM10 2022), KAAPA Ravenna (VOC 2023), Green Plains Central City (PM10 2023). For the three checked in ECHO (POET Fairmont, Green Plains Wood River, KAAPA Aurora): 12 of 12 quarters in significant violation, 0 formal actions.
- **Lincoln Premium Poultry, Fremont** (NOx 2020): ECHO 12/12 significant, 0 formal.
- Small municipal power plants: Falls City, Wahoo, Sargent, Kimball, Ord, Neligh, Stuart, Wilber, Curtis. Mostly NOx or CO.
- Swift Beef Grand Island (H2S and SO2, 2023), Wholestone Farms, Union Pacific North Platte (PM10, 2022).
- Only 5-6 of the 24 are operating Title V sources, depending on the cut [q11, q25]. So most are smaller plants, not only big emitters.

### The dull explanation
- **Nebraska resolves them in state files that never reach ICIS.** Partly tested: the channel carries 15 formal actions since 2015, so it isn't empty. It can't be settled without Nebraska's own records (NDEE). One records request answers it.
- **Nebraska over-designates.** Possible. The jump from 3 to 25 HPV facilities fits a 2019 policy change. But designating an HPV and never addressing it is still the finding.

**Hit means:** by EPA's own tracking, Nebraska sits on a third of the country's stale, never-actioned high-priority air violations, including ADM and half a dozen ethanol plants.
**Miss means:** if NDEE has consent orders for these, it's a data-reporting failure. That's smaller, but EPA still can't see them.
**Next join:** NDEE enforcement records; EPA's State Review Framework report for Nebraska (not in the warehouse); ICIS-Air stack tests for the flagged plants.

### The table's own traps [q03, q16]
- `BEGIN_DATE` = **10/19/2014 on 213,850 rows (47%)**, the ICIS-Air migration date, not a start date.
- Bulk-load dates: 10/27/2015 (Colorado, 9,477 rows), 09/29/2025 (Illinois, 8,565), 03/19/2026 (Texas, 4,918, including Title V). A "new program" count by year shows these as spikes.
- 3,987 rows begin in 1969, 30 in 2027-2028, 1 in year 218.
- Status is per program, not per facility: 15,286 facilities are "operating" in one program and "closed" in another. Title V has **more closed (14,680) than operating (13,905)** program rows.

### Peer test: parent companies within their own industry [q15, q18]

- Sector baselines vary a lot: hazardous waste 44.7%, chemicals 21.9%, machinery 8.4%.

**Why probed:** a clean, well-keyed facility directory. Nothing story-grade in it alone.
**Next join:** TRI releases by year (only 2023 is landed) against ECHO status; the Nebraska ethanol plants above link here.

## Traps found (for the ledger)

**NPDES facilities**
- `FACILITY_UIN` (FRS ID) is not one facility. One ID holds **362 permits under 127 names** (ND, "CITY OF MERCER - 2017 WATER SUPPLY IMPROVEMENTS"). Several Arizona IDs hold 200-350 construction permits each. 3,772 permits have none. Joining on FRS ID fans out.
- The glance's "110070617548 holds 4.4K" was **not reproduced**. The biggest real ID holds 362.
- `IMPAIRED_WATERS` is one label ("303(D) Listed") or null, and coverage depends on the state: NJ 84%, OH 81%, FL 66% vs MS 1.5%, AZ 2%, SC 2.8%, GA 3.9%. Don't compare states on it.
- `STATE_CODE` has junk: GE 480, XX 56, GB, CN, AB, MB, JA, CZ.
- 82,152 rows have latitude 0 or null.
- QNCR history has **future quarters** (2026Q4 through 2033Q2, about 750 rows). 2026Q3 is partial. `HLRNC` codes are undocumented here; I used `NUME90_Q` only.
- NPDES formal: 2012 EPA penalties sum to $6.76B, a single outlier. Informal `ACHIEVED_DATE` has years 2029, 3036, 5202, 8202.

**ICIS-Air programs**: see section 2 (migration date on 47%, bulk-load dates, per-program status). Also: 1,301 FRV rows with no date sit at facilities where every program is closed.

**TRI facility**
- `FRS_ID` empty on all rows; `EPA_REGISTRY_ID` is the key.
- `FAC_LATITUDE` is DDMMSS on 48,748 rows. `PREF_LONGITUDE` is positive on all 36,755 filled rows (the sign is dropped). `PREF_ACCURACY` = 99999.00 on 1,101. This confirms the memory trap, with counts up slightly.
- One registry ID holds 7 Ford Dearborn TRI facilities.
- `FAC_CLOSED_IND` = 1 on 5,464 facilities, and 140 of them filed for 2023 (Targa Sand Hills, 20.3M lb on-site). The flag is today's status; WestRock Tacoma closed after 2023 (outside knowledge). Not a trap, but don't read it as "closed in 2023."

**NRC copy**: an exact duplicate; EXTRA_COL_1-5 empty; names drift; RESPONSIBLE_STATE is the home state.

**PHMSA**: `NRC_REPORT_NUMBER` holds text on 13 rows ("...REQUIRED BUT NOT MADE" 9, "...NOT REQUIRED" 4). 8 are blank.

**SDWA areas**: FIPS only on county rows.
