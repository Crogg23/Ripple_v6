## 1. SDWA_PN_VIOLATION_ASSOC: Yuma's 21 months over the chlorine-dioxide limit. **live (small)**

**The physical thing:** a public-notice violation (code 75). The state files it when a water system didn't do the public notice a violation required. This table links each one to the violation it covered.

**Checked**
- Shape (S09): 387,627 links, 210,081 PN violations, 385,263 related violations, 42,034 systems. One release (2026Q2).
  - Violation IDs are only unique **inside one PWSID**. 130,474 bare PN IDs vs 210,081 with PWSID. Join on PWSID + ID.
- Join to the violations table, deduped on PWSID + VIOLATION_ID (S12):
  - PN side: 100% land, and every one is code 75.
  - Related side: 93.4% land. 25,622 links point to a violation that isn't in the table.
  - The row's `VIOLATION_CODE`, dates and contaminant **belong to the related violation**, not to the PN.
- What the notices covered (S12):

| Related violation | Links | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring and reporting (MR, MON, RPT, Other) | 307,802 | 79.4% |
| MCL, treatment technique or disinfectant level, tier 2 | 49,262 | 12.7% |
| **Acute, tier 1** (MCL, TT, MRDL) | 4,941 | 1.3% |
| Related violation missing | 25,622 | 6.6% |

- **Peer, by state** (S14). Health-based violations begun 2015 on, lead-inventory code 5200 excluded. The rate is the share of tier-1 violations that carry a PN link:
  - California 14 of 4,120 (0.3%), New York 1 of 324, Oklahoma 14 of 1,453, Washington 0 of 693
  - Texas 896 of 2,711 (33%), Pennsylvania 513 of 1,519 (34%), West Virginia 86 of 163 (53%), Maine 107 of 191 (56%)
  - **Median state: 6.0%**
  - Nationally: 2,740 of 21,614 tier-1 violations (12.7%), at 1,457 systems
- **Yuma, all rows read** (S17). AZ0414024, City of Yuma, 103,264 people:
  - Code 13, contaminant 1008 (chlorine dioxide). The limit on every row is 0.8 mg/L.
  - One violation every month from 2020-07 to 2022-03, 21 in all. Levels 0.9 to 2.0, median 1.2.
  - Each one links to a PN violation that starts the same month. All 42 rows are now Resolved.
  - Every row carries the same state enforcement codes, dated 2022-03-22 to 2022-04-08. SOX (compliance achieved) is 2022-04-07.
- **Peer, national** (S20): 54 code-13 violations since 2015, at 20 systems.
  - Yuma has 21 (39%). Next: LA1005035 with 6, then KS2010703 with 3. Everyone else has 1-2.
  - Only 6 systems have a PN link on any of theirs.

**Hit means:** a city of 100K had disinfectant above the acute limit for 21 straight months. The state's own records say it didn't give the required notice, month after month.

**Miss means:** if the violations had been spread thin or had no PN links, it would be routine noise.

**Boring explanation, and whether I tested it**
- *A 2022 paperwork sweep.* The codes all dated March-April 2022 fit a state review that found the misses afterward. That doesn't make the exceedances less real, but it may mean a notice went out in April 2022. **Not ruled out.** Pull ADEQ's 2022 file and Yuma's April 2022 notice.
- *Already reported.* Not checked; Yuma's local press is the first search.
- *Code 13 meaning.* I read it as the acute chlorine-dioxide MRDL. That needs EPA's code lookup to confirm.

**Second thread: chronic nitrate** (S16)
- 89 systems had nitrate MCL violations in 8 or more of the years since 2015: 41 in California, 31 in Texas.
- Texas files PN violations on 28% of nitrate violations; California on 0.3%.
- Eola WSC, TX0480011 (165 people): 45 nitrate violations 2015-2026, median 35 mg/L against a limit of 10. 40 have PN links.
- South Midland County Water System, TX1650077 (165 people): 45 violations, median 16.3 mg/L, 41 PN links.

**Traps**
- A link row isn't a failure. Herndon Community Water, WV3305535 (56 people), is the battery's "top" system: 3,342 links, but 583 PN violations. One notice covers up to 47 missed tests. Three Mississippi systems have just 2 PN violations each, and one notice covers 350 to 454 linked violations (S23). Count distinct PN violations, and look at health-based ones.

## 2. NPDES_INFORMAL_ENFORCEMENT_ACTIONS: two states went dark, and one lead died. probed

**The physical thing:** a warning letter, notice of violation or phone call from a state or EPA to a site holding a water-pollution permit.

  - `ACHIEVED_DATE`: 17,835 before 1990 (down to 0001-01-01), 6 in the future (up to 8202), 6,326 blank.

- Codes split in Sep-Oct 2019: AER, PHEMAIL, UNDREV, ENFMTG, LRE, IRL, NFA and ONV end there. The same kinds continue under an S suffix (AERS, PHEMLS, UNDREVS...). Add both families together.
- Dates: 0001-01-01 to 8202-06-10. Filter to 1990 through today, and count the blanks separately.

## 3. ICIS_AIR_POLLUTANTS: carcinogen plants look worse, the dull reason stands. probed

**The physical thing:** one row per pollutant an air permit regulates at one facility. It says what a plant is permitted for, not what it emitted.

**Checked**
- Shape (S04, S06): 948,342 rows. 133,679 are `FACIL` placeholder rows: facility-level class only, no chemical. 814,663 are real pollutant rows.
  - CAS number filled on 379,789 real rows (46.6%). SRS ID on 97%.
- Join to `ICIS_AIR_FACILITIES` (S05): every pollutant row's ID lands. 23,643 facility rows have no pollutants at all.
- **Peer test** (S11, 184,066 operating facilities). Count facilities with an HPV that began 2020 or later. Compare with what state-and-class peers without that chemical predict:

| Regulated for | Operating facilities | With HPV since 2020 | Peers predict | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ethylene oxide | 260 | 13 | 4.8 | 2.7x |
| 1,3-Butadiene | 1,336 | 52 | 17.1 | 3.0x |
| Vinyl chloride | 442 | 24 | 11.1 | 2.2x |
| Benzene | 7,809 | 177 | 93.1 | 1.9x |
| Formaldehyde | 20,031 | 216 | 142.2 | 1.5x |
| Hexavalent chromium | 563 | 18 | 13.5 | 1.3x |
| Chloroprene | 25 | 0 | 0.7 | 0 |

- **One named case** (S22). LANXESS Corporation, Charleston SC. Major source, regulated for ethylene oxide.
  - 3 HPVs under the MACT rules, all naming ethylene oxide. They began 2024-11-20, 2025-04-04 and 2025-12-02. None is resolved.
  - What's on record: state notices of violation (2024-09-18, 2025-08-27). The last formal action is a 2002 state order for $9,500, plus one undated EPA civil case with no penalty.
  - It's the only one of the 260 ethylene-oxide facilities with an open HPV.

**Hit means:** plants permitted for the strongest carcinogens break the serious rules 2-3x as often as similar plants in the same state.

**Miss means:** a ratio near 1 would have said the chemical label predicts nothing.

**Boring explanation:** "Major" lumps a small plant with a refinery. Carcinogen plants are bigger and more complex, and inspectors visit them more, so they log more violations. **Not tested**; I didn't control for inspection count. On LANXESS: open HPVs are common. 604 operating facilities have one, so it's one case, not a pattern.

**Traps**
- `FACIL` is not a pollutant. It's the facility's class, and it can appear more than once: 133,679 rows at 111,903 facilities.
- The same facility-pollutant pair can sit under two classes. VOCs: 127,447 rows at 116,663 facilities.
- Pollutant class vs facility class: 659 "minor" and 1,021 "synthetic minor" facilities are listed Major for at least one pollutant.
- Junk pollutants: ADMIN (9,270 rows), POLLUTANT X (9,260), OTHER (13,005). Acetaldehyde has two codes, one labeled "(duplicate pollutant)".
- In the violation-history table, `HPV_RESOLVED_DATE` is blank on 2,737 of 5,952 HPVs since 2020 (46%). The Bay Area leads: Chevron Richmond has 652 of 654 open, Tesla Fremont 310 of 310. "Open" usually means "never closed in the data".
