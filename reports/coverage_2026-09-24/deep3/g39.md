## 1. ENVIRONMENT__FED_EPA_GHGRP_EMISSION: **live**

**What it is:** one row per facility, year, sector, subsector and gas. 346,683 rows, 2010-2023. Nothing after 2023.
**No lookup for sectors or gases in the warehouse.** I named sectors from each one's facility industry codes and rule subparts (q04):
2 waste/landfills · 3 power plants · 4 refineries · 5 chemicals · 6 metals · 7 pulp & paper · 8 minerals · 14 other · 15 oil & gas systems.
Suppliers (fuel sold, not burned on site): 10 petroleum products · 11 natural gas · 12 industrial gases · 13 CO2 · 16 equipment. 17 is CO2 injection. Gas 8 is biogenic CO2.

### Trap 1: the table's sum is not emissions [q03, q14]
- The glance said "sum 98.6B." **54.95B of the 98.56B tonnes (55.8%) is supplier rows**: fuel that refiners, importers and gas utilities sold. That fuel gets burned somewhere else and gets counted again there.
- The glance's "max 238.4M" is **Valero Marketing and Supply Company**, TX, 2023, a petroleum-product supplier row. Not a smokestack.
- Direct emissions (sectors 2-8, 14, 15, no biogenic): **3,058 Mt in 2015, 2,578 Mt in 2023 (-15.7%)**.

### Trap 2: the empty rows are placeholders, not a broken load [q14]
- 28,806 rows have no CO2e (8.3%).
- **21,669** of them are facility-years the facility table marks "stopped reporting". No gas, no number, just a row carried forward.
- 2,856 are supplier and injection rows (sectors 10, 11, 17) with the gas set but no number.
- 4,281 are no-gas rows in years the facility did report.
- 2023: **3,839 of 26,272 rows are empty (14.6%)**, not "a third." But **3,399 of 11,235** facility IDs in 2023 (30.3%) have only empty rows. 3,150 of those stopped reporting.
- That likely explains the old fable-hunt C4 miss ("deciles 8-10 empty"): a facility with only empty rows sums to null, and null sorts last, into the top deciles.

### Trap 3: an oil-and-gas "facility" can be a whole basin
- Onshore production reports one "facility" per operator per basin: "ConocoPhillips Permian (430)" went 0.16 → 0.30 → 3.06 Mt (2015, 2019, 2023).
- Buying another operator moves emissions onto your facility ID. Growth on these rows isn't one site growing. (Deal history is outside knowledge, not checked in the warehouse.)

### Peer + time: who grew [q03, q04, local b3-b5; re-checked in q13]
Direct emissions, 2015 vs 2023. Facilities with a number in both years: **6,021**.

| Sector | Facilities in both years | Their total change | Median facility | Share that grew |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Power plants | 1,191 | -21.4% | -5.0% | 45% |
| Oil & gas systems | 1,266 | **+6.1%** | -5.8% | 44% |
| Refineries | 134 | -6.9% | -3.7% | 43% |
| Metals | 254 | -12.8% | -3.7% | 44% |
| Waste | 1,172 | -7.2% | -6.6% | 45% |

- **Oil & gas is the only sector that grew, and one facility did most of it.** Net +9.65 Mt; Sabine Pass alone +6.08 Mt (63%). The median oil-and-gas facility shrank 5.8%.
- **Rank of Sabine Pass's growth: 1 of 6,021.** Next: ConocoPhillips Permian +2.90 (the basin trap above), ExxonMobil Baytown +2.78.
- Counting new plants too, 103 facilities grew by more than 1 Mt. 62 are fossil power plants, 39 of them new since 2015; 29 are oil-and-gas IDs. Dull reading: new gas plants replacing coal (fuel type not checked). Total direct emissions still fell.

### One join: Sabine Pass in the air-enforcement records [q04 → q06, q07, q10, q11]

## 2. ENVIRONMENT__FED_EPA_ICIS_AIR_ICIS_AIR_TITLEV_CERTS: probed

**What it is:** one row per state or local review of a major plant's yearly Title V compliance certification. `ACTUAL_END_DATE` is when the review finished, not the year certified. 24,125 plant IDs; all land in `ICIS_AIR_FACILITIES`.

### Traps [q07, q15]
- **Loaded twice:** 9,897 `ACTIVITY_ID`s appear twice (one three times): same plant, same date, one copy with the flag blank and one filled. Dedupe on `ACTIVITY_ID` and keep the filled one. 489,215 real reviews.
- **The flag follows the state's habit.** Among flagged reviews 2020-2025:

| State | Flagged | Said Y | Y rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texas | 4,677 | 3,130 | **66.9%** |
| California | 3,457 | 1,605 | 46.4% |
| Louisiana | 3,066 | 1,273 | 41.5% |
| Median state (50+ majors) | | | 31.0% |
| Pennsylvania | 3,120 | 353 | 11.3% |
| North Carolina | 1,799 | 123 | 6.8% |
| Colorado | 1,275 | **8** | **0.6%** |

- Never filled 2020-2025: Georgia, Minnesota, Alaska (100% blank). Connecticut 99%, Nebraska 98%, Indiana 97%. That's where "57% blank" comes from.
- **Colorado's N doesn't track violations.** Suncor's Denver refinery (`CO0000000800100003`, Commerce City): all 24 filled reviews since 2015 say **N, no deviations**. The same records hold HPVs opened 2019-08, 2021-10, 2022-11 and 2023-09, and state penalties of **$3,839,910 (2020) and $2,245,200 (2024)**.
- Dates: 5,997 empty; 15 before 1990, including years 0015, 0017, 0019, 0206, 0515, 1015, 1016.

### Check 1: do plants that admit deviations face enforcement? [q07, q09, q10, q11]
Plants with a filled flag in 8+ of the 10 years 2016-2025: 7,682. Enforcement = any violation, informal action or formal action on the same ID, 2016-2025.

| Group | Plants | Any enforcement | Formal action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Said Y every year | 681 | **78.4%** | 56.8% |
| Mixed | 4,744 | 64.3% | 34.2% |
| Never said Y | 2,257 | 34.0% | 15.4% |

- On the whole, admitting a deviation brings enforcement. No "admitted and ignored" story nationally.

### Check 2: Texas, where the record is thin [local a5-a6, q15]
- 106 Texas plants said Y every year. **40 have nothing in ICIS-Air 2016-2025**, even across every ID sharing their registry number. Louisiana: 0 of 56. California: 3 of 53.
- 37 of the 40 are gas compressor stations, gas plants or storage sites; 3 are power plants. Each got 8-18 full evaluations in the decade.
- **Dull explanation, tested:** Texas sends little to the federal database. Per operating major plant, 2016-2025:

| | Texas | Median state |
|---|---|---|
| Violations entered | 0.53 | 2.7 |
| Informal actions (NOVs, letters) | 0.81 | 3.18 |
| Formal actions | 0.87 | 0.89 |

- Formal actions flow normally, and there Texas isn't an outlier: 42% of its every-year admitters got none, vs Ohio 61%, Iowa 79%, Wisconsin 78%.
- So "40 plants admit it and nobody acts" fails. What's left is a data gap: **ICIS-Air, the database behind ECHO, holds far fewer Texas violations and NOVs per plant than other states have.** Whether Texas finds fewer or sends fewer needs TCEQ's own records. Louisiana enters almost no violations at all (40 for 476 majors).

### Check 3: saying "no deviations" while a serious violation was open [local a9]
- In the 37 states with a Y rate of 25%+: 3,239 reviews were filed while an HPV at least a year old was unresolved. **31.5% said N**, vs 55.8% of all flagged reviews there. Consistent with the flag working. No story.

### Dead end: Title V plants with no review
- 15.5% of operating Title V IDs in the program before 2021 (1,264 of 8,167) have no review 2022-2025. But all 290 Texas IDs in that group have no review and no full evaluation 2022-2025 either; 261 never had a review. Stale IDs, not missing reviews. `ICIS_AIR_PROGRAMS.BEGIN_DATE` is a system date on thousands of rows (10/19/2014 on 4,991; 03/19/2026 on 2,471).

**Verdict: probed.** Usable within one state after dedupe. Never compare the flag across states.
