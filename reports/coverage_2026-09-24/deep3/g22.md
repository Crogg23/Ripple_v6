## 1. VOTEVIEW_ROLLCALLS: **live**

**What the table is**
- 3,364 roll calls. It covers the 118th and 119th Congresses only, 2023-01-03 to 2026-06-25.
- S02: every row matches the full Voteview table `POLITICS__FED_VOTEVIEW_ROLLCALL_META` on date, tally and result: 3,364 of 3,364.
- ⚠ META runs to 2026-08-08, **95 roll calls further**. ROLLCALLS is a stale slice of it.
- For history I used META (1947 on). ROLLCALLS was checked against it row by row first.

**Checked**
- S03: House roll calls in the first 538 days of each Congress. That's ROLLCALLS' own window, Jan 3 2025 to Jun 25 2026.
  - 119th: **587**. 118th: 997.
  - Fewest at that point since the 101st (551, 1989-90). The 97th had 520. Pre-1973 counts are lower, but that was before electronic voting.
- S04: what kinds of votes, same window. **The drop is amendments, almost entirely.**

| House, first 538 days | 118th | 119th |
|---|---|---|
| All roll calls | 997 | 587 |
| Amendment roll calls | 463 | **70** |
| Passage votes | 124 | 153 |
| Suspension votes | 179 | 135 |
| Rule / previous-question votes | 107 | 113 |
| Days with a vote | 174 | 159 |

  - Total fell 410. Amendments fell 393.
  - Amendment roll calls, 102nd-118th (1991-2024), same point: median **362**. The lowest was 146 (117th, 2021-22). The 119th has 70.
  - Days with a vote: 159. That matches the 117th (159) and 116th (162). The House isn't voting on fewer days.
  - Longest gap: 54 days from 2025-09-19, the shutdown recess. Fall gaps of 63-76 days were common in 1989-1999.
- S06, an independent check from a different table. Delegates (DC, PR, GU, VI, AS, MP) can vote only in the Committee of the Whole, which is where amendments get voted.
  - Roll calls with any delegate vote: 118th **454**, 119th **61** (`VOTEVIEW_VOTES` joined to `FED_VOTEVIEW_MEMBERS` on ICPSR).
  - Every one of them is an amendment question. That matches the text count.
- S06 completeness check: Voteview rows vs the Clerk's highest roll number each year. 1-5 short per year (2025: 361 vs 362, 2023: 719 vs 724). The table is complete enough for a miss to count.
- **S16, test inside the group.** Of bills that reached a passage roll call, how many got **any** recorded amendment vote?
  - 119th: 14 of 151 (9.3%). 104th-118th: 32.7% to 69.9%, median 53.2%.
- **S17, dull-explanation test.** Congressional Review Act resolutions (H.J.Res.) allow no amendments. The 119th passed a lot of them. So S17 counts only H.R. bills.

| Congress | H.R. bills with a passage roll call | With any amendment roll call | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 104th-118th (1995-2024) | 69-128 each | 39-73 each | 39.2%-74.1%, **median 60.8%** |
| 115th (previous low) | 125 | 49 | 39.2% |
| 118th | 95 | 41 | 43.2% |
| **119th** | **118** | **14** | **11.9%** |

- The effect is spread out, not carried by 3-4 bills. It's a per-bill rate. When a bill does get amendment votes, the median count is 2.5. The historical median is 2-5.
- The NDAA is the like-for-like peer, and it dropped less. HR3838 got 17 amendment roll calls. The three House NDAAs before it got 30 (HR2670), 24 (HR8070) and 23 (HR4350) (S05, S16). So the collapse is mostly on other bills.
- S20 eyeball (bill titles joined from `FED_GOVINFO_BILLSTATUS`):
  - With amendment votes: NDAA HR3838 (17), Energy-Water approps HR4553 (11), farm bill HR7567 (9), Defense approps HR4016 (7).
  - Close passages with **zero** amendment roll calls:
    - HR1 reconciliation, 215-214
    - HR4 rescissions, 214-212
    - **HR8646 FY2027 Agriculture appropriations, 213-210** on 2026-06-04
    - HR1968 full-year CR, 217-213
  - For scale: the 118th's Agriculture approps bill HR4368 drew 23 amendment roll calls (S05).

**Hit means:** House members got a recorded vote on changes to about 1 bill in 8 that reached the floor. From 1995 to 2024 it was 3 in 5. That's a checkable number for a story about a closed floor. It sits alongside *(from memory)* the Rules Committee minority's closed-rule counts, measured from the other end.

**Miss means:** the data only sees **recorded** votes. If the Rules Committee still allowed amendments but they passed by voice vote or were folded in en bloc, the floor is less transparent, not closed. Separating the two needs amendment-level data (amendments made in order, voice votes). That isn't in the warehouse.

**Boring:**
- "The 118th was the outlier." Tested. It's measured against 15 Congresses, and the 119th sits under a third of the previous low.
- "CRA resolutions are closed by law." Tested in S17: H.R. bills only, still 11.9%.
- "Fewer days in session." Tested: 159 vote days, same as the 117th.
- Reconciliation and rescission bills are normally closed. They're 2 of the 104 H.R. bills with no amendment roll call, so they don't explain it.

**Senate, same table (S07, context, not the lead)**
- 851 roll calls by day 538. That's third since 1947, behind the 94th (995) and 93rd (860).
- Votes on nominations (a PN number, or "nomination" in the question): 302 from Jan-Sep 2025, then 126 from Oct 2025 to Jun 2026. That's 33.6 a month, then 14.0.
  - The break lines up with the en bloc confirmation rule *(from memory: fall 2025)*.
  - The 119th is **not** a record for nomination votes. The 117th had 475 by day 538; the 119th has 428.
- 253 failed votes by day 538. The 110th (257) and 108th (254) had slightly more, so it's not a record.
  - They're mostly budget "vote-a-rama" amendments: HR1 36, S2 26, S.Con.Res.7 23, H.Con.Res.14 20, HR4 16, S.Con.Res.33 15.
  - Plus 14 failed votes on the September 2025 stopgap, HR5371. Known material.

## Traps for the ledger

- ⚠ **VOTEVIEW_ROLLCALLS is a stale copy.** It matches META row for row for the 118th and 119th but stops 2026-06-25. META has 95 more roll calls, to 2026-08-08. Use META.
- **META `DATE` and `ROLLNUMBER` are text.** Parse them before sorting or joining.
- **META `VOTE_RESULT` and `VOTE_QUESTION` are blank before 1989.** In the 101st House only 511 of 879 are filled. Result and question history starts 1991.
- **META `VOTE_DESC`/`DTL_DESC` are blank on many rows**, for example 780 of 1,366 in the 112th House by day 538. Don't classify rules by description across Congresses.
  - A side count that desc-based matching gives: failed rule votes 118th 7, 119th 4, no earlier Congress over 3. For the 107th-117th it's a floor.
- **Battery metrics on label columns mean nothing.** "Top 1% hold 43% of CONGRESS" sums Congress numbers.
- ⚠ **FEC link: two copies.** POLITICS (2024, 2026) and FINANCE (2018-2024) overlap on 2024: 8,584 of 8,585 by link ID.
- ⚠ **FEC: one person, many candidate IDs.** 357 principal committees each map to 2+ IDs of one surname. Counting CAND_ID overcounts people. Dedupe by committee or name first.
- FEC names lost ñ: "MU?OZ".
- FEC `CAND_ELECTION_YR` has typos. One row sits 892 years past its cycle; others sit 63-82 years out.
- **OWID milspend battery repeats are fake.** "133493544 x67" and "98394296 x67": no value repeats 3+ times inside any country. "0 x193" is really 180. That's the APPROX_TOP_K inflation g09 found.
- **OWID milspend mixes 18 SIPRI regions and World into the country rows.** Regions have a blank code; World has a code. Filter both before summing.
- OWID milspend is stored as text with no unit. The values are whole constant US dollars.
- OWID CPI stops at the 2024 edition.
- **DOJ Epstein crawl reads page 1 only** of 8 of 12 data-set listings (49-50 links each).
