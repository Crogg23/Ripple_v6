## 1. FINANCE__FED_SEC_13F_FILERS: live

**What it is.** The cover page of every 13F filing: who filed, which quarter, and whether it amends an earlier one.
Every manager with $100M+ in US stocks files one each quarter, due 45 days after the quarter ends.
It is joined to `SEC_13F_SUBMISSIONS` on accession number, which adds the filing date and CIK.

**The finding: a wave of managers owning up to years of missing 13F reports, all at once.**
- An "original" 13F-HR (not an amendment) filed more than a year after its quarter:
  - 2013 to Sep 2022 (35 full quarters): **9.5 per 1,000** originals.
  - Jan 2024 to May 2026 (10 quarters): **31.0 per 1,000**. That is 3.3x.
  - More than **5 years** late: 1.0 per 1,000, now **8.9 per 1,000**.
  - Managers filing any report more than a year late in a quarter: 8 on average before, **26.5** now.
- Managers who back-filed 8 or more overdue reports: **105 started in 2024 to May 2026**. 81 started across 2013-2022. By yearly pace that is about 5x.
- The biggest back-filers (a report counts as "late" when filed more than a year after its quarter):

| Manager | Where | Late reports | Filed | Reaches back to | Latest US holdings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tetrad Corp | Omaha NE | 71 | all on 2025-08-01 | Q4 2006 | $1.0B |
| Employees Provident Fund Board | Malaysia | 68 | 10 days, 2026-03-17 to 04-22 | Q1 2008 | $13.2B |
| E. Ohman J:or Asset Mgmt | Sweden | 65 | 2024-04-05 | Q4 2006 | $3.8B |
| Dixon Mitchell Investment Counsel | Vancouver | 65 | 2024-01-17 | Q4 2006 | $2.9B |
| Northwest & Ethical Investments | Toronto | 59 | Jul 2025 | Q3 2009 | $4.3B |
| PZU General Pension Society | Warsaw | 43 | Mar 2025 | Q2 2013 | $0.4B |
| Swiss Life Asset Management | Zurich | 39 | Dec 2025 | Q1 2015 | $20.4B |
| Desjardins (Federation des caisses) | Quebec | 26 | Aug 2024 | Q1 2017 | $27.8B |

- The oldest back-filed report sits just over the $100M filing line: Tetrad $111.6M, EPF $125.8M, Ohman $113.5M, Dixon Mitchell $134.1M, PZU $142.0M.
  So they went back to the quarter they first crossed the line. That reads as a legal duty they missed, not a voluntary tidy-up.
- Peer group: 7,553 managers with 12+ original reports. **Median late share: 0. Two-thirds were never late.**
- The effect isn't a few firms. The top 5 carry 328 of the 2,150 back-filed reports (15%). The median back-filer filed 17.
- Mix: 69 US managers, 36 foreign, going by the SEC state code.

**Checks run.**
- Time: filing quarter by filing quarter, 2013-2026 (46 quarters).
- Peer: each manager's late share against all managers with 12+ originals.
- Concentration: top 5 share, median unit.
- One join: FILERS to SUBMISSIONS on accession number. 336,124 of 344,109 rows land. The 7,985 misses are one whole zip; see traps.
- Eyeball: 8 back-filers' reports joined to HOLDINGS. **0 of 436 back-filed reports are empty.** The median report runs 8 to 2,706 lines, depending on the manager.

**The dull explanations, tested.**
- *"Someone else was reporting their holdings."* In that case they would have filed 13F-NT notices. **0 of the 105 filed one.** Their affiliates' notices cover the same old quarters, under new CIKs: Swiss Life France 45, and 5 Desjardins units 37 each.
- *"They filed before under another CIK."* A name search of all 16,311 filer CIKs finds no earlier 13F-HRs for the top names.
- *"Typo in the quarter."* No. The batches run in unbroken order, 26 to 71 quarters each, starting at the $100M line.
- *"More filers overall."* Originals per quarter rose about 30% (6.9K to 9.1K). Late-by-a-year rose 3.3x per 1,000. Size doesn't explain it.
- Not tested: **why** 2024. It fits an SEC staff push on non-filers, but nothing in the warehouse says so. Unverified.

**Hit means.** A measurable enforcement-shaped event: dozens of institutions, some with $10B+, admitting years of missed federal filings at once.
**Miss would have meant.** Late filings are background noise at the same rate every year. They aren't.

**Caveat: the table has a hole in the middle.** Filings from Oct 2022 to Dec 2023 aren't loaded. The wave may have started in 2023; we can't see it.

**Side list: 50 filings (25 managers, 2013-2025) where the SEC denied confidential treatment. HOLDINGS shows what they tried to hide.**
- Simon Property Group: $476M of Macerich (Dec 2014).
- Fore Research: $377M of SPDR Gold calls (2016).
- Maplelane Capital: $231M of Palantir puts (Dec 2020).
- Westwood Global Investments / WGI DM (Boston): 4 quarters, Q4 2024 to Q3 2025, denied 2025-12-12. $453M Itau Unibanco, $442M Credicorp, $315M Shell.
Good color for a portfolio piece. Not a lead on its own.

**Traps (for the ledger).**
- **Six SEC zips were never loaded:** 2021q3 and 2022q4 through 2023q4. The quarters 2021-06-30 and 2022-09-30 to 2023-09-30 hold 197-393 filings each, against 7,000-9,000 normally.
- **FILERS has the 2021q4 zip, SUBMISSIONS doesn't.** So 7,985 rows fall out of any join on accession number.
- **HOLDINGS `VALUE_USD` is 1,000x too big on some pre-2023 filings.** UBS O'Connor shows $424.8B for 4.0M Maxim shares. The filer typed dollars where thousands were due, and the load multiplied again.
- **HOLDINGS has placeholder lines**: issuer '0', CUSIP 000000000, repeating a real line's value. Westwood's Q4 2024 amendment has them. Summing double-counts.
- `REPORTCALENDARORQUARTER` and `FILING_DATE` are 'DD-MON-YYYY' text. Parse them before sorting. One row says 1900-01-01, one says 1987.
- `ISAMENDMENT` is blank on 190K+ rows. Use `SUBMISSIONTYPE` = '13F-HR' for originals.

**Next join.** SEC administrative proceedings or litigation releases by CIK, if we ever land them. Did any back-filer get charged?

## 2. FINANCE__FED_FATCA_FFI: live (moderate)

**What it is.** The IRS list of foreign banks and funds registered under FATCA, the US law on offshore accounts. 516,298 rows, one per GIIN (the IRS's ID for a registered bank). US payers check this list before paying a foreign bank.
One load, 2026-08-05. There is no date column.

**The finding: 29 institutions OFAC has blocked are still on the IRS's list.**
- Method: the FATCA name, with legal-form words stripped, must match an OFAC SDN name or alias. **And the country must agree.** SDN names came from the OpenSanctions pull, rows sourced from the SDN list.
- 109 GIINs matched on name alone. The country test threw out 80, mostly generic names (Aurora, Helios, Phoenix). **29 survived.**
- **28 of the 29 are confirmed in our own OFAC SDN table**; the 29th, SDK Garant, only through OpenSanctions.
- By country:
  - **Russia 24** (21 banks, 2 fund depositories, 1 payment clearer): Bank Sinara, Guta-Bank, Finstar Bank, National Reserve Bank, Primsotsbank, Timer Bank, Chelindbank, Bank Accept, Kremlyovskiy, Mosoblbank and 14 more.
  - **Belarus 3**: Belinvestbank, and 2 Iran-linked banks (Trade Capital Bank, North European Bank).
  - **Armenia 1**: Mellat Bank CJSC, the arm of Iran's Bank Mellat.
  - **UK 1**: Havin Bank, the London bank Cuba owns.
- **17 of the 24 Russian entries first appear in the sanctions feed on 2024-11-21.** That is the day OFAC blocked Gazprombank and about 50 other Russian lenders. Nearly two years later, they're still IRS-registered.
- Plus 4 branch rows my strict match skips: Bank Melli Iran in Paris, Hamburg and Baku, and Banco de Venezuela's Curacao branch. Both parent banks are in our SDN table.

**The peer comparison.**
- The biggest blocked Russian banks are **not** on the list: Sberbank, VTB, Gazprombank itself, Russian Alfa-Bank, Promsvyazbank, VEB, Rosselkhozbank. A search on each name returns zero.
- So someone cleans the list, but only for the big names. 24 of about 212 Russian bank-like SDN entries (11%) remain.
- 61 Russian funds still carry the name "Gazprombank - ..." under the sponsor "AAA Capital Management". That sponsor isn't on the SDN list by name, so the funds don't count.

**The dull explanation.**
- A FATCA registration stays until the bank or the IRS cancels it. It gives a blocked bank almost nothing, since US persons can't deal with it anyway.
- So this is two arms of Treasury disagreeing in public. It isn't proof money moved.
- Not tested: whether these banks still operate. Many small Russian banks have lost their licenses. The warehouse has no Russian central bank data.

**Hit means.** A checkable inconsistency: OFAC blocks a bank, and the IRS keeps listing it as a registered partner.
**Miss would have meant.** The IRS scrubs sanctioned banks. It does for the giants only.

**Traps.**
- **This table is an exact copy of `ECONOMICS__FED_IRS_FATCA_FFI_LIST`.** Same 516,298 rows, same hash_agg.
- **The glance's GIIN counts are wrong.** It reported "RFNTC1.99999.SL.136 3.8K"; every GIIN is unique (516,298 of 516,298).
- `99999` in the middle of a GIIN is not a placeholder. It means "no sub-entity number" on a single bank: 309,217 rows (60%).
- The last 3 GIIN digits are the ISO country number. They agree with `COUNTRY_NAME` on every row.
- Names are self-registered, with no vetting. A Russian row is literally named with an obscenity.
- Cayman Islands holds 103,042 rows (20%), mostly funds. A country map without a denominator just shows fund domiciles.

**Next join.** Match the 29 GIIN names against USAspending recipients and SEC filers (name plus country) for any US-side trace.

## 3. FINANCE__FED_NCUA_CHARTER_MERGER_EVENTS: probed

**What it is.** 27 credit-union mergers from one NCUA list, with no merger date.
Joined on charter number to `FED_NCUA_FEDERALLY_INSURED_CU_LIST` (2026 Q1, 4,250 credit unions). **24 of 27 merging charters land.** They were still open on 2026-03-31, so the mergers came after that.

**What the join shows.**
- Stated reasons: 22 "expanded services", 3 "inability to obtain officials", 2 "poor financial condition".
- **Gateway Metro CU** (St. Louis, $177M, reason: poor financial condition):
  - Return on assets **-7.3%**, the worst of 766 credit unions with $100-300M in assets. The median is +0.71%.
  - Net worth 5.87%, the 2nd lowest. The median is 11.6%.
- **Commonwealth One** (Alexandria VA, $509M, reason: **"expanded services"**):
  - Members **-22.8%** in 4 quarters, the 2nd worst of 660 credit unions with $250M-$1B. The median is +0.5%.
  - Return on assets -0.70%, 6th worst. It merged into Arlington Community, a near-equal at $541M.
- Harvester Financial ($82M) merged into a smaller credit union, $45M. Its members fell 27.5%, 5th worst of 881.

**Why only probed.** 27 rows, one quarter, and the reason field is self-reported.
"The stated reason understates the trouble" holds for 1-2 credit unions. That's a local business item, not a pattern.
Dull explanation: members often drop before a merger because dormant accounts get closed. Can't test.

**Traps.**
- There is no date. The asset figures come from different dates: Credit Union 1 (Lombard IL) shows $2.34B on one row and $2.29B on another.
- Alero Financial (charter 68215) is "Corporate America Family" in the insured list. It was renamed.
- 3 merging charters aren't in the 2026 Q1 insured list: PECO, North Bay, Alloy Employees.
- REGION is inconsistent for one survivor: Credit Union 1 is region 2 on one row and region 1 on the other.

**Next join.** FS220 call reports over several quarters, to see each merging credit union's slide.

## 4. FINANCE__FED_EPA_ICIS_FEC_CASE_FACILITIES: probed

**What it is.** Facilities named in EPA civil enforcement cases: 204,019 rows, 113,855 registry IDs.
There is no date column. The year is inside the case number: RR-YYYY-NNNN, where RR is the region or HQ.

**Time.**
- Distinct cases: 3,335 (2012), 2,255 (2019, the low), 3,138 (2024), **3,012 (2025, -4%)**, 2,652 (2026 so far).
- **No collapse in 2025 on the case count.**
- Refinery cases hit a series low in 2025: **17**. The earlier low was 28 (2020). But 2026 already has 18, so it may be a one-year dip.
- The mix shifted:
  - Cases naming a facility with no industry code: 47% (2012), **70% (2025)**.
  - Chemicals, oil and gas, refining, power and other manufacturing together: 874 cases (2012), 488 (2025), -44%. This is a slow slide, not a break.
  - Foreign or no-ID facilities: 6-42 a year in 2010-2021, **75-100 a year in 2023-2025**. Many are Ontario importers with state 'XX'.
- The 2016-to-2017 drop (2,912 to 2,667) is mostly water, sewer and waste cases: 395 to 151.

**Peer.**
- Refineries: 366 in the table, 110 with a case since 2015, median 2. Top: Shell Deer Park 9, Hovensa 9.
- A top of 9 against a median of 2 isn't story-grade.

**One join.** To ECHO on registry ID: **99.8% land** (113,680 of 113,854).

**Dull explanation.** Newer facility records often lack industry codes. So the "no code" rise may be partly coding, not a change in who EPA goes after. Not separable here.

**Traps (for the ledger).**
- **Registry ID 110009327022 ("Wauconda Sand & Gravel") holds 7,156 cases from 1973-2007**, one row per case. It's a catch-all ID, not a gravel pit. Drop it before any "most cases" ranking.
- **Rows are not cases.** Blue Cube Plaquemine: 780 rows, 13 cases. 3M Cottage Grove: 720 rows, 11 cases.
- **One stop-sale order is one case.**
  - PhoneSoap LLC: 139 cases in 2023-24.
  - Customs ports: Champlain NY 42, Pembina ND 33.
  - Medify Air: 22 + 17 cases, on two registry IDs.
  - Case counts measure paperwork, not violations.
- The 2006 HQ spike (2,664 HQ cases) is one-off. The HQ prefix runs 7-269 a year otherwise.
- Null registry: 787 rows, mostly foreign importers with state 'XX'.

**Next join.** ICIS FE&C enforcement conclusions with penalty dollars. They aren't loaded; that's the table that would turn case counts into money.

## 5. FINANCE__FED_FEC_COMMITTEES: probed

**What it is.** FEC committee master rows: name, treasurer, address, type, linked candidate.

**What was tested.**
- Concentration: 30,640 treasurer names.
  - Datwyler, Thomas: **533** committees. Kilgore, Paul: 413. Lisker, Lisa: 322.
  - Addresses: PO Box 30844 (20824) 280, PO Box 183 (54016) 256.
  - These are compliance firms.
- One join: to `POLITICS__FED_FEC_PAC_SUMMARY` on committee ID. **58.3% of IDs land.** Candidate committees aren't in the PAC summary, as expected.
- Scam-PAC test, 2024 cycle: non-connected PACs (types N, Q) with $500K+ from individuals, leaving out joint fundraisers, leadership PACs and super PACs. That leaves 75.
  - Median share passed to candidates, independent spending or affiliates: 17.6%. 21 PACs pass under 5%.
  - **No treasurer has more than 1 of those 21.**
  - The first cut "found" Hobbs, Kilgore and Lisker behind 11-16 low-giving PACs each. That came entirely from joint fundraising committees (Trump 47, Harris Victory Fund, NRSC Victory), which move money as transfers.

**Hit means / miss means.** A hit would have tied a few treasurers to many take-the-money PACs. **Miss:** the concentration is ordinary compliance work.

**Traps (for the ledger).**
- **Up to 4 versions of each committee are stacked, with no cycle column.**
  - 38,693 IDs in 60,031 rows: 21,750 once, 13,360 twice, 2,771 three times, 812 four times.
  - Versions differ on treasurer for 4,610 IDs and on sponsor for 12,489.
  - Pick a version on purpose.
- **The glance's top-name counts don't reproduce.** It said "442" for several names; the real top is 13 (COLLINS FOR CONGRESS).
- `TRES_NM` is blank on 2,019 committees, and filler 'N, N N' on 108.
- **PAC summary `PCT_DISBURSED_TO_OTHERS` ignores transfers to affiliates.**
  - SMP reads 3.3% for 2024 while it moved $312.9M to an affiliate and booked $0 in independent spending.
  - Harris Victory Fund reads 0% after moving $899M.
  - Never use it as a scam-PAC score.
- The PAC summary also has no cycle column. Take the cycle from `COVERAGE_END_DATE`.

**Next join.** Schedule B disbursements (operating spending) for the 21 low-giving PACs. That's where fundraising-vendor money would show.
