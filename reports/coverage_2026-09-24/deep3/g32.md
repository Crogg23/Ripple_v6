## 1. FINANCE__FED_SEC_DERA_SUB_2026Q1: live

**What it is.** One row per SEC filing that carried machine-readable financials, Jan 2 to Mar 31 2026. 6,169 filings. ADSH (the filing number) is unique.
This quarter is 10-K season, so it answers one question: **who filed their annual report on time?**

**How the test works.**
- Group: companies with a Dec 31 year-end that filed a Sep 30 quarterly report (10-Q) in the prior fall.
- Hit: a 10-K for that Dec 31 year, filed by the deadline.
- Small ("non-accelerated") companies have 90 days, so March 31. The test uses March 31 for everyone. That is loose for big companies, whose real deadlines come earlier.
- FY2023's deadline was **April 1 2024**, because March 31 fell on a Sunday. 285 small companies filed on April 1 2024. Using March 31 would count them as misses: 993 instead of 708.

**The finding.**
- FY2025: **439 of 2,236 small companies (19.6%)** had no 10-K by March 31 2026.
  - Large accelerated filers: 32 of 1,604 (2.0%). Accelerated: 23 of 598 (3.8%).
- Small companies, three years: **28.0%** (708 of 2,531), **23.2%** (523 of 2,252), **19.6%** (439 of 2,236).
- **It's a habit, not bad luck.**
  - 1,618 small companies are in all three groups. **120 missed all three years.** Chance alone predicts 9.
  - Missed in FY2024: 62.5% missed again in FY2025. On time in FY2024: 11.2% missed in FY2025.
- **Where you trade matters.** FY2025 small-company miss rate by listing (SEC ticker file):
  - Nasdaq/NYSE: **10.2%** (136 of 1,332). OTC: **39.6%** (166 of 419).
  - Same shape in FY2023 (10.6% vs 39.3%) and FY2024 (11.0% vs 42.4%).
- **China/HK-based small companies miss at 2.5-3x the US rate, every year:**
  - FY2023: 64.3% (27 of 42) vs US 26.4%.
  - FY2024: 54.8% (23 of 42) vs US 21.5%.
  - FY2025: 48.5% (16 of 33) vs US 18.2%.
  - Country comes from each company's filings *before* the blank-country trap began.
- **The 120 repeat missers:** 30 list a Nasdaq/NYSE ticker, 63 OTC, 9 no exchange, 18 not in the ticker file.
  - 26 of them were more than 15 days late (past the legal extension) in *both* FY2023 and FY2024. Then no FY2025 10-K by March 31.
  - 27 if you count Conectisys, which shows no 10-K at all (see traps).
  - 7 of those 26 still list a Nasdaq/NYSE ticker. Days late (FY2023 / FY2024):

| Company | Ticker | FY2023 | FY2024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| WeTouch Technology (China) | WETH | 16 | **164** |
| Laser Photonics | LASE | 18 | 85 |
| Xcel Brands | XELB | 18 | 58 |
| Smith-Midland | SMID | 52 | 57 |
| BIO-key International | BKYI | 65 | 23 |
| Global Interactive Technologies (Korea) | GITS | 106 | 30 |
| EON Resources | EONR | ~32 (see traps) | 16 |

- Full list of 120: `deep3/g32/chronic_late_filers_120.csv`.

**The join, and it missed.** Did insiders sell before a missed deadline? SEC Form 4 insider trades, matched on CIK. Insider data ends March 2025, so this used the FY2024 cycle.
- Small companies with no 10-K in the Q1 2025 file (517):
  - Insider open-market sales, Jan-Mar 2025: 7.0% of late filers, 3.4% of those that went dark.
  - On-time small peers: **17.5%**.
- Late filers' insiders were *quieter*. Any Form 4 at all: 41% and 37%, vs 66% for on-time peers.
- The insider table is complete through March 2025, so this miss means something: **no cash-out signal in aggregate.**

**Checks run.**
- Peer: filer size (3 classes), listing venue, country, SPAC vs not.
- Time: three deadline cycles, same calendar dates.
- Persistence vs chance.
- One join (insider trades) plus a second (ticker file for the venue).
- Completeness: about 98% of large companies hit, all three years, so the files aren't missing big chunks.

**The dull explanations, tested.**
- *Legal extension (NT 10-K, 15 days).*
  - FY2023: 299 of 708 misses filed within 15 days. FY2024: 238 of 523.
  - Among the 120 repeat missers, the median was exactly 15 days late both years. **Most repeat missers use the full extension every year.** That's legal, and it's the honest frame.
  - FY2025 extension filings can't be seen yet: the data stops March 31 2026.
- *Takeovers.* The companies that went quiet include takeovers. The 19 large companies that stopped filing all look like takeovers (Marathon Oil, Avangrid, Stericycle and more; checked from memory). Small ones include SPAC wind-downs and bankruptcies (Danimer Scientific, Gold Flora).
  - The 2% large-company rate is the takeover-plus-data-gap floor.
- *SPACs.* Blank-check shells miss more (54.7%, 41.2%, 21.6%). Without them, small-company rates are 25.2%, 21.8%, 19.4%. The trend holds.
- *Falling trend = churn.* Late filers drop out of later groups. Among the 1,618 in all three years, rates *rose*: 15.9%, 17.5%, 20.1%.
- Not tested: auditor turnover (no auditor field). The May 2024 BF Borgers ban hit this kind of company.

**Hit means.** A named, checkable list: 120 companies that miss every year. Some are on major exchanges, where listing rules bite.
**Miss would have meant.** Misses scattered at random. They aren't: 13x chance.

**Traps (for the ledger).**
- **COUNTRYBA/COUNTRYMA go blank for foreign filers from 2025Q2:** 6-8 rows per quarter through 2025Q1, then 66, 240, 307, 404.
  - CITYBA is still filled (399 of 404). Unilever (London) and Vancouver miners are among them.
  - The blanks are already in the raw landing table (404), so they came in with the file or the loader, not the mart.
  - Fix: take the country from the same CIK's earlier filings.
- **EIN `000000000`** on 21-27% of rows every quarter. It's not just foreign companies: 13.4% of US-incorporated rows (1,974 rows over three quarters).
- **PERIOD can be off.** EON Resources' FY2023 10-K says period 20231031. It was filed May 3 2024.
- Conectisys Corp filed all nine of its 10-Qs across the 13 quarters, and no 10-K. Either it never files annual reports, or its 10-K isn't in the SEC data set. Unresolved.
- **Joined-table trap:** in `SEC_INSIDER_NONDERIV_TRANS`, one OpGen Form 4 (0002034863-24-000002) puts the total dollars in the price-per-share field.
  - 1.8M shares at "$913,265" reads as a **$1.64 trillion** sale. The filing's two lines total $1.92T. One row can swamp any sum.
- The glance's "ADSH top 49" is wrong. ADSH is unique in all three SEC tables.

## 2. FINANCE__FED_SEC_DERA_SUB_2025Q3: probed

**What it is.** 6,541 filings, Jul 1 to Sep 30 2025. Mostly 10-Qs (5,249).

**Completeness check.** Of the large companies that filed a 10-Q this quarter, 1,892 of 1,924 (98.3%) show up again in Q4.
Accelerated: 97.4%. Small: 95.1%. The quarters are close to whole.

**The finding: a shell factory with one phone number.**
- Phone 561-464-2841 / 55 NE 5th Ave, Boca Raton FL sits on **8 SEC filers** across 9 quarters.
- 3 started as empty blank-check shells (SIC 6770) and turned into operating companies under the same CIK:
  - Venetian-1 Acquisition to **Lomond Therapeutics** (OrbiMed funds are reported holders)
  - Aspen-1 Acquisition to **Deep Isolation Nuclear**
  - Surfside Acquisition to **Deep Fission** (nuclear reactors)
- The panel has 64 shell-to-company switches in all. **This phone number has 3.** The next-best number has 2.
- 5 fresh shells from the same address filed Q3 2025 to Q1 2026: Orielle, Los Altos Ventures, VAR, Range, Primus Acquisition.
- Insider join: all three original shells' 2022 ownership filings list the same two reporting owners, Ian Seth Jacobs and Mark N. Tompkins of Boca Raton.

**Also found: headquarters that are mail drops.**
- 8 filers give 30 N Gould St, Sheridan WY as their business address. That is a well-known registered-agent address (from memory, not checked).
- Their phones are in Toronto (647), Nova Scotia (902) and New York (646, 212). All are tiny non-accelerated filers.
- Other mail drops seen: 1309 Coffeen Ave, Sheridan (2), 16192 Coastal Hwy, Lewes DE (3), 8 The Green, Dover DE (6).

**Checks run.** Concentration: CIKs per address and per phone. Time: which quarter each shell first appeared. One join: insider reporting owners. Quarter-to-quarter completeness.

**Dull explanation.** Form 10 shells sold for reverse mergers are legal. They are a known route to going public without an IPO. Nothing here shows harm.
**Hit means.** A named sponsor pipeline: two nuclear startups went public through it in 2025.
**Miss means.** No harm or enforcement found. It's a business-press lead, not an investigation.

**Traps.** Same family traps as #1. A name-change search is useless: FORMER/CHANGED hold only the latest rename and are blank on 43% of rows. Only 47 companies show a 2025 change date.

## 4. FINANCE__FED_CFTC_COT_FINANCIAL_HIST: probed

**What it is.** Weekly positions of dealers, asset managers, hedge funds and others in 41 financial futures markets. 2010-07-20 to 2012-12-31, futures only. 4,615 rows, no duplicates.

**The finding is a hole in the timeline.**
- The main table, `EDUCATION__FED_CFTC_COT_FINANCIAL`, starts **2013-01-08**. It holds 2013 to Aug 2026.
- This table is the 2010-12 slice. `scripts/cftc_cot_history_load.py` parked it here because the older files name a date column `REPORT_DATE_AS_MM_DD_YYYY`, not `_YYYY_MM_DD`.
- The loader also planned **2006-2009** from CFTC's 2006-2016 bundle. The checkpoint log has no entry for it.
  - No week before 2010-07-20 is in either table.
  - The series starts mid-2006 (per the loader's own notes). So **mid-2006 to mid-2010 is missing, including the 2008 crash.**

**Within the table.**
- How concentrated: median share of short positions held by the 4 biggest traders.
  - The median market: 41%.
  - S&P GSCI: **94%**. Brazilian real: 89%. 10-year swap futures: 78%.
- Time: in VIX futures, hedge funds went from net short 16.9% of open interest (Jul 12 2011) to net long 3.1% (Aug 16 2011), through the August 2011 sell-off.
- VIX open interest averaged 125K contracts (late 2010), 181K (2011), 320K (2012).

**Consistency check.** Reported plus non-reported positions equal open interest on 4,520 of 4,615 rows. The other 95 are off by 1 contract, all on the three "Consolidated" markets.

**Dull explanation.** This is known market history. No story.
**Hit means.** Usable once it's stacked under the main table.
**Miss means.** Leave it alone until then.

**Traps.**
- The split itself: a time series on the main table silently starts in 2013.
- Numbers are text with leading spaces. Codes carry trailing spaces.
- Trader-count columns use `.` for "not shown" on up to 1,789 rows (other-reportables long). Treat it as missing, not zero.
- **The "Consolidated" rows (S&P 500, NASDAQ-100, DJIA) are sums of other rows:** the big contract plus the mini, scaled (S&P and NASDAQ minis divided by 5, the Dow $5 mini by 2). Checked to within 1 contract every week. Adding open interest across markets double counts them.
