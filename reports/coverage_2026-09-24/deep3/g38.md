## 1. FracFocus water sources: in 2025, 40% of Diamondback's frack water was fresh groundwater. Its Permian peers: 10%. **live**

**Physical thing:** each row is one water source for one frack job, as the operator filed it with FracFocus: fresh groundwater, surface water, produced water or brackish water, plus its share of the job's water. Joined by DISCLOSURE_ID to the disclosure list, which gives the gallons.

**Checked**
- Profile (S09, S10): 23,747 rows, 20,302 disclosures.
  - All of them land in the disclosure list.
  - Water rows only exist on form version 4, plus 37 on version 3.
  - 20,265 of the 34,795 version-4 disclosures (58%) carry any water rows.
- ⚠ **Reporting is patchy by operator** (S15, local).
  - The share of an operator's wells with a water row runs from 0 to 100%.
  - Across operator-county cells with 20+ wells: 25th percentile 14%, median 79%.
  - Example: Endeavor Energy Resources, which Diamondback bought in 2024, filed 206 version-4 disclosures and **zero** water rows.
  - So each comparison below uses **complete reporters only**: operators with a water row on 90%+ of their version-4 wells in that county.
- Fresh groundwater means the operator's own code "Groundwater, < 1000TDS". Gallons = that percent x TOTAL_BASE_WATER_VOLUME.
- **Time** (S18, raw job dates, parsed locally). 15 core Permian counties: TX Midland, Martin, Andrews, Howard, Glasscock, Reeves, Upton, Reagan, Loving, Ward, Ector, Culberson, Winkler; NM Lea, Eddy.

| Job year | Diamondback wells | Diamondback fresh GW share | Other complete reporters' wells | Their fresh GW share | Diamondback GW gallons | Everyone else's GW gallons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 379 | 37.3% | 1,851 | 14.6% | 3.37B | 5.76B |
| 2025 | 506 | **40.5%** | 1,753 | **10.4%** | **4.80B** | **3.92B** |
| 2026 (to July) | 253 | 17.2% | 755 | 6.9% | 1.10B | 1.17B |

- **Peer by distance, all years:**
  - Diamondback's complete-reporter wells were 36.4% fresh groundwater.
  - Wells of 35 other operators within 5 miles of them (840 wells): 22.5%.
  - Diamondback is above its 5-mile neighbors in 8 of its 10 counties. It's below in Glasscock (28% vs 39%) and Upton (35% vs 53%).
- **Midland County, 2025:** Diamondback 63.8% on 98 wells. Other operators 0.9% on 159 wells.
  - All years, within 5 miles: 46.9% vs 4.5%. The neighbors are 302 wells, mostly Chevron and Apache.
- **Placebo:** the same 5-mile test on the other operators with 100+ Permian wells.
  - Apache 11.9% vs 24.6%. Mewbourne 4.8% vs 14.0%. Cimarex 3.3% vs 11.6%. Chevron 10.3% vs 10.2%. COG 14.0% vs 11.9%.
  - They sit under, level with or just over their neighbors. Only Matador is clearly above: 19.0% vs 10.1%.
- **Not a few wells:**
  - The median Diamondback 2025 well was 48.5% fresh groundwater.
  - 61.5% of its 2025 wells used some.
  - Martin County holds 335 wells and 2.62B of the 4.80B gallons. Leave Martin out and Diamondback is still 44.3% (all years).
- **Robustness:**
  - A wider list of 38 Permian counties gives 2025: 4.89B vs 4.13B, 40.5% vs 10.2%.
  - Drop the complete-reporter filter and 2025 reads 4.80B vs 5.88B, 40.5% vs 11.0%. Diamondback is 17% of the wells and 45% of the fresh groundwater.

**What a hit means:** by its own filings, one operator uses fresh aquifer water at about 4 times the basin rate in 2025, in a drought-stressed basin.
- Against the wells within 5 miles, all years, it's 1.6 times (36.4% vs 22.5%).
- It holds by year, by county (8 of 10), by 5-mile radius and by the median well.
**What a miss means:** a missing water row says nothing about the job. 42% of version-4 disclosures have none, and some operators never file one.

**Dull explanation:** Diamondback's acreage has less produced water on hand to recycle, or its water supplier labels water differently.
- Tested in part: the 5-mile neighbors, drilling the same rock, recycle far more (Midland 4.5% fresh).
- Not tested: the "< 1000 TDS" label is self-coded by each operator. A supplier's labeling habit could move it.
- **Next step:** the fresh vs recycled numbers in Diamondback's own sustainability report for 2024-2025, set against these filings. Then the Texas Water Development Board groundwater-use permits for Martin and Midland.

**Traps found**
- ⚠ The mart's **JOB_START_DATE and JOB_END_DATE are null on all 248,835 disclosures**. The raw landing holds them as text like `7/7/2014 12:00:00 AM`. The cast dropped them. 15 are blank in raw, 34 start after they end.
- Water rows only exist on form version 4. Versions 1-3 (214,040 disclosures) can't be read for water source at all.
- 267 disclosures' percents sum under 100, and 249 rows hold 0%.
- 107 disclosure-plus-type pairs repeat. They're distinct sources of the same type, like 3% and 97% groundwater.
- 154 wells (API) carry two water disclosures. Refracs or amendments: dedupe by API before counting wells.

## 2. AQS air-monitor sites: monitors follow people, not emitters. **probed**

**Physical thing:** every air-monitoring site EPA's Air Quality System ever held, open or closed, with a point and open and close dates. It has no pollutant list, so a site can be an ozone-only monitor.

**Checked**
- Profile (S07, S08): 20,994 sites, one row each. 4,744 have no close date.
- **Time** (S11, local). Sites open at year end:
  - 5,951 in 1980, 4,415 in 1990, 4,219 in 2000, 4,534 in 2015, 4,720 in 2025.
  - No recent collapse. Closures run 23-41 a year since 2020, openings 40-64.
  - Puerto Rico lost 2 sites on 2017-10-01, right after Maria. One is Guayanilla. Only 3 opened there since.
- **Join** (S12, S13, local): TRI 2023, 21,761 plants with coordinates.
  - Distance to the nearest site open in 2023 (4,692 sites): median 5.3 miles.
  - People within 3 miles, from 2020 tract population centers.
- **Concentration:**
  - 8,717 plants released cancer-listed chemicals to air: 60.7M pounds.
  - The top 100 hold 34%. Their median distance to a monitor is 8.4 miles.
  - 49 of the 100 have none within 10 miles. 25 have none within 20.
- **Peer comparison**, as expected counts for those top 100:
  - Same-state TRI plants predict 34.9 beyond 10 miles. Same-sector plants predict 36.1.
  - **Same population decile predicts 46.2.** Actual is 49.
  - Beyond 20 miles: 25 actual vs 22.4 expected.
- Most of the top 100 are fiberglass boat, bathtub and pool makers releasing styrene (61 of 100). They sit in small towns.

**What a hit means:** a plant with no monitor of any kind within 10 miles has no government air reading near it. That's a fair claim, because the site list is complete.
**What a miss means:** a monitor nearby doesn't mean it measures the plant's chemical. Most sites are ozone or PM only.
**Dull explanation, tested:** monitors go where people are. It holds. Once population is matched, the big emitters are no more remote than other plants.
- Single cases still read well, e.g. Formosa Plastics Point Comfort TX. It released 200,131 lbs of cancer-listed chemicals to air. The nearest open monitor is 29 miles off, and one 1.5 miles away closed in 1980.
- But there's no systemic story here.

**Traps found**
- 13,080 of 20,994 establish dates (62%) fall on Jan 1, and 8,893 of 16,250 close dates on Dec 31. Most dates are only good to the year.
- 2 sites are "established" after the 2026-06-26 extract date, the latest on 2026-12-01.
- 874 sites have 0 or blank coordinates, 19 of them open.
- 1,948 have a blank or UNKNOWN setting.
- "Open" means no close date. It doesn't prove a monitor is running.

## 3. EPA corporate crosswalk: can't sum pollution per company. **dead**

**Physical thing:** one row per EPA registry ID (5,300,149, all distinct). A name match puts a company ID (LEI) on the facility, and sometimes a parent, an SEC CIK and a UEI.

**Checked**
- Profile (S03): 279,621 rows matched (5.3%).
  - Brand: 205,673 rows, only 56 companies.
  - Exact: 59,121 rows, 17,821 companies.
  - Fuzzy: 14,827 rows.
- MATCH_CONFIDENCE is a method label, not a score: 1.00 exact, 0.85 brand or fuzzy, 0.80 fuzzy.
- REVIEW_FLAG is False on 1,477,946 rows and blank on the rest. It's never True.
- Top matches (S04, S05):
  - **Con Edison: 50,931 "facilities"**, mostly manholes and service boxes. That's 18.2% of all matches.
  - Branded gas stations go to the oil company: Exxon 9,793, Chevron 7,302, Shell 3,821, and Sunoco 5,404 to Energy Transfer. Most are dealer-owned.
  - "UNION PACIFIC RES CO" wells go to the railroad. That was a separate oil company.
- **Second-field test against TRI 2023** (S12, local). TRI plants file their own parent name.
  - 2,212 of 21,761 TRI plants are matched (10.2%). They hold 12.5% of TRI cancer-chemical air pounds.
  - **16,274 of the 18,143 TRI plants that name a parent (89.7%) get no match.** Unmatched: CRH 417 plants, Quikrete 212, Berkshire Hathaway 198, Tyson 92, Marathon Petroleum 91.
  - Where both exist, 72.9% share a name word. Many misses stop at the subsidiary, like Lubrizol with no Berkshire.
  - Some matches are wrong because the registry keeps an old occupant's name:
    - P&G Greensboro matched to Teva.
    - Owens Corning Joplin matched to Mars Petcare.
    - MECS matched to Monsanto/Bayer.
- PARENT_CIK is filled on 119,382 rows, only 220 of them TRI plants.

**What a hit means:** a match is a brand or name match. Treat it as a lead, not ownership.
**What a miss means:** nothing. 90% of heavy-industry parents are missing.
**Dull explanation:** it was built against a list of big public brands. It does what it was built for, which isn't this.

**Traps found**
- Brand matches put dealer and franchise sites under the brand owner.
- Con Edison manholes swamp any "facilities per company" count.
- The FRS facility name can be an old occupant's.
- ⚠ PARENT_UEI is filled on 34,359 **unmatched** rows. It's the circular UEI already in the ledger.

## 4-5. FRS NAICS and SIC code tables: lookups. **skip**

  - NAICS: 2,179,702 rows, 1,673,834 registry IDs.
  - SIC: 1,085,211 rows, 833,695 registry IDs.
- No exact duplicates, except TRIS: 152 in NAICS, 2 in SIC.
- NAICS TRIS has 147 blank codes.

**Traps found**
- ⚠ **Short NAICS codes:** 682,849 of RCRAInfo's 1,196,332 codes (57%) are under 6 digits, like `56299`. EIS has 15,055 and RMP 24,870. A 6-digit join misses them.
- ⚠ **REGISTRY_ID holds a program ID on 15,561 NAICS rows and 2,361 SIC rows** (S16):
  - RCRA handler IDs like `AKR000211722`
  - NPDES permits like `AKG572138`
  - 18-character AIR IDs
  - a few with a NAICS code glued on, like `CABAA 324110`

  Filter with `LEFT(REGISTRY_ID,3)='110'` before joining.
- Top codes: NAICS `56299` (368.6K, all RCRAInfo) and `236115` (NPDES construction stormwater). SIC `1521` (169.7K). These are construction and waste sites, not factories.
