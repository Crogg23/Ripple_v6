## 1. FAERS OUTC: the 2012 cancer-drug death "spike" is a pile of late paperwork

**Headline.** In 2012q4, FAERS got 13,752 death cases naming Tarceva, Avastin, Xeloda, Rituxan or Herceptin as the prime suspect.
In 2011q3 the same five drugs had 198. The 2012q4 reports reached the FDA a median 253-898 days after the company first knew of them. In 2011q3, 2013q3 and 2014q2, the same five drugs took 3-9 days.

**Per drug, 2012q4** (query #19; days = company-aware date MFR_DT to FDA receipt FDA_DT)

| Drug | Cases | Death cases | Median days, company to FDA | Over 15 days | Over 1 year | Same drug in 2011q3: median days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tarceva (incl. "ERLOTINIB TABLET") | 8,920 | 7,963 | 898 | 7,903 | 5,159 | 8 |
| Avastin | 4,243 | 3,648 | 253 | 3,274 | 1,584 | 8 |
| Xeloda | 1,377 | 935 | 511 | 950 | 782 | 8 |
| Rituxan | 915 | 694 | 270 | 768 | 374 | 8 |
| Herceptin | 777 | 512 | 260 | 528 | 305 | 8 |

- Senders (query #15): Roche sent the Avastin, Xeloda, Rituxan and Herceptin reports. Astellas, Tarceva's US license holder, sent the Tarceva ones.
- 2012q1 had a smaller batch like it: Tarceva 890 cases, median 543 days. Avastin 1,184 cases, 567 of them over 15 days.
- **(memory)** In June 2012, European regulators said Roche had failed to process about 80,000 US patient-support-program reports, including about 15,000 deaths. The size and timing match. Check the EMA release.

**The same pattern elsewhere (query #19):**
- Tracleer (Actelion), 2010q4: 2,883 cases, 2,756 deaths, median 859 days. Next quarters: 9-12 days.
- Glivec (Novartis), 2012q4: 2,256 cases, 2,060 deaths, median 2,239 days (6 years). Gleevec 2011q2: 1,501 cases, median 1,086 days.

**Checks**
- #11: new death cases per suspect drug per year, counted by case not report version, against the drug's own prior 2 years. Found the jumps.
- #15: per quarter, who sent them, and the event date to FDA gap.
- #19: company-aware to FDA gap in spike quarters vs 7 control quarters, same drugs.

**Hit means:** the "spike" is years of old reports filed at once. It is not new deaths. The story is the late filing.
**Miss would have meant:** fresh reports with normal 1-2 week gaps. That would point to a real new safety signal.

**Dull explanation, tested:** 2012q4 is the quarter the FDA switched to its new system (FAERS). Maybe the switch made old cases look new?
Test: other drugs in that same quarter had normal gaps. Tracleer 9 days, Gleevec 6 days. So the switch did not cause it.

**Traps**
- EVENT_DT is blank on most dump reports (244 of 7,416 Tarceva first-time cases have one). Use MFR_DT to FDA_DT for the lag.
- Drug names split: TARCEVA and ERLOTINIB TABLET. GLEEVEC turns into GLIVEC at the 2012q4 switch. Add up all the spellings.
- A case has several report versions. Count cases (C_CASE / CASEID), not ISRs.

**Next join:** none needed in the warehouse. Next step is outside it: the EMA 2012 release and FDA inspection records for Genentech/Roche.

## 2. FAERS REAC: the biggest heart-attack spike in 10 years of FDA data is one company's backlog

**Headline.** In 2014q2, GSK filed 20,863 Avandia cases listing a heart attack or stroke. The six quarters before that had 340-1,054 each.
438 of those cases carry an event date. For them, the median gap from event to FDA was 2,730 days (7.5 years), and 291 of the events happened before 2008.

- Company-aware to FDA: median 100 days (#19). 24,508 of the 25,352 Avandia cases that quarter were past 15 days, but only 38 were past a year. So GSK learned of them in early 2014 and filed them months later, about events that were years old.
- Reporter type: consumer. For heart attacks, 14,239 of the 14,941 cases (#14). Only 2 are coded lawyer.
- 416 of the 20,863 have a death outcome (#17).
- **(memory)** The FDA eased Avandia's prescribing limits in November 2013, six months before this wave. Check the date.

**How I found it (#10):** for every side-effect term, I counted new cases per quarter against that term's own average over the prior 4 quarters. I flagged 3x jumps with 300+ cases.
Then I drilled 18 of the biggest (#14): the top suspect drug, and who filed.

**In 9 of those 18 spikes, one drug made up 80% or more of the new cases. Most are litigation or company batches:**

| Spike | Drug | Cases | Filed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heart attack, 2014q2 | Avandia | 14,941 | 14,239 consumer, via GSK |
| Type 2 diabetes, 2014q1 | Lipitor | 3,132 | 3,124 lawyer (99.7%), via Pfizer |
| Tardive dyskinesia, 2011q3 | metoclopramide (2 spellings) | 6,230 | 5,972 of all 6,393 new TD cases lawyer (93%) |
| Cardiovascular disorder, 2005q4 | Vioxx | 2,194 | 2,094 lawyer (95%) |
| Breast cancer, 2010q2 | Provera | 2,958 | 1,269 lawyer |
| Device expulsion, 2011q3 | Mirena | 4,257 | 1,994 consumer, 0 lawyer |
| Nausea / abnormal dreams, 2010q3 | Chantix | 8,019 / 2,983 | 6,631 / 2,265 consumer |
| Incorrect dose, 2009q3 | Humira | 3,912 | 3,086 consumer |
| Injection-site pain, 2007q4 | Enbrel | 2,885 | 2,756 consumer |

**Hit means:** a raw spike in FAERS reactions is usually a lawsuit or a company program dumping reports, not a new danger. Any "reports of X surged" story built on this table needs the reporter-type and lag check first.
**Miss would have meant:** spikes spread across many drugs and reporters, which looks like a real signal.

**Dull explanation:** these are known "stimulated reporting" effects. That's true. The Avandia wave is the story because of its size, its timing, and events 7.5 years old.

**Traps**
- The glance's "top report IDs" were fake. ISR 8390205 has 104 rows, not 19.3K (#3). APPROX_TOP_K makes up counts on nearly unique columns. It's the same artifact the earlier deep pass flagged.
- IDs switch at 2012q4: ISR goes blank and PRIMARYID fills in (#1). Join on `coalesce(ISR, PRIMARYID)`. Case = `coalesce(C_CASE, CASEID)`.
- 2012q3 is a short quarter (503K rows vs 835K next door), because the old system ended partway through.
- Some terms show up from nothing: MATERNAL EXPOSURE DURING PREGNANCY in 2011q3 (prior average 0.3), PERIPHERAL SWELLING in 2014q2, INJECTION SITE BRUISING in 2012q4. That's likely a change in the coding dictionary, not new events.
- No duplicate loads: distinct report+term pairs equal the row count in every quarter except 2013q2, which has 35 extra rows (#1, #2). 99.9999% of REAC rows land on a DEMO report (#10).
- The table ends at 2014q2, a series that stops (known trap).

**Next join:** DEMO + DRUG, already used. Outside the warehouse: the Avandia MDL settlement timeline.

## 3. Guttmacher monthly abortions: Texas-at-home up, the old Texas travel hubs down

**Headline.** Guttmacher's middle estimate for Texas, a total-ban state, averaged 3,530 a month in Jan-Mar 2026, up from 2,797 in Jan-Mar 2024 (+26%).
Over the same months, New Mexico fell from 1,727 to 1,440 (-17%) and Kansas from 2,090 to 1,663 (-20%).

**Same quarter, two years apart, with Guttmacher's own low-high range** (monthly average; queries #8, #13)

| State | Q1 2024 | Q1 2026 (low-high) | Change | Survives the range? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TX (ban) | 2,797 | 3,530 (3,017-4,147) | +26% | yes: the 2026 low is above the 2024 figure |
| All 11 ban states | 5,807 | 7,907 (7,253-8,710) | +36% | yes |
| NM | 1,727 | 1,440 (1,387-1,497) | -17% | yes |
| KS | 2,090 (2,040-2,167) | 1,663 (1,583-1,763) | -20% | yes, the ranges don't touch |
| CO | 2,287 (2,157-2,500) | 1,947 (1,753-2,197) | -15% | no, the ranges overlap |
| FL | 8,177 (7,753-8,733) | 5,520 (4,953-6,157) | -32% | yes |

- **Louisiana**: 563 a month in Jul-Sep 2024, then 733 (Oct-Dec 2024), 833 (Q1 2025), 867 (Q1 2026, range 640-1,230).
  **(memory)** Louisiana made the abortion pills controlled substances from Oct 2024 and indicted a New York doctor in Jan 2025. No drop shows up. The 2026 low end (640) is still above the pre-law quarter (563).
- **Ban-state peers, per 100K residents, Q1 2026** (2020 census, total population, not women 15-44): MS 18.8, LA 18.6, AL 13.7, TX 12.1 ... the median ban state is TN at 9.3, and WV is lowest at 3.9. MS and LA are about double the median ban state.
- Florida: April 2024 hit 9,900, then May 5,510 (from memory, its 6-week ban started May 1, 2024).
- National: Q1 2024 99,133 a month, Q1 2026 97,377. The total barely moved while the geography shifted.

**Hit means:** in ban states the counted abortions keep rising, and they are almost all pills by mail. The NOTES line says telehealth is included, and a total-ban state has almost no clinic abortions to count. Meanwhile, the border states that took Texas travelers are shrinking.
**Miss would have meant:** flat ban-state counts and steady hubs.

**Dull explanation:** these are Guttmacher's own modeled estimates, and Guttmacher publishes these trends itself. The added value here is the same-quarter comparison with ranges, and the per-person ranking of ban states. The table can't show where each patient came from. "Texans switched from travel to mail" is an inference.

**Traps**
- 2,040 rows = states + DC + a **US** row + two aggregate rows ("States with total bans", "States without total bans"). Summing MEDIAN counts everyone three times (10.8M). The glance's "160 rows over 10x median" is mostly those aggregate rows.
- The 11 ban states have **no rows before July 2023**. The ban aggregate reads 0 for Jan-Jun 2023. That's "not measured", not zero. MO, ND and WI also read 0.0 in early 2023.
- Low = high = middle for ban states until the end of 2024. Guttmacher gave no range for those months.
- MONTH is a mid-month text date ('2023-01-15'). SOURCE and PUBLISHDATE are constant.
- The states add up to the US row within about 150-360 a month (rounding to 10).

**Next join:** CORE.DIM_STATE + DIM_COUNTY for population (done). CDC WONDER births would give a harm angle.

## 4. FAERS INDI: fentanyl sprays sold for cancer pain were reported for back pain and migraine

**Headline.** Actiq and Fentora are fentanyl products approved only for breakthrough cancer pain.
FAERS cases list a named non-cancer reason 810 times (Actiq 420, Fentora 390), against 195 cancer reasons (#18).
The top non-cancer reasons are back pain (123 Actiq, 116 Fentora), migraine (42 / 55), fibromyalgia, neck pain and complex regional pain syndrome.

**Robustness (#21): is it just consumers? No.**

| Period | Health professionals: % non-cancer | Consumers: % non-cancer |
|---|---|---|
| 2004-2011 (before the class-wide safety program) | 54% (213 of 398; cancer 84) | 72% (342 of 474) |
| 2012q1-q3 (program on, old format) | 43% (of 56) | 67% (of 97) |
| 2012q4-2014q2 (new format) | 19% (of 134) | 41% (of 184) |

- Peer brands that had the restricted program from launch mostly name cancer: Onsolis 68 cancer vs 4 non-cancer, Subsys 33 vs 4.
- **(memory)** Cephalon pleaded guilty in 2008 over off-label promotion of Actiq. The class-wide TIRF safety program (REMS) started in 2012. Check both.

**Hit means:** the FDA's own reports carry the off-label pattern the DOJ prosecuted. Even health professionals named non-cancer reasons 2.5x as often as cancer before 2012.
**Miss means:** Subsys (Insys) reports mostly name cancer. That proves nothing about Insys: the reason field repeats whatever paperwork says, and Insys's case was about false paperwork.

**Dull explanation:** reporters write the reason field themselves, and reports are not prescriptions. The health-professional split answers the "consumers skew it" objection. The drop after 2012 partly overlaps the format switch, but it already shows up in the old format (54% to 43%, small numbers).
This is an old story. Use it as a method piece or a sidebar, not a scoop.

**Traps (#2, #20)**
- **2014q1 is missing from INDI.** 41 quarters, not 42. REAC and OUTC have it.
- The link to a drug changes at 2012q4. Old format: `INDI.DRUG_SEQ` = `DRUG.DRUG_SEQ` (a long global ID). New format: `INDI_DRUG_SEQ` + PRIMARYID = `DRUG_SEQ` (1, 2, 3 within a report).
- "Unknown reason" switches from capitals to mixed case at 2012q4 (2012q4 has both: 2,838 upper, 59,552 mixed). It makes up 13-24% of lines. Match without case.

**Next join:** DEMO age, to test antipsychotic use in children (not done).

## 5. CMS pending physicians: probed. Florida's list looks stale, not new

**What the table is:** 7,240 NPIs with a first Medicare enrollment still pending. All distinct. 7,237 are well-formed. It has no application date.

**Join results (#4, #6)**

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Found in NPPES | 6,879 (95%); last name agrees on 6,342 |
| NPI deactivated in NPPES, never reactivated | 215 |
| NPPES says it's an organization NPI | 90 (physicians' own "MD PA" / "PLLC" NPIs, #22) |
| On the OIG exclusion list (LEIE, real NPI, names agree) | 7 people, 8 exclusion rows. None reinstated. 6 in FL, 1 in NC |
| Billed Medicare Part B in 2024 | 310, $10.05M. Only 19 changed state, so "they moved" is not the reason |
| Also on the non-physician pending list | 17 |

**Peer comparison (#7, #12): pending doctors per 1,000 Part B physicians.** Median state 7.9. PR 48.9, FL 21.0, AL 19.1, DC 16.7, GA 15.2.
- Elsewhere the pending pile is new doctors. The biggest addresses are academic medical centers with brand-new NPIs: 80 at one Birmingham address, 34 Yale, 32 Hershey, 28 Duke (0-2 old NPIs each).
- In Florida it's old ones: 572 of 1,008 FL NPIs were issued before 2016. That's 42% of all such old NPIs on the list (FL + PR: 50%). Florida has 6.6% of Part B physicians.
- Stale NPPES records (last updated before 2017): FL 390 of 1,008 (39%), PR 81 of 164 (49%), everywhere else 362 of 5,492 (6.6%).
- It's not one clinic (#16). The top FL address has 23, and they spread across Miami 100, Tampa 54, Jacksonville 46.
- FL old-NPI mix: chiropractors 115, dentists 45, internal medicine 42, family medicine 34, optometrists 26.

**Hit means:** Florida and Puerto Rico's part of the list is full of long-established or long-dormant providers. So either their Medicare contractor holds old applications open, or veteran providers there keep re-applying.
**(memory)** FL, PR and the USVI share one Medicare contractor (First Coast). Check.
**Miss would have meant:** Florida looks like everywhere else, new grads.

**Why only probed:** there is no application date, so I can't say how long anything has been pending. A hostile editor asks "stale list or real backlog?" The table can't answer that.
The 7 excluded people are pending, not approved. The system may be about to say no.

**The 7 LEIE matches (data match, not verified against primary records):**
Gerald Abraham (FL, 1128a4, 2022), Enrique Rodriguez (FL, 1128b4, 2020), Clara Salazar-Vust (FL, 1128a3, 2022), Tommy Louisville (FL, 1128b4 2020 and 1128a1 2024), Michael Scheer (FL, 1128b4, 2022), Daniel Hauser (FL, 1128a4, 2013, NPI deactivated 2018), Gary Kushner (NC, 1128a1, 2013, NPI deactivated 2015).
Two LEIE addresses are Coleman FL and Butner NC. Both towns hold federal prison complexes. Not verified that these are prison addresses.

**Traps**
- A pending "physician" can be an organization NPI (90), with typos vs NPPES (e.g. SHLRUFF vs SHULRUFF).
- 361 NPIs are not in NPPES at all. The NPPES load tops out at 2026-06-05, so these may be newer.
- NPPES deactivation reason is blank for every row.

**Next join:** LEIE (done). A CMS revocation list would test the re-applying explanation. The warehouse doesn't have one.
