## 1. REFERENCE__CALENDAR: skip, with two traps

Ripple's own date table. Built by dbt: `library-onboarding/ripple_dbt/models/marts/reference/reference__calendar.sql`.
One row per day, 1700-01-01 to 2125-12-31.

**Checked: is it a clean calendar?**
- 155,593 rows. 155,593 distinct days. That's the exact day count for the range. No gaps, no doubles.
- Zero mismatches against Snowflake's own date functions on:
  day of week, ISO week, ISO year, weekend flag, month end, week start.
- Election cycle (the even year) and the election-year flag: zero mismatches.
- Congress number from 1935-01-03 on: zero mismatches.
- Fiscal year under the Oct 1 rule: zero mismatches. Modern dates are fine.

**⚠ Trap 1: Congress number is one too high on 4,320 days.**
- Before the 20th Amendment, a new Congress started March 4, not January 3.
- The model uses Jan 3 all the way back to 1791.
- So Jan 3 to Mar 3 of every odd year, 1791-1933, gets the next Congress's number.
  That's 72 odd years × 60 days = 4,320. The test counted exactly 4,320.
- Example: 1933-02-01 says **73**. That day was in the **72nd** Congress (Mar 1931 to Mar 1933).
- The model's own comment says the 1st Congress met March 4. The code doesn't carry that rule forward.

**⚠ Trap 2: fiscal year is wrong before October 1976.**
- The model applies "FY starts Oct 1" to every date back to 1700.
- The Oct 1 start only began with FY1977. From 1843 to 1976, the federal year started **July 1**.
- So July-September of 1843-1976 carries a fiscal year one too low. 134 years × 92 days = **12,328 days**.
  (The test ran from July 1842 and counted 12,420. 1842 still used the calendar year, so its 92 days are an edge case.)
- July-September 1976 was the "transition quarter". It belonged to no fiscal year. The table calls it FY1976 Q4.
- Before 1843 the fiscal year was the calendar year. The table labels Oct-Dec as the next FY. That's logic, not tested.
- Pre-1789 rows carry election cycles (1776 is "an election year"). Meaningless, harmless.

**Who's hit today:** nobody yet. No dbt model uses the calendar. Only the time-standard test does.
**Who'd get hit:** anything historic joined on Congress number (old votes, old bills) or on fiscal year (old federal budget series).
**Fix is two CASE lines.** Not done. Read-only pass.

## 2. REFERENCE__FED_ITIS_TAXON_AUTHORS_LKP: probed

Who named each species, as ITIS writes it: "Saini, 2024".

**Checked: is it a clean lookup? Yes.**
- 214,445 rows. 214,445 distinct author IDs. One load run, 2026-08-08. No blank names.
- By kingdom: animals 152,676, plants 45,920, bacteria 10,299, the rest under 2,600 each.
- 213,553 distinct author strings. The same string can sit under two kingdoms.
  113 extra rows repeat a string inside the same kingdom under a different ID. Minor.
- 3,615 author IDs (1.7%) aren't used by any name in the catalog.
  Every author ID the catalog uses is in this table.

**⚠ Trap: the glance's "top authors" were noise.**
- The glance used APPROX_TOP_K. It returned five author strings, each at 1,577 rows.
- Real maximum: **5 rows** for any author string ("Ehrenberg").
- Five identical counts means the column is nearly unique and the sketch gave up.
  Any glance "top" list with five equal counts should be ignored.

**Checked: time. Did the catalog stop growing?**
- ITIS ships one release a month. Almost every month's rows fall on a single day.
- Test: new names per year, January-July only. Names from `TAXONOMIC_UNITS.INITIAL_TIME_STAMP`. New author strings from this table.

```
Jan-Jul   new names   median month   new author strings
2019         6,579*        1,637          1,113
2020         4,192           522          1,339
2021        11,808           733          3,260
2022        13,196         2,691          3,711
2023        10,886         1,606          2,451
2024        15,186*        2,754          2,861
2025        22,551         2,595          3,139
2026         6,016           949            896
* 2019: Feb-Jun releases only. 2024: releases in Feb, Apr, Jun, Jul only.
```

- 2026 is down 73% from 2025. New author strings hit their lowest Jan-Jul in the window.
- **Dull explanation: bulk loads.** One month can carry 7,700 names (Jan 2025).
  Test: drop each year's two biggest months.
  2026 leaves 3,115 over 5 months. 2021 leaves 3,244. 2020 leaves 2,209. Same range.
- The feed didn't stop: a release landed every month, Jan-Jul 2026.
- Side note: no new names or authors are dated October 2025. That was the federal shutdown month.
  But 2024 also skipped January, March and May. A gap alone proves nothing.

**Hit would have meant:** the federal species catalog stalled in 2026.
**Miss means:** a slow year inside a lumpy series. That's what this is.
**Watch:** re-run after the next dump. Two more slow quarters would make it a break.

## 3. REFERENCE__FED_ITIS_JURISDICTION: probed

Where each species lives (7 places) and whether it's native or introduced there.

**Checked: is it a clean lookup? Yes.**
- 161,922 rows. One per species per place. 117,932 species IDs. One load run.
- Every species ID lands in the main names table.

**⚠ Traps**
- **14,931 rows (9.2%) sit on names ITIS no longer accepts** (old synonyms).
  Filter `NAME_USAGE in ('valid','accepted')` before counting species per place.
- "Caribbean territories" (lowercase) is its own category. 1 row. Uppercase before grouping.
- **Coverage is lopsided.** Mexico lists 2,325 plant species and 17,371 animals.
  Central Pacific Territories lists 92 plants. This isn't a full flora for those places.
  "Not listed in Mexico" means nothing.
- UPDATE_DATE is the last edit, not when the row was made. Its spikes (10,580 rows in 2020) are bulk edits.

**Checked: peer comparison. Share of accepted species marked Introduced**

```
place                        all      plants    animals
Hawaii                      28.5%     41.1%      19.7%
Caribbean Territories       18.0%     23.8%       6.4%
Central Pacific Terr.       10.6%      2.2%*     11.9%
Canada                       7.5%     16.5%       3.3%
Continental US               6.7%     15.9%       2.6%
Alaska                       5.9%     10.1%       1.4%
Mexico                       0.8%      1.4%*      0.8%
* thin plant coverage, see traps
```

- Hawaii: 2,259 of 7,926 accepted species are introduced. Plants: 1,345 of 3,275.
- **Dull explanation:** Hawaii is the textbook invaded island chain. Everyone knows it. Not a story.
**Use:** a native/introduced flag you can attach by species ID. It's clean enough for that.

## Traps for the ledger
1. CALENDAR: Congress number +1 on Jan 3 to Mar 3 of odd years 1791-1933 (4,320 days).
2. CALENDAR: fiscal year uses the Oct 1 rule before FY1977. July-Sept 1843-1976 is one year low (12,328 days). The 1976 transition quarter is labeled FY1976 Q4.
3. Glance: APPROX_TOP_K on a nearly-unique column returns five equal, fake counts (1,577 here; the real max is 5).
4. ITIS JURISDICTION: 9.2% of rows are on unaccepted names. Mexico and Central Pacific have almost no plants listed.
5. ITIS OTHER_SOURCES: "None" as text in SOURCE_COMMENT (35). 26 SOURCE_TYPE spellings.
6. ITIS VERN_REF_LINKS: 85 orphan links to deleted common names. 19 rows dated 1900-01-01.

parked: the calendar fix is two CASE lines in `reference__calendar.sql`. That's a rebuild of a REFERENCE table, so it's gated.
