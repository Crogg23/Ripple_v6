## The lead: the 5th Circuit became the federal government's route to the Supreme Court

**The claim, in one line:** starting in the 2021 term, the U.S. government kept losing at the 5th Circuit and taking those losses to the Supreme Court. The other 11 circuits, taken together, stayed flat.

### Federal government as the petitioner, argued cases [q13]

Federal petitioner = SCDB petitioner code 1 (U.S. attorney general), 27 (United States) or 300-499 (federal departments and agencies).

| Source circuit | Terms 2015-2020 | Terms 2021-2024 |
|---|---|---|
| **5th** | **1 of 26** (4%) | **16 of 38** (42%) |
| 9th (the usual #1) | 12 of 57 (21%) | 9 of 43 (21%) |
| All other 11 circuits together | 28 of 246 (11.4%) | 19 of 166 (11.4%) |

- The 5th supplied **16 of the 35** federal-petitioner cases from the 12 regional circuits in 2021-2024 (46%). In 2015-2020: 1 of 29.
- The government won **11 of those 16** (petitioner won).
- A 17th is CFPB v. Community Financial Services (2023). SCDB codes that petitioner 12, outside the federal code range. Counting it: 12 of 17 won.
- **Not 3-4 units carrying it:** 16 cases, 11 different agencies and officials (FDA ×3, the attorney general ×2, United States ×3, EPA, FCC, NRC, SEC, HHS, Interior, the Surgeon General, the President). Texas is the respondent in 4.

### Time: the 5th's share of the Court's argued docket [q10]

| Term | 5th Circuit cases | All argued cases | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | 3 | 62 | 4.8% |
| 2017 | 3 | 64 | 4.7% |
| 2018 | 4 | 68 | 5.9% |
| 2021 | 10 | 62 | 16.1% |
| 2023 | 9 | 59 | 15.3% |
| **2024** | **12** | **60** | **20.0%** |

- **20.0% in the 2024 term is the 5th's highest share in all 79 terms since 1946.** The old high was 15.7% (1959). Median term: 7.7%.
- In 2024 the 5th sent more argued cases than any other circuit, for the first time since 1990. The 9th led in 2019-2023.
- Peer check: the 9th hit 34% in 2011. A circuit holding a fifth of the docket isn't new. **The 5th doing it is.**

### What a hit means / what a miss means
- **Hit:** a story about the 5th Circuit as the chokepoint for federal policy. Checked counts, a 79-term time series, a peer group and a denominator.
- **Miss:** if SCDB's petitioner codes were wrong, the fed split would fall apart. The case names back the codes (15 distinct federal names, 16 cases; U.S. v. Texas appears twice, 2021 and 2022).
- **Caveat a hostile editor will raise:** the 5th-vs-SCOTUS fight got heavy coverage in 2023-2025. The new pieces here are the 1-of-26 → 16-of-38 federal-petitioner jump against a flat 11% everywhere else, and the per-appeal denominator.

## 1. POLITICS__XC_JCS_MEDIANS: **live (via the join)**, plus traps

**What it is:** 102 rows, one per year 1924-2024. It holds the president's score, the House, Senate and Court medians, and a median and spread for each of 13 appeals circuits. Every number is stored as text, with 'NA' for missing.

**Trap 1: the 5th Circuit median never moves.** It's **0.414 in all 17 years, 2008-2024**, through six Trump appointees (Willett 0.631, Ho 0.631, Oldham 0.6305, Duncan 0.508, Engelhardt 0.508, Wilson 0.414).
- Why: 5 of the 81 listed 5th Circuit judges share that one score (Elrod, Haynes, Owen, Prado, Wilson). The median lands on a plateau and stays.
- So the column can't show the shift the join found. Use it for placement, not for change.

**Trap 2: circuit medians are often just a president's score.** 133 of 1,079 circuit-year cells equal a president's exact score (3 decimals).
- Federal Circuit: **43 of 43 years.** DC Circuit: 53 of 88. These two circuits have no home-state senators, so every judge gets the president's number.

**Trap 3: medians of even-sized benches flip on one seat.** The 2nd Circuit reads 0.403 in 2020 and -0.284 in 2021. The 3rd reads -0.206 in 2023 and 0.068 in 2024.

**Other gaps:**
- 1924 appears twice (president scores 0.396 and 0.445). Joining on YEAR doubles 1924.
- President score blank 1929-1932: four rows, the Hoover years.
- The Court median stops at OT2022. The 2024 row reads 'NA', but SCDB has votes through OT2024.
- 11th Circuit starts 1981, Federal Circuit 1982. Those are the years they were created, not gaps.

## 3. POLITICS__XC_JCS_COA: **probed** (the score is borrowed)

**What it is:** 705 rows, one per judge per circuit, 693 distinct names.
- 12 names appear twice. All 12 are judges moved when the old 5th Circuit split in 1981; the 11th Circuit started that year. Same score on both rows.
- 2 rows hold 'NA' for the score: Hutcheson and Sibley, 5th Circuit. The typed mart `JUDGE_IDEOLOGY_COA` drops them (703 rows); otherwise identical.

**The trap: an appeals judge's JCS score isn't measured from their rulings.** It's copied from the appointing president, or from home-state senators of the president's party.
- **227 of 703 judges (32%)** carry a president's exact score. -0.358 (Obama) on 28 judges. 0.693 (G.W. Bush) on 28. 0.692 (Reagan) on 26. 0.403 (Trump) on 22.
- Only 281 distinct scores across 703 judges.
- Matched to FJC appointments by last name + first name + circuit (658 of 703 matched): **319 of 321** Democratic appointees score below zero, and **313 of 323** Republican appointees score above zero.
- **So "rank the most conservative appeals judges" ranks their appointers.** It can't carry a claim about how a judge rules.

**Coverage stops early 2024.** The newest judge listed is Kolar (commissioned 2024-01-31). Of 18 appeals judges commissioned since June 2023, 6 are in the list. Missing: 5 Biden appointees (Aframe, Berner, Maldonado, Ritz, Kidd) and all 7 second-term Trump appointees (Bove, Tung and others). A miss on a newer judge means nothing.

**Name-join traps:** Priscilla Owen is "Richman, Priscilla" in FJC (name change). Andy Oldham is "Andrew" there. "Coney Barrett, Amy" doesn't match "Barrett". Last name plus first name misses about 6%.

## Traps for the ledger

1. **Appeals-judge JCS is the appointer's score, not the judge's.** 227 of 703 judges carry a president's exact number. Party of the appointer predicts the sign for 632 of 644 matched judges.
2. **XC_JCS_MEDIANS 5th Circuit median = 0.414 every year 2008-2024.** Plateau from 5 judges sharing one score. It can't show turnover.
3. **Federal and DC Circuit medians are presidents' scores**: 43 of 43 and 53 of 88 years.
4. **1924 appears twice** in both medians tables. Hoover's score is blank (1929-1932).
5. **JCS stops at OT2022; SCDB runs to OT2024.** Court median for 2024 is 'NA'.
6. **The JCS appeals list stops at Jan 2024.** 12 of 18 judges commissioned since mid-2023 are missing.
7. **JCS_MEDIANS duplicates XC_JCS_MEDIANS** (4 score columns, 0 differences). **JUDGE_IDEOLOGY_SCOTUS duplicates XC_JCS_SCOTUS** (0 differences) with IDs added.
8. **Judge name joins miss name changes** (Owen → Richman) and nicknames (Andy/Andrew).
