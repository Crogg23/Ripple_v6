## 1. OPINION_CLUSTERS: live

Each row is one court decision. There are 10.1M rows. The court comes from a join to DOCKETS on DOCKET_ID.

**The lead: the 4th Circuit is publishing more while every other circuit publishes less**

Published opinions per year. Deduped on case name + filing date, so consolidated cases posted twice count once.

```
           2018  2019  2020  2021  2022  2023  2024  2025   2020->2025
4th Cir     219   251   209   249   251   276   332   364     +74%
peers (10 circuits, median change)                            -13%
  range: 1st -4, 2nd -9, 3rd -20, 5th -23, 6th -8, 8th -15, 9th -12,
         10th +5, 11th -18, Fed -19
```

- Other window: the 2018-19 average vs the 2024-25 average. **4th Circuit 235 to 348 (+48%).** Peer median **-22%**. The only other riser is the 6th Circuit, +5%.
- The 4th's unpublished count held flat at 2,086 (2020) and 2,092 (2025). Its published share went from **9.1% to 15.5%**.
- It climbed nearly every year, not one spike: 209, 249, 251, 276, 332, 364.
- Left out of the peers: the 7th Circuit and DC Circuit, whose feeds broke (see traps).

**Checked**
- Peer and time: 13 circuits, 2010-2025 (q16), deduped 2018-2025 (q22).
- The dull explanation is a scraper change. Tested: every 4th Circuit published row from 2020 to 2025 comes from the court's own website (source C, 388 of 388 in 2025). Duplicates are removed. Total opinions are steady (2,296 in 2020, 2,507 in 2025).
- Why it matters: in this data the 4th Circuit had the highest unpublished share of any circuit in 2018-2021 (90-92%). Unpublished opinions set no precedent.

**What a hit means:** the court changed how many of its decisions become binding precedent. The obvious question is whether new judges did it. Six years of steady climb is not noise.
**What a miss would mean:** if the official US Courts count (Judicial Business Table B-12) doesn't show the rise, this is a CourtListener labelling change and it dies.
**Not done:** the B-12 check (not in the warehouse). A per-judge split (the table has no author column; JUDGES is the panel as free text).

**Traps found (for the ledger)**
- **Source G floods the counts from 2019.** Status "Unknown", source code G, almost all from federal district courts (S.D.N.Y. 58,752; E.D. Cal. 52,862; N.D. Cal. 23,863 in 2019-26). Before this, 2018 had 7,363 such rows. From 2020 there are 107-114K a year. All but a handful have CITATION_COUNT 0. Yearly opinion totals before and after 2019 are not comparable.
- **Harvard imports are always "Published."** 4th Circuit, 2014: 1,054 source-U rows, all Published. The court's own feed (C/CU) marked 232 of 3,329 Published. Publication rates before about 2018 are inflated.
- **Feeds that broke:**
  - 7th Circuit: 2,008 opinions in 2020, 551 in 2025. Zero unpublished in 2023-25.
  - Dallas (Texas 5th Court of Appeals): 25 opinions in 2025 against 1,726 new appeals.
  - Houston (Texas 14th Court of Appeals): 0 opinions in 2025 against 1,164.
  - Texas courts of appeals: status is always "Published."
  - DC Circuit: no "Unpublished" at all before 2020.
- **Blank case names from 2025.** 24,727 rows in 2025. In 2026 so far it's 29,936, which is 94% of that year's 31,717 "Unknown" rows. All are in the source-G "Unknown" group.
- DISPOSITION is filled on 0.4-3% of rows a year in 2016-2024. DATE_FILED has 731,924 rows before 1900 and 2 in the future.

## 2. INTL_EU_SANCTIONS: probed

42,347 rows covering 5,994 people and companies. **Each row is exactly one detail line** (a name, address, birth, ID or citizenship). 0 rows have two details and 0 have none. No cross-join blow-up.

**The join:** to `JUSTICE__INTL_OPENSANCTIONS` (71K rows), matched on exact name, alias or ID number.
- **5,994 of 5,994 land (100%).** 248 had more than one candidate; I kept the one with the most matching names.
- Its "is it on the US list" flag comes from OpenSanctions's merged record (DATASET includes "US OFAC Specially Designated Nationals").

**Russia programmes (UKR, RUS, RUSDA), by the year the EU first listed them**

```
EU cohort   listed   also US SDN   also UK   US: firms   US: people
2014-21       189      63%          94%        58%         65%
2022        1,279      65%          80%        73%         64%
2023          442      48%          36%        60%         40%
2024          447      38%          20%        65%         21%
2025          389      28.5%        22%        45%         12%
2026 (Apr)    131      22%          18%        29%         12%
```

- The newest-looking lead is "the US stopped matching the EU." It doesn't survive:
  - **The dull explanation is time.** A 2022 listing has had four years for others to follow; a 2025 one, under one. There is no US listing date here to fix that.
  - **The UK control:** the UK overlap falls faster than the US (80% to 22%). Whatever is happening isn't US-specific.
- People fall hardest (64% to 12%). The EU lists propagandists and officials that nobody else does.
- A looser match counts any OpenSanctions record with the US flag that shares a name or ID. It adds little: 124 instead of 111 for 2025. So 28.5% is close to a floor, not far off.
- **Eyeballed 12 firms from 2025-26** (q23):
  - The 6 marked US all carry a US programme code (US-RUSHAR) in OpenSanctions: Enutek Makina (Turkey), Samara Plant Kommunar, Luch Plant, Solikamsk Plant Ural, an automation research firm, Moran Security Group.
  - An exact-name check against the warehouse's own OFAC table finds only 3 of those 6. Raw name matching against OFAC misses half; use the OpenSanctions flag.
  - The 6 marked not-US include LUKOIL-Volgogradneftepererabotka. Outside knowledge, **not checked here**: the US designated Lukoil in Oct 2025. Whether this refinery was named is unknown. If it was, the merge missed it and the US share is a floor.

**Traps**
- **The regulation on the entity line is not the listing date.** For 2,066 of 5,994 entities (34%), an earlier regulation sits on one of their detail lines. Use the earliest date across all six LEBA date columns.
- ID type "taxid" appears only on 2025-26 listings (129 lines). Older Russian firms carry "regnumber" (579 entities) or nothing.
- ADDR_NUMBER is empty.
- DATE_FILE is "05/06/2026" on every row. The newest regulation in the file is 2026-04-23, so the data can't decide between 5 June and 6 May. The EU writes day first, so probably 5 June.
- **OpenSanctions naming:** the 1.28M merged table calls the column DATASETS; the 71K table calls it DATASET. Both hold list titles, not codes. Searching for `eu_fsf` returns 0 rows (q03).

## 3. ORIGINATING_COURT_INFO: probed

There are 973,419 rows. Each one hangs off a docket via DOCKETS.ORIGINATING_COURT_INFORMATION_ID.

| Appeals court (via dockets) | Rows | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Texas courts of appeals (1st-15th) | 421,008 | 43% |
| Texas Court of Criminal Appeals | 247,631 | 25% |
| US Supreme Court | 191,293 | 20% |
| Texas Supreme Court | 47,369 | 5% |
| 13 federal circuits | 57,071 | 6% |

**Checked: can it rank trial judges?**
- Texas courts of appeals: the trial judge's name is on 366,815 of 421,008 rows (87%). An opinion exists for 196,649 (47%). **A disposition exists for 2,929 (0.7%).** There's no outcome to rank on and no caseload to divide by, so "most-appealed judge" just measures court size.
- The top names are long-serving trial judges in the Beaumont (9th) appeals district: Larry Gist, 1,378 appeals over 1981-2019; Layne Walker, 1,341 over 2002-2014. Without a caseload that's not a finding.

**Checked: one join.** ASSIGNED_TO_ID to CourtListener judges.
- It's filled on only 32,857 rows (3.4%). All of them land.
- Last names agree on 32,842.
- ⚠ **Trap:** ID 16072 ("Edward Smith") sits on 367 rows whose text says "Honorable BEA ANN SMITH." A surname match hides the wrong person.

**Checked: time.** Texas opened a statewide 15th Court of Appeals in Sept 2024 for cases involving the state. That's background; in the data, its dockets start in 2024. I took Travis County civil appeals to be cause numbers D-1-GN or C-1-CV, from the county's numbering format, and the 15th Court's sample cause numbers fit.

```
year   to Austin 3rd Ct   to 15th Ct   15th from elsewhere   3rd Ct total
2019        263               -              -                   959
2023        202               -              -                   876
2024        250              64             68                   840
2025        268              78            154                 1,034
```

- The new court added volume. It did not shrink Austin's Travis docket. Nothing story-grade here.
- Coverage looks complete: 9.2K-10.8K Texas appeals a year, with a 2020-21 dip to 7.2K and 8.7K in 2022. Not checked against Texas court statistics.

**Traps**
- For Texas Supreme Court rows, the "trial judge" is often "Honorable Per Curiam" (2,936). The lower court there is a court of appeals, not a trial court.
- US Supreme Court rows (191K) have no judge at all. DATE_JUDGMENT is filled on 147,712.
- ORDERING_JUDGE_STR is filled only in the 1st and 4th Circuits (3,567 rows).

## 4. FINANCIAL_DISCLOSURES: probed

Each row should be one judge's report for one year.

**Checked: how much is real**

```
YEAR_COL      rows    numeric ID   real SHA1   judge id
2000-2022   31,905      ~all         ~all       90%
junk year    2,133        331          319        319
null        32,249        472            0          0     page-count median 2,866 = junk
```

- **52% of the table is spill** from broken rows. Filter to YEAR_COL 2003-2020 and a numeric ID.
- Reports double in 2011: about 1,100 a year before, about 2,300 after. Bankruptcy judges enter in 2011 (deep-8).
- **IS_AMENDED is only real for 2011-2018**: 271-625 't' a year, then 2 in 2019 and 1 in 2020. REPORT_TYPE "2" exists only in 2011-2018; "-1" elsewhere.

**Checked: judges admitting errors in their own addenda**
- "Inadvertent" appears in 31-116 reports a year. It drops after 2018.
- The drop tracks the amended flag disappearing, not more careful judges. In 2011-18, the admission wording shows up in **11.3% of amended reports vs 4.6% of originals**.
- Among 1,876 judges with 8+ report years, 637 have at least one such year. The median judge has none.
- Real examples:
  - Edward Shea: 10 of 12 years; "inadvertently omitted from the prior years report."
  - Deborah Chasanow: 8 of 18; "Inadvertently failed to report closing of account."
- The wording search also catches harmless lines like "omitted pursuant to section 102(e)(2)" and name-change notes. The top two judges by count are those. Not story-grade alone.
- It's usable as color for the judge-stock lead (F-001), and it's OCR text, so read the PDF before quoting.

## Traps for the ledger (one line each)
- CourtListener opinions: source G "Unknown" adds about 110K district-court rows a year from 2019. Yearly totals break there.
- CourtListener opinions: Harvard (source U) rows are always "Published." Publication rates before 2018 are inflated.
- CourtListener opinions: the 7th Circuit feed loses unpublished orders from 2023. Dallas and Houston appeals courts have almost no 2025 opinions.
- CourtListener originating court: ASSIGNED_TO_ID can name a different judge with the same surname (16072 on "Bea Ann Smith" rows).
- CourtListener disclosures: 52% of rows are spill. IS_AMENDED and REPORT_TYPE only mean something for 2011-2018.
- EU sanctions: LEBA_PUBLICATION_DATE on the entity line is often a later amendment, not the listing date.
- OpenSanctions: DATASET(S) holds list titles, not codes. The two tables spell the column differently.
