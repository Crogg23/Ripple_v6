- Only California and Nevada are lower. Both have mild winters.
- **Time (S07), by the year the generator started:**
  - ERCOT built 11,551 MW of gas from 2012 to 2024. None of it is flagged as able to switch to oil. That includes 3,164 MW built 2021-24, after Uri.
  - ⚠ That's no longer unusual. Nationwide, only 11.8% of the 2021-24 builds can switch. PJM's are 0.2%, MISO's 0.4%, SPP's 0%. Florida and the Carolinas are the exceptions.

**Hit means:** Texas's gas fleet has less than half the oil backup of SPP and MISO, the two neighbor grids Uri also hit. Half of it sits with one owner. A third is in steam units built 1958-69, the kind next in line to retire.

**Miss means:** Oil backup is one winter tool among several. If ERCOT covers winter with gas-supply contracts and weatherization instead, the low share is a choice, not a gap.

**Boring:**

- ERCOT runs a firm-fuel program that pays some plants to hold backup fuel. From memory, check it. It isn't in this data.
- **Not tested:** past years. This is 2024 only. EIA-860 from 2020 would show whether switch-capable megawatts in ERCOT went up after Uri or not.

## 3. Boiler pollution rules: ten coal plants, a fifth of the nation's power plant SO2. **live**

**Checked**
- Profile (S02): 4,428 boilers, 4,428 unique plant+boiler keys, 1,555 plants, one load.
  - 2,999 boilers are operating (6 of them typed lowercase `op`).
- **Fuel** isn't in this table. S03 got it through the boiler-to-generator link table and the generator table.
  - 462 operating coal boilers at 207 plants.
- **Join (S03, S04): EPA's eGRID 2022**, on EIA plant code. 3,915 of 4,428 boiler rows land.
  - 173 of the 207 coal plants have SO2 measured only by EPA smokestack monitors. I used only those 173. Plants with estimated numbers are out (see ADM trap).
  - US total, all eGRID plants: 1,003,476 tons of SO2 in 2022.
- **Concentration:**
  - Labadie (Union Electric, which does business as Ameren Missouri): 44,265 tons, 4.4% of the US power plant total, #1.
  - Top 10 of the 173: 213,652 tons, **21.3%**. Only 4 of the 10 have a retirement year filed.
  - Labadie, W A Parish, Gerald Gentleman, Belle River, Martin Lake, Miami Fort, Harrington, Harrison, Shawnee, White Bluff.
  - Two big ones are left out because their number isn't fully monitored: Gavin (OH, 20,422 tons, mixed source) and ADM Decatur (an estimate, see traps).
- **Peer, the 173 monitored coal plants:**

| Group | Plants | Median SO2 rate (lb per million Btu) | Median tons | SO2, share of US total | Coal power, share of the 173 | Retirement year filed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate 0.3 or more | 42 | 0.471 | 7,649 | 38.2% | 20.4% | 8 (19%) |

  - Not three plants carrying it: without the top 3, the other 39 of the 42 still hold 28.2% of the US total.
  - The 0.3 line is mine. It sits between the two clusters (medians 0.083 and 0.471). The top-10 number doesn't use it.
- **The test inside the group:** retirement. The dull story is "they're closing soon anyway." But the dirty-rate plants file a retirement year *less* often than the clean ones (19% vs 24%).
- **What the boilers tell EIA:** their SO2 limits sit above what they emit.
  - Across 146 monitored coal plants with a per-fuel limit (code DP), the median limit is 2.4x the plant's actual 2022 rate.
  - Associated Electric Coop's New Madrid (MO) reports a 10 lb limit and emits 0.39 (26x). Thomas Hill (MO): an 8 lb limit, 0.37 actual (22x).
  - Labadie's limit is 40,837 **pounds per hour** (code DH), not per unit of fuel. It averaged about 10,100 lb/hour over 2022.

**Hit means:** a short, nameable list of plants is a fifth of the country's power plant SO2. Six of the ten have no retirement date on file. That's a map-and-list story: who lives downwind of each.

**Miss means:** they're legal. Their limits are loose, and the cap on SO2 is a federal allowance market, not these limits. "Legal but lopsided" is still a story, but not a violations story.

**Boring:**
- Known territory. Labadie's rank has been reported before. The list, the retirement test and the limit gap are the new part.
- eGRID is 2022, and the boilers are 2024. Some plants may have cut output since. Belle River and Harrington are on their way to gas (Harrington's unit 1 already reads gas in 2024).
- **Not tested:** enforcement. EPA ECHO would need an FRS-to-EIA link, which I didn't build.
- Parked: Entergy's White Bluff and Independence show no retirement year in 2024. From memory, Entergy agreed in a 2018 settlement to stop burning coal there by 2028 and 2030. Check it. If that's right, the EIA filing is missing a known date.

## Traps (for the ledger, not stories)
- **ENVIROEQUIP `STANDARD_SULFUR_RATE` mixes units.** Pounds per million Btu (DP, median 0.59), pounds per hour (DH, median 12.1, up to 40,837), and more. The glance's "sum 853.4K, max 87.3K" means nothing. Group by `UNIT_SULFUR` first.
- **`STANDARD_SULFUR_PERCENT_SCRUBBED` is a rule, not a scrubber.** It's the removal share the limit demands. 909 of the 1,329 filled rows are 0.
- **`STEAM_PLANT_TYPE` is a 1-4 code**, not a quantity. The glance's "top 1% hold 41%" is nonsense.
- **Hawthorn (MO) reports a 150 lb/MMBtu SO2 limit** on both boilers. That's typo-grade. The next highest among the monitored coal plants is 10 (New Madrid).
- **The SO2 strategy codes don't reliably mark scrubbers.**
  - `IF` boilers have the lowest measured rates (median 0.096), so it probably means a scrubber. Check it against the EIA layout.
  - But Martin Lake and Oak Grove measure like scrubbed plants and are coded `OT`. Miami Fort is coded `IF` and emits 0.53.
  - Classify by measured rate, not by code.
- **eGRID SO2 marked source `EIA` is an estimate, not a monitor.** ADM Decatur shows 17,902 tons (100,868 before the heat-and-power split) at 6.22 lb/MMBtu. That would make a corn-processing plant's boilers the biggest SO2 source in the country, by far. Rank only on `EPA/CAMD`-sourced rows.
- **Boiler-to-generator gaps:** 64 operating boilers link only to generators that aren't in the 2024 generator table.
- **MULTIFUEL `MULTIPLE_FUELS` is a constant:** 'Y' on 2,892 of 2,893 rows.
- **MULTIFUEL `STORAGE_LIMITS` and `AIR_PERMIT_LIMITS` are Y-or-blank.** Blank isn't "no."
- **The generator table's `SWITCH_BETWEEN_OIL_AND_NATURAL_GAS` is blank** on 1,715 of 6,339 operating gas generators. That includes 386 of 606 built 2021-24. Blank isn't "no." For new units it probably means "single-fuel, question skipped."
- **ADVANCED_METERS filler rows:**
  - 47 "Adjustment 2024" rows (utility 99999) carry 864,674 meters.
  - 128 "Behind the Meter" rows (Tesla, Sunrun, SunPower, repeated per state) carry 1,076,297 meters. They cause most of the 62 duplicate utility+state keys.
  - Drop both before any share.
- **RELIABILITY:** some big utilities (Con Ed, Duquesne, NYSEG) report under the "other" standard. Their IEEE columns are blank.
