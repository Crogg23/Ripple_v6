## 1. GHGRP: landfills that went quiet on EPA's climate registry. **live**

**What the table is**
- 136,005 rows, one per facility per year, 2010-2023.
- ⚠ 21,187 rows (15.6%) are placeholders. After a facility stops, it gets a row every later year with a `STOPPED_REPORTING_*` status, a blank type and no emissions.
- In 2023: 8,107 facilities reported. 3,026 were flagged "stopped, valid reason". 148 were flagged "stopped, unknown reason".
- Emissions come from `GHGRP_EMISSION`, joined on FACILITY_ID + year (S12). Every emission row lands on a facility row.
- "Direct" below leaves out the supplier sectors (9-13, 16, 17) and gas 8.
  - Gas 8 looks like biogenic CO2: pulp and paper holds most of it. That's inferred, not confirmed.
  - 2023 check: direct 2.58 billion t, supplier rows 4.67 billion t. Add them together and you triple the number.

**The whole "unknown reason" list first**

| Status in 2023 | Facilities | Last report ≥25,000 t | Median last report |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stopped, unknown reason | 148 | 95 (64%) | 40,917 t |
| Stopped, valid reason | 3,026 | 1,005 (33%) | 13,666 t |

- Of the 148: 61 are oil and gas basin reporters, 17 landfills, 6 underground coal mines, and the rest other industry.
- Oil and gas is muddy. When a company sells a basin, the buyer reports under its own ID (example: Earthstone Energy, last report 2022, 530,273 t). Not tested one by one.
- **Landfills are the clean cut.** The rule gives them no shutdown exit, and a closed landfill keeps making methane.

**The rule (40 CFR 98.2(i), read on law.cornell.edu today)**
- There are two ways out by the numbers: under 25,000 t for five years running, or under 15,000 t for three years running. Either way the operator has to notify EPA.
- The third way out is shutting down every process. It "does not apply to" municipal solid waste landfills or industrial waste landfills.
- **None of the 17 meets either number test.** All 17 were at 15,000 t or more in at least one of their last 3 years, and at 25,000 t or more in at least one of their last 5.

**Peer comparison: landfills only (1,523 ever in the registry)**

| Landfill status in 2023 | Landfills | Last report ≥25,000 t | Median last report |
|---|---|---|---|
| Still reporting | 1,286 | 1,020 | 56,066 t |
| Stopped, valid reason | 219 | **9** | 9,497 t |
| Stopped, unknown reason | 17 | **16** | **85,741 t** |

- Take the 1,045 landfills whose final report was at or over 25,000 t:
  - 1,020 still report.
  - 9 left with a valid reason.
  - 16 left with nothing on file.
- The 17 together reported 1,747,983 t CO2e in their last year.
- One unit is heavy. Black Warrior (Alabama) is 452,070 t of that, 26%. The median landfill is 85,741 t.
- **Time:** the flag sits for years. 15 of the 17 have been flagged 2+ years, and 11 for 5+ years. Nabors Sanitation Landfill (Arkansas) has been on the list every year from 2013 to 2023.
- **Place:** 4 of Puerto Rico's 13 registry landfills are on the list (31%). Nationwide it's 17 of 1,523 (1.1%). No state has more than 2.

**Join: through FRS to the air-permit file (ICIS-Air, S21).** Five of the 17 hold an air permit marked "Operating, major emissions":

| Landfill (data match) | Last climate report | t CO2e in that report | Latest air inspection or review | Air enforcement on file |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Black Warrior Solid Waste Disposal Authority, Coker AL | 2021 | 452,070 | 2026-04-22 | 2 formal actions, the last in 2010 |
| Foothill Sanitary Landfill, Linden CA (San Joaquin County) | 2020 | 126,736 | 2026-06-30 | 9 violations 2022-23. 15 formal actions, last 2024-05-23, $23,000 |
| North County Sanitary Landfill, Lodi CA (San Joaquin County) | 2020 | 93,473 | 2026-04-09 | 9 violations 2021-24. 19 formal actions, last 2025-02-14, $105,500 |
| Richfield Landfill, Davison MI | 2011 | 90,975 | 2025-07-31 | 39 violations 2013-24 |
| Toa Alta Landfill, PR | 2013 | 88,192 | 2025-09-04 | 6 violations in 2019 |

- Together, those five reported 851,446 t in their last year.
- Both San Joaquin County landfills stopped after the same year (2020). Both are flagged for 2021, 2022 and 2023.
- ECHO (S14) says the same thing. Foothill is "Significant Violation", noncompliant 12 of 12 quarters. North County is noncompliant 11 of 12.
- For comparison, 132 of the 1,129 still-reporting landfills found in ECHO (12%) have 8+ noncompliant quarters.

**Could they be reporting under a different ID? Tested five ways, no hit:**
- Same FRS ID, another registry facility, after the exit year: 0 of 17.
- Same ZIP, any facility after exit (checked for the 5 operating ones): 0.
- Name or city search across all registry names after exit: only unrelated hits (a Colorado "Foothills Landfill", a gas gathering system named for the Black Warrior Basin, filed from Texas).
- Same parent (County of San Joaquin, City of Jacksonville, Ozark Mountain district): no successor landfill.
- EPA's second copy of the registry numbers sits inside the combined emissions table (program `E-GGRT`, 2015 on). It never shows any of the 17 after its exit year (S15). It is patchy, though: Toa Baja shows only 2015-16 there, and Beulah is missing.

**Hit means:** landfills that must keep filing climate reports, and in five cases are still running under a major air permit, have filed nothing for 1 to 12 years (15 of them for 2+). EPA knows, because it's EPA's flag, and the flag just sits there. San Joaquin County and Puerto Rico are the local angles.

**Miss means:** they filed a notice EPA hasn't processed, or EPA's newer release (reporting year 2024, not in the warehouse) shows them back or cleared. Then it's a paperwork lag, not a gap.

**Boring explanations:**
- **Late filers.** Toa Baja (PR) and Simco Road (ID) were first flagged in 2023, the last year loaded. They may just be late. The other 15 have been flagged 2+ years.
- **Emissions really did drop** (gas collection, capping) but no notice was filed. That's still missing paperwork, and the data show no year under the line. Not testable further here.
- **Foothill's air ID is named "Ameresco San Joaquin"** in FRS. That's the gas-to-energy operator at the landfill, so the air enforcement may be against Ameresco's engines, not the county. Check before writing.
- **Closed ≠ quiet.** Jacksonville North and East Duval (City of Jacksonville) have "Permanently Closed" air permits. The rule still has them reporting, but that's a weaker story than an operating site.
- **Not tested:** whether EPA has open enforcement under the reporting rule itself. ECHO counts don't name the program. The ICIS-Air counts are air-permit actions.

**Side lines (not story-grade alone)**
- 6 underground coal mines are on the unknown list, and 5 were over 25,000 t. All six belong to Blackjewel (3 sites), Cambrian Coal, Alpha Natural Resources and Seneca. Pinnacle Mine (WV) last reported 692,519 t in 2018. From memory, check: all four operators went through bankruptcy around then.
- New unknown-reason exits per year: 20 (2019), 12, 15, 23, 45 (2023). The 2023 jump is partly late filers.

## 2. FRS facilities: the hub works, two flags don't. **probed**

**Checked**
- 5,300,149 rows, one registry ID each (S07).
- ⚠ **`IS_FEDERAL_FACILITY` true on 0 rows. `IS_ON_TRIBAL_LAND` true on 0 rows.**
  - The text columns disagree. `FEDERAL_FACILITY_CODE` is filled on 27,206 rows and `FEDERAL_AGENCY` on 10,273. `TRIBAL_LAND_CODE = 'Yes'` on 13,197.
  - The booleans are broken. Use the text columns.
- ⚠ No latitude on 1,114,455 rows (21%).
  - The 2012 batch is the worst: 501,906 rows created, 334,284 (67%) with no point.
- CREATE_DATE:
  - 789,415 rows dated 2000, the first bulk load.
  - Not one row dated 2001.
  - 2025 brought in 24,500 of the 40,559 brownfields sites.
- ⚠ **Duplicate IDs (S18).** 61,182 groups share the exact same cleaned name + address + ZIP. They hold 125,143 registry IDs, 2.7% of the 4,570,255 rows with a usable address. One name has 80 IDs at one Huntington Beach address. That's the "second registry ID" trap, at scale.
- **Joins:**
  - Superfund EPA_ID → FRS SEMS program IDs: 1,903 of 1,908 sites land.
  - 11,123 FRS rows carry a greenhouse-registry ID.
  - Active ECHO facilities: 1,637,810. Of those, 31,918 have no FRS row.
- **Peer comparison (S19): federal vs non-federal active ECHO facilities.** Matched on major flag and air/water/hazardous-waste program mix:
  - 5,269 federal facilities.
  - Chronic noncompliance (8+ of 12 quarters): 294 vs 270 expected (1.09x).
  - Any noncompliance: 1,019 vs 804 (1.27x).
  - Facilities with a formal action: 210 vs 265 expected (0.79x).
  - Interior is highest among the big departments: 37 chronic vs 22.5 expected (1.65x), but that's a small count.

**Hit means:** federal sites break the rules a bit more often and get formal action less often.
**Miss means:** at 1.09x it's noise, and "EPA can't easily penalize other agencies" is old news.
**Boring:** known pattern. Federal-on-federal enforcement runs through other channels, from memory, check. Not a lead.

## 3. Superfund site boundaries. **probed**

**Checked**
- 2,114 boundary features at 1,908 sites (EPA_ID).
  - 1,881 sites have one feature.
  - Portland Harbor has 100 features. Lower Duwamish Waterway has 44.
- NPL status: 1,328 final, 459 deleted, 36 proposed, 70 not on the NPL, 10 removed. That's the whole list, so a miss would mean something.
- ⚠ **Units trap (S05, S16).** `GIS_AREA_UNITS` goes by EPA region:
  - Region 4 (281 sites) and Region 7 (101) use square miles.
  - Region 9 (138 sites) says "Miles", which is probably square miles, not confirmed. 5 of those have no area at all.
  - Everyone else uses acres, spelled 3 ways (Acres, acres, ACRES).
  - 16 sites have no unit.
  - The glance's "sum 7.0M, median 26" adds all of these together.
  - Converted, the total is 10.2M acres, without Region 9.

- Time: 1,639 of 1,908 sites had their boundary last changed in 2020-2024. Nothing breaks.

**Hit means / miss means:** nothing story-grade in the table itself.
**Why keep it:** its EPA_ID is a clean key into FRS, and through FRS into ECHO and TRI.

## 4. USGS mineral sites. **probed**

**Checked (S09, S20)**
- 304,632 rows, one DEP_ID each. 266.6K are in the US.
- ⚠ **Empty on every row:** `DATA_SOURCE_NOTES` (the one column the menu suggested), `COMMODITY`, `PRODUCTION_QUANTITY`, `FIPS`, `YEAR`. Use `COMMOD1-3` and `DEV_STAT`.
- ⚠ **The database is frozen:**
  - Latest last-production year on any row: 2013. On "Producer" rows: 2009.
  - Latest discovery year: 2002.
  - 42,879 rows say "Producer". That was the status at the last edit, not today.
- ⚠ **Doubles:** 14,163 groups share a name and a point (rounded to 3 decimals, about 100 m). They hold 28,749 records (9.4%). In 7,445 of those groups the records disagree on development status. The same site is a "Past Producer" in one record and an "Occurrence" in another.
- No key to MSHA. `MAS_ID` is the old Bureau of Mines ID, not the MSHA mine ID.

**Hit means / miss means:** no checkable number here. Mines near water systems would need a spatial join. A mine point near a water utility's office address proves nothing.
**Boring:** it's a historic reference layer. Usable as a map with the caveats above.

## Traps for the ledger (columns and loads that look real but aren't)

- **GHGRP facility:** 21,187 of 136,005 rows are "stopped" placeholders with no emissions. Count facilities per year only on rows where `REPORTING_STATUS` is blank.
- **GHGRP facility:** `FRS_ID` is blank for 1,003 of the 8,107 facilities reporting in 2023. By type: 381 oil and gas production, 303 direct emitters, 176 suppliers, 89 gathering and boosting.
- **GHGRP emission:** supplier sectors 9-13, 16 and 17 are fuel *supplied*, not emitted: 4.67B t in 2023 vs 2.58B direct. Gas 8 looks like biogenic CO2 (inferred). Summing every row triples the total.
- **Combined emissions:** CO2 is in pounds for CAMD and EIS, but in metric tons CO2e for E-GGRT. Same column, different units by program.
- **FRS:** `IS_FEDERAL_FACILITY` and `IS_ON_TRIBAL_LAND` are constant false. Use `FEDERAL_FACILITY_CODE`, `FEDERAL_AGENCY` and `TRIBAL_LAND_CODE`.
- **FRS:** 21% of rows have no map point, including 67% of the 2012 batch. No CREATE_DATE falls in 2001.
- **FRS:** 125,143 registry IDs share an exact name + address + ZIP with another ID.
- **Superfund:** area units change by EPA region (acres, square miles, "Miles"). Never sum `GIS_AREA` raw.
- **USGS minerals:** `DATA_SOURCE_NOTES`, `COMMODITY`, `PRODUCTION_QUANTITY`, `FIPS` and `YEAR` are empty. "Producer" is frozen around 2009. 9.4% name-and-point doubles, and half of those disagree on status.
- **GEM:** the shaking column is empty. `VS30` and `MODEL_VERSION` are constants.

parked: the GHGRP "unknown reason" flag also works as a join target. Take any FRS ID flagged "unknown" and still showing an "Operating" air permit, and you get a list that updates itself. The 5 landfills are the first pull.
