## 1. POLITICS__VOTEVIEW_VOTES: **live**

**What it is.** One row per member per roll call, 118th and 119th Congresses (945,523 rows).
It has no party and no date. Party comes from `FED_VOTEVIEW_MEMBERS` (ICPSR is text there and a number here). Dates come from `VOTEVIEW_ROLLCALLS`.
Senate coverage: 691 roll calls (118th), then 851 through 2026-06-24.

**How a "break" is counted.**
- Party vote: most Democrats vote one way and most Republicans vote the other.
- Break: a member votes against their own party's majority on a party vote.
- Independents who caucus with Democrats (party code 328) count as Democrats.

**The finding: the Iran war-powers votes.**
- The 119th Senate held 17 roll calls on pulling US forces out of hostilities. 12 were about Iran. 3 were about Venezuela, 1 about Cuba, and 1 had no country named.
- On all 12 Iran roll calls, **Fetterman (D-PA) was the only Democrat voting with the Republican majority.** The Democrats split 46-1, 45-1 or 43-1.
- 10 of the 12 failed. **Two failed by one vote:**
  - 2026-05-13, S.J.Res.163 discharge: **49-50.** One Republican (Ricketts) didn't vote. A Fetterman yes makes it 50-49 and it passes.
  - 2026-06-16, S.J.Res.172 discharge: **47-48.** Also not voting: McConnell (R), Hawley (R), **and three Democrats: Sanders, Bennet, Booker.** Any two of those three voting yes would have passed it too.
- He broke with his party on 14 of the 17 war-powers roll calls. On the Venezuela ones he voted with Democrats.

**Peer check: who else decided close votes by crossing?**
- I took every simple-majority party vote in the 119th Senate. A crossing counts as "decisive" if flipping that one senator back to their party flips the result. On a tie, the VP breaks it for the Republicans. Cloture on bills (needs 60) is left out.
- Murkowski 4, Collins 4, Tillis 3, McConnell 2, **Fetterman 2**, Cassidy 1, Paul 1.
- Fetterman is the only Democrat on that list, and both of his are the Iran votes.

**Time check: his whole record.**
- Break rate on party votes: **5 of 415 (1.2%) in the 118th**, when the median Democrat was 0.56%. **151 of 742 (20.4%) in the 119th**, when the median Democrat was 3.9%. Next highest is Shaheen at 14.8%.
- Lone-Democrat crossings: the 119th Senate had 69 party votes with exactly one Democrat crossing, and **42 of them were Fetterman.** In the 118th he was 0 of 102.
- It holds for every kind of vote in the 119th: nominations 22.4% (all Democrats 6.3%), motions 27.1% (3.4%), bill passage 20.4% (4.8%).
- It's steady within the 119th: 21.1% in Jan-Jun 2025, 18.0% in Jul-Dec, 22.8% in Jan-Jun 2026.

**The dull explanation.** Fetterman is openly hawkish on Iran and pro-Israel, so his side isn't the surprise.
The story is the margin: one Democrat, plus three absent Democrats, twice stood between the Senate and a war-powers vote.

**Hit means.** The roll-call record shows two one-vote failures, with every senator named.
**Miss would have meant.** His crossings came on lopsided votes that decided nothing. They didn't.

**Tested and dead.**
- ⚠ **"House Republicans got more unified under Trump II" is fake. The vote mix explains it.**
  - Raw House GOP break rate: 6.8% (118th) to 2.4% (119th). Democrats: 3.3% to 3.7%. That looks like a story.
  - But the 118th House held **547 amendment roll calls** (506 in its first 18 months). The 119th held **66** in its first 18 months. Amendments are where Republicans split: a 12% break rate.
  - Within each vote type the GOP rate didn't fall by much, and on amendments it rose: passage 1.6% to 1.3%, rules 0.9% to 0.5%, amendments 12% to 15%.
  - Reweight the 119th to the 118th's vote mix and the GOP rate is 8.1%. That's *higher*, not lower.
  - The real change is the vote mix itself: amendment roll calls fell from about 23 a month to about 4 a month.
- Cuellar (D-TX), before and after the Dec 2025 pardon: 69 of 205 party votes broken (33.7%) Jan-Nov 2025, 70 of 207 (33.8%) Dec 2025 on. No change. Gonzalez (TX-34), Golden, Perez and Davis ran the same pattern month by month.
- The Senate's switch (Republicans rarely break in the 119th, Democrats more) follows who holds the majority, because the majority picks what comes to a vote. Nominations were 428 of 851 roll calls.

**Traps.**
- ⚠ **Party-unity numbers from different Congresses can't be compared without fixing the vote mix.** The same goes for `MEMBER_VOTING_RECORD.PARTY_UNITY_PCT`.
- `PROB` is filled on "not voting" and "present" rows too (17,590 of 20,259 not-voting rows in the 118th House). There it means nothing.
- `PROB` is blank on 22% of 119th House yea rows. Those are lopsided votes the model doesn't score.
- One row per chamber per Congress has no member match (478 rows).
- Months with zero House roll calls: every August (2023, 2024, 2025), October 2024 (campaign recess) and October 2025. A monthly series has holes that are real recesses, not missing data.

## 2. POLITICS__INTL_VOETEN_UNGA_VOTES: **live**

**What it is.** How often each pair of countries voted alike in the UN General Assembly, sessions 1-79 (1946-2024). Country codes are Correlates of War codes (US = 2, Argentina = 160).
⚠ It ends at the 2024 session. "Did it shift after 2025?" can't be answered here.

**The finding: Argentina under Milei.**
- Argentina's agreement with the US: **0.591 (2023) to 0.783 (2024), +0.193.**
- That's **the biggest rise of 178 countries** (counting only countries with 60+ votes both years). The median country *fell* 0.126. Next biggest rise: Tonga +0.065, then Israel +0.055.
- Peer group: **all 20 other Latin American countries fell**, by a median of 0.159. Latin America's median is 0.422. Next highest is Paraguay at 0.554.
- It ranks **5th of 190** in 2024, level with Czechia (0.783) and next to the UK (0.788). Behind only Israel (0.921), Micronesia (0.859) and the UK.
- Agreement with Israel: 0.513 to 0.764. Only the US and Micronesia are higher.
- Argentina's average agreement with *everyone* fell from 0.850 to **0.582**. That makes it the **third most isolated country in the Assembly**, after Israel (0.412) and the US (0.458). Russia is 0.583.
- Its ideal point (a score placing each country on one line of UN voting) moved **-0.03 to +1.38.** Only two moves since 1991 were bigger: Albania 1991 (+1.71) and North Korea 2015 (+1.51). Argentina's own next-biggest move was 1991 under Menem (+1.30).
- I read the raw rows for 2021-2024 in both directions (160-2 and 2-160). They match.

**The dull explanations, tested.**
- A few votes blowing up a ratio? No. Argentina had 101 votes in 2024, the most of any country (88 in 2023).
- The US moving toward Argentina? No. The US ideal point barely moved (2.218 to 2.206), and every other Latin American country moved away from the US.
- Milei said publicly he'd line up with the US and Israel. So the direction isn't news. The size and the peer ranking are.
- Not testable here: the table has no vote dates. A session opens in September, so "2024" runs Sept 2024 to Sept 2025. Most recorded votes fall Oct-Dec (still Biden). A few early-2025 Trump-era votes may be mixed in.
- Milei took office Dec 10, 2023, so the 2023 session is mostly the previous government. Before and after line up cleanly.

**Hit means.** One country left its regional bloc for the US-Israel corner in a single year, and it's measurable against 177 others.
**Miss would have meant.** Argentina moved with the pack. It moved against it.

**Context, not the lead: the NATO swing.**
- Median NATO ally's agreement with the US: 0.557 (2019), **0.829 (2022, highest since 1978)**, 0.726 (2024). Membership is counted by accession year.
- From 2023 to 2024, every NATO ally's agreement with the US fell. Among US allies only Israel rose.
- Their agreement with France held: 0.925 to 0.923. So the US moved, not the allies.

**Traps.**
- ⚠ **Every pair is stored twice** (A-B and B-A, exactly equal counts in every session). Sums and counts come out doubled.
- ⚠ **Vote counts can be tiny.** Turkey's 2024 score rests on 7 votes. Sao Tome's 2022-23 scores rest on 2-3, and Liberia's 2023 score on 21. Countries behind on UN dues lose their vote, and the agreement numbers swing wildly. Filter `NVOTESFP_X >= 60`.
- COL_0 is a row number. Every number is stored as text.
- There's no 1964 session: no recorded votes that year (the Article 19 crisis). It's real, not a load gap.
- The ideal point is blank for 3 country-sessions (1963, 1966, 2001).
- Germany is code 260 before 1990 and 255 after.

## 3. POLITICS__FED_MEDSL_PRESIDENT_RETURNS: probed

**What it is.** Presidential votes by state and candidate: 3,740 rows, 11 elections, 1976-2016, 51 states+DC each time.

**Checked.**
- ⚠ **It stops at 2016.** MIT's election lab publishes 2020 and 2024. `WHO_WON` (President rows) also stops at 2016: same source, same gap.
- Peer and time: the median state's third-party share was 22.0% in 1992, 10.8% in 1996 and 5.8% in 2016. Utah 2016 was 27.0%. That's all well-known history.
- Total votes changed +5.7% from 2012 to 2016 in the median state. Mississippi fell 5.9%, Texas rose 12.2%.

**Nothing story-grade.** It's usable as 1976-2016 context only.

**Traps.**
- ⚠ **TOTAL_VOTES repeats on every candidate row.** The glance's "sum 8.3B" is a repeated total, not a count.
- ⚠ **Nebraska 2000 has two totals:** 697,019 on five rows and **967,019** on one (a blank-candidate row with 478 votes). The digits are swapped.
- ⚠ **Minnesota's Democrats are labeled `democratic-farmer-labor`** in 2000, 2004 and 2012. A `PARTY = 'democrat'` filter drops Gore (1.17M), Kerry (1.45M) and Obama 2012 (1.55M) there.
- STATE_FIPS lost its leading zero ('6' for California).
- 266 rows have a blank candidate (write-ins, "other", scattering).
- 42 candidate names repeat within a state-year. These are fusion lines, mostly New York: Clinton 2016 also ran on the Working Families and Women's Equality lines. Counting rows is not counting candidates.

## 4. POLITICS__IRS527_EAIN: skip (ID crosswalk)

**What it is.** State and federal election ID numbers that 527 political groups list on IRS Form 8871. 17,853 rows.
There's one row per form per state. **All 17,853 land on `IRS527_8871_ORGS`** by FORM_ID_NUMBER.

**Checked.**
- Amended 8871s repeat the same ID. California: 6,849 rows, but only 3,007 distinct IDs.
- 99 of 10,981 state+ID pairs are listed by 2+ different EINs. The ones I looked at are one group re-filing under a new EIN (Enterprise Rent-A-Car NY, SEIU Local 2015, California Life Sciences) or placeholders ('APPLIED', 'NOTISSUED', '0000', 'IEPAC').
  - Odd one, not chased: Missouri ID 751398463 (EIN-shaped) is listed by five separate Kansas City candidate committees.
- `FD` means federal, not a state. It has 1,012 distinct IDs. 804 look like FEC committee IDs, and **347 (43%) land on `POLITICS__FEC_COMMITTEE`.** That table covers only some cycles, so a miss there proves nothing.

**Traps.**
- Junk IDs cluster by state: Nevada 41 of 79, New Mexico 24 of 50, Mississippi 12 of 22 (N/A, NONE, PENDING and the like).
- One load run (2026-08-05).

## 5. POLITICS__FJC_SCOTUS_CROSSWALK: skip (lookup)

**What it is.** 40 justices (1946 on), each linked from a Supreme Court Database code to a Federal Judicial Center ID.
I checked every row against `FJC_JUDGE` by birth year. 39 are right. Both Jacksons get separate IDs.

**⚠ Trap: one row is the wrong person.**
- `JHarlan2` (John Marshall Harlan II, first term 1954) points to FJC ID **1381826**, which is **John Marshall Harlan born 1833, died 1911.** That's his grandfather, Justice Harlan I.
- The right ID is **1381831** (born 1899, died 1971).
- It's the only row matched by `first_last_suffixstripped` at confidence 0.90. The label says matched; the match is wrong.
- LAST_TERM is 2024 for sitting justices, so the source is a term or two behind.
