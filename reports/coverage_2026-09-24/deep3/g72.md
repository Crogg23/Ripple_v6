## 1. DIM_TRACT: **live** (thin), and a trap for the ledger

**What it is:** every US census tract plus Puerto Rico, one row each. 85,391 rows = 85,391 distinct 11-digit codes. [q06]

### The table itself is clean [q06, q07]
- Every code is the right length. State, county and tract parts all agree with each other. No blanks, no nulls.
- Population adds to **334,735,155**. Take away Puerto Rico's 3,285,874 and you get 331,449,281, the official 2020 US count.
- It matches DIM_COUNTY on all 3,222 counties, and the tract populations sum to each county's number exactly.
- No island areas (Guam, USVI, American Samoa, N. Marianas): 0 tracts.
- 610 tracts have 0 people. All 357 water tracts (codes starting 99) are in that 610. **All 610 still have a "population center" point.** Nobody lives there, so that point isn't a population center. Don't map it as one.

### The trap: two map years in one table [q06, q09, q10, q15]
`VINTAGE` holds two values:

| Vintage | Tracts | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | 84,512 | 50 states minus CT, plus DC and PR |
| 2022 | **879** | **Connecticut only**, on its new "planning region" codes 09110-09190 |

This matches the known DIM_COUNTY trap: Connecticut there is on the 2022 codes too. Now it reaches the tract level.

**The join test, FEMA disaster aid registrations → DIM_TRACT** (first 11 digits of FEMA's block-group code; FEMA aggregated first):

| FEMA rows by map year | Rows | Land on DIM_TRACT |
|---|---|---|
| Connecticut, any year | 38,576 | **1** |
| Coded to 2020 tracts | 1,235,879 | 99.8% |
| Coded to 2010 tracts | 974,100 | 73.2% |
| Coded to 2000 tracts | 1,024,598 | 53.9% |
| Map year blank | 15,925,313 | 82.1% |
| **All rows with a tract code** | **19,198,466** | **81.1%** |
| No tract code at all | 7,052,454 | not joinable |

By disaster date, outside CT: declared before 2012 **68.2%** land, 2012-2021 **72.2%**, 2022 on **99.6%**.

**Same test on HMDA historic mortgages, 2015-2017** (built from state + county + tract; state and county codes are stored without leading zeros, CT shows as `9`):
- Outside CT: **71.5%** of rows land. 60,847 of 72,203 tracts (84%).
- Connecticut: **0 of 408,384 rows.**
- The rows fall off faster than the tracts do. The tracts the Census split in 2020 were the fast-growing ones, which had the most mortgages. **So the rows you lose aren't random: they're the growth suburbs.**

**Hit means:** any per-person rate built on DIM_TRACT from pre-2022 data quietly drops about 30% of rows and every Connecticut row. Connecticut ends up looking like it has nothing.
**Miss would have meant:** tract codes are stable, join freely. They aren't.
**The fix:** use a 2010→2020 tract crosswalk for older data. The warehouse doesn't have one; Census publishes one. For Connecticut, crosswalk to the 2022 codes, or build a 2020-vintage CT set.
The idea book's "redlined then, denied now" plan (HMDA_HISTORIC + DIM_TRACT) will hit this trap.

### Outliers (peer = the tract's own state) [q08]
- The Census aims for tracts of 1,200 to 8,000 people. 1,333 tracts (1.56%) were over 8,000 on census day.
- South Carolina, Texas and Florida: **3.8% each**, about 2.4x the national rate. Texas has 15 tracts over 15,000.
- Biggest: San Diego County tract 0187, **37,892** people, 9.0x California's median tract. Its point (33.31, -117.41) looks like Camp Pendleton, the Marine base, so this is probably barracks, not growth. That's from the coordinates only; not checked.
- Next: Bexar County TX 30,199; Harris TX 27,220; Orange County CA 25,232; Fort Bend TX 24,651. These are suburbs that outgrew their tracts before 2020.
- **Dull explanation:** Sun Belt growth. That's already known. Not a story. It flags which tracts will split for 2030, which means more code churn.

### The lead: FEMA registrations per resident (one join, peer = same disaster) [q11-q14]
**Built on:** disasters declared 2022 or later only, so FEMA's tract codes match DIM_TRACT (99.6% land). Registrations are counted once each by REGISTRATION_ID; rows = registrations, so no duplicates in this slice. Only tracts with 1,000+ residents.

Disaster names come from the numbers and dates (outside knowledge): 4673 Ian, 4724 Maui, 4856 LA fires.

**Step 1, the scan [q11]:** 50,187 tract-disaster pairs across 124 disasters. Only **2** have more registrations than 2020 residents:

| Tract | Disaster | Residents 2020 | Registrations | Per resident | Disaster's median tract |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lee County FL 0019.10 (Fort Myers) | Hurricane Ian, 2022 | 1,554 | 1,858 | 1.20 | 0.053 |
| Maui HI 0314.04 (Lahaina) | Maui wildfire, 2023 | 3,223 | 3,261 | 1.01 | 0.044 |

**Step 2, Fort Myers is explained [q12b, q13]:** only 70.6% of its registrations were for a primary residence. In its biggest block group, the most common home type is mobile home. These look like winter residents; the census counts them at their home up north. **Dull explanation fits. Dropped.**

**Step 3, Lahaina inside its own group [q12b].** The three tracts the fire gutted:

| Lahaina tract | Registrations marked destroyed | Registrations per resident | People listed per resident* | FEMA verified occupancy | Primary residence | Renters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **314.04** | 53.9% | **1.01** | **2.05** | **71.4%** | 85.2% | 73.0% |
| 314.02 | 51.7% | 0.75 | 1.63 | 86.3% | 94.2% | 70.5% |
| 314.05 | 45.1% | 0.50 | 1.09 | 88.1% | 94.9% | 70.1% |
| other 55 Maui tracts | 0.2% | 0.04 | 0.06 | 92.3% | 96.5% | 46.5% |

\* Household size is self-reported. Registrations per resident doesn't depend on it; lean on that column.

- For comparison, the hardest-hit tracts in the 2025 LA fires (Palisades, Altadena, Malibu): 1.06-1.25 people listed per resident, 83-94% verified.
- The destroyed share is about the same across the three Lahaina tracts, and so is the renter share. **Occupancy verification is not.**

**Step 4, is it the geocoder? [q13]:** No. 314.04's registrations are spread over all 3 block groups (1,170 / 1,073 / 1,018), all in ZIP 96761. A fallback dump would pile into one spot.
- The weak spot is block group 1: 1,170 registrations, **61.4% verified**; apartment is the most common home type.

**Step 5, is it late filers? [q14]:** No. 314.04 is lower at every stage:

| Filed | 314.04 verified | 314.02 | 314.05 |
|---|---|---|---|
| first week | 82.0% (1,692 regs) | 92.3% | 94.0% |
| days 8-30 | 66.1% | 84.4% | 88.4% |
| days 31-90 | 51.9% | 77.7% | 77.5% |
| after 90 days | 36.4% | 61.7% | 63.3% |

**Hit means:** downtown Lahaina drew more aid registrations than people counted there, and FEMA confirmed who lived there far less often than in the burn tracts next door, at every stage. That's a question for FEMA and its inspector general: how many of the roughly 930 unverified registrations were ineligible, duplicate or fraudulent?
**Miss means:** FEMA records show the extra registrations were real separate households: several families or roommates per apartment, each registering. Then the story flips. The census missed a crowded worker-housing district. Still a story, just a different one.
**Dull explanations not tested (no data for them in the warehouse):** crowded units with several households each; informal renters without a lease who can't prove occupancy; the 2020 census missing people. The 27% of FEMA rows with no tract code could make these ratios floors, not ceilings; that wasn't checked for Maui.
**Why "thin":** one tract, 3,261 registrations, one field (VERIFIED_OCCUPANCY) doing the work. It needs FEMA's own records before print.

## 2. OPEN_DATA__INTL_CA_OPEN_CANADA: **dead**

**What it is:** 500 listings from Canada's federal open-data catalog, 500 distinct IDs. [q03, q04]

- **Not a sample.** Every row's `METADATA_MODIFIED` falls between 2026-07-01 17:04 and 2026-07-02 18:51. The warehouse inventory has the load at 18:57 UTC, about 6 minutes later, if the portal's times are UTC (CKAN's default). The fetch took the **500 most recently edited** listings.
- So "72% Quebec" just means Quebec's feed updated 360 listings in about an hour on 2026-07-01 (18:02 to 19:08). The `METADATA_CREATED` years (2016-2026) are whatever happened to get edited that day. **Any trend or calendar chart on this table is a chart of one day's edits.**
- Traps:
  - `TAGS` is `[]` on all 500.
  - `FREQUENCY` is empty on all 500.
  - `TIME_PERIOD_COVERAGE_START` is the placeholder 0001-01-01 on 274; `TIME_PERIOD_COVERAGE_END` blank on 392.
  - The same three subjects (`form_descriptors`, nature, science) sit on 411 rows. Looks like a feed default, not a topic.
  - `RESOURCES` hides 4,197 files as JSON text: median 6 per listing, max 75.

**Hit would have meant:** a trend in what Canada publishes. **Miss means:** it's a one-day snapshot of edits.
**Verdict:** dead for stories. To use it, reload the full catalog, not one page.

## 3. OPEN_DATA__INTL_ES_DATOSGOB: **dead**

**What it is:** 1,000 listings from Spain's datos.gob.es, one API page. [q01, q02, q16]

- `URI`, `TITLE`, `DESCRIPTION`: **blank on all 1,000**. The "read the words" angle has no words to read.
- Filled: publisher code (33 distinct, top one 312 rows), sector link (71 combos, science/tech 220), download link (1,000 distinct), format (796), license (only 101).

- Also not random: two-thirds were edited this year.

**Verdict:** dead. Nothing to read, and publishers are codes with no names.
