## 1. HEALTH__FED_CDC_OVERDOSE — live

**What it is.** CDC's provisional overdose death counts by state and month (VSRR).
Each row is a 12-month rolling total, Jan 2015 through Jan 2026. It has 54 jurisdictions: the states plus DC, PR, NYC ("YC") and the US.

**The finding.**
- US: 111,466 deaths at the June 2023 peak, 67,895 in the 12 months to Jan 2026. That is **down 39%**.
- Arizona went the other way. It hit **2,980 in the 12 months ending Oct 2025**, the highest in the file.
  - Its old high was 2,829 (Aug 2023). The low was 1,173 (Apr 2015).
  - Only one other state peaked in 2025: Hawaii, in April.
- Same 12 months a year apart (Jan 2025 vs Jan 2026, CDC's predicted counts):
  - Across 52 places (50 states, DC, Puerto Rico), the median fell **14.4%**. 10 went up.
  - The states that rose sit mostly in the Southwest, Mountain West and Plains: NM +24%, SD +14%, AZ +11.5%, CO +11%, NE +7%, MT +4%, MN +4%, ND +2.5%. AK and NH are about flat.
- What's driving Arizona (Jan 2025 vs Jan 2026):
  - Cocaine deaths: 261 to **443 (+70%)**. The US fell 12%.
  - Fentanyl-class deaths: 1,599 to **1,982 (+24%)**. The US fell 22%.

**Checks run.**
- Time: same month, year over year, for every state. Also each state's peak month.
- Peer: every state against the median state. Arizona against its neighbors, which are rising too, so this is a regional cluster.
- Cross-file: the county table has Arizona rising too, and puts most of the rise in Maricopa (see #2).

**The dull explanations, tested.**
- Late reports: the newest months are incomplete, which pushes counts down. So Arizona's rise is a floor, not an inflated number.
- Pending cause of death: 0.15% of Arizona's deaths are pending. Too small to matter.
- Better drug coding: "percent with drugs specified" barely moved in Arizona, 99.35% to 99.38%.
- Population growth is about 1-2% a year. It can't make +11%.

**Hit means.** Arizona is a real outlier against both the national decline and its own history.
**Miss would have meant.** Arizona's rise was a lag or coding artifact. It isn't.

**Traps.**
- Every row from Jan 2025 on carries "Underreported due to incomplete data", in every state. The label comes from the month, not the state.
- YC (New York City) is a separate row, not part of NY.
- Every value, the predicted count and the completeness figures are all stored as text.

## 2. HEALTH__FED_CDC_INJURY_VIOLENCE_COUNTY — live

**The finding.**
- Maricopa County AZ overdose deaths:
  - 1,539 (2023), 1,478 (2024), **1,791** in the rolling window (Dec 2024 to Nov 2025).
  - Rate: 32.2 to 39.1 per 100K.
- That's **+313 deaths, the biggest rise of any US county**. Denver is next at +54.
- Peer group: 311 counties with 50 or more overdose deaths in 2024. **The median fell 17%, and 44 rose.**
- Inside Arizona: 12 counties have both numbers. Maricopa carries **313 of the +368 (85%)**. The median Arizona county rose 5.
  - So one county drives the state. Mohave County is the only large county that rose in both steps (72 to 93 to 103).
- It matches the state file (#1), which comes from separate CDC reporting.

**Checks run.** Time (2019 to 2024 plus the rolling window). Peer (large counties, and counties within the state). Whether a few units carry the effect (yes: Maricopa). Cross-file consistency.

**Dull explanation.** The rolling window is provisional, so it runs low. Maricopa's rise survives that. Maricopa suicides stayed flat (808 to 778), so it isn't a reporting-volume jump.

**Traps (for the ledger).**
- **The rolling windows don't line up.** Overdose covers Dec 2024 to Nov 2025. The other five causes cover Feb 2025 to Jan 2026.
- **Some counties have holes in the rolling window.**
  - Tulsa County OK: suicides 139 to 34, homicides blank.
  - El Paso County CO: overdoses 326 to 143 while suicides held (190 to 192). That looks like a toxicology backlog, not a real fall.
  - A county whose rolling number falls under 60% of its 2024 number on several causes at once is a hole, not a trend.
- **County sums don't match the state file.** Arizona 2024: counties add to 2,307, plus at most 27 hidden in suppressed rows. The state file says 2,531 (Dec 2024). Likely county of residence vs place of death, and final vs provisional numbers.
- Two loads under one run id: 2019-2024 is dated 2026-05-14, the rolling window 2026-06-22. 2019 has 3,142 counties, later years 3,143.
- Known traps confirmed: COUNT_SUP holds range text ('1-9' on 70,549 rows, '10-50' on 51). RATE uses -999 as filler on 6,549 rows.

**Context, not a lead.** Firearm suicides, using only the 474 counties with a count every period: 14,881 (2019) to 17,233 (2024) to 17,188 (rolling window).
Firearm homicides, 661 counties: 14,722 (2021) to 8,264. Both are well-known national trends.

## 3. HEALTH__FED_VA_ALLCAUSE_MORTALITY — live

**What it is.** VA's leading causes of death, 2018 to 2023.
Groups: all veterans, recent VHA users (veterans who used VA health care), and other veterans. Each by sex. Age-adjusted rates.
There is no non-veteran group in this table. The peer group came from VA_SUICIDE_NATIONAL (read whole, 1 statement).

**The finding.**
- Suicide among men who use VA health care, per 100K, age-adjusted:
  - 48.5, 49.5, 49.8, 50.4, 51.7, **53.4** (2018 to 2023). **It rose every year.**
  - VA_SUICIDE_NATIONAL agrees: 47.4 (2017) up to 53.0 (2023), also every year.
- Non-veteran men: 29.3 (2018), 29.0 (2023). **Flat.**
- All veteran men vs non-veteran men: 1.28x (2013), 1.33x (2018), **1.50x (2023)**.
- Heart disease and cancer fell for this group over the same years. Suicide climbed.

**The dull explanation, tested.** Maybe the VA is treating a sicker or growing group of men.
- Their overall death rate *fell*: 1,422 to 1,386. Not a sicker group.
- Their number *shrank*: 5.54M to 5.40M. Deaths still rose, 2,304 to 2,382.
- Not tested: whether the post-9/11 veterans are a bigger share now. Age adjustment doesn't fix that.

**Caveat.** VA publishes the overall gap itself. The less-told part is the unbroken yearly rise among the men the VA actually treats.

**Traps.**
- **Summing DEATHS counts each death about 8 times.**
  - All veterans = VHA users + other veterans (204,818 + 285,245 = 490,063 in 2018).
  - The "All" sex rows = male + female.
  - An "All cause" row sits beside the individual causes.
- **The same rate sits in two columns.** The veteran rows for both sexes combined use UNADJUSTED_RATE; every other row uses CRUDE_RATE.
- The bottom-ranked causes have a rank but no counts. That's most of why 38% of DEATHS is empty.
- The two VA tables disagree slightly: all veteran men, 2023, 43.9 vs 43.5.

## 4. HEALTH__FED_FDA_FAERS_DRUG — live, as history (the file ends 2014q2)

**The finding: lawsuits leave a footprint in FDA's drug side-effect database.**
- Reports filed by lawyers (reporter code LW), counting only the drug each report blames: **197,821 of 5.82M reports (3.4%)**.
- Metoclopramide (Reglan):
  - Lawyers filed **15,539 of 17,011 reports (91%)**.
  - **9,092 of those came in 2011q2 and 2011q3**, out of 9,455 reports in those two quarters (96%).
  - In the seven years before (2004 to 2010), the drug drew 1,414 reports in total.
  - The timing lines up with PLIVA v. Mensing, the June 2011 Supreme Court case on generic-drug warnings. **Not tested as the cause.**
- Other drugs with a large lawyer share: Vioxx 54% (35,347 of 65,772), Accutane 70%, Trasylol 59%, Provera 56%, Yaz 33%.
- For the five drugs checked quarter by quarter (metoclopramide, Reglan, Yaz, Avandia), no lawyer report went straight to FDA. Every one came in through the drugmaker.

**A second footprint: one bulk dump.**
- Avandia, 2014q2: **23,832 reports filed as coming from consumers**, all sent by GlaxoSmithKline and marked "country not specified".
  - The events date from 1999 to 2013. FDA first received all of them between Apr 24 and Jun 25, 2014.
  - Most Avandia quarters from 2012q4 on had 560 to 1,420 reports. One had 4,321.
  - The whole quarter has only 7 reports coded as from a lawyer. **So counting LW-coded reports underestimates reports that came from litigation.**

**Checks.** Time (every quarter, 2004q1 to 2014q2). Peer (each drug's lawyer share vs 3.4% overall). Dedupe (distinct cases: metoclopramide lawyer reports 15,539 = 13,559 cases, so it isn't inflated by follow-up reports).

**Dull explanation.** Lawyer reports are legitimate reports; FDA takes them from anyone. So the story is about how the database gets distorted, not about new harm.

**Traps (for the ledger).**
- **The FAERS column-shift trap (R-346) is stale for the marts.** ROLE_COD is a valid code on every row. GNDR_COD has 0 Y/N values. Re-check before blocking anything on it.
- Report ID switch: ISR is empty from 2012q4 on. The newer report ID, PRIMARYID, takes over. Join on `coalesce(nullif(isr,''), primaryid)`.
- DOSE_AMT is only filled from 2012q4. Its max, 5.7B, is junk. The battery's "top 1% holds 97% of DOSE_AMT" is noise, not money.
- DRUGNAME has **453,817 distinct spellings**.
- The file stops at 2014q2 (the trap is already known).

## 5. HEALTH__FED_FDA_ESTABLISHMENT_REG — live (moderate)

**What it is.** One row per registered device site × product. 25,461 sites, 263,374 rows.
544 sites are registered as contract sterilizers, 145 of them in the US.

**The join.** US contract sterilizers linked to EPA ethylene oxide (EtO) emitters.
- EPA emissions data (TRI and EIS) was linked to EPA's facility registry (FRS) by EPA's registry ID.
- The FDA site was matched on **the same state + city + street number** (the second field has to agree).
- **Land rate: 40 of 145 US sterilizer sites (28%)**, which is 43 EPA IDs.
- All 40 were eyeballed. The full street address agrees on every one.
  - Most names agree too.
  - The rest are a parent company or an older name at the same address: Royal Sterilization = DeRoyal, Bard = BD, Isomedix = STERIS, Griffith Micro Science = Sterigenics.
  - At LYNX Medical's two sites and at Isomedix Grand Prairie, EPA names Cosmed. Probably a sale. Not verified.

**The finding.**
- EtO from all TRI filers: **245,443 lbs (2018) to 120,728 (2024), down 51%**.
- Nine matched sterilizers have TRI numbers in both 2018 and 2024. **The median fell 45%.**
  - Five cut 45-97%. Midwest Sterilization in Laredo TX: 15,112 to 1,713.
  - Four rose: three BD/C.R. Bard plants (Sandy UT, Madison GA, Covington GA), and DeRoyal.
- **DeRoyal (Royal Sterilization Systems), New Tazewell TN:**
  - Main TRI filing: 1,305 lbs (2018), **1,950 (2022)**, 1,850 (2023), 1,205 (2024).
  - A second DeRoyal TRI filing sits under the same EPA site from 2023: 255, then 500 lbs.
  - The site's 2024 total, **1,705 lbs, ties for #1** among the 32 matched EPA sites that filed in 2024. Midwest Laredo has 1,713, down from 16,390 in 2019.
  - Inspections: ICIS-Air (EPA's air-inspection records) shows **1 evaluation since 2019** (last one Aug 2023). BD's Georgia plants had 21 and 24.
- Concentration: 1,786 device product types are listed at contract sterilizers.
  - 717 of them (40%) list only one sterilizer company.
  - Sotera Health (Sterigenics) is the only listed contract sterilizer for 255 types, 29 of them high-risk Class III. STERIS: 115 types.

**The dull explanations.**
- Apples to apples, DeRoyal's main filing is **down 8%** from 2018 to 2024. The site total only rose because a second filing appeared. **Say "among the top two", not "rising".**
- Few inspections are normal in the federal record. The median facility had 0 air evaluations since 2019 in TX, TN, GA, MO and PA. ICIS-Air mostly covers large sources; state-only inspections may not show up.
- Not tested: BD's 2020 jumps (Madison 3,196, Covington 2,406) may come from new ways of estimating emissions after the 2019 Georgia scrutiny, not more gas.
- Sole-source ≠ supply risk. Device makers who sterilize in their own plants aren't in "contract sterilizer" listings.

**Traps (for the ledger).**
- **POSTAL_CODE is empty for every US row.** 120.6K blanks = the 120.6K US rows. Match on city + state + street number.
- STATUS_CODE is '1' on 99.8% of rows. The 598 '5' rows are all one site (Amphenol, North Macedonia) with no FEI (the FDA's site ID). REG_EXPIRY_DATE_YEAR is always 2026.
- **ECHO's DATE_LAST_INSPECTION is stale.** For DeRoyal it says 2014-01-23. ICIS-Air's own table shows Aug 2023. Use ICIS_AIR_FCES_PCES.
- **The five Sterigenics sites checked have no TRI rows at all for 2017-2021**, any pollutant, in the combined emissions table. A 2015-to-2024 trend on them spans a hole.
- **EPA's two sources disagree 10-100x for the same plant.** STERIS/Isomedix El Paso: EIS 2020 says 6,467 lbs, TRI 2022 says 46. Sterigenics Santa Teresa: 7,449 vs 963.
- One EPA site ID can hold two TRI filings (DeRoyal). Summing by site ID merges plants.
