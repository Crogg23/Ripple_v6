## 1. GLEIF: EU registrars renewed sanctioned clients' IDs after the EU listed them. **live**

**What the table is:** 3,382,301 LEIs (the 20-character global company ID), one row each.
- Snapshot date: 2026-07-23 (latest update in the file).
- Current (ISSUED): 1,940,941 (57.4%). Lapsed: 1,185,130. Retired: 247,276.
- 40 registration offices (LOUs) manage them. Each office has its own LEI, so it names itself in the file.

**Checked**
- **Join, sanctions → GLEIF on LEI** (S07, S09, S13):
  - OpenSanctions company targets carrying an LEI-shaped ID: 1,390. 1,246 land in GLEIF (89.6%). Same as deep2 found.
  - **OFAC's own SDN list** prints "Legal Entity Number" in its remarks: 234 LEI-shaped hits, **167 land**.
    - 61 of the 67 misses are Indian company numbers (CIN) that fit the pattern. The other 6 are crypto addresses, ID numbers and one LEI-looking code for Qingdao Fushen Petrochemical that isn't in GLEIF.
  - **The EU's own list file** (dated 05/06/2026) prints 3 LEIs that land. Two are still current (below).
- **Second field:** for the renewed eight, the name and city agree on the GLEIF record (Hamburg, Tehran/Paris, Milan, Dubai, Singapore, Kowloon). Name changes like Coral Energy → 2Rivers show up in GLEIF's own "other name" and event fields.
- **Listing dates** come from the official EU file (S14), not from OpenSanctions text:
  - Iranian banks: 2025-09-29, Reg 2025/1980. That's the snapback re-listing ("suspended since 16.1.2016").
  - 2Rivers DMCC, 2Rivers PTE, BX Energy: 2025-07-19, Reg 2025/1476.
  - Alghaf Marine DMCC: 2026-04-23, Reg 2026/509, with its LEI printed in the listing.
- **"Renewed after listing" test (S16):**
  - Across all 1.94M current IDs, only 11 have a renewal date more than 430 days past the record's last touch. None of those 11 are at the five offices behind the eight.
  - So a renewal date D was written no earlier than D − 430 days.
  - If D − 430 days is after the listing, the office rewrote the date after the listing.
- **Peer: each registrar against its own country's list, and against its own normal current rate:**

- **Alghaf Marine, step by step:**
  - 2026-04-23: the EU lists "Alghaf Marine DMCC" and prints LEI 984500SAI1E9CB88DC54.
  - 2026-06-11: GLEIF logs a legal-name change to "ALGHAF MARINE FZCO", effective that day. The old name stays on as an "other name".
  - The record is current, due 2027-08-06. By the 430-day test, that date was written on or after 2026-06-02.
- **The Russian office:** NSD (253400M18U5TB02TW421) is on OFAC's SDN list with that LEI printed in OFAC's remarks (entry 37638). OpenSanctions has it on the EU and UK lists too.

- **Non-Russian offices, SDN only (context):** 60 of 241 SDN-listed IDs are current. For 46, the renewal date was set after the listing date. The listing date here is a proxy: the SAM reciprocal date or OpenSanctions' first-seen date.
  - 14 of those 46 are on OFAC's own printed list. They include Indian petrochemical traders designated 2025-10-09 and renewed at LEI India or Ubisecure.
  - Non-US offices aren't bound by OFAC, so this is context, not a lead.

**Hit means:** EU registrars kept serving EU-sanctioned non-Russian clients at about their normal rate. They pushed 8 renewal dates past the listing, and one of those came with a name change. UK and US registrars let theirs lapse. The system banks use to identify counterparties keeps some sanctioned IDs looking current, and whether that happens depends on the registrar.

**Miss means:** it's lawful routine if a national authority licensed the fee. EU asset-freeze rules allow authorized payment of fees for routine upkeep of frozen assets. It's also routine if the fee was prepaid before the listing and the office only rolled the date forward.

**Boring:**
- **Prepaid multi-year plans.** Partly tested. At Ubisecure, 3,177 of 15,026 IDs registered Aug-Oct 2025 (21%) already show a renewal date past their first year. Prepaying is common. The 430-day test proves the office rewrote the date after the listing. It can't show when the money moved.
- **Payment channels.** Russian clients can't easily pay EU offices. Iranian bank branches in Hamburg, Paris and Milan have EU accounts. That could explain the Russia/Iran split. **Not tested.**
- **Licences.** Not testable from the warehouse.
- **Wrong LEI link.** 2 of the 8 LEIs are printed by the EU itself. The other 6 come from OpenSanctions links, with name and city agreeing.
- **Small n:** 19 units in the EU group. There's no median unit to report. Each of the 8 is its own row, above.
- Known context (from memory, check it): the Sept 2025 UN snapback re-imposed the EU's Iran listings, and 2Rivers (Coral Energy) is widely reported as a Russian-oil trader.

**Traps found**
- The glance's top names ("Alterius non sit B.V.", "AWARD INTERNATIONAL TRADING", "KAILASH GULATI", each "3.8K") **each appear once**. The real top name is FUTURA S.R.L. with 42. The glance's top-name counts were an artifact.
- ENTITY_ENTITYCREATIONDATE: 2,887 before year 1600 (the 0001-01-01 sentinel), 82 after the snapshot (up to 2027-03-11), 612,520 blank.
- CONFORMITYFLAG = NON_CONFORMING on 1,294,305 rows (38%). 1,183,911 of those are simply LAPSED.
- An LEI-shaped regex over OFAC remarks catches 61 Indian company numbers. Always check the land rate.

**Next join:** GLEIF_RELATIONSHIPS on the 8 (who owns them), plus the official UK list (`JUSTICE__XC_UK_SANCTIONS_LIST`) and OFAC, to see who else lists them.

## 2. MTS receipts: the customs refund wave. **live**

**Checked**
- Shape: 7,490 rows = 136 monthly statements (2015-03 to 2026-06), 50-57 lines each. All from Table 4. No missing months, no duplicate lines.
- **Time, customs duties, same months across years:**
  - Net customs: $5-7B a month through 2024. $15.6B in April 2025, $31.4B at the October 2025 peak, $22.1B in April 2026.
  - **Refunds:** $0.01-1.09B a month from 2015 through FY2025. FY2026 through April: $0.6-1.9B.
  - **May 2026: $21.97B. June 2026: $49.18B.**
  - June 2026: gross customs $23.63B, minus $49.18B refunds = **−$25.55B net**. May: −$0.04B. The first negative customs months in the file.
  - FY2026 through June: refunds $81.30B vs $5.27B a year earlier. Net customs is still up: $163.02B vs $108.02B.
- **Is it customs only?** Total refunds in June 2026 were $68.0B vs $16.8B in June 2025. Everything except customs: $18.8B vs $16.3B. Normal.
- Total net receipts, June 2026: $495.8B vs $526.4B a year earlier (−$30.6B), while gross rose from $543.3B to $563.8B.

**Hit means:** the refund wave is big enough to flip a whole revenue line negative. It's a clean, dated number for a tariff-refund story.

**Miss means:** it's the government's own published number. Anyone reading the June MTS has it. The value here is the month-by-month series and the trap below.

**Boring:** the likely cause is court-ordered refunds of the emergency (IEEPA) tariffs. That's from memory, not checked here. The data shows the refund wave, not the reason.

**Traps found**
- ⚠ **The table holds no negative numbers.** 0 negatives across 39,425 filled amount cells. All 25 times refunds beat gross, the net cell is **blank**, including customs in May and June 2026. Summing CURRENT_MONTH_NET_RCPT_AMT silently drops the refund wave.
- The "Adjustments Attributable to Prior Years" lines are mostly blank (FICA: 87 of 303 net cells filled). That's likely the same negatives-to-blank loss.
- A label typo splits one line in two: "Adjustments Attrbutable to Prior Years-SECA" (101 rows) and "…Attributable…-SECA" (202 rows).

**Next join:** none by key. The natural partner is daily refund timing from the Daily Treasury Statement, if it gets landed.

## 3. Pub 78: clean list, the revocation file lies. **probed**

**Checked**
- 1,435,544 rows = 1,435,544 distinct EINs. All 9 digits, no 000000000 filler.
- **Join to the IRS master file** (EO BMF, loaded 2026-08-10): 1,434,549 land (99.93%). The master file agrees gifts are deductible on every landed row: code 1 on 1,434,165, treaty code 4 on 384. There's no contradiction (S17).
- **Join to the auto-revocation list** (latest posting 2026-08-04): 125,660 Pub 78 EINs were revoked at some point.
  - 19,417 show a latest revocation with **no later reinstatement date**. That looks like "revoked but still listed".
  - **Tested:** 19,407 of them are in the master file, 19,398 with status 01 (exempt).
  - 16,806 have an IRS ruling date after the revocation, which means they re-applied and were approved.
  - The other 2,601 have an old ruling date, but most filed returns for 2024-2025 (tax period 2025: 1,310; 2024: 830).
- Peer by state: nothing story-grade once the re-approvals are removed.

**Hit means (if it had held):** the IRS would be telling donors that revoked groups are deductible.
**Miss means:** it didn't hold. Pub 78 and the master file agree. The revocation file doesn't record re-approvals.
**Boring:** tested, and it wins.

**Traps found**
- ⚠ The auto-revocation file's REINSTATEMENT_DATE misses re-approvals. "Revoked, no reinstatement" is **not** "currently revoked". 16,806 of 19,417 were re-approved.
- ⚠ The FORGN ("foreign") status is wrong on at least 75 rows. 55 say country AFGHANISTAN and 20 say GEORGIA, and all 75 are US charities ("Albuquerque Nm", "Dacula Georgia"). The state field is blank on every one. Afghanistan is the first entry in a country dropdown; Georgia is the US state picked as a country. That's 75 of 871 FORGN rows.
- Two revocation tables hold nearly the same list (AUTO_REVOCATIONS 1,207,295, REVOCATION 1,206,627). I used AUTO_REVOCATIONS because it has typed dates.

## 4. Debt to the Penny: the fast year is the ceiling catch-up. **probed**

**Checked**
- 8,329 business days, 1993-04-01 to 2026-06-15. No duplicate dates, no gap over 5 days. The 334 missing weekdays are holidays.
- **Time, days per trillion:** $37T → $38T took 71 days (2025-08-11 → 2025-10-21). $38T → $39T took 147 days (→ 2026-03-17).
- **12-month growth:** June 2025 → June 2026 +$3.08T. The year before: +$1.54T. The year before that: +$2.64T.
- **Dull explanation, tested:** the debt ceiling froze the total at $36.21-36.22T from February to July 2025. The raise released it: the biggest one-day jump in the file is +$366.4B on 2025-07-07. Averaged over two years (June 2024 → June 2026), it's $2.31T a year, below the 2023-24 pace.
- Record public share: 80.60% on 2026-06-03. Intragovernmental holdings peaked at $7.72T on 2026-05-26.

**Hit/miss:** no hit. The fast year is catch-up after the freeze.

**Traps found**
- The public/intragovernmental split is blank before 2005, except 45 month-end and year-end days from 1997 to 2004. That's the glance's "64% filled". Use TOT_PUB_DEBT_OUT_AMT for the long series.
- 2025-08-04: public + intragovernmental exceeds the total by exactly $10.0B. It's one row.
- Ceiling freezes make long flat stretches. Don't read them as "no borrowing".

## 5. Average interest rates: the known climb, now flat. **probed**

**Checked**
- 4,961 rows, 305 months (2001-01 to 2026-05). No duplicate month/security keys.
- **Time:** total marketable 1.424% (Jan 2022) → 3.386% (May 2026). December values: 3.174 (2023), 3.336 (2024), 3.362 (2025). The last month at or above today's rate was Oct 2025 (3.393%).
- **By security:** bills fell from 4.316% to 3.690% in the last year. Notes (3.248%), bonds (3.413%) and trust-fund securities (3.194%) are at their post-2022 highs in May 2026.

**Hit/miss:** no hit. It's the Treasury's own series, widely charted.

**Traps found**
- The label "TotalMarketable" (no space) appears on 2005-07-31, and 2004-12 has no Total Marketable row. Filter on one spelling and you lose two months.
- Foreign Series reads 0.000 for 37 months (2023-03 to 2026-03), then stops. Zero means nothing outstanding, not a 0% rate.
- The glance's "max 10" is I-bonds (Savings Inflation Securities, 10.251% in 2022). That's a real rate, not an outlier.
- "Inflation-Indexed Bonds/Notes" became "TIPS" in 2004-05, with 14 blanks each on the old names.
