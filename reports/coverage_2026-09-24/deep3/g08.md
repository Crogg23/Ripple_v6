## 1. FDA Drug Master Files: **live**

**What it is:** one row per file a company sends the FDA about how it makes a drug ingredient (Type II) or packaging (Type III).
Filing is legal. A file is not an approval. The FDA only reads it when a drug application points to it.

**Time check (query 5):** Type II files per year
- 2019: 634 · 2020: 662 · 2021: 678 · 2022: 729
- 2023: 917 · 2024: 1,087 · 2025: 1,297 · 2026 (to June 30): 668
- All types: 894 in 2019, 1,836 in 2025. Doubled in six years.

**Peer check: the busiest 4-year window for every ingredient since 1980 (query 24)**

| Ingredient | Best 4-year window | Files |
|---|---|---|
| **Semaglutide** | 2023 to June 2026 | **83** |
| Erythromycin | 1996-99 | 61 |
| Penicillin | 1995-98 | 57 |
| Tetracycline / ampicillin | 1995-98 | 45 each |
| **Tirzepatide** | 2023 to June 2026 | **40** |
| Esomeprazole | 2013-16 | 36 |
| Sitagliptin | 2022-25 | 33 |
| Apixaban | 2015-18 | 29 |

(Generic words like MANUFACTURING and HUMAN were dropped from the ranking. The method takes the first word of the subject, so a subject that doesn't start with the drug name gets missed.)

- Semaglutide: 83 files in 2023-26, 10 in 2019-22 (query 6).
- Tirzepatide: 40 files from 38 companies. The first came 2023-08-30. None before.
- **Hit means:** the GLP-1 ingredient rush is bigger than any generic-drug wave in the list's history. Its nearest modern rival, esomeprazole at 36, is below even tirzepatide.

**Who files (query 15):**
- Tirzepatide: 28 of 38 holder names contain a Chinese city or province, or the word "Chinese". Four more look Chinese by name only.
- Semaglutide: 65 companies filed since 2023. 49 have a Chinese city or province in the name.
- Country comes from the name only. The table has no address.

**The join (queries 25, 29, 31): master-file holders against FDA drug recalls**
- 1,524 recalling firms. 87 (5.7%) match a Type II file holder once punctuation is stripped.
- Second field that has to agree: the recalled product has to contain the file's ingredient.
- **Harbin Jixianglong Biotech Co., Ltd. (Haerbin, China)**
  - Filed semaglutide and tirzepatide ingredient files, both dated 2024-02-28.
  - Opened a Class II recall 2026-02-13: semaglutide ingredient "For Rx compounding use only", 1g to 25g packs, nationwide.
  - Reason, verbatim: "CGMP Deviations This recall has been initiated due to failing to complete process validation and bacterial endotoxin method validation before distribution."
- The GLP-1 recalls around it are US compounders: Thrive Health, Boothwyn, Aequita, GenoGenix, ProRx, New Life Pharma, Payless Compounders. The usual reasons are sterility or subpotency.
- Also in 2026: Sunny Pharmtech (Taiwan) recalled cyclophosphamide, 4 Class I rows, 2026-07-17. It holds a cyclophosphamide file.

**Dull explanation:** the generic clock.
- Tirzepatide was approved in May 2022. Generic applications can start about four years after approval. Semaglutide patents run out in 2026 in several big non-US markets. Compounding boomed while both drugs were on the FDA shortage list.
- Partly tested: tirzepatide's 40 files already beat the best 4-year window of any modern non-GLP-1 blockbuster (36). Generic timing alone doesn't explain the size.
- Not tested: which files actually feed US compounders. Only the Harbin recall ties one to compounding in writing.

**Traps**
- 52 files are dated 1940-01-01, a filler date. 253 files are dated before 1960. One file has no date.
- One row has status PASSIGN.
- No duplicate file numbers.
- The subject is free text, so the first-word match misses some files.

**Next join:** holder names to FDA import alerts or warning letters, if either is landed. Otherwise the FDA Data Dashboard for Harbin Jixianglong.

## 2. CDC NNDSS weekly: **live**

**What it is:** CDC's weekly table of reportable diseases. One row per disease, per area, per week.
It covers 2022 through MMWR week 30 of 2026, despite "2024" in the name.
Each year has exactly one row per area, disease and week (query 10). No double loads.

**Traps (queries 10-12, 23): count these before you add anything up**
- **Area names change case.** 2022-24 is `ALABAMA` and `US RESIDENTS`. 2025-26 is `Alabama` and `U.S. Residents`. Normalize with `replace(upper(REPORTING_AREA),'.','')`.
- **A blank is often a zero.** A blank count with flag `-` means zero cases. `N` means not reportable there. `U` means unavailable.
- **Regions sit next to states.** TOTAL, US RESIDENTS, the nine regions and the territory rows are all in the table. Summing everything counts cases twice.
- **Diseases get renamed.**
  - Hansen's disease becomes Leprosy (Hansen's disease) in 2025.
  - Varicella morbidity becomes Varicella disease.
  - "Hepatitis, B, acute" splits into Confirmed and Probable.
  - Coccidioidomycosis gets relabeled twice.
  - A series that seems to stop is usually a rename.
- Week 53 appears in 2025. That's real: MMWR 2025 ran 53 weeks.

**Lead A: cyclosporiasis, 2026 (queries 21, 28, 32)**
Year-to-date at week 30, US residents:

| 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | **2026** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,171 | 1,804 | 1,222 | 1,491 | **17,197** |

- Weekly counts, weeks 27-30: 874 / 2,275 / 2,163 / 2,393 in 2026. The same weeks of 2025: 65 / 129 / 123 / 158.
- Michigan 7,395 (4-year high for the same week: 28). Ohio 4,408 (high: 89). Together that's 69% of the US.
- **Is it just two states?** No.
  - Without Michigan and Ohio it's still 5,395, which is 3.6x the whole US in 2025.
  - 41 states reported cases. 23 more than doubled their 4-year same-week high.
  - The median state went from 16 to 44.
  - Kansas 610 (high 25), Oklahoma 384 (21), Kentucky 382 (32), Nebraska 211 (25), New Jersey 166 (2), West Virginia 65 (1).
- **Trap inside the lead:** Michigan's year-to-date (7,395) is 5.3x the sum of its weekly counts (1,386). About 6,000 cases came in as back-filled totals. Ohio's two numbers agree (4,408). US: weekly sum 8,510, year-to-date 17,197.
- **Dull explanation:** a lab-testing change (GI panel tests), a backlog dump, or provisional data. Or it's real: a produce-linked outbreak.
  - Even the weekly-count sum is 13x the same weeks of 2025 (8,510 vs 663).
- **Outside check needed:** CDC cyclospora outbreak notices, and the Ohio and Michigan health departments. My knowledge stops June 2026, so I can't say if this was already news.

**Lead B: measles, 2026 (queries 21, 22, 28)**
- US indigenous measles by week 30: 2,027. Same week in 2025: 1,129. In 2024: 133.
- South Carolina 686 (4-year same-week high: 1). Utah 512 (11). Virginia 176 (1). Pennsylvania 175 (11). Florida 137 (10).
- Virginia's weekly counts show months of spread, not one dump: weeks 19-27 read 13, 11, 25, 17, 11, 3, 14, 26, 3.
- South Carolina and Utah are probably known news. Virginia and Pennsylvania may be less covered.

**Probed, not leads**
- Florida chikungunya: 154 (high 3). That's 78% of the US. 20 cases came in week 1 and it tapers after. Looks like travel cases, not local spread.
- Tennessee Candida auris: 293 (high 11), while the US is flat (3,544 vs 3,809). Cases arrive steadily from week 1, which looks like Tennessee starting to report, not an outbreak.
- New Jersey Candida auris: 55 against 0 in every prior year. New Jersey is a known C. auris hotspot, so the 0 means it wasn't reporting here. A zero is not always a zero.
- Guam chlamydia 449 and gonorrhea 196 against 0 in prior years; American Samoa dengue 394 against 0. Could be reporting starting up or real outbreaks. Can't tell from this table.

**Next join:** the state rows to a census population table for per-person rates. Or the counties in CDC_INJURY for overdose context. The key is the state name, normalized.

## 3. CMS Hospital General (Care Compare): **live**

**Peer and denominator check (queries 30, 33).** Only hospitals scored on 7 or more of the 8 death-rate measures count, which leaves the bigger ones:

| Area | Hospitals | Worse than US on ≥1 | Worse on ≥2 | Better on ≥1 | Worse flags / better flags |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **US** | 1,408 | 282 (20.0%) | 77 | 441 (31.3%) | 390 / 903 |
| **Mississippi** | 16 | **10 (62.5%)** | 7 | 1 | 22 / 1 |
| Arkansas | 19 | 9 | 6 | 1 | 17 / 1 |
| Alabama | 25 | 11 | 3 | 1 | 16 / 1 |
| Tennessee | 33 | 13 | 6 | 1 | 21 / 2 |
| Louisiana | 23 | 8 | 2 | 1 | 11 / 2 |
| Missouri (contrast) | 37 | 12 | 5 | 12 | 17 / 19 |

- Mississippi, worse on 2 or more of 8:
  - North Mississippi Medical Center (Tupelo): 4
  - South Central Regional (Laurel): 3
  - Singing River (Pascagoula): 3
  - Magnolia Regional (Corinth): 3
  - Anderson Regional (Meridian): 2
  - St Dominic-Jackson: 2
  - Forrest General (Hattiesburg): 2
- The median big Mississippi hospital has 1 worse flag. The national median is 0. Not 3-4 hospitals carrying it: 10 of 16.
- Worst single hospital nationally: Catawba Valley Medical Center (Hickory NC), worse on 5 of 7.
- **Dull explanation 1:** big hospitals have the numbers to get flagged. Tested: that would push flags both ways. The Deep South gets almost no "better" flags. Missouri's big hospitals split 12 worse, 12 better.
- **Dull explanation 2, not tested:** poorer, sicker patients. CMS death models adjust for clinical risk, not income. This Southern pattern may already be in the research.
- **Hit means:** a region where big hospitals almost never beat the national death rate. **Miss would have meant:** the flags follow hospital size.
- Vintage: the Care Compare release date isn't in the table.

**Join to POS (queries 4, 17, 20, 26): hospitals sold since 2023. Died as a story.**
- Land rate: 3,114 of 3,115 acute hospitals, all 1,378 critical access, all 635 psych.
- Stars compared with same-state peers, leaving the hospital itself out of its peer group:

| Group | Rated | Avg stars | vs state peers | 1-2 stars |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Not sold since 2023 | 2,581 | 3.18 | +0.01 | 28% |
| Sold in a same-day, multi-hospital deal | 38 | 2.71 | −0.49 | 42% |
| Sold alone | 51 | 2.82 | −0.24 | 43% |

- The gap sits in the bundle deals:
  - 2024-10-30 Florida: Coral Gables, Hialeah, North Shore, Palmetto General (3, 3, 1, 2 stars).
  - 2024-10-01 Massachusetts: Morton, Good Samaritan, Saint Anne's (1, 2, 3).
  - 2024-03-27 California: three UCI Health hospitals (2, 1, 2).
  - 2025-03-01 Illinois: seven Chicago-area hospitals.
- **Dull explanation holds:** these are sell-offs out of distressed chains, and the stars cover years before the sale. The finding is about what buyers took on, not what they did.

**Traps**
- **Ownership lags a sale.** "BROWN UNIVERSITY HEALTH MORTON HOSPITAL" still reads Proprietary. So do the three UCI Health hospitals. Current owner type is not safe after a sale.
- A CHOW (change of ownership on Medicare's record) is not always a sale. Lee Health's three hospitals show a 2024-11-01 CHOW and are still labeled government.
- The five "group measure count" columns are constants: 8, 8, 11, 15, 10.
- No star rating for any psychiatric, children's, DoD or rural emergency hospital.
- **FINDINGS.HOSPITAL_CLOSURE_RISK (the join partner) is broken for margins.** Operating margin is negative on 95% of the acute hospitals that land, median −10.1%. FY_END is text (MM/DD/YYYY): MIN returns "01/31/2024" and MAX returns "12/31/2023".

**Next join:** county poverty or uninsured rates to the hospital's county, to test dull explanation 2. The table has COUNTY_PARISH as a name only, so match on name plus state.

## 4. CMS Provider of Services (POS_OTHER): **probed**

**What's in it (query 1).** Only five provider categories:
- 21: 15,324 rows, 100% skeleton records
- 01 hospitals: 13,540 rows
- 12 rural health clinics: 12,864 rows
- 19: 1,982 rows
- 06: 719 rows

No nursing homes, surgery centers, hospices, home health or dialysis. Hospitals are complete (3,114 of 3,115 land).

**Time check (query 2).** Hospital ownership changes by year. POS keeps only the latest CHOW per CCN (Medicare's certification number), so older years undercount.
- 2019: 60 · 2020: 41 · 2021: 29 · 2022: 14 · 2023: 44 · **2024: 70** · 2025: 34 · 2026: 1 (the file ends around March 2026)
- The 2024 spike is driven by a few same-day bundle deals (see the hospital section).

**Enforcement check (queries 19, 34, 35).** PGM_TRMNTN_CD tells why a provider left Medicare.
- Code meanings come from the CMS POS layout **from memory, not checked against the layout doc.** 05 = failed health and safety rules. 06 = failed the provider agreement.
- Hospitals ended with code 05: 169 ever, 22 in 2015-19, 9 since 2020.
- Hospitals ended 2024-25 with code 05:
  - Insight Hospital and Medical Center Trumbull (Warren OH, 346 beds, CHOW 2024-10-30)
  - Insight Rehabilitation Hospital Hillside (Warren OH)
  - Louis A Weiss Memorial (Chicago, 6 CHOWs)
  - East Ohio Regional (Martins Ferry OH, CHOW 2020)
  - Pontiac General (MI)
- **Test: does a history of owner churn predict getting kicked out?** Short-term hospitals only:

| Group | Hospitals | 3+ owner changes | Any owner change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Active | 3,097 | 19.0% | 64.6% |
| Ended 05/06 since 2015 | 22 | 27.3% | 63.6% |
| Ended 01 (merger or closure) since 2015 | 691 | 10.7% | 35.2% |

- **Miss:** 6 of 22 against a 19% baseline is noise at this size. The Ohio and Chicago cluster is a few anecdotes, not a pattern.

**Traps**
- MLT_FAC_ORG_NAME (the chain name) is blank on all 44,429 rows.
- Category 21 is 100% skeleton records: basic fields only.
- About 3,100 hospital-category rows have CHOW_CNT > 0 and no CHOW_DT (5,670 with a count, 2,532 with a date).
- Only the latest CHOW is kept.
- Centro Medico del Noreste (PR) joined Medicare 2025-03-12 and was terminated the next day. It's a paperwork row.

**Next join:** code-05 hospitals to CMS hospital enrollments (INCORPORATION_DATE, legal name) to see whether a new LLC showed up just before termination.
