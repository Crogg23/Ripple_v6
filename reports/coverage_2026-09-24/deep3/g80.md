## 1. XC_WAYBACK_REPLAY_DOJ_DEEP_PAGES: the Epstein file listing shrank, then grew back. **live**

What it is: 2,542 links scraped from 37 Wayback Machine copies of 5 deep pages of DOJ's "Data Set 10 Files" listing (page 1000, 4000, 5500, 9098, 10025).
Each copy holds 50 PDF links plus about 19 menu, share and footer links.

**Checked**
- Pulled every row (S02). Diffed the 50 PDF links per page, copy to copy.
- Read the pager's "Last" link on every copy. That's the listing's page count on that day.
- **One join, twice:**
  - S10: the Wayback replay of every data-set listing page (`INVESTIGATIONS__XC_WAYBACK_REPLAY_DOJ_LISTING`), "Last" page per copy, 416 copies.
  - S11: the Wayback capture index (`OPEN_DATA__XC_WAYBACK_DOJ_EPSTEIN`), 1.5M captures. Someone's crawler grabs every listing page every day, so pages captured per week = pages the listing had.
- Peer: data sets 1-8 and 12 over the same weeks. Time: Dec 2025 to Jun 2026.

**What the numbers say**

| Data set | Before | During the dip | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 10,071 pages (Mar 3-4) | **5,569 pages**, Mar 11 to mid May (S10: 10 copies; S11: 5,569 pages crawled every day, 9 weeks) | 10,081 pages from the week of May 18 |
| 9 | 10,675 pages (Mar 30 to Apr 20) | **770 pages**, about Apr 24 to May 17 (S10: 4 copies; S11: 3 weeks at 770) | 10,675 from the week of May 18 |
| 9, earlier | 10,678 (week of Mar 9) | 4,980 pages, about Mar 14-22 | 10,675 by Mar 30 |
| 11 | 6,634 (week of Mar 30) | 5,549 pages, about Apr 10 to late May | 6,634 by Jun 1 |
| 1, 2, 8 | 62-63, 11, 219-220 pages | no dip | same |

- At 50 links a page, Data Set 10's dip hid about **225,000 file links**; Data Set 9's hid about **495,000**.
- **It was real on the server, not a scraper quirk.** Mar 8, 04:40 UTC: the copy of page 9098 came back showing page 5,569's files (EFTA01889446 to EFTA01889529, pager 5561-5569). DOJ's site had clamped the request to the new last page.
  - Copies of pages 1000, 4000 and 5500 taken one to ten minutes later still said Last = 9098. Those were stale cached copies, which dates the drop to between Mar 4 and Mar 8.
- **What vanished was the tail.** Pages 1000-5500 kept nearly the same files through the dip; their windows moved 0 to 287 EFTA numbers.
  - During the dip, the listing ended at EFTA01889529. After it, page 9098 starts at EFTA02144824 and page 10025 at EFTA02208445.
  - So everything in Data Set 10 numbered above about EFTA01889530 was out of the listing for 9 weeks.
- **No single file was seen being pulled.** Where the same number range shows up in two copies, the lists match exactly (0 dropped, 0 added).

**Hit means:** for weeks, anyone browsing DOJ's listing couldn't reach half of Data Set 10 or 93% of Data Set 9.
**Miss means:** nothing yet. Pages 1000-5500 staying put says the change hit the back end of the numbering, not random files.
**Dull explanation:** DOJ rebuilt its search index, or re-processed files and re-listed them. The files may have stayed reachable by direct link or site search.
**Not tested.** The capture index holds no PDF addresses (S09: 1,505,015 of its 1,537,348 rows are data-set listing pages; the rest are court-record listings). So the warehouse can't say whether the PDFs stayed online.
**Next:** check a handful of PDFs from the missing tail against the Wayback Machine directly, dated inside the dip.

**Glance correction:** "PAGE_DIGEST top 69" is just links per copy. "HREF top /jmd/media/1425556" is the footer's EEO policy link.

## 2. INTL_EC_SERCOP: one-bidder public works in Los Ríos town halls. **live**

What it is: 132,995 Ecuador procurement records (OCDS), RECORD_DATE 2025-01-01 to 2026-06-05.
**82% are catalog purchase orders**, median $120. That's why "top 1% holds 64%" means nothing.

**Checked**
- Pulled every row, 26 working columns (S05). Counted bidders from the parties list: parties tagged "tenderer" per process.
- Scope: 13,222 awarded competitive processes (reverse auctions, tenders, quotes). Only 1 of 13,222 has no bidder list, so the count isn't blank-driven.
- **Expected one-bidder rate** for each process from its type (e.g. works tender 12.1%, reverse auction 16.1%), then from its size (fifths by estimate).
- Peers: same buyer type (town halls), same province, same process type.
- **One join** (S07): 656 OpenSanctions entities tagged Ecuador, on tax ID and on name. 0 ID hits; the name hits are all two-word coincidences.
  - That list holds 11 ID numbers in all, so the miss means nothing.

"Town halls" below means buyers whose name says municipal, municipio or cantón, so it takes in a few town-owned utilities.

**What the numbers say**
- **Los Ríos town halls, public-works tenders (LICO):** 29 of 66 had one bidder (44%), across 11 buyers.
  - Expected from contract size: **6.7**. Town halls elsewhere: 138 of 1,233 (11.2%).
  - Median bidders: **2** in Los Ríos, **5** everywhere else.
  - Money: $13.41M of $28.79M. One award, Quevedo to CONSTRUMOTION S.A. for $6.31M, is almost half of it. Without it: $7.10M of $22.48M.
  - 29 different winners. No ring, no repeat contractor.
  - Montalvo 5, Mocache 5, Babahoyo 4, Quevedo 4. The rest are 1 or 2 each.
- **All competitive buying by Los Ríos town halls:** 72 of 199 one-bidder, **1.9x** the type-predicted rate. Town halls nationally: 1.1x.
  - 11 of 13 Los Ríos town halls with 5+ awards sit above expected. It's not 3 or 4 units.
  - 45.5% of their competitive money went through one-bidder processes. The national town-hall share is 16.2%.
- **The within-province test:** same province, same contractors, different buyers.
  - Los Ríos works tenders by everyone else (provincial government, university, parishes): 4 of 31 (13%), about the national rate.
  - The same town halls' reverse auctions for goods: 16 one-bidder of 93, **14.5 expected**. Normal.
- **Next door, El Empalme (Guayas):** 11 of 17 competitive awards had one bidder, 3.38 expected. **#1 of 102 town halls** with 15+ awards (their median ratio is 0.99). Mocache is #2.
  - $6.80M of its $6.95M went through one-bidder processes. All 4 works tenders and all 4 works quotes: one bidder.
  - CASQUETE CEVALLOS JOSE JAVIER won two of them, $3.53M. CASQUETE CEVALLOS JHONNY ORLANDO won a third, $100K. Same two surnames, different tax IDs. Relationship not checked.
- **Mocache (Los Ríos):** 11 of 18 one-bidder, 3.83 expected. 8 of its 14 priced awards were for **exactly** the estimate, to the cent. Town-hall median: 0%.
- Price: one-bidder town-hall works tenders nationally land at 98.1% of estimate; with competition, 92.8%.

**Hit means:** Los Ríos town halls run public-works tenders that draw one bidder four times as often as their size predicts (29 vs 6.7). That's a red flag for tailored tenders or a closed local market.
**Miss means:** a normal rate means nothing by itself. One-bidder is a screen, not proof.
**Dull explanations, tested:**
- Thin local contractor market: **doesn't fit.** The province's other buyers get normal competition from the same pool.
- Bad bidder data: **doesn't fit.** The same town halls' goods auctions look normal.
- Remoteness: **doesn't fit.** Los Ríos sits next to Guayas, the biggest contractor market. The Amazon provinces (Morona, Zamora, Sucumbíos) run high too, and there remoteness fits.
**Not tested:** tender specs, deadlines, or who sits behind the winning companies.
**Limit:** 2026 records only. 2025 holds only catalog orders and direct consultancy (0 tenders, 0 auctions), and Sep-Dec 2025 is missing, so there's no year-over-year.

**Also checked, nothing there:** bunching under legal thresholds. Services just under the $72,125 line: 71 vs 31 just over. That's a mild step, the usual kind.

## 3. INTL_LEIDEN_RUSSIAN_OPS_EUROPE: a curated list, readable, not rankable. **probed**

**Checked** (S01, all 150 rows, read by hand where it mattered)
- **Only 146 are incidents.** 4 rows are the researchers' notes: "ToDo 8 January 2026", "Re-organize multi-year operations so they don't count double?"
- **January is fake.** 34 January rows; 25 are ranges starting Jan 1. Long campaigns get a fresh row each year: Doppelganger 4 times (2022-25), Portal Kombat/Pravda 3, GPS jamming 3, Amsterdam "peace demonstrations" 3.
  - Single-day incidents by year: 5, 10, 54, 28 (2022-25).
- **The shift, by type:** 2024 had 13 of the 14 arsons. 7 were in Poland (DIY stores, Warsaw mall, depots).
  - 2025 had 1 arson and **12 drone incidents, Sep-Dec**: Danish, Norwegian, German, Belgian bases and airports, Zelensky's jet in Ireland.
  - The researchers chose to log some drone waves as one incident "to avoid flooding the dataset". Counts are editorial.
- Countries (split on ";"): Germany 36, Poland 30, France 29. Germany and France are mostly non-physical (influence, spying): 28 of 36 and 23 of 29. Poland is mostly physical, 17 of 30.

**Hit means:** a good index to cases. Most rows name a specific event you can look up. The table holds no source links.
**Miss means:** nothing. It's a hand list, not a census.
**Dull explanation for "rising 2022-24":** the list started in 2022 and the team logs more as the news grows. Can't be separated here.

## Traps for the ledger

- **SERCOP CONTRACT_VALUE_AMOUNT has unit blow-ups.** 21 rows are 10x to 98,000x their own award. They hold **$5.22B of the $7.26B** column total.
  - The glance's "$4.8B contract" is Mocache's $49,000 award with a contract value of $4,802,000,000.
- **SERCOP 2025 is catalog-only.** 2025 rows are catalog orders and direct consultancy: 0 tenders, 0 auctions, 0 special-regime. Sep-Dec 2025 is absent. Any 2025-vs-2026 comparison is broken.
- **SERCOP RECORD_DATE is the release date,** not the award date. AWARD_DATE is 13% filled.
- **SERCOP IDs carry a tail.** BUYER_ID and SUPPLIER_ID add "-number" (4,561 buyer IDs for 2,838 real tax IDs). Cut to the 13 digits before grouping.
- **SERCOP constants:** LANGUAGE is "es" on every row (the glance's "disparity by LANGUAGE" is empty). TENDER_DATE_PUBLISHED is empty everywhere. Reverse auctions have no TENDER_VALUE. 24 suppliers are named "null".

- **Wayback PAGE_DIGEST changes every copy,** even with identical links. It can't detect a content change.
- **Leiden:** 4 note rows, and January inflated by yearly re-entries.

- **bioRxiv:** two row families stacked; per-family columns look 86% or 14% filled but are 100% or 0% within each family.
