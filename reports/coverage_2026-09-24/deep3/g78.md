## 1. TRANSPORT__FED_FRA_RAIL_DEATHS_BY_RAILROAD: **live**

**What it is:** 5,152 rows. One row = one railroad × one year × one kind of person. 5,152 distinct combos, so no duplicate rows. [q04]
Runs 1975 to **May 2026**. 2026 is a partial year. 522 railroad codes.
**It's a roll-up of FRA_CASUALTIES.** Deaths match that file exactly in all 18 years, 2008-2025, for trespassers and for everyone else. [q04 vs q07]
So every number below can be traced to person-level records.

### The known part
Trespasser deaths nationwide went from 664 (2017) to 838 (2025). That's menu lead F-030. Not new.

### The new part: which railroads carry the rise [q06]
Yearly averages, 2015-2017 vs 2023-2025:

| Railroad | 2015-17 | 2023-25 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **All railroads** | 620.7 | 824.3 | **+203.7** |
| **Union Pacific** | 97.3 | 199.7 | **+102.3** |
| BNSF | 81.0 | 121.7 | +40.7 |
| Brightline | 0.7 | 27.7 | +27.0 |
| Norfolk Southern | 75.0 | 85.7 | +10.7 |
| CSX | 83.7 | 83.3 | -0.3 |
| Amtrak | 137.7 | 129.3 | -8.3 |

- **UP alone is 50% of the national rise.** UP + BNSF + Brightline = 83%.
- 60 railroads went up, 44 went down. Most railroads had zero in both windows.
- So the "national" rise isn't national. It's two western freight railroads and one Florida passenger line.
- UP's share of all trespasser deaths: 15.7% → 24.2%.

### Time: UP year by year [q08]

| Year | 2010 | 2013 | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UP | 83 | 86 | 95 | 105 | 118 | 142 | 144 | 196 | 194 | **217** | 185 | 197 |
| CSX | 94 | 89 | 87 | 83 | 88 | 80 | 80 | 85 | 93 | 86 | 76 | 88 |
| BNSF | 96 | 83 | 80 | 93 | 99 | 110 | 108 | 119 | 105 | 127 | 117 | 121 |

- In 2010 UP killed fewer trespassers than CSX or BNSF.
- In 2023 UP killed 217. That's more than CSX and Norfolk Southern combined (168).
- The climb starts in 2018-2019. It jumps again in 2021.
- **Same months, 2026:** UP had 92 trespasser deaths in January-May. That's the highest January-May since at least 2019 (2025: 85; 2019: 61). [q18] It isn't slowing down.
- UP's other deaths (crossings, workers, passengers) stayed at 37-48 a year from 2017 to 2025. So deaths weren't just moved from one category into another.

### Inside UP: spread wide, not 3 states [q09b]
- **21 of UP's 22 states went up.**
- California is 43% of the rise: 34.7 → 78.3 a year.
- Texas 27.3 → 42.3. Nevada 2.7 → 11.0. Oregon 3.7 → 8.7. Illinois 2.3 → 7.0. Arkansas 3.0 → 7.0.

### Peers: the same states and years, a different railroad [q20, q11]
- There are 20 states where both UP and BNSF report trespasser deaths. In them: **UP 94.0 → 187.3 (+99%). BNSF 76.3 → 114.3 (+50%).**
- UP rose more than BNSF in 13 of the 20. Median change per state: UP +2.2 a year, BNSF +1.2.
- California: UP **+126%**, BNSF +34% (21.7 → 29.0).
- Los Angeles County: UP 3.7 → 12.0. BNSF 1.7 → 1.3. Passenger railroads (Amtrak, Metrolink, Caltrain) 11.0 → 13.0.
- Sacramento County: UP 3.0 → 7.3. Fresno County: UP 1.0 → 6.0, BNSF 3.0 → 5.7.

### What the dead were doing [q10, q12]
The railroad codes this itself. It's **self-reported**, so it adds detail but can't carry the claim alone.

| Yearly avg | UP 2015-17 | UP 2023-25 | CSX 2015-17 | CSX 2023-25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lying on the track | 30.3 | **88.7** | 22.3 | 21.7 |
| Walking | 31.3 | 54.3 | 28.0 | 26.0 |
| Sitting or standing | 13.0 | 30.7 | 13.3 | 17.7 |

- In 2023 UP trains killed **109 people lying on the track**. That's more than all of CSX's trespasser deaths that year (86).
- UP's most common code switched from "Walking" to "Laying".

### The chain
- **Checked:** deaths per railroad per year, matched to the person-level file. Class I peers, same-state peers, same-county peers, and the same months in 2026.
- **A hit means:** something changed at Union Pacific around 2018-2021. Either it didn't happen to BNSF in the same states, or UP changed how it reports.
- **A miss would have been:** UP rising only as fast as BNSF, which would point to a cause shared across the region. Instead it rose twice as fast in the same states.

### Dull explanations
1. **Homelessness and drug deaths in the West.** Partly true. BNSF rose 50% in the same states, while CSX stayed flat in the East. But this doesn't explain why UP rose twice as fast as BNSF in the same states.
2. **More trains, more deaths.** ⚠ **Not testable here: the warehouse has no train-miles.** Outside knowledge, not checked: UP ran fewer, longer trains after late 2018. If so, its deaths per train rose even faster than its deaths.
3. **Suicides classified differently.** ⚠ **Not tested.** This load can't separate suicides: `COVERED_DATA_CODE`, `COVERED_DATA_REASON` and `CASUALTY_OCCURRENCE_CODE` are empty on every fatal row, 2008-2025 [q07]. If UP changed how it classifies suicides, this number would move.
4. **Deaths moved from passenger trains on UP track onto UP.** Tested, and it fails. Passenger railroads didn't drop: Metrolink went 10.3 → 21.7, Caltrain rose, Amtrak was about flat.

**Next pull:** FRA_CASUALTIES has lat/lon, so join it to CDC county overdose deaths, 2019-2024 (mind the COUNT_SUP trap). That tests explanation 1 county by county. Train-miles would have to come from outside the warehouse (FRA operational data, or UP's filings).

### Traps
- **UP writes no narrative.** All 891 UP trespasser deaths in the two windows have a blank narrative [q10]. BNSF went from 225 blank out of 243 to 0 blank out of 365.
- **Codes split, which breaks time series.** Caltrain's code changed from PCMZ (8.0 a year in 2015-17, 0 in 2023-25) to PCJX (first used 2022). CPKC gets a new code in 2023, after the CP-KCS merger. CN's US lines get CNRW in 2025. Name drift: OKKT/OKT, TREX/TRE. If you rank by code, add these together first.
- **A label typo:** "Workeron Duty–Volunteer" is its own category (1 death, 2018).
- **2026 stops at 2026-05-31.**
- **It's a roll-up.** Never add this table to FRA_CASUALTIES; that counts every death twice.

## 2. HISTORY__FED_SLAVEVOYAGES_TRANSATLANTIC: probed

### Which port is which (the codebook isn't in the warehouse) [q14, q17]
These are my inferences from famous voyages. Not verified against the codebook.

- **20101** = Bristol, RI. Its most common owner is James D'Wolf.
- **20404** = Boston. Its first voyage is 1645.
- **21606** = Mobile. The Clotilda (1860) left from there and landed there.
- **21401** = Savannah (the Wanderer, 1859).

### Peers: voyages arriving 1850-1866, grouped by where they sailed from [q16]

| Sailed from | Voyages | Avg captives | Stopped by people (FATE4=3) | Delivered (FATE4=1) |

FATE4 labels come from the SlaveVoyages codebook (outside knowledge). 3 = "original goal thwarted, human agency", which mostly means the ship was captured.
- Death share on the voyage: N. America 15.7% (30 voyages), Caribbean 13.7% (30), Brazil 14.2% (18). Too few voyages to call it a difference.

### The chain
- **Checked:** decade series, voyages after the ban, a same-era peer comparison by departure region, and port labels from famous voyages.
- **A hit means:** American ports got back into the slave trade in the 1850s, 50 years after the ban.
- **A miss would have been:** a flat trickle after 1808. The 1850s count, 72, is more than 5 times the three decades before it combined (13).

### Dull explanation (tested)
**We mostly know the port for ships that got caught.** Capture produced court records that name the port.
- Voyages with a known departure port: 58-86% were stopped by people.
- Voyages with an unknown port: 27%.
- So 138 is a floor, and "the US share of voyages" isn't a clean share.

**Why probed, not live:** the numbers check out, but the story is published. Outside knowledge: John Harris's *The Last Slave Ships* (2020) covers the New York trade after the ban.
It works in a portfolio as a data explainer. It has no join: the owners lived 150-300 years ago, and nothing in the warehouse links to them.

### Traps
- Every number column is text. Cast with `try_to_number`.
- SLAXIMP and SLAMIMP are **editors' estimates**, not counts. 154 voyages show more captives landed than embarked.
- Death share (VYMRTRAT) is filled on only 6,477 of 36,108 voyages (18%).
- Place codes need the SlaveVoyages codebook, which isn't in the warehouse. The first digit gives the broad region (2 = mainland North America; Bristol, Boston, Mobile and Savannah confirm it).
- OWNERA is filled on 21,682 of 36,108 (60%).
- STATUS is always "PUBLISHED" and INTRAAMER is always 0: two constant columns.
- There's no `_INGESTED_AT` column. My first profile query failed on it.
