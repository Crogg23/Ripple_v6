## 1. USAspending top-tier agencies: **live**

**What the table is**
- 111 agencies. FY2026, "through Q3". ACTIVE_FY and ACTIVE_FQ are the same on every row.
- CURRENT_TOTAL_BUDGET_AUTHORITY is one number repeated ($13.40T). The agency rows add to $12.79T (95.5%).
- Budget authority includes money carried in from past years. So "obligated / budget" is not a spend rate.

**Check 1: paid vs committed, peer group** (S04)
- 34 agencies have over $1B budget authority. Their median: paid out 100.5% of what they committed.
- **DHS: 31.6%.** $281.3B committed, $89.0B paid. That leaves a **$192.3B gap**.
- Next lowest: Army Corps civil works 66%, Energy 77%.

**Check 2: same nine months, eight years** (S11, contract data R2)
- DHS contract obligations, Oct-Jun, in $B:

  | FY19 | FY20 | FY21 | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | **FY26** |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 10.40 | 12.76 | 11.53 | 11.98 | 13.91 | 13.46 | 12.42 | **50.97** |

- Peers over the same window: Defense $308.8B vs a $290.4B median (flat). VA $51.8B vs $24.4B (up 2.1x). DHS is the biggest jump.

**Check 3: is it a few awards?** (S14, S16) Yes. Mostly.
- By DHS part, FY2026 vs FY2023-2025 in the same window:
  - Customs and Border Protection: **$31.95B** (was $2.3-3.4B)
  - Coast Guard: $7.69B (was $1.6-1.8B). Ships
  - ICE: $4.97B (was about $1.95B)
  - FEMA: $0.34B (was $1.5-2.3B). Down about 80%
- Top five FY2026 recipients are all border-barrier builders. Together: $24.87B, 49% of the total.
  - Fisher Sand & Gravel Co: **$13.35B**, 14 delivery orders
  - Barnard Construction: $4.54B
  - SLS Federal Services: $2.38B
  - Spencer Construction: $2.36B
  - Southwest Valley Constructors: $2.24B
- Fisher rows, checked one by one:
  - 24 transactions Oct-Jun. No repeated rows.
  - One $440.4M order was reversed a month later. The $13.35B is net of that.
  - Award totals equal the summed obligations. These are obligations, not ceilings.
  - Biggest single: $2.594B, Big Bend sector, Texas, 2026-06-02.
  - By place: Texas $4.59B, Arizona $4.50B, California $2.46B, New Mexico $1.79B.
- Fisher's nine months ($13.35B) is about the size of **all** DHS contracting in the same nine months of any year FY2019-2025 ($10.4-13.9B).

**Second finding, same table: the NSF slowdown** (S06, S13)
- The table: NSF has committed 14% of its budget authority. That's 7th lowest of 34.
- The award data, new NSF grants Oct-Jun:

  | FY19 | FY20 | FY21 | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | **FY26** |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 5,933 | 7,302 | 6,009 | 6,480 | 6,304 | 5,793 | 4,076 | **2,513** |

- That's down 59% from the FY2019-2024 median (6,157).
- **Test for the dull cause, the Oct 1 - Nov 12, 2025 shutdown:** drop October and November. Dec-Jun: 2,467 new grants vs an FY2022-2024 average of 5,763. Still down 57%. New-grant dollars fell 46% ($1.15B vs $2.15B).
- **Is it one division?** No. 31 NSF divisions made 50+ new grants a year in FY2022-2024. The median one fell 64%. 25 fell more than 40%. One rose.

**Hit means:** the committed-vs-paid gap in the top-tier table is border construction money, and one company holds a quarter of it. For NSF, the table's low commit rate matches a real collapse in new grants.

**Miss means:** if the wall orders are later cut back, like the $440M one, the FY2026 number shrinks. It hasn't yet.

**Boring**
- The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025) funded the wall and ICE for several years. Construction gets obligated up front and paid as it's built. So a big committed-not-paid gap is expected. The story is the size and the concentration, not the gap.
- NSF usually makes most of its awards in July-September. FY2026 award data stops July 15 for NSF. A fourth-quarter catch-up is **not ruled out**. July so far: 639 new grants by the 15th.
- Both stories have been in the news. These are checked numbers, not a scoop.

**Traps**
- ⚠ **EPA's row is empty.** Budget $600,491, $0 committed, $0 paid. The award data shows $8.11B of EPA grants and $0.69B of EPA contracts in Oct-Jun FY2026.
- ⚠ GSA shows $0 across the board. GSA contracts Oct-Jun FY2026: $17.46B.
- FCC budget authority is negative (-$9.77B). 13 agencies show under $1M.
- The contract R2 table's FEDERAL_ACTION_OBLIGATION is FLOAT. The column file says TEXT. `TRY_TO_NUMBER` on it fails (that was S07).

## 2. BLS QCEW: **live**

**What the table is**
- 3,619,437 rows, **2022 only**. It's one year of annual averages. No time series inside it.
- 44 aggregation levels: nation, metro areas, states, counties, each by ownership and industry depth. The same national total (150.0M jobs) shows up at many levels. So the glance's "5.4B jobs" is a sum across levels. It means nothing.
- SIZE_CODE has one value. QUARTER is empty.

**The join: QCEW jobs as the denominator for OSHA injury filings** (S08, S12)
- OSHA's form 300A is the yearly injury summary. Most workplaces with 20+ workers in listed industries must file it online. Hospitals are on that list. All workplaces with 250+ must file too.
- I set QCEW private jobs by state and industry against the OSHA 300A worker counts (filing years 2023 and 2024, private, one row per site, 20+ workers, cleaned for typos).
- A state score: filed workers ÷ what the state's industry mix predicts.
  - Median state: 0.98.
  - Lowest in 2023: New Mexico 0.54, Wyoming 0.61, Montana 0.62, Vermont 0.63.
- **Test for the dull cause, small workplaces in rural states:** redo it using only industries that average 50+ workers per site (138 of them).
  - Vermont 0.37 / 0.32 (2023 / 2024). New Mexico 0.50 / 0.61. Hawaii 0.51 / 0.64.
  - Rural states with the same small average site size: New Hampshire 1.03, Idaho 1.14, Maine 1.00, West Virginia 0.99, North Dakota 0.94.
  - So workplace size doesn't explain it.
- **In Vermont, New Mexico and Hawaii, three industries carry 86-97% of the gap. General hospitals are the biggest in every low state.**

**Hospitals, state by state** (S12, S23)

| State | QCEW private hospital jobs (2022) | OSHA 2023 | OSHA 2024 | OSHA 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vermont | 13,453 | 3,174 (24%) | 2,356 (**18%**) | 3,058 (23%) |
| New Mexico | 24,729 | 7,623 (31%) | 9,303 (38%) | 5,929 (24%) |
| Hawaii | 13,926 | 4,638 (33%) | 6,616 (48%) | 6,662 (48%) |
| Median state | | 91% | 94% | not run |

2023 and 2024: private sites with 20+ workers and sane hours. 2025: private sites with 20+ workers; the hours check wasn't pulled.

**Who is missing** (S15, S17, S23): name search in every state, all three years, plus a list of every Vermont filer with 250+ workers.
- **Vermont:** no row for UVM Medical Center (Burlington), Rutland Regional or Central Vermont Medical Center, in any year. The filers are smaller hospitals, 243-1,050 workers each: Brattleboro Memorial, North Country, Brattleboro Retreat, Northeastern Vermont Regional, Gifford, Springfield. Southwestern Vermont Medical Center filed in 2023 and 2025, not 2024.
- **New Mexico:** Presbyterian filed for Española (534) and Clovis (580). No row found for its Albuquerque hospitals. No row found for Christus St. Vincent or San Juan Regional.
- **Hawaii:** one "Queen's Health System" row with 56 workers (2024). Nothing in 2025. Hawaii Pacific Health's hospitals (Kapiolani, Pali Momi, Straub, Wilcox) have no hospital row. Its name appears only on a pharmacy with 22-35 workers.

**Hit means:** the largest hospital employers in three states skip a filing OSHA requires. Their injury rates are invisible in the national data.

**Miss means:** if OSHA's own records show these hospitals filed and the rows were held out of the public file, it's a publication gap. That's smaller, but still a story about the data.

**Boring**
- *Chains filing from headquarters:* tested. These hospitals' names were searched in every state. No hits.
- *State-plan rules:* Vermont, New Mexico and Hawaii run their own OSHA plans. But other hospitals in those same states do file. So the system reaches them. **Not ruled out** that a state plan treats some employers differently.
- *Odd names:* possible. The Vermont 250+ list has no Burlington hospital under any name.
- *Year mismatch:* QCEW is 2022 and OSHA is 2023-2025. That doesn't explain a named hospital with no row at all.

**Traps**
- ⚠ **OSHA repeats a hospital system's total on every clinic.** Erlanger Health (Chattanooga) filed 40 rows in 2024, each claiming 6,743 workers: eye clinic, allergy clinic, primary care offices. That's 269,720 phantom workers (258,760 in 2023). It makes Tennessee look like it files 311% of its hospital jobs.
- ⚠ OSHA 300A 2023 raw worker counts add to 403.1M, three times US private jobs. Typos. Keep 1-50,000 workers and 500-4,500 hours per worker.
- ⚠ **QCEW hidden cells are zeros.** 2,012,990 rows (55.6%) are marked N. 1,980,378 of them still count workplaces but show 0 jobs and $0 wages.
- ⚠ YOY_EMPLOYMENT_PCT_CHANGE is 0 on 2,549,583 rows (70%) and never null. Every hidden row is among those zeros. Zero means "hidden" there.
- AVG_ANNUAL_PAY does equal wages ÷ jobs (median ratio 1.00001). It's fine; small cells drift because the job count is rounded.

## 3. IRS revocations: probed

**Checked** (S02, S18)
- 1,206,627 rows. Dates are text like `2021-05-15`; all parse. WAS_REINSTATED is True exactly where a reinstatement date exists.
- **It's a copy.** Against `ECONOMICS__FED_IRS_AUTO_REVOCATIONS`:
  - 1,187,317 EINs in both. The same last revocation date on 1,187,258 (99.995%).
  - AUTO has 659 EINs this table lacks. 315 were revoked after 2026-03-15; the newest is 2026-05-15.
  - AUTO has 943 reinstatements this table lacks. Never the other way.
  - This table has 49 EINs AUTO dropped.
  - Verdict: a snapshot about two months older. Use AUTO.

**Time** (S19)
- 501(c)(3) revocations by revocation year: 2019 30,642 → 2022 44,594 → 2024 53,091 (+73%). 2025 so far: 47,880.
- The IRS posts most of them in one August batch each year (Aug 2024: 46,617; Aug 2025: 40,494).
- **Not tested:** the dull cause. Groups formed in 2020-2021 that stopped filing would get revoked three years later. That needs a count of charities by year formed.

**Test of the parked "revoked but deductible" list** (S20, S22)
- 1,011,981 EINs have no reinstatement in this file. 30,736 of them are in today's IRS master file.
- Deductibility code 1 (what `REVOKED_BUT_DEDUCTIBLE` uses): 22,435 here, 22,512 in that view.
  - **16,593 of 22,435 (74%) have a master-file ruling date after the revocation.** They were re-recognized. They are not "revoked but deductible".
- The strict leftover: ruling date before the revocation, still on Pub 78, filed a return after. **2,205 EINs, $3.02B revenue.**
  - Top of it: University of Calgary ($1.27B), UMSJ Health System LLC ($606M), University of Minnesota Health Clinics and Surgery Center ($173M), Bon Secours Medical Group Greenville Specialty Care LLC ($156M).
  - These are big, open operators. It reads as missing reinstatement records, not groups fooling donors.

**Hit means:** nothing story-grade. **Miss means:** the table works as a revocation roster once you know it's the older copy.

**Traps**
- ⚠ Duplicate of AUTO_REVOCATIONS, two months stale. Text dates, not DATE.
- ⚠ `REVOKED_BUT_DEDUCTIBLE` is 74% re-recognized groups. That confirms the deep2 parked note.
