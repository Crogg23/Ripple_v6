## 1. FINANCE__FED_SEC_DERA_SUB_2024Q1: **probed**

The SEC's index of filings that carry XBRL financial data, filed Jan-Mar 2024.

**Checked**
- Shape (S07, S08): 6,028 rows, 6,028 distinct ADSH. 5,506 companies. Filed 2024-01-02 to 2024-03-29.
  - ⚠ The glance said one ADSH holds 45 rows. **Wrong.** Every ADSH is on 1 row.
  - Forms: 10-K 3,974. 10-Q 1,044. 20-F 319. S-1/A 125. 40-F 105.
- **Time, same quarter each year:** 10-Ks filed in Q1 were 3,974 (2024), 4,171 (2025) and 4,262 (2026). All nine loaded quarters hold 6,028-7,675 filings. No hole.
- **Late filing, by filer size (S14).**
  - The rule: the 10-K is due 60 days after year end for large accelerated filers, 75 for accelerated, 90 for the rest. The 10-Q is due at 40 or 45 days.
  - I added the 15-day (10-K) or 5-day (10-Q) extension a company gets by filing an NT form, plus 3 days for weekends.
  - 10-Ks filed in Q1 that were late even so:

| Filer size | 2024 Q1 | 2025 Q1 | 2026 Q1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large accelerated | 3 of 1,762 (0.17%) | 3 of 1,730 (0.17%) | 1 of 1,697 (0.06%) |
| Accelerated | 3 of 618 (0.49%) | 0 of 622 | 1 of 595 (0.17%) |
| Non-accelerated | 37 of 1,593 (2.3%) | 24 of 1,819 (1.3%) | 29 of 1,970 (1.5%) |

- Flat. No break in the series.
- **The big-filer late list (S16), all nine quarters:** 152 filings, 81 companies.
  - Q1 2024's 11: Party City (10-K for period 2022-12-31, filed 2024-03-28), Tanger*, Chemours, Cutera, Infinera, Tupperware, Lifecore Biomedical, Mitek, Inspired Entertainment, Mesa Air, Exela.
  - Later quarters include Super Micro Computer, Trimble, B. Riley, Compass Diversified, 3D Systems, Veradigm (10-K for period 2022-12-31, filed 2025-03-18, 808 days later).
  - A company that wants the extension has to file a public NT form, so most delays are public events. The list is a watch tool, not a scoop.
- **Join 1, insider trades (S16, S22).** Did insiders sell between the extended deadline and the late filing?
  - ⚠ The insider trade table effectively **stops in March 2025**: only 10 rows are dated after 2025-04-01.
  - 96 late filings (57 companies) have their whole gap inside that coverage.
  - Open-market sales inside the gap: **3.** Two are date artifacts: Tanger* and AutoZone (its quarter really ended Feb 15, not Jan 31).
  - That leaves Pagaya Technologies: 1 sale, $143,364, during its late 2023 10-K.
  - 44 of the 96 had insider sales in the year before. So insiders go quiet while filings are late. The blackout holds.
- **Join 2, ticker list (S17).** 2024 Q1 10-K filers with a ticker today: 89.2% of large accelerated, 83.0% accelerated, 66.0% of the rest.
- **Shared addresses (S26).** Only 14 street addresses carry 6+ filers. All are fund families: 10 Ridgewood Energy funds (Montvale NJ), 9 Grayscale trusts, 8 Invesco DB funds, Blue Owl, Blackstone, Goldman BDCs. No shell factory.

**Hit means:** a big company blew even the extended deadline. That's a governance flag worth a call.
**Miss means:** nothing. Most delays are announced and covered.
**The dull explanation:** restatements and audit reviews at companies that announced the delay. NOT checked name by name. The one test run, insider selling in the gap, came back empty.

**Traps (for the ledger)**
- ⚠ **PERIOD is rounded to the nearest month end.** The XBRL file name carries the real date. In Q1 2024, 288 of 4,027 10-K/10-Q file names disagree with PERIOD: 266 by 1-16 days (52/53-week years like AutoZone, Albertsons, Domino's), 22 by more.
  - Any deadline math on PERIOD flags on-time filers as late.
- ⚠ **PERIOD can be a year off.** *Tanger's FY2023 10-K says 2022-12-31, and its file name says 2023-12-31. On PERIOD it looks 417 days late, and 8 insider sales ($14.4M) fall "in the gap." Both are artifacts. GPB Automotive and KBS Growth & Income show the same thing.
- ⚠ **Filer size is self-reported.** CIK 1639327 (Gushen Inc, later Zhuoxun Hongtu) tags itself a large accelerated filer.
- The file-name date is only a rough check. Some file names hold a number that isn't a date, like 2003-xx or 2005-xx.
- EIN is `000000000` or blank on 669 of 3,974 10-Ks (16.8%). Known trap.

### Side finding next door: leadership PACs pass on less (probed-grade)
The glance's question, run on `POLITICS__FED_FEC_PAC_SUMMARY` rows with designation D (S12-S25).
"Passed on" = contributions to other committees + independent spending + transfers to affiliates, as a share of all spending.
The two Trump committees are left out: Never Surrender ($471.5M spent, $0 passed on) and Save America ($111.6M, 15.1%).

- **Time, same PACs only** (PACs with $250K+ spending in both cycles):

| Cycles | Leadership PACs | Median share, before → after | Median change | Real independent PACs, median change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 → 2020 | 134 | 51.5% → 52.2% | +1.4 pts | 0.0 pts (62 PACs) |
| 2020 → 2022 | 138 | 52.5% → 46.9% | −5.9 pts | −1.1 pts (68) |
| 2022 → 2024 | 148 | 50.7% → 42.8% | −3.7 pts | 0.0 pts (83) |

- Union and company PACs didn't move either: their median change was 0.0, −0.1 and 0.0 points.
- ⚠ **A peer check changed the story.** My first peer group (S18, S21) mixed in union and company PACs. Their sponsors pay the overhead, so they pass on about 92%. With those removed (S24, using ORG_TP):
  - Leadership PACs funded mostly by individual donors passed on a median 21.7% in 2024. Independent PACs funded the same way: 20.0%.
  - Small-dollar-heavy (half or more unitemized), 2024: leadership 14.5% (18 PACs), independent 11.1% (58 PACs).
  - **So small-dollar leadership PACs are no worse than small-dollar PACs in general.** The "donors' money gets eaten by fundraising" angle is a PAC-wide pattern, and it's well known.
- What is leadership-specific is the **drift inside the same PACs** above.
  - Leadership PACs that aren't funded mostly by individuals hold at 52-54% as a group, every cycle.
  - The group holds flat while the same PACs fall. That points to aging PACs giving less, while new ones start high.
- **Named cases, 2022 → 2024** (data matches, sponsor not in the warehouse):
  - Future First Leadership PAC (IL): 59% (2018), 60% (2020), 20.5% (2022) → **0.0%** of $1.145M. It took $570K in transfers from affiliated committees.
  - Getting Stuff Done PAC (AZ): 40% (2018) → **1.1%** of $2.31M. It took $659K from other PACs and ran its cash from $1.07M down to $7K.
  - Defend America PAC (AL): 47% (2018), 54% (2020) → **0.8%** of $664K. Its cash went from $664K to $0, and its coverage ends 2023-09-30.
  - Country Roads PAC (WV): 43% (2018) → **6.3%** of $1.74M.
  - Hellfire PAC (TX): 61.2% (2022) → **8.3%** of $3.66M, raising $4.22M from individuals.

**Hit means:** a member's PAC stopped funding other candidates and kept spending. That's the "zombie PAC" story.
**Miss means:** the PAC gives through channels this measure can't see (state candidates, in-kind staff), or it's winding down legitimately (charity gifts, final bills).
**The dull explanation, NOT tested:** retiring or departed sponsors winding down. **The warehouse has no leadership-PAC-to-sponsor link.** The table that should carry it carries 22.
**Why not live:** the effect is a few points, and the named cases can't be tied to a sponsor or an office status from the data.
**Next:** the FEC API `sponsor_candidate_ids` for the 1,253 D committees. Then split the same-PAC panel by whether the sponsor is still in office.

## Traps found (ledger candidates)
1. `FED_FEC_LEADERSHIP_PAC` is the 2024 candidate-committee link file. 22 of 8,619 rows are leadership PACs.
2. DERA `PERIOD` is rounded to month end (266 of 4,027 off by 1-16 days in Q1 2024), and sometimes off by a year (Tanger).
3. DERA `AFS` filer size is self-reported: CIK 1639327, a tiny shell, tags itself large accelerated.
4. `FED_SEC_INSIDER_NONDERIV_TRANS` effectively ends March 2025. There are 10 rows after 2025-04-01.
5. The ticker list is an Aug 2026 snapshot. Delisted companies are absent. The glance's top-ticker counts were wrong.
6. OCC thrift `RSSD` is an ID. Summing it means nothing. There's 1 spreadsheet-formula row.
7. BDC `FILING_DATE` years are 00YY. 49 of 212 BDCs are inactive.
8. The PAC summary has no connected/unconnected flag. Type N/Q with designation U mixes union and company PACs with independent PACs. Join the committee dim's ORG_TP before any peer comparison.
