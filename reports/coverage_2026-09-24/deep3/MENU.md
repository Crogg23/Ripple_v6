## How to read it

- **size** = money, harm, people touched. **novelty** 1 = famous story, 5 = nobody has it. **defensible** = survives a hostile data editor.
- **sum** = size + novelty + defensible. Rank is by sum, then defensible, then size, then a skeptic CONFIRMED, then fewer days.
- **days** = solo analyst, from here to publishable, including the one outside check each lead still needs.
- **join now**: cross = already joins two publishers on a real key. same = joins two files from one publisher (CMS to CMS, EOIR to EOIR). no = one table, or the second table is only a denominator or a cross-check.
- **old-menu slot** = where it would land among the 111 rows of the full menu in `../menu.md`. **old #N** = that menu's row N. **jN** = a join dossier. **F-0NN** = a ledger finding.
- **#N gNN** = the lead's number in the deep-3 list and the group file it came from (`gNN.md`).

## Five for the next join pass

Picked for the pitch, "I can join public datasets": size, a real key, two or more publishers, and every core table already landed.
The top two by score are left out on purpose: FAO India (#61) and customs refunds (#55) are one-publisher facts.

### D1. The immigration enforcement surge, contract to cell to courtroom
- **Leads:** #49 habeas flood, #74 (+#72) detentions on old open cases, #75 hold rooms, #58 DHS contracts, #70 DHS ads as color. Old #7 (F-027 detainers) and old #12 (F-025 bond).
- **Chain:** DHS contracts and subawards (USAspending) → ICE detention stints and facility codes → immigration court cases and proceedings (EOIR) → federal habeas and appeals (FJC).
- **Keys:** EOIR case ID; detention ZIP to ICE facility code (92% matched in g69); ICE stay ID; federal district; award key plus UEI. All landed.
- **Why:** the biggest size on the menu. Three agencies' records (DHS, DOJ's immigration courts, Treasury's spending site) plus the federal courts, every piece re-derived by a skeptic. Each piece has had press; the chain, as far as we know, hasn't (unchecked).
- **Could kill it:** the Deportation Data Project and TRAC publish from the same ICE release. Check what they have first.

### D2. The San Fernando Valley post-acute mill
- **Leads:** #9 home health boom, #1 Arizona hospices with LA phones. Old #14 (banned LA home health owners), old #16 (Houston hospice suite).
- **Chain:** home health enrollments → home health owners → federal ban lists (LEIE, SAM) → hospice enrollments → NPPES phones and addresses → PECOS.
- **Keys:** CCN, enrollment ID, NPI, owner ID, street address, phone. All landed. Per-agency Medicare money needs the home health and hospice use-by-provider files: check whether they're landed.
- **Why:** two CMS files already agree on the 35%. The join adds banned owners, shared buildings and phones, and the spread into Arizona. Same method as j5, new place.
- **Could kill it:** LA hospice fraud is well covered, and Friar St may already be in the CA State Auditor's 2022 hospice report. Frame by address and ownership, never by ethnicity.

### D3. Hospitals missing from OSHA's injury file, by name
- **Lead:** #59 (skeptic CONFIRMED). Same OSHA file as old #4 (SpaceX, Ford).
- **Chain:** QCEW state jobs → CMS cost reports (HCRIS) staff counts by CCN, plus the provider-of-services file → OSHA 300A by EIN plus ZIP → IRS master file to confirm the EIN.
- **Keys:** CCN, EIN, ZIP. All landed.
- **Why:** turns "Vermont covers 18%" into a national list: each hospital's paid staff next to the workers it reported to OSHA, including zero. Hospitals are among the most injury-prone workplaces in the country (OSHA's own line, from memory, not checked).
- **Could kill it:** Vermont, New Mexico and Hawaii run their own OSHA plans. If those plans never adopted the electronic filing rule, it's a rule gap, not missing filings. One rule check.

### D4. Sanctioned banks that still hold live global IDs
- **Leads:** #35 FATCA, #56 GLEIF. Old #86 (sanctioned people in UK firms), old #30 (shadow fleet).
- **Chain:** OFAC, EU and UK sanctions lists → IRS FATCA bank list → GLEIF legal-entity IDs and ownership links → UK Companies House.

- **Could kill it:** if the IRS has no duty to drop sanctioned banks, it's two lists overlapping, not a failure. Also check the Bank of Russia's revoked licenses: a dead bank still listed is the dull answer.
