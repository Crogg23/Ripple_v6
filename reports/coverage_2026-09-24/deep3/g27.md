## 1. SEC insider filings, Q1 2026: foreign executives' first reports. **live**

**The physical thing:** a Form 3. It's the first "here is what I own" report a company director or officer files with the SEC.

**Background (checked on the web, not in the warehouse):**
- The Holding Foreign Insiders Accountable Act was signed Dec 18, 2025.
- From **March 18, 2026**, directors and officers of foreign private issuers (FPIs) must file Forms 3, 4 and 5. FPIs are foreign companies with US-registered stock.
- Their initial Form 3s were due March 18.
- On March 5, 2026, the SEC exempted issuers incorporated in **Canada, Chile, the EEA, South Korea, Switzerland and the UK**. The condition: they already report under local rules.
- 10% owners of FPIs stay exempt.

**Shape first** (S02, S05)
- 69,259 filings, all filed 2026-01-02 to 2026-03-31. One row per filing: no duplicate accessions.
  - Form 4: 56,853. Form 3: 10,272. Form 5: 972. Amendments: 1,162.
- **5,031 Form 3s carry the date 18-MAR-2026**, from 646 issuers.
  - 3,993 were filed that day. 1,038 came later.
  - The top issuers are TSMC, Mitsubishi UFJ, Nomura, Vale, Itau, Sony and Takeda. It's the FPI wave.
- ⚠ **This table is an island** (S05): 0 of 69,259 accession numbers appear in INSIDER_SUBMISSION, REPORTINGOWNER, NONDERIV_TRANS or DERIV_TRANS.
  - So: no owner names and no trades for Q1 2026.
  - INSIDER_SUBMISSION stops at 2025-03-31. **April to December 2025 is missing from every insider table.**

**Checked**
- **Universe** (S06, S07): companies that filed a 20-F or 40-F in the DERA filing tables, 2025Q1 to 2026Q1.
  - They also need an NYSE or Nasdaq ticker in the SEC ticker list. That list was loaded 2026-08-10, so every company was still listed months after the deadline.
  - That's 1,064 listed FPIs.
- **Exempt or not:** by country of incorporation. When that's blank (19%), I fell back to the business address.
  - Exempt: 325. **91 of them (28%) filed anyway.**
  - Not exempt: 739. **596 (80.6%) had a Form 3 on file by March 31**, 13 days after the deadline.
- **Peers by country** (not exempt, business address, 5+ firms):

| Business address | Listed FPIs | Form 3 by Mar 31 | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| China | 207 | 147 | **71.0%** |
| Hong Kong | 60 | 43 | **71.7%** |
| Singapore | 58 | 43 | 74.1% |
| Israel | 82 | 76 | 92.7% |
| Japan | 15 | 14 | 93.3% |
| Brazil | 25 | 25 | 100% |
| Mexico | 13 | 13 | 100% |
| Median country | | | 86.2% |

- **Peers by size.** SEC filer status stands in for size. The gap is entirely in the small companies:

| Filer status | China/HK address | Other, not exempt | p (Fisher) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large accelerated | 34 of 34 (100%) | 141 of 152 (92.8%) | 0.22 |
| Accelerated | 26 of 28 (92.9%) | 74 of 79 (93.7%) | 1.0 |
| **Non-accelerated (small)** | **130 of 205 (63.4%)** | **191 of 240 (79.6%)** | **0.0002** |

- **Dull explanations, tested:**
  - *Newly listed companies are slower.* I split small filers by CIK age (CIK ≥ 1,900,000 means registered around 2022 or later).
    - Older: 65.4% vs 86.1%, p = 0.0005.
    - Newer: 61.2% vs 73.6%, p = 0.06. The gap holds.
  - *Misread exemptions.* 44 of the "other" firms have a UK, EEA or Canadian address. DERA codes Shell, IHG and Grifols as incorporated in "US".
    - Dropping those 44 moves the small-firm "other" rate to 80.1% (p = 0.00015).
    - The misreads pull "other" down, so the gap is conservative.
  - *They filed; the table missed it.* EDGAR check on 4 misses:
    - **EHang (EH):** 7 Form 3s on 2026-04-01. It's an accelerated filer.
    - **Sunlands (STG, NYSE):** 4 on 2026-04-01/02.
    - **WiMi Hologram (WIMI):** first 2026 Form 3 on **2026-05-08**.
    - **Jiade (JDZG):** 6 on **2026-06-11**, 12 weeks late.
    - So the table is right. A miss means **late**, not "never filed".
- **The 77 China/HK misses:** 75 are small filers. 75 are on Nasdaq; the other 2 (Sunlands, Eason Technology) are on NYSE.
  - List: `g27/china_hk_no_form3_by_2026-03-31.csv`.
- Among firms that did file, the first Form 3 came after March 18 for 16.3% of China/HK firms (31 of 190). For others it was 11.8% (48 of 406).

**Hit means:** a named list of US-listed companies whose executives missed a new federal disclosure deadline. China/HK small caps are over-represented, measured against same-size peers. The SEC has run Section 16 late-filing sweeps before.
**Miss means:** the table only runs to March 31, so it can't say how late anyone was. That needs April-June filings (not loaded) or EDGAR by hand.

**Boring:** tiny companies with thin compliance staff miss deadlines. The size split shows that's part of it, but not all: same-size non-China peers were 16 points better. Two weeks late is not fraud.

**Other probes (no story)**
- Form 4 lag, filing date vs period date: 10.7% are more than 2 business days late. Holidays aren't adjusted.
  - Median issuer late rate is 2.9% (991 issuers with 20+ Form 4s).
  - 116 Form 4s are over a year late. The oldest is Koppers, period 2002-02-26, filed 2026-03-02.
- The 10b5-1 plan checkbox is set on 8.3% of Form 4s.

**Traps**
- ⚠ `AFF10B5ONE` and `NOT_SUBJECT_SEC16` mix `0/1` with `false/true` in every month. Combine them before counting.
- `PERIOD_OF_REPORT` is text like `18-MAR-2026`, so it sorts wrong. `FILING_DATE` is a real date.
- `NO_SECURITIES_OWNED` is filled only on Form 3s. `FORM3_HOLDINGS_REPORTED` and `FORM4_TRANS_REPORTED` are filled only on Form 5s.
- The CIK is 10-digit padded; DERA CIKs are bare. Match on the number.
- DERA `COUNTRYINC` is blank on 19% of listed FPIs and wrong for some (Shell = "US").

## 2. SEC 13F, spring 2026 window: T. Rowe Price files in thousands. **live**

**The physical thing:** the `value` field on a 13F holding line. Since January 2023 the SEC wants it in **dollars**. Before that it was thousands.

**Shape first** (S08, S09)
- ⚠ **The table is an exact duplicate** of the `01mar2026-31may2026_form13f.zip` window inside `FINANCE__FED_SEC_13F_HOLDINGS`.
  - 9,716 accessions, 3,822,885 lines, $81.54T. Zero line-count mismatches.
  - Union the two tables and you double-count.
- Inside: 8,741 original Q1 2026 reports (3.22M lines), 127 Q1 amendments, and late or amended filings for quarters back to 2022.
- `FILING_DATE` and `PERIODOFREPORT` in the 13F filings table are text like `01-APR-2026`. MIN/MAX on them is wrong.

**Checked**
- **Peer test, same stock:** for every CUSIP with 20+ stock lines (no options), I took the median value per share. Then I flagged lines priced 300x+ below that median (S11).
  - **373 of 9,605 filings** have most lines 1,000x low. They typed thousands.
  - They report $2.6B combined. Times 1,000, they hold **$2.6T**.
  - No filing is flagged on the high side.
- **Who** (data match):
  - T. Rowe Price Associates: 4,507 of 4,510 lines low. Reported $862M.
  - T. Rowe Price Investment Management: $144M reported.
  - Van Eck: $133M. SG Americas Securities: $90M. Natixis Advisors: $72M.
  - Also Bessemer, NY State Teachers' Retirement System, NC State Treasurer, Colorado PERA and Kentucky Teachers.
- ⚠ **Concentration:** T. Rowe Price Associates alone is 33% of the $2.6T. The top 5 are 50%.
  - The median flagged filing is about $484M once corrected, with 100 lines.
- **EDGAR check:** T. Rowe's own cover page (accession 0000080255-26-000381) says `tableValueTotal` = **864,926,710**. The error is in the filing, not the load.

## 3. World Bank International Debt Statistics: Senegal's 2026 wall. **live (known story)**

**The physical thing:** a row per country per debt measure, with a text column per year from 1970 to 2032.
- 2024 is the last actual year.
- **2025-2032 are scheduled payments** on debt owed at the end of 2024. They're not forecasts of what will be paid. Edition "Last Updated: 12/03/2025".

**Shape first** (S01)
- 62,983 rows: 120 countries, 14 group codes (LMY, IDA, SSA and so on), 577 measures.
- ⚠ 5 junk rows: 3 blank rows and 2 footer lines ("Data from database: International Debt Statistics", "Last Updated: 12/03/2025").
- ⚠ **The lender is always "World"** (62,978 rows `WLD`). The triage angle "China vs multilateral lenders" **can't be done** here. Only lender types: bilateral, multilateral, bonds, commercial banks.
- The group rows are sums of the countries. LMY interest $114.5B (2019) and $144.5B (2024) match the country sums. Don't add groups to countries.

**Checked** (debt the government owes or guarantees, "PPG", vs 2024 exports of goods, services and income)
- **Peer:** scheduled 2026 payments as a share of 2024 exports, 108 countries with export data.

| Country | Paid 2024 | Scheduled 2026 | 2026 as % of 2024 exports |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Senegal** | $1.90B (23.2%) | **$4.44B** | **54.0%** |
| Bhutan | $0.16B | $0.49B | 50.2% (nearly all bilateral) |
| Pakistan | $11.22B | $15.60B | 37.8% |
| Comoros | $0.02B | $0.05B | 33.5% |
| Egypt | $24.27B | $21.98B | 32.9% |
| Median of 108 | | | **8.4%** |

- Among the 52 countries with no interest arrears, the median is 7.7%.
- **Time:** across 81 countries paying $200M+ in 2024, the median scheduled 2026 / paid 2024 is 1.18x. Senegal is 2.33x.
- **Senegal by lender** (US$ B, 2022 → 2024 → 2026 scheduled):
  - Commercial banks: 0.08 → 0.43 → **1.52**. That's 18.4% of exports; the median country is 0.56%. #1 of 108.
  - Bonds: 0.21 → 0.40 → 1.31.
  - Multilateral: 0.27 → 0.53 → 0.89.
  - Bilateral: 0.31 → 0.46 → 0.58.
- **Bond principal due 2026-28 vs 2024 reserves** (52 countries with bonds due; median 12.5%): Maldives 89%, Bolivia 51%, Dominican Republic 45%, Montenegro 45%.

**Hit means:** a ranked, sourced "2026 debt wall" chart. Senegal is on top by a wide margin, with a lender-type breakdown.
**Miss means:** n/a.

**Boring:**
1. Senegal's oil and gas exports started in 2024-25. 2025+ exports will be bigger, so the ratio shrinks. That can't be tested; the table has no 2025 exports.
2. Scheduled ≠ paid. Refinancing and restructuring change it.
3. ⚠ **Senegal's hidden-debt crisis is already big news.** The Maldives and Bolivia bond walls are too. The World Bank publishes these ratios itself.

**Traps**
- ⚠ **2030 spike:** China +$135B, Indonesia +$30B and Brazil +$28B in 2030 alone. The all-country total jumps from $361B (2029) to $513B (2030). Unexplained. Don't read it as a real 2030 cliff.
- Countries in default show schedules they aren't paying. Lebanon: $3.56B scheduled for 2026, $0.18B paid in 2024, $9.69B interest arrears.
- 2025 looks like an actual year but is a schedule.
- Every value is text. Group codes sit in `COUNTRY_CODE`.

## New data traps (for the trap file)

1. **SEC insider tables:** April-December 2025 is missing everywhere. Q1 2026 exists only as filing headers (EDGAR_INSIDERS), with no owners and no trades.
2. **EDGAR_INSIDERS flags:** `AFF10B5ONE` and `NOT_SUBJECT_SEC16` mix 0/1 and false/true.
3. **13F_SUBMISSION** is a duplicate of the 13F_HOLDINGS Mar-May 2026 window.
4. **13F dollars era:** 373-666 filings per window still typed thousands, $2.6T-$4.7T missing per window. Includes T. Rowe Price Associates every window.
5. **WB IDS:** lender is always "World". 2025+ are schedules. Footer rows are loaded as data. China, Indonesia and Brazil have an unexplained 2030 spike.
6. **DERA COUNTRYINC** is blank on 19% of listed FPIs and says "US" for Shell, IHG and Grifols.
7. **OSFI names** carry double spaces. The series/class glance counts were wrong.
