## 1. EOIR cases: long-time residents are being detained almost 4x as often. **live**

**What the table is**
- 12,631,225 rows. CASE_ID never repeats within a custody group.
- One case record per person-case: court, custody, nationality, entry date, detention date, city. No judge, no decision.
- It's a snapshot. The latest address change is 2026-06-01. June 2026 has only 10 detention rows, so the data runs through **May 2026**.

**The mechanic**
- A case carries a detention date (DATE_DETAINED) and an entry date (DATE_OF_ENTRY).
- I fixed the group ahead of time: everyone with an entry date on or before 2021-12-31.
  - That's 5,390,212 cases.
  - The group can't grow. New arrivals can't join it.
- Then I counted how many of them had a detention date in January-May, the same five months each year.

**Checked (S07, S08, S11)**

| Jan-May | Detained, entered by 2021 | Entered 20+ years earlier | Of those, case opened before that year |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 3,541 | n/a | 1,259 (36%) |
| 2024 | 3,490 | 651 | 1,247 (36%) |
| 2025 | 8,927 | n/a | 2,768 (31%) |
| 2026 | **13,265** | **2,550** | 4,809 (36%) |

- Per 10,000 cases in the group: 6.5 in 2024, **24.6 in 2026**.
- Every case with a detention date, Jan-May: 56,721 in 2024, 89,076 in 2026.
- Border cases (entered 30 days or less before detention), Jan-May: 14,810 in 2024, 1,840 in 2026.
  - So it isn't the border. It's people who have lived here for years.

**Case number as a clock (S09)**
- CASE_ID goes up steadily over time.
  - Median case number of border cases: 7.64M (2015 Q1), 11.51M (2023 Q1), 15.38M (2026 Q1).
- I used that to tell "already had a court case" from "case opened at detention".
  - Cutoff: roughly the first case number issued each January.
- About 36% of the fixed group already had a case, and that held steady every year.
  - So about 64% got a brand-new case when they were detained.
  - This is not just people picked up at courthouse hearings.

**Peer comparison, by nationality (S11)**
- 16 nationalities had 30 or more detentions in Jan-May 2024.
  - **Median rise: 3.82x.** Lowest: Dominican Republic, 124 → 225 (1.81x).
- Not a few countries carrying it:
  - Without Mexico: 2,334 → 9,078 (3.89x).
  - Without Mexico and Venezuela: 3.63x.
- **Venezuela is the outlier.** Of 128,445 Venezuelan cases in the group:
  - Jan-May detentions: 56 in 2023, 49 in 2024, 160 in 2025, **777 in 2026 (15.9x vs 2024)**.
  - That's 60.5 per 10,000 cases, against 24.6 for everyone.
  - Tested inside the group: flat for two years, then the jump comes in 2026.
  - Possible reason, **not tested**: TPS ended for Venezuelans in 2025.

**Second source: ICE's detention records (S13)**
- IMMIGRATION__FED_ICE_DETENTION_STINTS, counting distinct stays by book-in month.
  - Only an aggregate, not a row join. The two tables share no person key.
- ICE data ends mid-March 2026, so this compares January-February only.

| Jan-Feb | ICE stays, entered by 2021 | Entered before 2006 | No criminal record ("Other Immigration Violator") | Convicted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 3,083 | 626 | 260 | 2,200 |
| 2026 | **11,567 (3.75x)** | 2,333 (3.7x) | **4,439 (17x)** | 3,783 (1.7x) |

- In this group, the no-record share went from 8.4% to 38.4%.
- Two separate agencies' records land on the same multiple, 3.8x and 3.75x.

**The dull explanations, tested**
- *"The border closed, so the mix just shifted."* ➔ No. The counts are absolute, in a group that can't grow.
- *"EOIR started filling in the detention date."* ➔ No (S12). DATE_DETAINED is filled on 97.5-99.8% of detained or released cases in every case-number band since 9.0M.
- *"The entry date got filled more often."* ➔ It went the other way. Entry-date fill on detained cases fell from 64% to 29%.
  - ICE's fell from 82.6% to 41.8%.
  - **So every count above is a floor.**
- *"May 2026 is incomplete."* ➔ Maybe a little: monthly totals slip from 20.7K in January to 15.7K in May. That can only make 2026 look smaller.

**What a hit means:** interior detention of people who have lived here for years has roughly quadrupled. Court records and ICE records agree. In ICE's records, people with no conviction and no pending charge are 38% of this group in 2026 and about half the rise: 4,179 of the extra 8,484.
**What a miss would have meant:** a flat fixed group would have pinned the rise on new arrivals.
**Not checked:** what happened to these people afterward. No decisions were loaded.

**Traps**
- 100 rows are misparsed lines (FIELD_COUNT ≠ 39). Language codes like SP and ENG landed in CUSTODY.
- 130,511 rows have a null CUSTODY.
- 2,836 "never detained" cases carry a detention date.
- DATE_DETAINED has sentinel dates: 1900-01-01, 2100-06-11, plus 33 dates in the future.
- One entry date is in the year 2209. 55 are before 1940.
- IS_LPR is null on 11,623,221 rows (92%). Only 7,076 say true. Can't carry an LPR claim.
- ALIEN_ZIPCODE is '00000' on 214,887 rows.

## 2. DOL OFLC: contractors write H-1B workers down a level and pay the floor. **live**, ⚠ stale

⚠ **This is the FY2019 file only.** Decisions run 2018-10-01 to 2019-09-30. There's no second year to compare against. DOL publishes FY2020-FY2026. Load those before this goes anywhere.

**What the table is**
- 664,616 labor condition applications (LCAs). One per CASE_NUMBER.
- 578,640 certified H-1B LCAs, covering 966,182 positions.
- 211,838 of them say the worker will be placed at another company (SECONDARY_ENTITY_1 = 'Y'), and they name that company.

**The mechanic (S05, extract of 41 columns; local math in `clients.py`, `clients2.py`)**
- I took the client company a contractor names (like "Apple Inc.").
- Matched it to that company's own H-1B filings.
  - Name, plus two more fields that must agree: the same job code (SOC), and the same worksite state.
  - I kept only computer jobs (15-11xx, 15-12xx).
  - Each job-and-state cell needs at least 10 filings on each side.
- Then I compared the offered wage, turned into a yearly figure, filings between $20K and $1M.
- 45 client companies qualify.

**Checked**
- **Median client: contractor-placed H-1B workers offered 20.6% less.** Weighted: 19.7% less.
- 42 of 45 clients show a gap. 36 of 45 show a gap over 10%.
- The companies were picked by how many placements they get, not by how big the gap is.

| Client (data match) | Contractor filings | Contractors' offer | Client's own offer | Gap | Level I/II, contractors vs client |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apple | 2,780 | $98,967 | $140,214 | −29.4% | 85% vs 18% |
| Verizon | 1,815 | $90,433 | $119,927 | −24.6% | 85% vs 11% |
| American Express | 1,545 | $79,097 | $114,923 | −31.2% | 81% vs 14% |
| Microsoft | 440 | $92,592 | $136,508 | −32.2% | 83% vs 63% |
| AT&T | 670 | $88,941 | $126,044 | −29.4% | 87% vs 17% |
| Capital One | 1,028 | $86,578 | $92,487 | −6.4% | 86% vs 85% |

- Offers are medians, weighted by contractor filings across matched job-and-state cells. Full list: `g68/client_gap.csv`.
- **Up close, Apple software developers in California:**
  - Contractors: 673 filings, 621 of them at Sunnyvale. Median offer **$109,242**, exactly the Level II prevailing wage there. 93% filed at Level I/II.
  - Apple's own: 126 filings. Median **$160,000**. 10% at Level I/II.
  - Top contractors: Larsen & Toubro Infotech 75, Quest Global 60, Infogain 33.
- **Same level** (client + job + state + wage level, 5 or more filings per side):
  - 273 cells, 45 clients.
  - Weighted gap **−6.6%**. Median cell −4.6%. Contractors lower in 60% of cells, and at 31 of 45 clients.
  - Level II alone, which is 11,671 contractor filings: −7.3%.
  - **So about two-thirds of the gap is the level the contractor picks.** About a third is lower pay within the same level.
- **At the floor:** 52.2% of contractor-placed computer filings offer exactly the prevailing wage. For the clients' own filings it's 28.4%.

**The dull explanation:** contractor workers may just be more junior.
- An LCA can't show experience, so this is **not tested**.
- The wage level is the employer's own pick, a self-coded field. The fair claim is "filed at lower levels and offered the floor", not "underpaid for the same work".

**A miss that means something:** 151 LCAs offered less than the prevailing wage they listed themselves (same pay unit).
- 135 were denied, 16 withdrawn, **0 certified**. DOL's screen catches it. No story there.

**Traps**
- WILLFUL_VIOLATOR is a box the employer ticks: 280 'Y' across 191 employers.
  - Georgia Institute of Technology ticks it on 3 filings.
  - It can't carry a claim.
  - West Coast Consulting LLC (CA) has 36 'Y' filings, 32 certified. That's a lead to check against DOL's actual findings, not a finding.
- WILLFUL_VIOLATOR is typed FLOAT in the TIMELINE schema copy. A Y/N field can't survive that. **Not checked.**
- The 15,079 blank WILLFUL_VIOLATOR rows are E-3 and H-1B1 visas (15,057 of them) plus 22 H-1B rows.
- CASE_SUBMITTED goes back to 2012: 25,361 filings were submitted before July 2018.
- PREVAILING_WAGE_1 is blank on 33,247 rows. 25,519 of those are certified-withdrawn.
- Client names come in 24-84 spellings per company ("AT&T", "AT&T SERVICES INC", ...).
  - Naive prefix keys pull in Apple Tree Dental and Cisco College. They were dropped.
- No employer ID in this year's file (FEIN arrives in FY2020). Any join to another table is name-only.

## 3. DHS OHSS: the loader kept the totals and dropped the breakdowns. **probed**

**What the table is**
- 50,740 rows from 13 monthly spreadsheet releases.
  - Data runs September 2023 through November 2024. The last file came out 2025-01-16.
  - ⚠ **Nothing from 2025.**
- 52 sheet names.

**Checked (S04, whole table; `ohss.py`)**
- **37,665 rows (74%) hold no value in any data column.** Only 10,311 rows (20%) carry a number.
- 14 columns are empty on every row: REPORT_MONTH, TABLE_NAME, ENCOUNTER_TYPE, REGION_OR_SECTOR, CITIZENSHIP, FAMILY_STATUS, CRIMINALITY, INITIAL_ARRESTING_AGENCY, BOOK_OUT_OUTCOME, REPATRIATION_TYPE, CREDIBLE_FEAR_RESULT, CHNV_PROCESS_TYPE, CALENDAR_YEAR, EVENT_COUNT.
- The suggested angles need those labels: arrests by agency, by citizenship, by family status. **None of them can be done.**
- What the loader kept:
  - The "Total" column of each wide sheet.
  - BORDER/INTERIOR on the arrest-location sheets.
  - 5 country columns on the CHNV parole and CBP One sheets.
- Fiscal year sits only on each year's "Total" row, so months need a forward fill.
- Revisions: 977 month cells show up in two or more releases, and 307 of them changed.
  - Biggest change: ICE removals for July 2024, 22,940 → 24,990 (+8.9%). Recent months get revised up.

**Time (latest release, "ICE ERO R&R by Arrest Loc")**

| FY | ICE removals and returns | From arrests inside the country | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 315,940 | 100,970 | 32.0% |
| 2018 | 256,090 | 95,360 | 37.2% |
| 2019 | 267,260 | 85,960 | 32.2% |
| 2023 | 142,580 | 44,260 | 31.0% |
| 2024 | **271,480** | **47,730** | **17.6%** |

- FY2024 was the busiest year since FY2014, and it was mostly border cases.
- This is DHS's own published number, already reported. Nothing new, and it stops before 2025.

**Traps**
- Sheets get renamed between releases: "Arrest Agcy" → "Arrest Loc", "CF Completed by Result" → "Nationwide CF by Result".
  - There are also two different "CHNV Paroles" sheet names.
- FISCAL_YEAR holds footnote text on 500+ rows ("Notes: ...", "X Not applicable.").
- 'X' is a placeholder for a suppressed cell, not a number.
