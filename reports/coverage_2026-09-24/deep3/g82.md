## 1. REGULATORY__FED_FEDERAL_REGISTER_DOCUMENTS: **live**

**What it is:** 485,585 documents from Jan 4, 2010 to Sep 4, 2026. That's 485,585 distinct `DOCUMENT_NUMBER`s, so there are no duplicate loads.
Yearly counts stay flat at 27K-33K through 2024, then 23.7K in 2025. The table is complete enough for a miss to mean something.

### Lead: FEMA stopped putting disaster declarations in the Federal Register as they happen [q19, q22, q23, q27, q20]

**What was checked:** FEMA notices from February through August each year, sorted by title.

| Feb-Aug | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | **2025** | 2026 |

- The flood-map notices kept coming, so FEMA was still publishing other notices.
- **The join:** FEMA disaster numbers from `HOUSING__FED_FEMA_IA_HOUSING_REGISTRATIONS` matched to Federal Register docket tags like `FEMA-4860-DR`.

- **Publication days:** FEMA published declaration notices on only 3 days between Jan 2025 and Aug 2026:
  - Aug 19, 2025
  - one day in Sep 2025, carrying 67 notices
  - May 6, 2026, carrying 64 notices

  In Jul-Dec 2024 it published on about 8 days.
- **The peer:** SBA published its own disaster-loan declarations in 19 of those 20 months (5-33 a month).
- **Longest wait:** Kentucky DR-4860 was declared Feb 24, 2025. Its first Federal Register notice came May 6, 2026, **436 days** later.
- DR-4856 (California, declared Jan 8, 2025) has no notice tagged with its number at all. The omnibus notice may cover it, but that notice's docket carries no DR number.
- **The dull explanation, found and tested:** the Aug 19, 2025 and May 6, 2026 notices are omnibus notices. Each covers months of past declarations: Oct 2024-Jan 2025, Jan-May 2025, and Apr-Oct 2025. So FEMA switched to publishing in batches. It didn't stop declaring disasters: the IA table shows 16 IA disasters declared Feb-Aug 2025.
- **Hit means:** the government's legal notice of which counties got which aid now runs months behind. As of Sep 4, 2026, the latest omnibus notice stops at October 2025. Only 2 of 12 IA disasters declared Jan-Jun 2026 have any notice.
- **Miss means:** if FEMA posts all of this on fema.gov right away, the harm is to the record, not to aid. That's still a transparency story, and the lag numbers are checked.

## 2. LABOR__FED_MSHA_MINES: **live** (joined to MSHA violations)

**What it is:** 91,906 mines = 91,906 `MINE_ID`s. Status counts: 69,237 Abandoned, 8,854 Abandoned and Sealed, 6,631 Active, 5,704 Intermittent, 748 Temporarily Idled, 376 NonProducing, 356 New Mine.

### Lead: operator churn goes with unpaid fines [q21, q24]

**What was checked:** every violation since 2010, grouped by mine and by violator ID. Only IDs in the same format as operator IDs were kept, which drops contractors.
- "Unpaid" = amount due minus amount paid, on violations dated before 2024, so there has been time to pay.
- "Working mine" = status Active, Intermittent, Temporarily Idled or NonProducing.

| Working coal mines | Mines | Due | Paid | **Unpaid** | Median mine unpaid | Paid under half |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 operator since 2010 | 777 | $123.5M | $119.0M | **3.7%** | 0% | 49 (6%) |
| 2 operators | 235 | $52.8M | $40.6M | 23.1% | 0.4% | 40 (17%) |
| **3+ operators** | **120** | $29.1M | $20.8M | **28.7%** | **13.4%** | 34 (28%) |

- Metal and nonmetal mines show the same slope, but weaker: 1.4% → 7.5% → 14.3%.
- The effect doesn't rest on 3-4 mines: the *median* churn mine has 13.4% unpaid. For single-operator mines the median is 0.
- The median working coal mine had 1 operator. 120 of 1,132 had 3+, and 45 had 4+.

**The top mine:** MINE_ID 1518001, "Mine #1", Kentucky. It's Temporarily Idled now, and the current controller is Gary D Mitchell.
- 11 operator IDs since 2010. The chain starts J 1 Mining (2010) > BEC Coal > Somer Grace Coal > Mercy Mining > Deep Woods Mining. It ends > Prosper Energy (2018-22) > Maverick Coal (2026).
- **$580,971 due. $28,294 paid (5%). 1,492 violations.**
- Most runs are short: 5 of the 10 earlier operators show violations in one calendar year only.

**Other mines in the same pattern:**
- Mine #3 (1518973, KY): Little Tackett Mining, Lester And Sons, and Dude Branch III each left their fines at $0 paid ($39K, $46K, $71K).
- OSAKA (4407150, VA): about $1.47M unpaid. Almost all of it is Blackjewel L.L.C., 2015-19.

**By controller:** Sev.en Global Investments' 47 working mines carry $7.39M left unpaid by earlier operators. That's 42% of the $17.6M coal total.

**The dull explanation:** bankruptcy. Blackjewel is the clear case. Fines against a bankrupt operator often go uncollected, and bankruptcies cause operator changes. **Not tested here**, because there's no bankruptcy table in this pass. That's the next check.
**Hit means:** a mine that keeps changing hands keeps running up fines no one pays. **Miss means:** if most of the unpaid money sits with bankrupt operators, the story is bankruptcy law, not dodging.

### Probed, not leads [q06, q07]

  - ACNR Holdings: 17 of 24, 14 for 3+ years.

NonProducing covers mines under construction and development (Resolution Copper, Hermosa), so the dull explanation holds. At abandoned mines, 50 of 23,094 had later accidents.

**Traps:**

- The old quoted-ID trap does **not** apply here: 0 quoted `MINE_ID`s.

## 4. MONEY__DEBT_REPAYMENT_CLIFF: probed

**What it is:** 938 rows = 134 codes × 7 years, **2026-2032 only**. Every row is a projection.
The 2026 values match World Bank IDS `DT.TDS.DECT.CD` on all 134 rows. The projections cover debt already owed, not new borrowing, so the total falls by design: $1,074B in 2026 → $420B in 2032. 79 of 134 rows peak in 2026, the first year.

### Best number: Senegal [q14, q18, q25]
Public external debt payments (PPG) at their 2026-28 peak, as a share of 2024 exports. The PPG view removes private project finance.

| | Peak 2026-28 | % of 2024 exports | 2024 actual % | vs own 2019-24 max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Senegal** | $4.44B | **54.0%** | 23.2% | **2.33x** |
| Bhutan | $0.49B | 50.2% | 16.2% | 3.1x |
| Pakistan | $15.6B | 37.8% | 27.2% | 1.13x |
| Median of 113 countries | | 9.7% | 6.5% | 1.2x |

- **Why probed, not live:** Senegal's debt crisis is already front-page news. Bhutan's jump is hydropower loans. This table adds a checked ratio, not a new story.
- **The dull explanation, found:** the top *total* cases are private project debt, not governments.
  - Mozambique owes $13.87B in 2026 (142% of exports), and $12.97B of that is private non-guaranteed (LNG).
  - Mongolia: $6.0B of $8.0B is private non-guaranteed.

**Traps:**
- `IS_AGGREGATE` is false for "Least developed countries" (LDC). Any all-country sum double-counts about 7%: $73.4B of $1,074B in 2026.
- The table starts at 2026, so the real cliff year is invisible. Senegal went from $3.44B (2024 actual) to $7.87B (2025, +129%). Mozambique went from $4.54B to $19.17B.
  - `IS_REPAYMENT_CLIFF` only measures jumps inside the projection. Neither country's 2025 jump shows as a row or a cliff flag.
- `TOTAL_DEBT_SERVICE_USD` ≠ principal + interest on 563 of 938 rows. The total includes IMF charges; the Gabon 2026 gap is 17%.
- `FINANCE__INTL_WB_IDS.COUNTRY_CODE` is space-padded (`'MOZ        '`). A join on `= 'MOZ'` returns nothing. TRIM it.

## 5. LAND_AND_TERRITORY__FED_BIA_TRIBAL_GEO: probed (map layer)

**What it is:** 335 tribal Land Area Representations, with 335 distinct `LAR_ID`s.
- `GEOMETRY_JSON` is Esri JSON (`{"rings": ...}`), not GeoJSON. `TRY_TO_GEOGRAPHY` fails on **all 335** as stored.
- **Fix that works:** flatten the rings and parse each as its own polygon. 4,928 of 4,931 rings parse. A point is inside if an odd number of rings contain it.

### The join: MSHA mine points inside tribal land [q08b]
- **252** mines fall inside: 114 abandoned, 76 intermittent, 50 active (in 30 land areas), 9 idled, 2 sealed, 1 nonproducing.
- Biggest active ones:
  - Navajo Mine (Bisti Fuels, coal, 357 employees, NM)
  - Santan Pit (Gila River Sand & Gravel, 83)
  - Bonanza Mines (American Gilsonite, 70, Uintah and Ouray)
- **0 uranium mines** by SIC code. That miss means nothing: 48% of MSHA mines have no coordinates, and the old uranium mines are the likeliest to lack them.

**Traps:**
- `GIS_ACRES` is inflated about 1.9x at 43°N. The ratio tracks the Web Mercator stretch (1/cos² of latitude), so that projection is the likely cause. Pine Ridge's single ring measures 2.78M acres on the globe; `GIS_ACRES` says 5.27M.
  - The inflation is about 1.5x in Arizona and 1.9-2x in the Dakotas. Don't sum or rank acreage across latitudes.
- 3 rings fail to parse, including Cheyenne River's main outline (its biggest parsed ring is 1,427 acres). Mines inside it are missed.
- `SHAPE_AREA` is in square degrees. It's useless as area.
