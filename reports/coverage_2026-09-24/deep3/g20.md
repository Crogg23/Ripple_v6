## 1. POLITICS__FJC_APPOINTMENT: live

**What it is.** Every federal judicial appointment from the Federal Judicial Center, 4,766 rows, 1789 to 2026-06-24.
One row = one judge's seat. `AYES_NAYS` holds the Senate tally as text, like "53/43".

**The finding.**
- Trump's second term (nominated on or after 2025-01-20, confirmed through 2026-06-24): **46 Article III judges.**
  - **34 of 46 (74%)** got zero votes from Democrats or independents.
  - All **7 of 7** appeals judges got zero.
  - The median judge got **0** crossover votes. The most any judge got was 14 (Harold Mooty III, N.D. Alabama, 66/32).
- Biden, 118th Congress (2023-24), same test in reverse: **32 of 130 (25%)** got zero Republican votes. The median judge got **3**.
  - Appeals: 7 of 17 got zero.
- Own-party defections are rare both ways: 5 GOP no votes across all 46 Trump judges, and 22 Democratic or independent no votes across 130 Biden judges.

**The long trend (Article III judges, recorded votes only).**

| President | Judges | Share with a roll-call vote | Median no votes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clinton | 378 | 13% | 2.5 |
| G.W. Bush | 326 | 59% | 0 |
| Obama | 326 | 69% | 0 |
| Trump, 1st term | 234 | 86% | 30 |
| Biden | 233 | 97% | 43 |
| Trump, 2nd term | 46 | 100% | 45.5 |

- Voice-vote confirmations are gone. Every Trump second-term judge got a roll call.
- Same point in each term (day 520, i.e. June 24 of year two): Trump 2nd 46 judges, Trump 1st 43, Biden 69, Obama 35, G.W. Bush 58, Clinton 75.

**Checks run.**
- Time: every president since Clinton, and the same point in each term.
- Join: FJC tallies against Voteview's Senate "On the Nomination" votes on the same day. **938 of 947 (99%)** recorded confirmations since 1989 match to the exact yea and nay count. 98% of those matches name the judge's surname in the vote text.
- Join 2 (statement 10): Voteview member votes and party codes for the 118th-119th Senate. All 176 joined judges (130 Biden, 46 Trump) matched. The party sums equal the official tally on **all 176**.
- Eyeballed 10 rows. For example, Emil Bove (3rd Cir., 2025-07-29): 50/49, 0 Democrats yes, 2 Republicans no. Anthony Mattivi (D. Kan., 2026-06-09): 51/46, 0 Democrats yes.

**The dull explanations.**
- Senate Democrats opposing Trump nominees as a bloc *is* the story, not an artifact of the data.
- Blue slips (tested, statement 20). All 39 of Trump's second-term district judges sit in states with two Republican senators, so no Democrat has a home-state stake. The fair comparison is Biden's district judges in states with two Democratic or independent senators, where no Republican had a stake:
  - Biden: 88 judges, 23 with zero Republican votes (26%), median **3**.
  - Trump 2nd term: 39 judges, 27 with zero Democratic votes (69%), median **0**.
  - Blue slips don't close the gap.
- Voteview adds the President's position as an extra "yea" on 143 of these votes. It isn't a senator, so it's excluded. After excluding it, the sums match the official tallies exactly.
- Topic risk: partisan judicial confirmations are widely covered. The 34-of-46 vs 32-of-130 comparison is freshly computed. Whether the press already has this exact number was not checked.
- Speed caveat: Trump's second-term median of 76.5 days from nomination to confirmation (Biden 98, Trump 1st 136) is **biased fast**. The table only holds confirmed judges, so slow nominees still pending don't count. The zero-crossover count isn't affected by this.

**Hit means.** Confirmation votes on Trump's second-term judges run close to pure party line, and far more often than Biden's did in the Senate right before.
**Miss would have meant.** The nay counts were a few Democrats crossing over per vote, like the 2023-24 pattern. They aren't.

**Traps.**
- ⚠ **This table is a 16-column copy of `POLITICS__FED_FJC_SERVICE`.** They match on every count checked: 4,766 rows, 4,067 NIDs, the same max dates, 46 Trump 2nd-term judges, 1,040 recorded votes. FED_FJC_SERVICE has more columns, including `SENATE_VOTE_TYPE` (Roll Call / Voice / Unknown).
- `CONFIRMATION_DT` is empty on all 4,766 rows. Use `CONFIRMATION_DATE` (text, parses cleanly).
- `AYES_NAYS` counts the Vice President's tie-breaker (51/50). Voteview doesn't. Three 1800s rows have a double slash, like "10//14".
- Accented names are garbled ("M�ndez-Mir�"), so surname matching misses them.
- 139 "None (reassignment)" and 39 null-party rows are transfers, not appointments.
- `_INGESTED_AT` is one epoch-micros constant on every row.

## 2. POLITICS__FED_VOTEVIEW_ROLLCALL_META: live

**What it is.** Every House and Senate roll call from Voteview, 113,512 rows, 1789-05-16 to 2026-08-08. Congress + chamber + roll number is unique on every row.

**The finding: the Senate votes more, the House votes less.**
- **Senate, 2025: 659 roll calls.** The only year with more was 1976 (700).
  - **356 (54%) were on nominations** (PN numbers). That's the most in any year since the data starts tagging nominations in 1989. The prior high was 320 (2019).
  - At the same point in each Congress (August 8 of year two), the 119th Senate had 890 roll calls, the most since the 95th (1977-78, 936).
- **House, 2025: 361 roll calls**, the fewest in any odd year since 1981 (353).
  - At the same point in each Congress, the 119th House had 643, against 1,118 in the 118th. That's the lowest since the 97th (592) in the electronic-voting era.
  - The House took zero roll calls in August 2025 and zero in October 2025.
- **The Senate changed its rules mid-stream, and you can see it in the data.**
  - Roll call 515, 2025-09-11: "Decision of Chair Not Sustained," 45-53, on S.Res. 377. That's an executive resolution for **en bloc** (batch) nominations.
  - 5 en bloc resolutions have been adopted (S.Res. 377, 412, 532, 690, 817). A sixth (S.Res. 520) failed cloture.
  - Nomination roll calls fell from **37.3 a month (Jan-Jul 2025) to 12.6 a month (Jan-Jul 2026).**
- **Party-line votes tripled** (join to member votes, statement 10):
  - 119th Senate: **41.5%** of roll calls had zero crossover votes. The 118th had **13.5%**.
  - On nominations alone: 51% vs 14%.
  - Party-unity votes (most of one party against most of the other): 93.1% vs 77.9%.

**Checks run.**
- Time: calendar years back to 1789. Same day-of-Congress back to the 74th (1935). Month by month for 2025-26.
- Join: `POLITICS__VOTEVIEW_VOTES` + `FED_VOTEVIEW_MEMBERS` party codes, rolled up per roll call.
- Duplicate check: no repeated congress-chamber-roll keys. All 113,512 dates parse.

**The dull explanations.**
- The Senate record is a tactic. Nominees got a cloture vote and a confirmation vote each (2025: 187 cloture votes, 170 "On the Nomination"). July 2025 alone had 114 Senate roll calls, including the reconciliation amendment votes.
- "Record since 1989" is only as long as the tagging. Before 1989, nominations aren't coded. Text search finds only 10-17 nomination votes a year in 1985-88, so an all-time record is likely but not proven here.
- The House low is partly the fall 2025 shutdown recess (October is zero). Even so, 361 is low for any first session.
- Known-story risk: the vote counts and the rule change were covered in real time. The 13.5% to 41.5% party-line jump is computed here. Whether it's already been published wasn't checked.

**Hit means.** One chamber is voting at a 49-year high, mostly on personnel, and more of it along pure party lines. The other chamber is at a 44-year low.
**Miss would have meant.** 2025 was an ordinary year. It isn't.

**Traps.**
- `VOTE_RESULT`, `VOTE_QUESTION`, `SESSION` and `CLERK_ROLLNUMBER` are blank before the 101st Congress (1989). That's 69% of all rows.
- `NOMINATE_MID_1` and `NOMINATE_SPREAD_1` read 0.0 on 11,907 rows (10.5%). These are near-unanimous votes Voteview didn't scale: 99.4% have a losing side under 2.5%. Zero means "no score," not "centrist."
- The member-level `VOTEVIEW_VOTES` table lags this one: 851 Senate roll calls for the 119th, against 890 here.
- Member votes include the President's announced position under an ICPSR that isn't in the members table: 393 Senate roll calls in the 118th-119th. Drop it before summing by party.

## 3. POLITICS__IRS527_RELATED_ENTITIES: probed

**What it is.** Groups that 527 political organizations list as "related" on IRS Form 8871. 64,835 rows, 8,979 EINs, 16,517 filings.

**The trap that dominates it.**
- ⚠ **40,503 rows (62.5%)** come from 9 Nationwide Insurance state PACs. Each amended 8871 (340 filings) re-lists 110-170 Nationwide subsidiaries. That's why "One Nationwide Plaza" topped the glance.
- Keep only the latest filing per EIN and the table shrinks to **13,134 rows**. Nationwide drops to 5.2%.
- After that dedupe, the hubs are ordinary federations: NFIB (43 groups), National Federation of Republican Women (36), Stand for Children (24), RSLC (23), Florida Chamber (23).

**The lead I chased: 12 swing-state 527s, one DC "Liberty Group, Inc."**
- 12 527s list "Liberty Group, Inc." (Washington DC) as their Connected organization. They were registered 2022-04-11 to 2022-10-04, before the midterms.
  - Arizona: Arizona For Us, Arizona RINO Hunters Liberty Group, Brighter Future Arizona Liberty Group
  - Michigan: Michiganders for Freedom, Michigan RINO Hunters Liberty Group, Brighter Future Michigan Liberty Group
  - Pennsylvania: Keep the Republic PA, Make Freedom Ring in Pennsylvania, Pennsylvania RINO Hunters
  - Wisconsin: Patriots for Wisconsin, Wisconsin RINO Hunters, Brighter Future Wisconsin
- All 12 share one custodian, contact and treasurer: Mike McCauley. The purpose on each is "To influence elections."
- All 12 claimed the exemption from IRS money reports (Form 8872). So the IRS money tables hold **0 reports, 0 Schedule A donors and 0 Schedule B payments** for them.
- "Liberty Group Inc" is **not** in the IRS exempt-organization master file.

**Why it's probed, not live. The dull explanation wins.**
- McCauley looks like a Salt Lake City CPA who works as treasurer for hire (McCauley & Associates). In the 2026 FEC cycle, 27 committees list "McCauley, Mike" as treasurer, and 3 more list a "McCauley, Michael". He is also the listed treasurer or custodian on 527s like Friends of Mia Love (2011), Republicans4Shapiro (2022) and Forward Party Victory Fund.
- The exemption is normal. 73.6% of 527s registered in 2022 claimed it (2,263 of 3,076). It means the group reports to a state instead.
- The DC addresses are shared office buildings. 1140 3rd St NE houses 36 tax-exempt orgs, including AFSCME locals.
- The name echo breaks on address. "American Liberty Group" (the telemarketing vendor paid by the charity-named 527s in join j8) sits at 611 Pennsylvania Ave SE, not at Liberty Group Inc's addresses. There's no second field agreeing, so no link.
- The only shared thread is the accountant. McCauley & Associates was also paid small fees by a few of j8's 527s: Veterans In Defense of Liberty $28K, Committee for Police Officers Defense $61K. That's what a CPA does.

**Hit would mean.** A single funder ran attack groups ("RINO Hunters") and positive groups ("Brighter Future") in four swing states while showing nothing to the IRS.
**Miss means.** Nothing in the warehouse can tell. The money sits with the AZ, MI, PA and WI election offices, which aren't loaded. **Primary check:** state committee filings for the 12 names, and the DC corporate registry for Liberty Group, Inc.

**Other traps.**
- 70 blank entity names. `ENTITY_RELATIONSHIP` is free text with 525 labels ("Connected", "COMMON DIRECTION", "1").
- `ENTITY_ID` is unique per row. It's a line number, not an entity key.
- EINs are clean 9-digit text in this table (no zero fill), but the other 527 tables need `lpad(...,9,'0')`.

## 4. POLITICS__INTL_FREEDOMHOUSE: probed

**What it is.** Freedom House scores, 2,723 country-editions, editions 2013-2025, 208-210 places each. No duplicate country-editions. TOTAL always equals PR + CL.

**What I looked at.**
- Biggest 10-year drops (2015 to 2025 editions): Nicaragua -40 (54 to 14), Tunisia -35, Tanzania -28, El Salvador -28, Niger -26, Hong Kong -25. The regional medians run -1 to -7.
- Peer group: the 58 countries that scored 85+ in the 2013 edition. Median change -1.5.
  - Worst: Hungary -23, Nauru -18, Poland -11, Malta -10, **United States -9 (93 to 84)**.
  - The US lost one point each on 9 sub-questions (B3, B4, C3, D1, D3, F1, F2, F4, G3).
- Declines outnumber gains in every edition. 2025 edition: 62 down, 37 up.

**Why probed.** Every one of these is in Freedom House's own reports. There's no hidden number.
**Data limit.** ⚠ The 2026 edition, which covers calendar 2025, isn't loaded. The table stops right before the most-watched year.

**Traps.**
- `EDITION` is the report year. It scores the *previous* calendar year.
- Every sub-score is text. Only TOTAL is numeric.
- PR = A + B + C − ADD_Q. For 5 Gulf monarchies (Brunei, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, UAE) in the 2013-2017 editions, PR also *adds* ADD_A. That's 25 rows where the simple sum fails.
- ADD_A is blank on 1,676 of 2,723 rows.

## 5. POLITICS__ST_CANNABIS_POLICY_BUNDLES: skip

**What it is.** A state-by-year panel of cannabis law codes. 50 states × 30 years (1994-2023) = 1,500 rows. No DC. A reference table for before/after tests, not a place to find stories. One pull (statement 3) confirmed the shape.

**Traps for whoever uses it.**
- ⚠ **Stops at 2023.** 24 states are marked legal for recreational use in the last year, which matches the end of 2023. Anything from 2024-25 is missing.
- ⚠ **California is marked `REC_CANN_APPROVED = 1` in 1997**, then 0 again in 1998. California's recreational law passed in 2016. Take "first state to legalize" from this column and you get CA 1997, not CO/WA 2012.
- `NO_DISP_LIMIT_MML_APP` = 4 on Virginia 1998. It should be 0/1.
- `COL_53` and `COL_78` are blank spacer columns.
- `FIPS` is the 2-digit state code, not a county.
- The `*SCALEZ` columns copy `*SCALE` (1 row differs). Several flags (e.g. `LEGISLATIVE_ACTION`) are stored as "0.0"/"1.0" text.
