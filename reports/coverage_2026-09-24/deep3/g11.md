## 1. Prop 65 list: **live** (through TRI 2023)

**Headline.** In 2023, California sites that report to TRI put 1,052,601 lbs of Prop 65 cancer-listed chemicals into the air. Styrene was 784,691 lbs of that, or 74.5%.
Six fiberglass shops put out 542,633 lbs, or 51.6%. They make tanks, pools, bath fixtures, boats and truck caps.
The median California site with any Prop 65 air release reported 333 lbs.

**The six (data match, not verified).** The "Makes" column is inferred from each company's name and NAICS code (326199, 326191, 336612, 336214). The warehouse does not record what they make.

| Site | City | Makes | Styrene to air, lbs | Share of CA cancer-listed air | US rank of 1,137 styrene reporters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Xerxes Corp | Anaheim | fiberglass tanks | 155,167 | 14.7% | 37 |
| Containment Solutions (NOV) | Bakersfield | fiberglass tanks | 107,202 | 10.2% | 61 |
| Latham Pool Products | Williams | fiberglass pools | 104,621 | 9.9% | 63 |
| Florestone LLC | Madera | bath and shower pans | 72,000 | 6.8% | 103 |
| Fineline Industries | Merced | boats | 61,230 | 5.8% | 121 |

**The refineries report much less.** The Tesoro LA refinery at Carson reported 18,074 lbs of cancer-listed air. Chevron El Segundo reported 2,941 lbs, and Phillips 66 Rodeo 2,367 lbs.
Most refinery Prop 65 pounds are methanol, n-hexane and toluene. Prop 65 lists those three for reproductive harm, not cancer.

**Checks run:**
- **The join.** Prop 65 CAS_NO matched to TRI's CAS column (S14 and S20). 218 TRI chemicals match. Delisted chemicals and footnote rows were dropped first.
- **Blind spot, bounded.** Group listings like "lead and lead compounds" have no CAS number, so the join cannot see them. S26 adds up every California air pound the join could be missing: unmatched metal, PAC and isocyanate rows, plus anything TRI itself flags as a carcinogen. That is at most 25,888 lbs.
  With that added in, styrene is still at least 72.8% and the six shops at least 50.3%.
- **Peers, state.** In S21, Xerxes is 34x the median Prop 65 air pounds of California Plastics and Rubber sites (4,505 lbs, 28 sites).
- **Peers, nation.** In S23, Xerxes is 9.5x the US median for its industry code, NAICS 326199 (16,286 lbs, 270 sites). The California shops are big, but they are not national freaks. The finding is about California.
- **Duplicates.** In S25, 664 site-plus-chemical pairs in TRI have 2 to 7 separate forms, each with its own document number. Tesoro Carson files 7 per chemical. These are partial-facility reports, so they get summed, not deduped.
  None of the six shops has a second styrene form (S21 lists each with one styrene entry).
- **Enforcement join.** S21 matches ECHO on FRS ID. All 12 of the top 12 California sites land.
  ECHO's status field reads "No Violation Identified" for Xerxes, Containment Solutions and Latham Pool. Latham has 0 inspections on record.
  Fineline has 8 formal actions and status "Violation Identified".

**What a hit means:** California's own cancer list, applied to 2023 TRI air releases, points mostly at open-mold fiberglass, not at refineries.
**What a miss would mean:** if the group listings had carried big air pounds, the styrene share would collapse. They don't; the bound is 25,888 lbs.

**The dull explanation. Say it first.**
- Open-mold fiberglass layup gives off styrene by design. These are the state's biggest composite shops.
- A pound is not a unit of risk. Per pound, styrene's cancer potency is far below benzene's or hexavalent chromium's. There is no toxicity-weighted score (RSEI) in the warehouse, so this was **not tested**.
- TRI numbers are the facilities' own estimates, and only sites above TRI's size and usage thresholds report.
- There is only one year of TRI (2023), so no trend.
- TRI also flags styrene as a carcinogen, so the finding does not rest on California's list alone. The share was not recomputed on TRI's own flag.
- ECHO formal-action counts cover recent years only, and DATE_LAST_FORMAL_ACTION does not say which program filed the action (see the existing trap note).

**Second angle, national (S20).** TRI has no field for reproductive harm. Take the 36 chemicals that Prop 65 lists for birth defects or reproductive harm and that TRI does not flag as carcinogens. In 2023 they account for 151.6M lbs of air releases, which is 29.0% of all 522.97M TRI air pounds.
Methanol alone is 116.4M lbs of total releases (all routes, not just air). A hostile editor will say pounds are not exposure, and methanol breaks down fast. That makes it context, not a lead.

**Time (S11).** Of the 990 dated rows, 475 (48%) were listed in 1987 to 1990. From 2018 to 2026 there were 44 listings. The table holds no listing dated 2024. That was not checked against OEHHA's site.
Styrene was listed 2016-04-22. Vinyl acetate was listed 2025-01-03; in 2023, 126 US TRI sites released 1.22M lbs of it.

**Traps in this table:**
- 31 rows have no date and no toxicity type. They are footnotes pulled in from the safe-harbor sheet, like "Neonatal infant boys, age 0 to 28 daysb", "Adultb" and "a Where a source...".
- 25 chemicals were delisted but are still in the table, with "Delisted <date> [Click here...]" pasted into the CHEMICAL name. Cyclohexanol is one of them, and it would falsely match 868,848 lbs of TRI releases.
- 110 live listings have a CAS of `---`. These are group listings: lead and lead compounds, nickel compounds, arsenic compounds, wood dust, tobacco smoke. A CAS join misses them.
- 16 listings only count for one route of exposure: ingested, gas, airborne. Ethylene glycol is listed only when ingested. Without a filter, 23.9M lbs of TRI releases would count for it, and Sumitomo San Marcos' 70,200 lbs of air would too.
- 10 rows hold two CAS numbers in one cell, like `12054-48-7; 12125-56-3`.
- LISTING_MECHANISM is free text, like "SQE - developmental FR - female, male". TYPE_OF_TOXICITY has spelling variants, like "developmental,   male" and "developmental male".
- COL_6, COL_7 and COL_8 hold '' (an empty string, not null) on all 1,021 rows.

**Trap in the join partner, TRI 2023:** one site plus one chemical can have up to 7 forms, each with a different DOC_CTRL_NUM. Sum them. 770 rows are in grams (dioxins) and must be kept out of pound totals.

**Next join:** use FRS ID to reach ICIS-Air registry IDs, then pull formal actions and stack tests for the six shops. Then take ECHO's PCT_MINORITY around each site.

## 2. UDS Table 3A patients: probed

**Headline:** 1,356 health centers, 32,746,392 patients in 2025. The median center has 14,879 patients; the biggest has 312,739 and the smallest 135.

**The trap. It goes to the ledger.**
- `--` is **not** a blank. It is a suppressed count between 1 and 15.
  - The smallest count shown on any age line is 16 (S09).
  - At 590 of 1,356 centers, the age lines add up to less than the line-39 total. Divide each center's shortfall by its number of `--` cells: the answer runs 2.5 to 15.0, with a median of 9.7 (S10).
  - Centers with no `--` cells add up exactly (0 mismatches).
- The `--` cells hide 68,923 patients in total, 0.21% of all patients.
- A single `-` is a different mark: a true blank. It shows up in 177 cells, all on the child age lines and line 38, and it hides nothing.
- Line 39 (the total) is never suppressed.
- plain.json says "`--` marks a blank count". **That is wrong.**

**Peers (S17).** Patients per weekly open site-hour, with the site roster joined on grant number. 1,342 of 1,356 centers land (99.0%). The national median is 45.6.
The top outliers are big clinics with a single site: Fenway Boston runs 514 per hour on 1 site open 60 hours; Anchorage Neighborhood runs 235 per hour against an Alaska median of 17.3.
The dull explanation is that roster hours are not service hours (hospital-based care, contracted care, telehealth). It was not tested. Nothing story-grade here.

**Next join:** site NPIs from the roster to Medicare billing, or the HPSA shortage-area score by BHCMIS org ID.
