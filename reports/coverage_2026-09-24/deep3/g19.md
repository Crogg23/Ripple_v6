## 1. POLITICS__IRS527_8871_ORGS — live

**What it is.** Form 8871 notices. A 527 political group files one with the IRS when it forms, changes or closes.
77,591 filings, 2001 to July 2026. The IRS publishes every one.

**Finding A: the IRS political-group list filled up with businesses.**
- I tagged each new-group notice (the INITIAL flag) as political or not, from its purpose text and its name.
  - I hand-read 40 random 2025 notices tagged non-political. 38 were businesses or charities, 1 was political, 1 was unclear.
- Non-political share of new-group notices:

| Window | New-group notices | Non-political | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan 2019 – Nov 2022 | 10,748 | 731 | 6.8% |
| **Dec 2022 – Dec 2025** | 10,464 | **2,596** | **24.8%** |
| Jan 2026 – Jul 2026 | 2,388 | 110 | 4.6% |

- Month by month the break is sharp. The share was 3-8% through Nov 2022, then 15-20% from Dec 2022.
  - It hit 35-40% in mid-2025. It fell to 3% in Jan 2026 and has stayed at 3-8% since.
- What they are: towing firms, a deli, daycares, painters, property LLCs.
  - Some wrote in the purpose box asking the IRS to verify their EIN, send a lost EIN letter, or confirm a number for "BANK ACCOUNT OPENING."
  - New-group purpose texts mentioning EIN, verification or a bank account went from 13-35 a year (2015-2023) to 66 in 2025. This is a loose count; a few are political.
- So the 2025 "record" is fake. There were 4,025 new-group notices, the most in the file, but only 2,851 were political.
  - 2,851 is 19% above 2023 (2,402) but about level with 2022 (2,886).

**Finding B: 141 look-alike LLCs, filed in batches.**
- In 2025, 141 notices say only "help small business grow" (plus typos like "HEP BUSINESS GROW" and "help businness grow").
  - The same text appears on no notice before or after 2025.
  - Every one lists `no@email`, and every one is an LLC.
  - All 141 are first-time notices: none amended, none closed. They come from 34 states.
- The names follow a template: a surname, then made-up brand words, then LLC.
  - Examples: "ZAPATA Zyllem Commerce LLC", "KINDLE Daily Grind Commerce Group LLC", "SANTOS VEXLA COLLECTIVE LLC".
  - Brand phrases repeat under different surnames in different states: BOXYARD TREASURES, CURIO CLUB GOODS, THE DIGITAL STOCKROOM, SPHERE COMMERCE GROUP, VITAL CORE WELLNESS, MADE PULSE CREATIONS.
  - The custodian's surname is in the LLC name on 99% of rows. The "established" date is within 10 days of the filing on 91%.
- **EIN test, done inside the group.** The IRS hands out EINs in number order, so two EINs a few hundred apart were issued minutes apart.
  - Within the 141, pairs of EINs 500 or fewer apart are **49x** more common than among 2025's other new filers (prefix 39), and **29x** more common for prefix 33.
  - 16 batches of 3 or more hold 68 of the 141.
  - Example, July 8, 2025: 8 LLCs in 7 states (MO, NY, OR, NC, AZ, FL, TX). EINs 393094246 to 393095356. All filed between 18:08 and 18:40.
- Money: none of the 141 EINs appears in the 8872 money-report table.
  - That miss is weak. Only 62 of 892 political 2025 groups that must file 8872 reports show up there either.

**Checks run.**
- Time: every month from 2019, three windows, same flag every time.
- Inside the group: EIN gaps against the same year's other filers, split by EIN prefix. Also the batch timestamps and repeated brand phrases.
- One join: 8871 EINs to the 8872 money reports (no hits for the 141).
- Trap checks: blank flags, fake dates, placeholder emails, a duplicate table (see traps).

**The dull explanations, tested.**
- "A big election year." 2025 is an off year, and the jump is in non-political notices.
- "One mass filer." Among 2025's new groups that must file money reports, no custodian has more than 11 notices and no address more than 4. The wave is spread out.
- "Many people copied one online tip." That fits Finding A. It does not fit Finding B: strangers in 7 states don't get EINs minutes apart and then file within 32 minutes.
- "A formation service filing for real customers." **Not ruled out.** Batch timing fits a service as well as a scheme.
  - The data can't tell which. State business registries can (not in the warehouse).

**Hit means.** Something in the 8871 filing path let businesses in from Dec 2022 until about Jan 2026.
And one operator, a service or a scheme, put 141 LLCs on the IRS political-group list. Their owners' names and home addresses are now public as "political organizations."
**Miss would have meant.** The 2025 surge was real political growth. It isn't, for about 1,174 of the 4,025.

**Next step.** Look up 10 of the 141 in their state's business registry: do they exist, when were they formed, who is the registered agent? Then check the IRS political-organization search pages for the same EINs.

**Traps.**
- `ECONOMICS__FED_IRS_527_ORGS` is the same 77,591 filings (same 58,916 EINs; only 5 rows fail to match on EIN plus name).
  - But its `AMENDED_FLAG` marks 62,741 rows as amended. This table marks 14.4K.
  - Its `STATUS_FLAG` and `AMENDED_FLAG` agree with none of the IRS flags more than 69% of the time. Use the POLITICS copy.
- Rows recorded in 2001-2002 (7,122) mostly carry no initial, amended or final flag. EXEMPT flags are blank on 6,967 rows.
- `ESTABLISHED_DATE` runs back to 1808 (typos) and is blank on 6,824 rows.
- `no@email` sits on 3,712 rows. It's a placeholder, not a contact.
- The purpose box holds EIN-shaped numbers on 17 rows and SSN-shaped numbers on 2. Don't publish PURPOSE text raw.
- `POLITICS__IRS527_8872_REPORTS` holds at most 850 filers a year. A miss against it proves little.

## 2. POLITICS__FEC_CANDIDATE — live (moderate)

**What it is.** The FEC candidate list for the 2024 and 2026 cycles. 17,900 candidate-cycle rows, 13,240 candidates.
`CAND_ELECTION_YR` is the year of the candidate's last filed race, not the cycle.
So the 2026 list includes people whose last race was decades ago, because their campaign committee is still open.

**The finding: campaign accounts that outlive the campaign.**
- The test: 2026-cycle candidates whose last race was 2016 or earlier.
  - No newer candidate ID can use the same committee.
  - No current candidate can share the surname and state.
  - Then join to FEC_CANDIDATE_SUMMARY for the money.
- **139 committees.** In the 2026 cycle (through Mar 2026) they hold **$7.98M**, spent $908K, and took in $1.53M.
  - Of that $1.53M, **$2,067 came from individual donors.** The rest isn't donor money; interest or asset sales is the likely source.
- Same committees in the 2024 cycle: spent **$3.37M**.
- A few carry it. The top 10 hold **92%** of the cash. The median committee holds **$366**, and 49 of 139 spent anything this cycle.
- Named (data match):

| Committee (FEC ID) | Last race | 2024-cycle spent | Cash, Mar 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tallon for Congress (C00153684), Robin Tallon SC | 1992 | **$1,391,737** | $89,221 |
| Friends of Tom Byrne (C00347591), NJ | 2000 | $449,905 | $1,265,111 |
| Citizens for Joe Kennedy 1988 (C00222455), Joseph P. Kennedy II MA | 1998 | $263,289 | $1,203,602 |
| Evan Bayh Committee (C00306860), IN | 2016 | $26,214 | $2,232,144 |
| Lautenberg for Senate (C00382457), NJ (died 2013) | 2014 on file | $376 | $86,928 |
| Committee to Reelect Tom Foley (C00002592), WA (died 2013) | 1994 | $44,359 | $19,910 |

- **Tallon is the lead.** A committee from a 1992 race spent $1.39M in 2023-24.
  - It took in $351,974 that cycle with $0 from individuals. It started the cycle near $1.14M (back-solved from the summary).
  - Its treasurer changed between the 2024 and 2026 committee records.
- Where it didn't go: gifts to candidates. All 139 committees together gave $109,155 to candidates over both cycles, from 10 committees. Tallon gave none.

**Checks run.**
- Time: the same committees in the 2024 cycle vs the 2026 cycle.
- Peer: each committee against the median of its group ($366 cash; most spent $0).
- Joins: FEC_COMMITTEE (names, treasurers, still registered in both cycles) and FEC_CAND_CMTE_LINK (each linked only to the old candidate ID).
  - Also FEC committee-to-candidate gifts (non-memo rows only).

**The dull explanations, tested.**
- "They're sitting members who switched chambers." Removed: any committee tied to a newer candidate ID. That took out Cotton, Daines, Young, Heinrich, Peters, Lummis and Lankford.
- "Stale election year on an active candidate." It happens. Adam Miller (OH) has an old ID with year 2000 and a 2026 ID, both on committee C00856823. The shared-committee rule drops him.
  - The 139 were checked for new committee IDs too. The newest (C00925016) belongs to a filer with $0.
- "Zombie campaigns are old news." True for the genre (reporters have covered it since 2016). What's new is the names and dollar figures for 2024-2026.

**Hit means.** A handful of old accounts still move real money, and Tallon's 2024-cycle spending is the outlier to chase.
**Miss would have meant.** Old committees just sit there. Most do: the median holds $366.

**Next step.** Pull Tallon's 2023-24 FEC disbursement schedules. Spending records by line item (FEC Schedule B) are not in the warehouse.

**Traps.**
- **Summing FEC_CANDIDATE_SUMMARY double counts.** 325 committees appear under 2 or more candidate IDs with identical figures.
  - 2024 receipts are overstated by **$1.57B of $6.85B**.
  - Harris and Biden share committee C00703975, so $1.175B is counted twice. House-to-Senate movers (Gallego, Trone, Schiff, Rosen) add the rest.
  - Sum by `PRINCIPAL_CMTE_ID`, not by candidate.
- P40010415 "NORRIS, JIM ALEXANDER SR" reports **$775.8M cash on hand** (2024), then $775.8M spent and -$775.8M cash (2026).
  - That's the largest cash figure in the file, it's junk, and the ID isn't in the candidate table.
- `CAND_ELECTION_YR` runs past 2060 on 23 rows (2064 up to 2929), and past 2034 on 142.
- `PRINCIPAL_CMTE_ID` is blank on 2,610 rows (1,759 in 2024, 851 in 2026). Most are status N and presidential filers.
- There are 2,335 presidential filers in the 2024 cycle, and 127 reported any money. Don't count "candidates" by rows.

## 3. POLITICS__JUDGE_IDEOLOGY_SCOTUS — probed

**What it is.** A Judicial Common Space score for each justice, each term, 1937-2022. 782 rows, 49 justices.

**What I found.**
- The biggest career drift is Blackmun, +0.52 to -0.49 (a range of 1.04). Then Frankfurter, Souter, Black and Stevens. All already known.
- Recent terms: Thomas's score fell every term from 2016 (0.709) to 2022 (0.648). Alito's rose from 0.586 to 0.671.
  - Alito passed Thomas as the Court's most conservative by this score in 2022.
  - Kavanaugh (0.278) was the median justice in 2022.
  - Scholars have covered this movement already; nothing new here.
- **The series stops at the 2022 term.** Newer terms need a reload from upstream (check what's published).

**Checks run.** Time (each justice across terms). Peer (the justices sitting in the same term). A copy check against XC_JCS_SCOTUS.

**Traps.**
- `POLITICS__XC_JCS_SCOTUS` is an exact copy: 782 of 782 rows match on name, term and score.
- `JUSTICE_CODE` and `FJC_NID` are null on 32 rows. Those are the 9 justices who sat only before 1946 (Cardozo, Brandeis, Hughes, Stone, and others), so they won't join to the vote tables.
- TERM is a year. The glance's "TERM sum 1.5M" means nothing.
- Some terms have 10-11 rows because of mid-term replacements (1937, 1938, 1956, 1958, 1961, 1975, 2005).

## 4. POLITICS__CA_LOBBY_EMPLOYER_FIRMS — dead

**What it is.** Which California employers hired which lobbying firms, by session, 1995-2001. 524 rows, 177 employers.

**Why dead.**
- It's a slice. CA_LOBBY_EMPLOYER lists 1,726 employers for the 1999 session. This table has 118 of them (**6.8%**).
- Nothing here supports a claim about who hired whom.

**Trap (for the ledger).**
- **FIRM_NAME often holds the client's name.**
  - On 64 of 477 rows that can be checked (13%), the name differs from the firm's name in CA_LOBBY_FIRM.
  - On 52 rows it equals the employer's own name. Example: Aaron Read & Associates' FIRM_ID labeled "PACIFIC GAS AND ELECTRIC COMPANY" on PG&E's row.
  - 29 of 131 firm IDs carry more than one name. Get the name by joining FIRM_ID to CA_LOBBY_FIRM.
  - The glance's "top FIRM_NAME" counts are off because of this.
- `TERMINATION_DT` is empty on all 524 rows.
- `CA_LOBBY_EMPLOYER` itself holds only the 1999 session.
