## 1. FED_IRS_990_EFILE_INDEX: the IRS revoked charities that its own index says filed. **live**

**What it is.**
The IRS's index of every e-filed nonprofit return, 5,544,626 rows, from 2017 to 2026.
Each row has the EIN, the tax period, the form, the processing year and an OBJECT_ID for fetching the full return.

**The rule being tested.**
- A nonprofit that files nothing for **3 years in a row** loses its exemption automatically.
- The revocation date is the due date of the third missed return. That's the 15th of the 5th month after the year ends.
- So the three missed tax years end exactly **5, 17 and 29 months** before the revocation date.
- If the IRS had a return for any of those three years before the revocation date, the revocation shouldn't happen.

**The join (S10, S12).**
- `ECONOMICS__FED_IRS_AUTO_REVOCATIONS`: 1,207,295 revocations. 2019 onward: 369,606.
- Joined on EIN to this index, 990 / 990-EZ / 990-PF only. **50,305** revoked orgs (2019 on) have at least one e-filed return (13.6% land). They are the denominator.
- A hit is a return whose tax year ends in one of the 3 missed months, processed **before** the revocation.
  - "Before" means a full timestamp earlier than the revocation, or an earlier calendar year.
  - From 2022 on the index keeps only the year, so a return processed in the same year as the revocation is **not** counted. The count is a floor.

**The finding.**

| Step | Orgs |
|---|---|
| A 990-series return anywhere in a loose 5-40 month window before revocation | 470 |
| Tax year ends exactly in one of the 3 missed months, and the name on the return agrees | 111 |
| Minus 1 whose "tax year" was processed before that year had even ended | **110** |
| No reinstatement on the revocation list | **88** |
| ...and not on the IRS's current exempt master list (EO BMF, 2026-08-10 load) | **61** |
| Filed **both** of the two missed years this check can see | 16 |

- Forms among the 110: 990-EZ 46, 990 36, 990-PF 28. By state, California leads with 21.
- None of the 61 has e-filed a return for any period after its revocation.

**Time comparison (S15 denominators = revoked orgs with any e-filed 990-series return):**

|---|---|---|---|

- It isn't only the 990-EZ e-file mandate: 990-PF hits went from 0-2 a year to 12, 9 and 4, and 990 hits from 0-3 to 6, 10 and 11.

**Peer comparison: what the IRS's own master list says now (S13-S15).**
Among revoked orgs with no reinstatement on the list:

|---|---|---|---|

- An org back on the master list with its old ruling date looks like the IRS treated the revocation as void. The public revocation list never recorded that.
- Other revoked orgs that come back mostly show a **new** ruling. They reapplied.
- (89 includes the one org later dropped for an impossible tax year. It is off the master list, so the 27 hold.)

None of these seven shows a reinstatement, and none is on the master list.
OBJECT_IDs for every row are in `g58/out_S12.csv`. The full list of 110 is `g58/core_strict_recomputed.csv`.

**Hit means:** two IRS publications contradict each other.
The revocation list says these groups filed nothing for 3 years. The e-file index says the IRS processed a return for one of those years first.
The revocation costs the group its exemption and its donors their deduction.

**Miss would have meant:** zero hits. The two lists agree, and the revocation program checks what it has.

**The dull explanations.**
- *Wrong EIN typed on someone else's return.* Tested: every one of the 110 shares a name word, and 95 are near-identical strings (similarity 0.85 or higher, `g58/local_990.py`). The rest are the same group by eye. A few are chapters (a PTA, a Little League) where the revocation list prints the parent's name.
- *Bad tax period in the index.* Tested (S17): only 58 of 2.35M timestamped rows were processed before their tax year ended. The one such hit was dropped.
- *The group's year-end differs from what the IRS has on file.* Handled: only exact month matches count. The 359 other candidates are set aside. That fiscal-year mismatch may be its own story.
- *Late returns.* **Not settled.** Of the 22 hit rows with full timestamps (19 orgs), 21 came after the return's own due date and 15 after the 6-month extension. The statute says "fails to file", and a late return is still filed, but the IRS's own practice isn't checked here.
- *Returns filed before the IRS recognized the group, which then never posted.* **Not tested.** Many hits have new EINs (87-, 88-, 92-). That would still be an IRS systems failure, not a non-filer.
- *The IRS fixed it quietly.* Partly true: 27 of 88 are back on the master list. 61 are not.

**Next join.** Pull the XML for the 61 by OBJECT_ID from the IRS's bulk 990 downloads. Confirm the tax year and form on the actual return. Then check each EIN on the IRS's Tax Exempt Organization Search.

**Traps found on the way (for the ledger):**
- ⚠ **SUB_DATE is fake from 2022 on.** 3,192,934 rows (58%) hold only a year, stored as January 1. The 2022 index is split: 242,003 rows with full timestamps and 656,503 with the year only.
- ⚠ **The form label changes over time.** `990O` (228,639 rows, 2017-2020) and `990EO` (140,343, 2017-2021) are full 990s: they never overlap the `990` label for the same EIN and period, and under 0.1% are private foundations. `990PR` (30,085 rows, Jun-Oct 2020) is really the 990-PF: 92% of the filers the master list knows are private foundations, and the 990-PF count drops from 64,614 (2019) to 27,883 (2020). A time series on `RETURN_TYPE = '990'` alone shows a fake 2021 jump.
- 990-T appears only from September 2021.
- RETURN_ID is blank on 279,505 / 336,956 / 271,153 rows in the 2024 / 2025 / 2026 index years. Use OBJECT_ID, which is unique on all 5,544,626 rows.
- 51,481 EIN + period + form combos repeat (104,900 rows), 24,894 of them across index years. Those are amendments or reprints. Dedupe before counting returns.
- **Two copies of the revocation list:** `ECONOMICS__FED_IRS_AUTO_REVOCATIONS` (1,207,295 rows, posted through 2026-08-04) and `ECONOMICS__FED_IRS_REVOCATION` (1,206,627 rows, through 2026-06-09, dates stored as text). They differ by 668 rows, almost all 2025-26.
- The revocation list prints the **parent's** name for group chapters. EIN 237130058 reads "Benevolent & Protective Order of Elks of the USA". Its own return says "Elks No 1526".

## 2. FED_US_SEC_EDGAR: 25 big companies, and JPMorgan drowns out the rest. **probed**

**What it is.** 48,984 filings by 25 companies, one load (2026-07-01, `_INGESTED_AT` stored as epoch microseconds). One row per accession. No duplicates.

**Checked (S04, S11).**
- ⚠ **The window isn't the same for each company.** JPMorgan: exactly 2025-07-01 to 2026-07-01, 25,351 rows. The other 24: their last ~1,000 filings (978-1,018 rows), reaching back to 2013-2024. ASML: 590 rows back to 1995. The pull looks like EDGAR's "recent filings" block: one year or 1,000 filings, whichever is more.
- **Only 2025-07-01 to 2026-07-01 is complete for all 25.** Any peer comparison has to stay inside it.
- In that year: 29,000 filings. **JPM 25,238 (87%). 21,943 are 424B2**, the pricing sheets for its structured notes.
  - Monthly: 1,612 to 2,221 a month, rising into 2026. The median business day has 80; the peak was 260 (2026-03-03).
  - No other note-issuing bank is in the table, so there's no peer. Big banks issuing structured notes is well known.
- **Peer, Form 4 speed (insider trades must be reported within 2 business days):**
  - In the common year, 125 of 2,054 Form 4s came more than 2 business days after the trade date. 91 of the 125 are exactly 3 days. My business-day count doesn't know about federal holidays, which likely explains most of those.
  - Only 6 in the common year are more than 21 business days late.
  - JNJ 21.9% and GOOGL 16.4% top the list. Median company about 5%.
- **Join (S18):** the 74 Form 4s filed more than 21 business days late (all years) matched on accession to the insider tables. **57 of 74 land.**
  - 29 contain a gift (code G). Before 2023, gifts could wait for the year-end Form 5.
  - Of the other 28 that land: 11 have no plain-stock trade rows, 9 bundle trades from several dates (the period of report is the earliest one), and 8 are single-date trades.
  - Nothing there a data editor would take.
- Constant or empty: `ISIN` is empty on all rows. `SOURCE_ID` is one value. `PERIOD_OF_REPORT` is null on 31,442 (64%), mostly the 424B2s. The 25 "addresses" are just the 25 companies.

**Hit / miss.** A company filing insider reports much later than its peers would have been a lead. The late ones are gifts and bundles: the dull explanation, tested.
**Boring.** It's a filing index. Use it for dates of 8-Ks and 10-Ks inside the common year, not for counts across companies.
