## 1. IMMIGRATION__FED_ICE_DETENTION_FACILITY_CODES: **live**, through its join

**What it is:** a lookup. 1,490 rows = 1,490 codes [q03].
**Why it matters:** it's the only place a facility is labeled **Hold** (a field-office hold room) or **Staging**. The stint table has no type column.
**The join:** `DETENTION_FACILITY_CODE` → `IMMIGRATION__FED_ICE_DETENTION_STINTS`. 705 of 707 stint codes land, covering **2,571,968 of 2,571,975 stints** (all but 7) [q03].

### The mechanism
A hold room is a locked room in an ICE office, used before transfer to a jail.
ICE's detention standards cap hold-room time at **12 hours** (outside knowledge, not in the warehouse).
A stint = one booking into one facility, with a book-in and book-out timestamp. Length = book-out minus book-in.
Rows that ICE's own file flags `DUPLICATE_DROP_ROW = True` are dropped.

### Time: same months (Oct 1 - Feb 28) across four fiscal years [q07]

| Window | Hold stints | Median hours | Over 12 h | Over 24 h | Over 72 h | Over 7 days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oct 2022-Feb 2023 | 39,259 | 4.8 | 3.7% | **182** (0.5%) | 27 | 14 |
| Oct 2023-Feb 2024 | 42,236 | 4.2 | 4.1% | **288** (0.7%) | 35 | 19 |
| Oct 2024-Feb 2025 | 53,929 | 4.2 | 8.2% | **2,519** (4.7%) | 1,483 | 453 |
| Oct 2025-Feb 2026 | 184,929 | 5.8 | **24.3%** | **26,374** (14.3%) | **5,079** | 571 |

- Stints went up 4.4x from the FY24 window. Stays over a day went up **92x**.
- Over-12-hour stints in the FY26 window: **44,864**.

### Monthly break [q12]
- Oct 2022 to Dec 2024: hold rooms logged **21 to 411** stints over 24 h a month. 23 of those 27 months were under 100.
- Jan 2025: 679. Feb 2025: 1,666.
- **June 2025 to Feb 2026: 3,907 to 6,792 a month.** The peak was Jan 2026.
- Hold sites with any stint over 24 h in a month: **10-21** before 2025, **56-75** from June 2025.

- **24,875 distinct people.** 3,288 of the stints were women. 94 were people born 2008 or later.
- Next stop in the same stay: a dedicated detention center 12,459; a non-dedicated site, mostly county jails, 3,457; another hold or staging site 3,590; federal 1,455.
- **4,375 of these stints were the last in their stay.** 3,744 of the over-a-day stints end with the reason "Removed": deported straight from a hold room after more than a day there.

### Checks that could have broken it
- **Paper booking?** If the person were booked somewhere else at the same time, the hold stint would be a paperwork artifact. Overlap with any other stint of the same person at another code: **0 of 18,506** at the top six sites, **6 of 7,875** elsewhere [q15]. Hit: the body was booked there. Miss: none found.
- **Did the clock change?** Staging sites are the control, measured the same way: over-24-hour share **56.1% → 57.2% → 57.4% → 58.4%**, flat across all four windows [q07]. Only hold rooms moved.
- **Date-only timestamps?** Book-outs stamped exactly midnight: 627 of 184,929 (0.3%) [q07].
- **Still open?** 7 stints with no book-out in the FY26 window.
- **Types are one snapshot:** the lookup was loaded once (one run ID), so a site keeps one label across all years. No relabeling.

### Dull explanation
- **More arrests, same rooms.** Hold stints went up 3.4x on FY25 and 4.4x on FY24, and the rooms filled up. That doesn't explain it away. It *is* the mechanism, and it breaks the 12-hour rule.
- **ICE may have changed its hold-room rule in 2025.** That isn't in the warehouse. Check it before pitching.
- **A recording change can't be ruled out from this table.** Overnight holds before 2025 might have been logged elsewhere. The flat staging control argues against it.
- **Known in outline:** the press covered some of these rooms in 2025 (outside knowledge, not checked). What's new here is the national ranking of 177 hold sites over the same months in four years.

**Hit means:** ICE held 24,875 people in field-office hold rooms for more than a day in five months. Over-a-day stints were 92x the same months two years earlier. A handful of offices (Baltimore, Mesa, Atlanta, Dallas, Phoenix) did it routinely.
**Miss means:** nothing broke it. The one open door is a 2025 policy change.
**Next join:** stint → `IMMIGRATION__FED_ICE_DETAINERS` (same PERSON_HASH) to see who came from local jails. Then court filings on Baltimore and Mesa.

## 2. IMMIGRATION__FED_EPA_SDWA_SDWA_SERVICE_AREAS: probed

**What it is:** one row per water system per kind of place served (homes, schools, mobile home parks, restaurants). 422,464 rows, 378,450 systems, **no duplicate system+code pairs**, one quarterly release (2026Q2) [q01].
**Clean join:** 0 orphans against `SDWA_PUB_WATER_SYSTEMS`. Only 2,068 of 142,891 active systems (1.4%) have no row [q01].

| MP | 1,041 | same words **65.6%** |

| HM | 3,289 | hotel/motel/lodge **43.9%** |

So MH and MP are mobile home parks, SC schools, DC daycares. That matches the EPA code list (outside knowledge).

### Peer test 1: mobile home parks vs other home water systems [q11, q14]
Peers: active, groundwater, community systems, **privately owned, 25-500 people**, compared **within the same state and size band**. Health violations counted if the compliance period began in 2021-2025, deduped by VIOLATION_ID.

- Mobile home parks: **1,294 of 5,316 (24.3%)** had a health-based violation.
- Expected if they matched residential neighbors in their own state and size band: **1,081 (20.3%)**.
- Worse in **19 of 28** states. Median state gap: **+4.9 points**.
- Missed tests (monitoring violations): 56.5% vs 47.2% expected. They test less, so the health number is a floor.
- Biggest gaps: North Carolina **24.3% vs 8.2%**, Texas 48.0% vs 32.9%. I picked those two *because* they're extreme, so the next test runs inside them.

### Peer test 2: the dull explanation, inside NC and TX [q16]
In this table `ORG_NAME` is a contact person, e.g. one name sits on 544 NC residential systems. So "operator size" = systems per contact name.

| State | Operator size | Mobile home parks with a health violation | Residential |
|---|---|---|---|
| NC | under 10 systems | 73 of 245 (**29.8%**) | 55 of 166 (**33.1%**) |
| NC | 10+ systems | 1 of 60 (1.7%) | 15 of 691 (2.2%) |
| TX | under 10 systems | 132 of 240 (55.0%) | 197 of 397 (49.6%) |
| TX | 10+ systems | 24 of 85 (28.2%) | 104 of 518 (20.1%) |

- **The NC gap is operator mix.** Its residential systems are mostly run by big operators. Match the operator size and the parks are no worse.
- The TX gap shrinks to 5-8 points.

### Peer test 3: schools and daycares on their own wells [q11]
No gap. Systems serving 25-100 people with a health violation: schools 19.8%, daycares 19.0%, industrial/farm 20.2%, other workplaces 21.5%.

**Hit would have meant:** mobile-home-park residents drink worse water than their neighbors because of what they are.
**Miss means:** it's small operators, not mobile homes. Small operators do break the rules much more (NC 30% vs 2%). That's a small-systems story, not a mobile-home story.
**Usable:** yes. This table is the only way to filter water systems by who they serve.

## Traps for the ledger
1. **SDWA service areas is in the IMMIGRATION schema.** It's EPA drinking-water data and has nothing to do with immigration. There's also a same-name object in `LIBRARY_MARTS.TIMELINE` with no row count, probably a view.
2. **SDWA service areas primary flag:** blank on 99,470 rows (23.5%). 62,275 systems have no primary, and 605 have 2-8 "primaries".
3. **SDWA `ORG_NAME` is a contact person** ("MILLIRON, BRENT" on 582 of the NC peer-set systems). Treat it as an operator stand-in, not the owner.
4. **ICE facility types live only in FACILITY_CODES.** The stint table has none. A name join to FACILITY_LIST lands 38%.
5. **CBP Jan 2026 nationwide total is 70 off its parts** (7,992 vs 7,922).
6. **The Swiss catalog is a modified-date slice** with no datasets created after 2023-09-17. `PRIVATE` and `TYPE` are constants.
7. **Catalog summary for FACILITY_LIST claims a duplicate that isn't there.**
