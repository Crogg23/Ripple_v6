## 1. Google political ads by state: official House offices. **live**

### What the table is
- 614,078 rows. One row = one advertiser in one state, cumulative since 2018. No dates.
- 16,102 advertiser IDs, 15,567 names. $2.53B summed.
- The US only: 614,077 US rows, 1 GB row. Not a world map.

### The key: who is an official House office
- The sibling stats table holds the ID each buyer gave Google.
- **114 accounts give the tax ID 53-6002523.** Every one carries a House name, like "REP. LUCY MCBATH - U.S. HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES". Zero exceptions.
- So these are members' official offices, not their campaigns.
  - That the ID is the House's own EIN comes from the names. Not checked against IRS records.
  - Who paid (official funds or not) is not in the data.
- 114 accounts, 4,988 ads, $5.94M by Google's totals. The weekly table sums to $5.98M, 2018-2026.

### Check 5, time: the pre-election blackout
House rules stop members' unsolicited mass communications for a set window before an election they're on the ballot for.
The rule text was not checked here. The data show where the line sits.

Share of each year's official spend that ran in the last 60 or 90 days before the November general election:

| Year | Last 60 days | Last 90 days |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 (election) | 0.9% | **1.1%** |
| 2022 (election) | **2.9%** | 28.7% |
| 2024 (election) | **0.08%** | 36.7% |
| 2019, 2021, 2023, 2025 (same weeks, no federal election) | 10-30% | 14-39% |

- Ads switch off right at the line. The usual rate is 6-8 official ads ending per 4 days:
  - 2020: **152 ads end** in the 4 days up to the 90-day line (Aug 5). 1 at the 60-day line.
  - 2022: **135** end at the 60-day line (Sep 9). 1 at the 90-day line.
  - 2024: **151** end at the 60-day line (Sep 6). 0 at the 90-day line.
- So the window acts like 90 days in 2020 and 60 days from 2022 on. That's a rule change, dated by the data alone.
- The Goldman account's CA-34 ads end Sep 5, 2024, and Steel's MN-8 ad ends Sep 6, 2024. Both sit on the 60-day line.

**Who spent inside the window anyway**

| Account | Election | Spend in window | FEC lists him as that year's candidate? |
|---|---|---|---|
| REP. KEVIN MCCARTHY | 2022 | **$12,400**, Sep 11 - Nov 6. Two new national ads started Sep 29-30, 450K-525K impressions | Yes, CA-20, status C |

- Of 33 official accounts spending in 2022, McCarthy's is the only one listed as a 2022 candidate, and not retiring, that spent in the last 60 days.
- Dull explanation for McCarthy: his account ran national, leadership-style ads. A leader's office may sit under different rules. Not tested.

### Check 6, peer: do members in tight races buy more?
- Denominator: all 451 House members of the 118th Congress.
- "Tight race" stand-in: outside money spent against them in the 2024 cycle (S19).
- Bought official Google ads (2023-26, district-targeted):

| Outside money against, 2024 | Members | Bought | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0 | 333 | 61 | 18% |
| under $100K | 61 | 10 | 16% |
| $100K-1M | 14 | 6 | 43% |
| $1M-5M | 13 | 8 | 62% |
| $5M+ | 30 | 9 | 30% |

- $100K+ against: 23 of 57 buy (40%). Everyone else: 71 of 394 (18%). One-sided Fisher p = 0.0002.
- ⚠ The dollars run the other way. The $0 tier holds $1.72M of $2.69M. Gottheimer alone is $332.5K of it.
- Party: 64 of 221 Democrats buy (29%), 30 of 230 Republicans (13%).

### The single biggest official buy: Josh Gottheimer (NJ-5)
- **$328,300 over 12 spending weeks**, Nov 24, 2024 - Mar 1, 2025. 10 video ads, all aimed at NJ-5, 24.75M-29.25M impressions.
- The median official buyer spent $13,975 over 2023-26. His office is 24x that. Next biggest: Goldman, $114.7K across two accounts.
- Timing: the ads stopped 101 days before the June 10, 2025 New Jersey governor primary. He ran in it (public record, not checked here).
- That's outside any 60- or 90-day blackout. Legal on its face. The story is scale and timing, not a broken rule.

### Hit, miss, dull
- **Hit means:** official money that's hard to trace (generic names, wrong names), growing fast, with buyers bunched in tight races. Plus two members the FEC lists as that year's candidates who ran official ads inside the blackout.
- **Miss means:** clean official spending: home districts, blackouts obeyed, named correctly. That's true of most of it: 90 of 92 named accounts are named right, and 97%+ of each election year's money stays out of the last 60 days.
- **Dull explanations:**
  - Google counts any ad showing a sitting federal officeholder as an election ad. So routine town-hall or casework ads land here. Not tested.
  - Offices may be moving from mail to digital. Can't test without the House's spending reports.
  - The mislabels and generic accounts are most likely a shared vendor or central House account. That's a transparency gap, not wrongdoing.
  - Tight-race members advertise more in every channel.
- **Next join:** the House Statement of Disbursements. It shows each office's paid advertising, the primary record. Check Gottheimer's office for Q4 2024 and Q1 2025, and McCarthy's for Q3-Q4 2022.

## Traps for the ledger
- **Google geo spend rounds every state row UP to the next $100.**
  - 432,631 of 614,078 rows read exactly $100. A $100 row means "more than $0, up to $100".
  - Summing states overstates each buyer. $2,534.6M in this table vs $2,496.6M in the stats table for the same 16,102 IDs.
  - Buyers whose stats total is $100 or less: $2.30M here vs $118.5K in stats (19x). $100-$1K buyers: 4.8x.
  - 935 buyers read exactly $100 on every row they have (up to 57 places). Their median total in the stats table is $100.
  - Out-of-state shares: subtract $100 per row. It moves the median official account from 17.4% to 0.0%.
- **Google geo spend, blank state = unplaced money, not a total.** 11,301 rows, $54.1M. They match the buyer's state sum only 0.5% of the time.
- **Google geo spend is US only.** 614,077 US rows, 1 GB row. The other-currency columns checked (EUR, GBP, INR, AUD, BRL, MXN) are 0 on every row but one.
- **Google geo spend, one name, many accounts.** 16,102 IDs, 15,567 names. "Inmo Khang" is 12 accounts here, with no ID given to Google.
- **Google creative targeting labels carry a map year** ("(2012 redistricting)"). Match districts to members by that map, not the ad date, or old-district ads land on the wrong member.
- **EIA-861 Frame and Mergers: the loader ate the first data row as the header.** Abbeville SC (34) and Energy Harbor Generation (6389) are missing.
- **EIA-860 enviro association STEAM_PLANT_TYPE is a 1-4 code.** Never sum it.
- **FEC committees dim:** C00928036 (Eric Rouse) has a committee ID in its CAND_ID column.
