## 1. POLITICS__MEMBER_VOTING_RECORD: **live**

**What it is:** one row per member per Congress, 118th and 119th only. Votes they could cast, votes they missed.
**The key:** BIOGUIDE (the member's Congress ID). 635 distinct. ICPSR (Voteview's member ID) sits inside it as a list.

### Is the count real?
- Rebuilt every row from Voteview's single votes (945,523 of them).
- **1,105 of 1,105 rows match exactly.** Votes they could cast = Voteview rows. Missed = Voteview "not voting."
- A "present" vote counts as voting, not missing.

### Who is running: the join
- Chain: member name + state → FEC candidate file (Senate and President, 2024 and 2026 cycles) → FEC money totals.
- A hit needed: same state, same last name, first name agreeing or checked by eye, election year = cycle.
- Checked by eye and thrown out: Rob Menendez Jr. landing on his father's Senate ID; a Garcia, a Gonzalez, a Williams, a Schmidt and a Brown landing on strangers.
- Kept after the eye check: Ashley Hinson (filed under ARENHOLZ, ASHLEY HINSON), Buddy Carter (CARTER, EARL LEROY), Mike Collins (COLLINS, MICHAEL A JR).
- Result: **12 Senate runners + Dean Phillips (President) in 2024. 16 Senate runners in 2026.** (Hits kept only where the member was in the House that Congress.) Every one shows FEC money raised, from $1.1M to $94.7M.
- Andy Kim also ran (2024). His 118th row mixes House and Senate votes (see traps), so he's left out.
- Governor races are not in FEC data. Members running for governor sit in "everyone else," which most likely shrinks the gap.

### Peer set
- Delegates (AS, GU, PR, MP, VI, DC) dropped. They vote on a smaller set of roll calls and top the raw list (American Samoa's delegate: 61%).
- Only members who were there for 90% or more of the votes. That drops deaths, resignations and late arrivals.
- 118th: 422 members. 119th (to June 2026): 421.

### 2026 runners: the same people, before and after

| Group | Jan-Nov 2025 | Dec 2025-Jun 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| 16 Senate runners, median member | 1.8% | **3.5%** |
| 16 runners, all their votes pooled | 3.7% | **6.8%** |
| 14 runners without Hunt and Moulton, pooled | 2.0% | **4.3%** |
| 405 other members, median | 2.0% | 1.4% |
| 405 other members, pooled | 2.9% | 3.1% |

Biggest movers (data match):
- **Seth Moulton (MA):** 2.4% in the 118th. 5.9% Jan-Nov 2025. **21.2%** Dec 2025-Jun 2026. Missed 14 of 36 in May 2026, 12 of 36 in June.
- **Jasmine Crockett (TX):** 2.3% → **11.0%**. Missed 11 of 30 in Feb 2026.
- **Julia Letlow (LA):** 3.0% → **10.6%**. Missed 20 of 36 in May 2026.
- **Wesley Hunt (TX):** 25.3% → 27.6%. The worst attendance of 421 full-time voting members in the 119th (155 of 587 missed).
  But he was already at 16.4% in the 118th. That's a long habit, not just the campaign. Missed 43 of 47 in Jan 2026.
- Not everyone: Chris Pappas (NH) missed 0 of 587. Harriet Hageman (WY) missed 2.

### 2024: does it replicate?
13 runners. Three windows. Median member's missed rate:

| Window | Ran in 2024 (13) | Left the House, not running (48) | Stayed (361) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan-Nov 2023 | 2.3% | 1.4% | 1.2% |
| **Dec 2023-May 2024** | **8.3%** | 4.0% | 1.9% |
| Jun-Dec 2024 | 5.6% | 6.7% | 2.1% |

- Primary season is where runners separate. After June, everyone leaving catches up. That's lame-duck behavior, not campaigning.
- Without Phillips (27.8% for the Congress), runners' pooled rate still goes 3.6% → 10.1% → 9.6%.
- Whole Congress: runners median 5.0%; leavers 3.8%; stayers 2.2%.
- Dean Phillips (data match): missed 118 of 131 in Nov 2023, 29 of 29 in Jan 2024 (the month of the New Hampshire primary).
- The spikes line up with state primary months. David Trone (MD): 38 of 61 in May 2024. Katie Porter (CA): 19 of 45 in March 2024.
- Counter-examples: Colin Allred raised $94.7M and missed 14 of 1,235. Elissa Slotkin missed 7.

### The dull explanation, tested
- "Members who run were always absent more." ➔ Tested with the same people before and after. 2026 runners were *below* average in 2025 (1.8% vs 2.0%).
- "They're leaving, and leavers check out." ➔ Tested against 48 departing non-runners. Runners separate in Dec-May, not after.
- "Two people carry it." ➔ Dropped Hunt and Moulton (2026) and Phillips (2024). Effect holds.
- "Campaigning costs votes" is a known genre (GovTrack posts missed-vote lists). The new part is the 2026 before/after with peers, from FEC filings.

**Hit means:** a House member running statewide is measurably absent in the months their primary is live. Same pattern in two cycles.
**Miss would have meant:** no gap once leavers and baselines are controlled. It didn't miss.

## 2. POLITICS__MEMBER_BILL_RECORD: probed

**What it is:** bills each member sponsored, moved and passed, per Congress (118th, 119th).
**Checks run:** rebuilt "advanced" from the bill-level table; second source for the top sponsor; majority vs minority; concentration.

### Trap: "advanced past committee" counts a subcommittee referral
118th Congress, bills flagged advanced, by what actually happened last:

| What happened last | House bills (HR) | Senate bills (S) |
|---|---|---|
| Sent to a subcommittee, nothing after | **3,448** | 0 |
| Subcommittee hearing | 104 | 0 |
| Other committee action | 720 | 536 |
| Reached the floor | 521 | 208 |
| Became law / passed both | 178 | 93 |

- Referral to a subcommittee is paperwork. The Senate doesn't do it, so Senate bills never get the free credit.
- That's why the median House member "advances" 50% of bills and the median senator 14.8%.
- Take the referrals out: 14.4% of House bills advanced, 14.8% of Senate bills. Same thing.
- **ADVANCED_RATE and ADVANCED_PAST_COMMITTEE_COUNT can't compare members across chambers, or House members against each other.**

### Top sponsor: Andy Biggs, 612 bills in the 118th (data match)
- Two sources agree: the Congress.gov bill table and GovInfo both list 604 House bills, 2 joint resolutions, 6 simple resolutions.
- **523 of them were introduced in March 2023,** with only 3 distinct title openings: "To provide for a limitation on availability of funds for [office] for fiscal year 2024." One bill per budget line.
- 189 of his 204 "advanced" bills were only sent to a subcommittee. Became law: 0.
- He's 5% of all House bills that Congress on his own. Any "most prolific" ranking is his cloned series.

### Peers
- 118th, laws per 100 bills sponsored: House majority 1.8, minority 1.0. Senate majority 1.3, minority 1.4.
- Laws are concentrated: in the 118th House, 135 of 444 members had any law; top 10% of members hold 50% of laws.
- Nothing story-grade: "prolific sponsor passes nothing" is the messaging-bill norm, and effectiveness scores already exist.

**Hit would have meant:** a member passing far more or less than same-chamber, same-party peers. Nothing beyond the known.

## 3. POLITICS__CA_LOBBY_CHG_LOG: probed

**What it is:** 85,765 changes to California lobbying registrations (CAL-ACCESS). Who added or dropped a firm, a client, a lobbyist.
**Checks run:** time series by action, dates, flags, revocations, duplicates. One load run for all rows.

### Trap: from 2021, the log stops recording drops

| Year | Added | Deleted | Terminated | In suspense |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | 2,154 | 1,418 | 327 | 16 |
| 2019 | 636 | 678 | 130 | 28 |
| 2020 | 981 | 1,210 | 140 | 11 |
| 2021 | 1,050 | **0** | 5 | 0 |
| 2022 | 702 | **0** | 3 | 1 |
| 2023 | 1,432 | **0** | 0 | 0 |
| 2024 | 1,315 | 18 | 3 | 2 |
| 2025 | 1,439 | **0** | 2 | 5 |

- 2021-2026 clients: 2,906 added, 13 deleted. Clients don't stop leaving; the log stopped logging it.
- "CHANGED" after 2020 is mostly responsible-officer swaps (3,128 of them).
- **"Who got dropped" works for 2000-2020 only.**

### Other traps
- 25 impossible log dates (years like 0001, 0101, 1001, 7002, 0202, 1515, 0156). They're typos for 2001-02, 2013, 2015, 2017-18. EFFECT_DT has the real year.
- 13 rows are shifted: dates blank, and 7 of them have a firm name in ACTION ("PLATINUM ADVISORS, LLC").
- ACTION is free text, 43 distinct values (WITHDREW, WITHDRAWAL, WITHDRAWN, WITJDREW, WITHRAWAL; TERMINATED, TEMINATED).
- CORRECTION_FLG is "Y" on 63,777 of 85,765 rows (74%). It can't mean "this is a correction." A few rows hold stray ID numbers.
- 2,206 of 2,946 suspensions are from 2001; the busiest days are in March and April. Looks like session paperwork, not enforcement. The table doesn't say.
- "REVOKE": 357 rows, all on filer type 4 (the type that holds people's names). Logged 2010-2017 (one in 2001), none after. 274 take effect July 1. Looks like an administrative cycle; no reason is given. Not a misconduct list.

**Hit would have meant:** a clean drop series to rank firms or clients by losses. Only through 2020.

## 4. POLITICS__CA_LOBBY_FIRM: dead

**What it is:** 256 lobbying firms, one session (2001-02), money by quarter.
**Checks run:** quarters vs totals; fill pattern; one join to the cover-page table (the firms' own filings).

- The join: firm ID → cover-page filer ID lands only 21 of 256 (8%). But for the 11 zero-first-half firms that land, **all 11 filed their quarterly reports (form F625) for Q1 and Q2 2002.** They filed; the amounts didn't make it into this table.

- CONTRIBUTOR_ID is 0 on all 253 non-blank rows. 15 firms total $0.
- **Ranking firms by SESSION_TOTAL_AMT compares a half-year with a full year.** Broken for the one thing it's for.

## Traps for the ledger
1. **MEMBER_FEC_ID only holds Senate IDs for House members who won.** 2024: it has the 7 winners (Gallego, Schiff, Kim, Slotkin, Banks, Curtis, Blunt Rochester), none of the 6 who lost. 2026: 0 of 16 runners. It has no presidential ID for Phillips. A miss there doesn't mean "didn't run."
2. FEC candidate names don't match Congress names: ARENHOLZ, ASHLEY HINSON; CARTER, EARL LEROY. Name + state also lands sons on fathers (Rob Menendez Jr. → Sen. Bob Menendez).
3. MEMBER_VOTING_RECORD: 4 members switched chambers mid-Congress; their House and Senate votes sit in one row under one label. Andy Kim's 118th "Senate" row holds 1,210 House votes and 23 Senate votes.
4. MEMBER_VOTING_RECORD: delegates sit in the House rows with a smaller vote set (538 vs 1,235). They top any raw missed-vote ranking.
5. MEMBER_BILL_RECORD / BILLS: "advanced past committee" includes a bare subcommittee referral. 3,448 of 4,971 House bills (118th).
6. BILLS: Biggs's 523 cloned funding-limit bills (March 2023) dominate any sponsor count.
7. CA_LOBBY_CHG_LOG: deletions, terminations and suspensions stop being logged in 2021.
8. CA_LOBBY_CHG_LOG: CORRECTION_FLG is "Y" on 74%; 25 typo years in LOG_DT; 13 shifted rows.
9. CA_LOBBY_FIRM: 2001 quarters are empty; 147 firms missing the first half of 2002 though they filed; one firm is an even split.
10. FED_FEC_API: a 500-row test pull, one day.

parked: governor runs (Sherrill, Swalwell, Mace, Stefanik, Bennet, Tuberville, Blackburn are widely reported) aren't in any FEC table. A state candidate list would make the 2026 comparison complete. It would likely widen the gap.
