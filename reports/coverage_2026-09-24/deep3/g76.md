## 1. NFIP community status book: towns out of the flood program, and the floods that hit them. **probed**

**Checked** (S05, S06, S08, local)
- 25,125 communities, 25,125 distinct IDs. 22,785 are in the flood program. **2,340 are out.** Every one of the 2,340 has a flood map date.
- **The sanction date is hiding in the entry-date column.** For 2,068 of the 2,340, the "regular/emergency program date" falls exactly one year after the first flood map. One year after the map is when FEMA's sanctions start.
  - Sanctioned in 1974-79: 868. In 2010-13 (the map-modernization wave): 540. Since 2020: 149.
  - Median time out: 24.3 years. 1,095 have been out 20 years or more.
- **Peer, by state** (share of mapped communities that are out). National is 9.5%. The median state is 8.1%.
  - Michigan has the most in raw count: 197 (15.7%). Missouri 194 (21.9%). Iowa 141. Texas 137. Oklahoma 122 (23.2%). North Dakota 121 (26.0%).
- **Map age, participating towns:** 1,880 of 20,937 (9.0%) are on maps 30+ years old. The median state has 2.95% of its towns on maps that old.
  - Idaho 42.1% (67 of 159), Vermont 41.0% (98 of 239), New York 36.9% (529 of 1,433).

**One join: FEMA household disaster aid** (S07, S09, S12-S15)
- The aid table: 26,250,920 rows = 26,250,920 distinct registrations. So there are no duplicate registrations.
- Flood-damaged rows: 3,078,130. They were aggregated first, then joined to the book on state + town name + county. All three have to agree.
  - 1,787,473 land (58.1%) on a city, town, village or borough. 523 ambiguous name keys were dropped.
- Strict timing: the disaster was declared **after** both the town's sanction date and its current map.

| Same 107 disasters | Owner registrations | Had flood insurance | Median town | Towns with zero insured |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 280 out-of-program towns | 3,993 | **3.7%** | 0% | 69.2% |
| Participating towns | 640,298 | 14.5% | 5.6% | 33.8% |

- **Size-matched** (towns binned by how many flood-damaged owners they had in that disaster). Out-of-program towns run 2.2-5.9%. Participating towns run 8.7-14.9%. Out-of-program is lower in all 5 bins.
- **Concentration:** the top 4 towns hold 32.2% of the registrations. The median town sits at 0%, so the effect is broad.
- **Time:** in 2002-16 it was 4.1% vs 11.2%. In 2017-26, 3.4% vs 17.1%. The gap widened.
- **Money:** FEMA paid home-repair grants to 3,090 of the 3,993 owners, $13.8M in all.
  - That's 77.4% of owners, against 71.2% in participating towns. The average grant is $4,468 vs $5,717.
- **FEMA's own denial code** "ISC - Sanctioned Community in SFHA" is on 246 of the 3,078,130 flood-damaged registrations, 2002-2026.
  - 59 of them land on an out-of-program town. 66 sit in a county whose unincorporated area is out.
  - Most of the rest land in towns that have been in the program for decades. Those households are likely just outside the town line.
- **Case, Belleville, Michigan** (Wayne County): first map 2012-02-02, sanctioned 2013-02-02.
  - Three floods since then: 43, 31 and 339 flood-damaged owners.
  - 12 were insured. FEMA paid $1.23M in repair grants.
  - In the 2024 disaster, FEMA marked 6 Belleville registrations "sanctioned community in SFHA".
- **Eyeball (S15):** the Belleville repair grants are $528 to $4,000. The high-water code is "B", read here as basement; the code list wasn't checked.
  - Eutawville, SC, 2015: 217 flood-damaged registrations for a town that's small (from memory, check it). All 4 sampled rows are ZIP 29048.

**Hit means:** once a town opts out, almost nobody in it has flood insurance when the water comes. Disaster grants, averaging $4,468, become the whole safety net. The law bars federal building aid in the flood zones of sanctioned towns: the Flood Disaster Protection Act, section 202(a) (from memory, check it). FEMA's own code name says the same. That code fires rarely.

**Miss means:** most flood-damaged homes in these towns sit outside the mapped flood zone. The sanction rule doesn't cover them, and few owners outside the zone buy flood insurance anywhere. Then there's no enforcement story.

**Boring:**
- **By design.** You can't buy federal flood insurance in a non-participating town. The low insured share is the rule showing up in the data. **Tested** against size-matched peers, and the gap holds. But the gap itself isn't news.
- **Postal city isn't the town line.** The aid table gives a mailing city. Belleville's and Eutawville's mailing areas reach past the town limits.
  - This pads town counts. It pushes the insured gap **toward zero**, because the county areas around a town are often in the program.
  - **Not fixable in the warehouse**: there is no ZIP-to-town or block-group-to-place file.
- Basement water from rain looks like most of the Michigan damage. That's outside flood-zone rules.
- **Not tested:** whether any repair grant went to a home inside a mapped flood zone in a sanctioned town. That's the only way this becomes an accountability story. It needs the flood-zone map layer, which isn't in the warehouse.

**Traps (ledger, not story)**
- `SFHA_DISCOUNT_PCT` and `NON_SFHA_DISCOUNT_PCT` are null on all 25,125 rows (known).
- **Two-digit years parsed forward:**
  - `INITIAL_FLOOD_INSURANCE_RATE_MAP_DATE` reads 2069 on 3 rows: Fairbanks North Star AK, Alexandria VA, Arlington County VA. Those are almost surely 1969.
  - `INITIAL_FLOOD_HAZARD_BOUNDARY_MAP_DATE` reads 2069-08-22 on Alexandria. That's where the glance's "to 2069" comes from.
- `CURRENTLY_EFFECTIVE_MAP_DATE` reads 2050-01-02 on 4 rows. FEMA's raw text says `01/02/50`, a placeholder.
  - Another 221 rows sit in 2026-10 to 2027-01. Those are real pending maps.
- The raw map date carries codes the parsed date drops:
  - (M) on 4,374 rows. (NSFHA) on 1,671. (L) 315. (>) 208. (All Zone D) 9. Blank 169.
- The "entry date" means **sanction date** for out-of-program towns, and program entry for towns in the program. Same column, two meanings.
- **Aid table:** `FIPS` lost leading zeros (4 digits for states under 10) and is blank on 6.77M rows. `HA_STATUS` is blank on 11% of 2025 flood rows.
- **Aid table:** `INCIDENT_TYPE_CODE` carries digit codes 2, 4 and 5 (for example 418,162 rows of "4" in 2024-26). They need a lookup.
- **Map age didn't hold up as a lead.** Within the same disaster, towns on 30+ year-old maps had lower insured shares in 27 of 42 disasters. The pooled 14.4% vs 24.1% is driven by a few big Texas disasters. Not claimed.

## 2. FHFA house price index. **probed**

**Checked** (S10, local)
- 184,807 rows. 16 series (type x flavor x frequency x level). 410 metros. Latest quarter is 2026 Q1.
- No duplicate keys on type + flavor + frequency + level + place + year + period.
- **Peer, metro vs the rest of its state, 2026 Q1** (all-transactions, not seasonally adjusted, so year over year uses the same quarter):

| Metro | Off post-2020 peak | Year over year | Same-state metros, median year over year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos, TX (peak 2022 Q2) | **-12.0%** | -0.8% | +2.1% (25 metros, median 0.0% off peak) |
| Punta Gorda, FL (peak 2023 Q3) | -11.6% | **-7.3%** | +1.7% (24 metros) |
| Cape Coral-Fort Myers, FL (peak 2023 Q2) | -8.4% | -2.7% | +1.7% |

- National: the median metro is +3.7% on the year. 27 of 410 fell. 9 are 5%+ off their peak.
- Small metros that peaked in late 2025 and dipped in 2026 Q1 (Brunswick GA, Hammond LA) are one or two quarters of noise. Not claimed.

**Hit means:** Austin and SW Florida are the country's two big post-boom slides.

**Miss means:** n/a. It's FHFA's published index, and these declines have been widely reported.

**Boring:** the pandemic run-up unwinding, plus storm and insurance costs in SW Florida after Ian. Not tested here. It needs a county-to-metro file to join FEMA damage to metros.

**Traps**
- **Flavor changes the answer.** On "expanded-data", 92 metros fell and 33 are 5%+ off peak, against 27 and 9 on "all-transactions". Austin reads -19.6% vs -12.0%. Say which flavor, every time.
- The index values are stored as text. 2 are blank: FHFA suppressed them for too few sales.
- `NOTE` is a bare tab character on 57,356 rows. 452 rows carry the "fewer than 1,000 repeat sales" note.
- `INDEX_SA` is blank on 89,372 rows. All-transactions has no seasonally adjusted series.
- The TIMELINE copy types the index as FLOAT. The mart keeps TEXT.
