## 1. JUSTICE__FED_FJC_IDB_APPELLATE: **live**

**What it is:** 988,183 rows. One row per federal appeal **closed** in a fiscal year (TAPE_YEAR 2008-2026), plus **35,267** appeals still open on 31 Mar 2026 (TAPE_YEAR **2099**, a pending file, not a year) [q01].
Inside each yearly file, one row = one appeal (circuit + docket + reopen number): rows = distinct appeals every year.
The data runs through **2026-03-31**. Closed appeals per year fell from 60,508 (FY2009) to 40,086 (FY2024).

**The key move:** NATURE_OF_SUIT **463** is "habeas corpus, alien detainee": a person in immigration detention asking a federal judge to order release or a bond hearing.

### What a hit means / what a miss means
- **Hit:** a checked, dated series from the federal courts' own records. One in four new federal civil cases. Detainees winning 9 in 10. And a government that files appeals in bulk and drops them within weeks, heaviest in two courts.
- **Miss:** if the JUDGMENT codes meant something else, the 90% flips. The join direction (government appeals after code-1 cases) makes that unlikely, but the codebook has to confirm it before print.
- **Hostile-editor caveat:** the habeas surge itself got news coverage in late 2025. The new pieces are the FJC count through March 2026, the 24% share, and the filed-then-dropped appeals.

### Traps (appellate)
- **TAPE_YEAR 2099 = the pending file** (35,267 open appeals), not a year. It has no JUDGMENT_DATE.
- **US_APPELLANT misses federal officials.** 9th Circuit: 1 of 132 detention appeals flagged, but name-matching finds 91 government appeals. 7th Circuit: 38 flagged vs 61. Use the names plus the trial-court join, not the flag.
- **AGENCY holds two code systems in one column:** numbers (-9 none, 6 = immigration board by its appellees HOLDER/GARLAND, 1, 3, 7, 8) and letter codes (BRB, FCC, DHS, SEC...).
- **OUTCOME / DISPOSITION need decoding.** From the data [q10]: DISPOSITION 1/2 = decided on the merits (OUTCOME filled), 4/5 = closed without a ruling (OUTCOME -8). -8 on OUTCOME is 460,644 rows.
- **The glance's "DOCKET 1116673 holds 1.6K rows" doesn't reproduce.** No circuit + docket has more than 8 rows [q02].

## 3. JUSTICE__FED_COURTLISTENER_PARENTHETICALS: probed

**What it is:** 6,408,887 short "holding that..." summaries one opinion writes about another [q07].
- Every ID is distinct. 185 rows repeat the same (described, describing, text) triple.
- SCORE is stored as text, but every value parses, 0 to 1.
- **No date, no court, no case name.** 1,203,305 described opinions, 1,852,737 describing opinions.
- The warehouse has **no CourtListener OPINIONS table** [q05]. Opinion IDs can't be named directly.

### Trap check: the only naming route breaks for a quarter of rows [q06b, q23]
The tempting move is DESCRIBED_OPINION_ID = OPINION_CLUSTERS.ID.
- **Old IDs: right.** 12 checked, and the text fits the case every time: *Kentucky v. Graham* (official-capacity suits), *Hill v. Lappin* (Twombly/Iqbal in prisoner screening), *Pearson v. Callahan* (overruling Saucier's sequence), *U.S. v. Dixon*, *State Oil v. Khan*, *Hart v. Massanari*, *M.A.P. v. Ryan*...
- **New IDs (≥ 9.4M): wrong, 17 of 17 checked.** The most-described opinion (15,330 parentheticals quoting "threadbare recitals", which is *Ashcroft v. Iqbal*) lands on *Boyd v. Central Coast Community Energy* (2023). The deficiency-and-prejudice test (Strickland's) lands on a California case.
- The giveaway on all 17: each is described 2,246-15,330 times, yet the cluster it lands on has a CITATION_COUNT of 0-4.
- Rows describing an ID ≥ 9M: **1,640,768 (25.6%)**, covering 131,335 opinions. IDs between 8M and 9.4M aren't checked.
- The "overruled" angle: 15,551 texts say "overrul", 7,785 "abrogat". The top ones are boilerplate ("only the Supreme Court can overrule its own precedent"). Nothing to rank.

**Hit means** a text lookup for how courts summarize an old case. **Miss:** no time series is possible (no dates), and names are unsafe for new IDs.

8. **CourtListener oral arguments: capture gaps by year** (Federal Circuit 2022 = 38), JUDGES mostly blank outside D.C., and Illinois state courts are mixed in.
