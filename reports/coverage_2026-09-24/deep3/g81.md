## 1. DOJ Epstein page log: 11 data-set pages "access denied", not "not found". **live**

**What the table is**
- 203,305 rows. Each row is one Internet Archive capture of a justice.gov/epstein page whose content or status changed.
- Captures run 2025-12-19 to 2026-06-04.
- 28,186 addresses, and 27,409 of them are the page-by-page file lists for data sets 9, 10 and 11 (S22, S32).

**Checked**
- Status mix (S08): 172,837 rows are "content changed", 200 → 200.
  - **159,894 of those (92.5%) moved 50 bytes or less** (S12). That's page churn, not edits.
  - The 96 changes over 2,000 bytes are a ±2.2 KB template toggle that flips back within a day (S19).
- **What a 403 ("access denied") looks like on this site (S29, S30):**
  - 1,163 rows answered 403. Median size: **937 bytes**, a bare block page from the site's front gate.
  - **Only 12 rows were a full-size DOJ page (10,456-10,574 bytes).** For comparison, DOJ's own 404 "not found" page is 10,431-10,447 bytes.
  - 11 of the 12 are data sets 13 through 23. The 12th is data set 9.
- **The probe, 2026-03-11 (S29):** someone's script asked for data-set-13 through data-set-40 in order, about one per second.

| Data sets | Answer | Size | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13-23 (11 pages) | **403, full DOJ page** | 10,456-10,462 B | 19:26:54-19:27:04 |
| 24-40 (17 pages) | 404, full DOJ page | 10,431-10,447 B | 19:27:04-19:27:19 |

  - Data set 23 (403) and 24 (404) came back **in the same second**.
  - The next captures in the log, 17 seconds later, were normal 200 pages (root, data set 8, court records). Nothing was blocking the crawler.
- **Time, the control case: data set 9 (S31).**
  - 2025-12-23 22:22: full-page 403, 10,574 bytes.
  - Next capture, 906 hours later, 2026-01-30 16:30: **200, live**. That was the day DOJ published data sets 9-11.
  - So on this site, the one full-page 403 we can follow was a data-set page that existed but wasn't public yet.
- **Could the table have missed 13-23 going live? (S32)**
  - Data sets 1-12 each have live (200) captures. First seen live: Dec 19-20 (1-7), Dec 23 (8), Jan 30-31 (9-12). Data sets 10 and 11 were first captured at 16:30 and 16:31 on Jan 30.
  - Data sets 13-40 have **one capture each, zero 200s**, through June 4.
  - The archive watched this site closely. If 13-23 had gone public before June 4, they would very likely show here.

**Hit means:** by March 11, 2026, six weeks after the last public release, DOJ's site knew about data sets 13-23 and kept them closed. Data sets 24+ didn't exist. So 11 more data sets were built and not published. If DOJ has called the Jan 30 release complete, this contradicts it.

**Miss means:** if the addresses now answer 404, or DOJ says they were empty placeholders, it's website scaffolding, not withheld files.

**Boring:**
- **A crawler block. Tested:** blocks here are 937-byte pages. These are 10.46 KB DOJ pages. The 403-to-404 switch happened inside one second, and the crawler got 200s right after.
- **The site answers 403 for any address under that path. Tested:** 24-40 are under the same path and got 404.
- **They went live and were pulled before March 11.** Partly tested: zero live captures from Dec 19 on, on a site archived within hours of each release. A short, unarchived window can't be ruled out.
- **The 403 means "unpublished page."** That's how Drupal-style sites usually behave, and data set 9 fits it. **Not confirmed** against DOJ's actual site software.
- **Not checked:** news, DOJ statements, and whether data sets 13+ went live after June 4. The table ends June 4.

**Next step (outside the warehouse):** load the 11 addresses on the live site today, and pull their Wayback CDX history after June 4.

**Traps**
- ⚠ **CHANGE_KIND "content_changed" is noise.** It fires on 85% of rows, and 92.5% of those changes are 50 bytes or less.
- ⚠ **403 means two different things here.** A 937-byte gate block (1,151 rows) or a full DOJ page (12 rows). Sort by size before reading any 403.
- 669 of the 761 addresses that ever answered 401, 403 or 404 answered 200 again later (S13). Most errors are temporary.
- The address count is padded: tracking tags (`?utm_source=chatgpt.com`), pasted text, `%0a`, and fake page numbers like `page=1717171717171717`. 21 of the 34 variants of the root page are junk.
- The court-record pages renamed to hide plaintiff names (Giuffre, Farmer, Bryant, Edwards...) are here too, 404 on 2026-03-05. **Already found** from the sibling table OPEN_DATA__XC_WAYBACK_DOJ_EPSTEIN. Not new.
- Six Maxwell trial-exhibit subpages and `/review-protocol` were first seen as 404 on 03-05. They were never seen live, so you can't call them removed.

## 2. Intra-American slave voyages: second voyages to Cuba and the Gulf. **probed**

**Checked**
- Shape (S06): 11,521 rows, **11,521 distinct VOYAGEIDs**, one file, one load run. Years 1550-1841.
  - Estimated embarked: 456,132. Estimated landed: 439,295.
- **Place codes need a codebook, and it isn't loaded.** I labeled the codes from the archive each row cites (S14, S15). These labels are my inference, not the codebook:
  - 31300 Cuba (Klein Havana dataset, Archivo Nacional de Cuba)
  - 21300 South Carolina (CO 5/509-510 shipping lists; its African arrivals spike in 1804-07)
  - 21600 Gulf coast / Louisiana (Le Glaunec, "William Jones NO dataset")
  - 35100 Jamaica (CO 142, Jamaica shipping lists)
  - 21100 Virginia and 21000 Maryland (the Walsh databases)
  - 42000 Río de la Plata (Uruguay national archive)
- **Peer, the region's own arrivals (S15):** people landed by a second voyage, compared with people landed straight from Africa (the transatlantic table).

| Region | Years | Second voyage | Straight from Africa | Second-voyage share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cuba | 1760-1807 | 98,558 | 83,239 | **54%** |
| Cuba | 1808+ | 7,550 | 671,452 | 1% |
| Gulf coast | 1760-1807 | 8,525 | 4,090 | **68%** |
| Spanish mainland (41200) | 1760-1807 | 13,142 | 4,296 | 75% |
| South Carolina | 1760-1807 | 12,245 | 100,432 | 11% |
| Virginia | 1700-59 | 6,557 | 65,554 | 9% |

- **Time, South Carolina → Cuba (S16):**

| Years | Voyages SC → Cuba | People landed in Cuba | Straight from Africa into SC, same years |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1783-1803 (21 years) | 29 | 858 | 9,630 |
| 1804 | 10 | 89 | 6,208 |
| 1805 | 32 | 490 | 7,561 |
| 1806 | 28 | 1,124 | 13,318 |
| 1807 | 50 | **2,856** | 18,879 |

  - 1804-07 total: 4,559 re-shipped to Cuba, which is 9.9% of the 45,966 landed in SC from Africa.
- **Peer, Cuba's second-voyage arrivals 1804-07 (S23):** 16,914 people.
  - SC: 4,559 (27%). Of the arrivals with a known origin: 52%.
  - Every other US region together: 34.
- **Concentration (S17):** the SC → Cuba route 1800-10 had 127 voyages. 94 of them list no owner. The top named owner, "Drake", has 14 voyages and 731 people. Not verified.

**Hit means:** a strong history explainer. When South Carolina reopened the African trade in 1804-07, Charleston became a transit port into Cuba, and 1807 alone was bigger than the 21 years before it.

**Miss means:** it's been published. Historians covered this route and the second-voyage shares, so there's no new fact here, just a checked chart.

**Boring:**
- These are the database editors' own research results (O'Malley and Borucki; McMillin on Charleston; Klein on Havana). **Not checked** against their published numbers.
- **One source drives the Cuba count. Tested:** the transatlantic table spikes on its own in 1804-07 (125 voyages into SC in 1807, against 1-3 a year in 1796-1803), so the timing isn't just one archive.

**Miss on the triage question:** "Domestic US slave shipping after 1808" **can't be answered here.**
- Only 39 North American voyages after 1808, 2,029 people.
- The New Orleans coastwise rows are 1831 only.

**Traps**
- ⚠ **The people counts are mostly editors' estimates (S18).** 7,218 of 11,521 rows (63%) have no recorded embarked count.
  - On 2,199 of the 3,605 rows with a recorded embarked count but no landed count, landed equals embarked. Zero deaths is assumed.
- Recorded deaths (VYMRTRAT) exist on only 649 rows.
- The battery's "VOYAGEID top: 113285 appears 85 times" is **wrong**. Checked: every ID appears once (S06, S07).
- DATE_BUY1 is filled on 1 row. Use YEARAM for time.
- The landing region is blank on 955 rows (6,551 people).
- No place-name codebook is loaded. Every region label above comes from source citations.

## 5. Revolving Door Project jobs. **skip**

**Checked (S04, S10, S21, S27)**
- 405 rows, 405 distinct jobs.
  - 190 Senate-confirmed, 213 appointive, 2 say `nan`.
  - 56 departments.
- Each row is a **job** (e.g., Attorney General, Director of OFAC) with the industries that job touches. **There are no people.**
- Industries per job: 1-16, median 2 for appointive, 3 for Senate-confirmed.
- Top industries: Finance-Private Equity (177 jobs), Real Estate (148), Finance-Banking (143), Agriculture/Big Food (133), Energy (119).

**Traps**
- ⚠ **The catalog summary is wrong.** It says the industries are "the ones the person in it came from". The descriptions show the industries a job oversees, and no person is named.
- ⚠ **SECTOR_COUNT = 16 on every row.** It counts slots, not ties.
- INDUSTRY_SECTOR is just a copy of SECTOR1 (405 of 405), and SECTOR1 is **not the main tie**:
  - The slots aren't alphabetical (only 112 of 255 multi-industry jobs have SECTOR1 < SECTOR2).
  - SECTOR2 is scored higher than SECTOR1 on 69 of 255 jobs, if 3 means strongest. The scale isn't documented.
- There's no SECTOR13, and a second SECTOR14 column called SECTOR14_1.
- Blanks are the text `nan`, not NULL.

Useful only if a list of who holds each job gets loaded. The catalog has no appointee or Plum Book table.
