## 1. Missouri registry: one chain's nursing homes house the registrants. **live**

### What the table is (S01, S02)
- 28,167 rows, but only **21,459 people** (name + birth date). One row per person per offense.
- One load, 2026-08-22.
- Addresses:
  - 17,609 people have a street address.
  - 2,555 say "Incarcerated in MO".
  - 647 say "Moved Out of State".
  - 321 say "UNKNOWN".
  - 327 are blank.
- ⚠ **Trap:** `IS_COMPLIANT` is false for **every** incarcerated, moved or UNKNOWN person. That's why the glance read 22% noncompliant.
  - People at a street address: 970 of 17,609 are noncompliant (5.5%).

### The join (S07 + S08)
- Registry street address matched to the 487 CMS-certified Missouri nursing homes (CMS file dated 2026-05-01).
- Key: house number + first street word + 5-digit ZIP. The ZIP always agrees.
- **Land:** 287 people at **87 of 487 homes**.
  - The city also agrees for 282.
  - The other 5 give a suburb name for the same place: Chesterfield vs Town and Country, Black Jack vs Florissant.
- **Eyeballed:** many list a room number at the home's own address.
  - "2800 HWY TT RM 201", Sedalia = Four Seasons Living Center
  - "11515 TROOST AVE RM 102", Kansas City = Bridgewood
  - "52435 INFIRMARY RD RM 202", Milan = Milan Health Care Center
- Statewide that's 8.2 registrants per 1,000 nursing-home residents.

### Who the 287 are
- Median age **65** (middle half 59-74). Other registrants at a street address: 54.
- 77 are under 60, and 15 are under 50.
- Tier 3 (the most serious tier): 237 (83%). Other registrants: 75%.
- About 135 have an offense worded as against a child. That's a rough keyword match (child, minor, statutory, incest).
- 58 (20%) are flagged noncompliant. At other street addresses it's 5.3%.
  - 17 of those 58 list Parkview Healthcare, Kansas City (see below).

### Peer test 1: is it just that Reliant takes psychiatric residents? (S12)
The dull explanation: g00 found Reliant runs Missouri's schizophrenia-heavy homes. Registrants may just follow the psychiatric placements.
The peer group is homes where 40%+ of residents are coded with schizophrenia (MDS item I6000).

| Psych-heavy homes (40%+) | Homes | With any registrant | Registrants | Per 1,000 residents |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reliant | 21 | 10 | 96 | **46.7** (compliant only: 39.4) |
| Everyone else | 21 | 7 | 26 | **16.1** (compliant only: 11.2) |

- Reliant has 56% of the residents in this peer group and 79% of its registrants. p ≈ 1e-7.
- ⚠ **But** Reliant homes aren't more likely to have *any* registrant: 10 of 21 vs 7 of 21 (Fisher p = 0.53).
- Drop Reliant's four biggest (Four Seasons, North Village Park, Bridgewood, Bernard) and the rest run at **13.8** per 1,000. The other psych-heavy homes run at 16.1.
- **So:** the chain gap is those four homes. Across the chain, psychiatric placement explains the rest.

**Hit means:** a few Missouri nursing homes, mostly one chain's, work as the state's de facto housing for registered sex offenders. The registrants' median age is 65. Most nursing-home residents are far older (outside knowledge, not tested here). The homes are 1-star, abuse-flagged and candidates for the federal watch list. That's a story about who gets placed next to frail elderly residents, and who's paid to take them.

**Miss means:** if the registry addresses are stale (people died or left), the counts drop. Parkview is the warning: 17 of its 22 are flagged noncompliant, which may mean they're gone. That's why the compliant count is shown.

**Boring:**
- Psychiatric specialization. **Tested** (peer test 1): it explains the chain, not the four homes.
- Size. **Tested**: the citation gap holds inside one size band.
- Registrants are old and need care. **Partly true**: median age 65. But 77 are under 60.
- Prison and state-hospital discharge placements. **Not tested**, not in the warehouse. St Joe Manor sits in Bonne Terre (a state prison town) and North Village Park in Moberly (another). This is outside knowledge. Check it.
- **Can't tie any citation to a registrant.** The deficiency table has no narrative. The next step is the inspection reports (CMS Form 2567) for North Village Park, Bridgewood and Four Seasons.

## 2. Supreme Court Database: the Court stopped hearing state-court cases. **live**

g23 already used this table for the 5th Circuit. This is a different cut.

### What the table is (S06, S10)
- 83,644 rows = one justice's vote in one case.
- 9,341 cases. Terms 1946-2024. Last decision 2025-06-30. One load.
- VOTE_ID is unique. CASE_ID and DOCKET_ID have the same count, so it's one docket per case.
- Argued decisions (decision types 1, 6, 7): 8,157.
- "From a state court" = SCDB source code 300-302 (state high court, appeals court, trial court). The state column alone would add 94 cases whose source code isn't a state court (codes 51, 55, 66, 96 and so on, likely federal district courts in that state). So it's not used.

### Time: share of argued cases from state courts (S10)

| Terms | Argued | From state courts | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1946-1968 | 2,555 | 697 | 27.3% |
| 1969-1985 | 2,404 | 524 | 21.8% |
| 1986-2004 | 1,869 | 362 | 19.4% |
| 2005-2009 | 368 | 55 | 14.9% |
| 2010-2014 | 352 | 40 | 11.4% |
| 2015-2019 | 314 | 51 | 16.2% |
| **2020-2024** | **295** | **19** | **6.4%** |

- 2020-24 is the **lowest five-term window in 79 terms**. The next lowest that doesn't overlap it ends in 2014: 11.4%.
- By single term, 2024, 2020, 2021 and 2023 are the 1st, 2nd, 4th and 5th lowest of 79. 2014 is 3rd, 2022 is 8th.
- 2015-19 vs 2020-24: Fisher p = 0.0001.
- Criminal procedure from state courts: 20 argued cases, then 6. All criminal-procedure cases: 69, then 56.

### The join that tests the dull explanation (S13)
Dull explanation: state courts just stopped sending petitions.
CourtListener's Supreme Court dockets list the court each petition came from. It's set on about 90% of them.

| Filed | State-court petitions | Federal-court petitions | State share | Argued per 1,000 state petitions | Argued per 1,000 other petitions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014-18 (terms 2015-19) | 6,432 | 28,411 | 18.5% | **7.9** | 9.3 |
| 2019-23 (terms 2020-24) | 4,967 | 21,706 | 18.6% | **3.8** | 12.7 |

- Filings fell overall after 2020 (about 7,700 a year down to about 5,400). **The state share didn't move.**
- **The dull explanation fails.** Petitions from state courts kept coming. The Court argued half as many per petition.
- The table lags one year (filed year to decided term), which is rough. A two-year lag gives the same picture: state 8.1 → 3.5 per 1,000, everything else 8.9 → 11.9.
- By single filing year, the state share sits between 17.6% and 19.5% every year from 2014 to 2025.

### Five rows eyeballed
All match the public record, as far as I know it:
- LANGE v. CALIFORNIA (2020): state appeals court, 9-0
- MOORE v. HARPER (2022): North Carolina Supreme Court, 6-3
- TRUMP v. ANDERSON (2023): Colorado Supreme Court, 9-0
- SMITH v. ARIZONA (2023): state appeals court, 9-0
- GLOSSIP v. OKLAHOMA (2024): Oklahoma's top criminal court, 6-2

**Hit means:** since the 6-3 Court formed, state high courts have become the last word far more often. That covers state criminal convictions and state election and constitutional rulings. The petitions didn't change. The Court's choices did.

**Miss means:** if CourtListener's appeal-from field changed how it's filled over time, the petition side moves. The blank share stays between 9% and 12% every year from 2002 to 2025, so that's not it. SCDB doesn't record summary orders or GVRs (grant, vacate, remand), so quiet handling of state cases isn't counted.

**Boring:**
- Covid. It doesn't explain 2022-2024.
- The Court takes fewer cases overall. **Tested:** the share and the per-petition rate both fell. Federal-court petitions got *more* likely to be argued.
- It may be known in legal scholarship. Check before pitching. The petition denominator is the new part.

⚠ **Traps in this table:**
- `IS_UNCONSTITUTIONAL_DECLARATION` is true on 83,635 of 83,644 rows. It's built as "the code is filled". Code 1 means *no* declaration. Real strike-downs: 671 cases (124 federal law, 482 state law, 65 local).
- `SPLIT_VOTE` is 1 on every row.
- The glance's "COURT sum 126.9M" and "TERM sum" add up codes and years. They mean nothing.
- There are no emergency-docket ("shadow docket") orders. Unsigned, unargued decisions: 14 in all of 2020-24.
- `IS_PETITIONER_WINNING` and `IS_PRECEDENT_ALTERED` match their code columns exactly (53,627 and 1,748 rows). Those two are fine.
