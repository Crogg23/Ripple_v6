## 1. Medicare outpatient by hospital and service: **live**

**What the table is**
- 116,182 rows, 3,126 hospitals (CCN).
- Only **72 service groups**, all "comprehensive" APCs like joint surgery, observation stays and ablations. It is not all outpatient care.
- ⚠ **52,664 rows (45%) are empty stubs.** The row exists, but every number is blank (suppressed). 133 hospitals are stubs only.
- **No year column.** Carbon date: the newest hospital in the file joined Medicare 2024-12-01 (Wayne Medical Center, a stub row). So it looks like DY2024 or later. Not verified against the CMS release.

**The mechanism, in plain words**
- Medicare pays a flat rate per procedure group.
- If a case looks far costlier than that rate, Medicare adds an "outlier" payment on top.
- "Cost" isn't measured. It's the hospital's **charge × its cost-to-charge ratio (CCR)**. High charges or a high CCR both push cases over the line.

**Checked**
- Outlier dollars = `OUTLIER_SRVCS × AVG_MDCR_OUTLIER_AMT`. I tested that the average is per outlier case, not per all cases: outlier-to-payment runs flat at 0.4-0.6 whatever the outlier share. That gives **$324.5M** of outlier money on $22.67B paid (1.43%).
- **Concentration:**
  - The top 10 hospitals hold 21.4%, the top 50 hold 50.5%, and the top 100 hold 66.5%. That's out of 2,953 hospitals.
  - Of the 1,212 hospitals with 1,000+ shown procedures, **687 (57%) got $0**.
- **Peer yardstick (same service):**
  - "Expected" = the hospital's procedures × the national outlier dollars per procedure *for that same APC*.
  - O/E = observed ÷ expected.
- **Penn State Health, 5 hospitals: $12.35M observed vs $0.76M expected (O/E 16.3).** 1,965 of 5,918 procedures were outliers. That's 3.8% of the national pot.

  | Hospital | Joined Medicare | Outlier $ | O/E | Outlier procedures |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Milton S Hershey Medical Center | 1970 | $8.55M | 17.2 | 1,315 of 2,770 |
  | Lancaster Medical Center | **2022-12-06** | $2.91M | **46.7** | 412 of 646 |
  | Hampden Medical Center | **2022-01-24** | $0.47M | 11.9 | 107 of 420 |
  | Holy Spirit (acquired) | 1966 | $0.35M | 3.1 | 120 of 1,145 |
  | St. Joseph (acquired) | 1966 | $0.07M | 1.5 | 11 of 937 |

- **Inside-the-group test** (I picked the system because Hershey was extreme): the two new builds are the extremes, and the two old acquired hospitals are mild. That points at the new-hospital CCR, not at the brand.
- **Join 1: HCRIS cost reports, by CCN.** 3,080 of 3,126 CCNs have an FY2023 report.
  - Lancaster's first CCR was **0.41** (FY ending 2023-06).
  - Hampden's went 0.61 → 0.33 → 0.26.
  - Hershey sits at 0.24-0.26 every year from FY2016 to FY2024.
  - So the new hospitals ran the system's charges through startup-inflated CCRs.
- **Level 5 joint procedures (APC 5115), same service:**
  - Nationally 7,291 of 208,008 get an outlier (3.5%).
  - Of the 1,066 hospitals with 40+ cases and a shown outlier count, 911 get zero.
  - Hershey got one on 185 of 192. Lancaster got one on 136 of 162: $8,808 on top of a $9,891 base.
  - In Pennsylvania, Penn State took **63%** of the state's 5115 outlier dollars on 5.4% of its cases (604 of 11,119).
- **Join 2: academic peers** (HCRIS resident FTEs of 200+): 189 hospitals, pooled O/E 2.4, median 0.98. They hold 54.9% of all outlier dollars.
  - **Hershey ranks #2 of 189 at 17.2, 7x the academic pool.**
  - #1 is John H. Stroger Jr (Cook County) at 49.9. The rest of the top of that list is mostly public safety-net hospitals.
- **Join 3: POS participation dates, national new-hospital test:**
  - 72 hospitals that joined in 2020 or later: pooled O/E **4.95**. The 2,736 that joined before 2010 (or have no date): 0.98.
  - Without the two Penn State new builds it's 2.71. The median new hospital is still 0.
  - 13 of the 72 (18.1%) sit at 5x or more, against 3.4% of older hospitals.
- **Observation stays (APC 8011), same service:**
  - Nationally 10,223 of 482,544 stays get an outlier (2.1%), $43.8M in all. The median hospital charges $19,434 a stay.
  - 9 hospitals get one on half their stays or more: $2.93M on 680 stays.
  - Elite Hospital Kingwood TX (6 beds, joined 2020-11): **56 of 56**. It charged $63,131 a stay, was paid $2,020, and got a $15,682 outlier on each. That's $878K.
  - Brushy Creek Family Hospital, Round Rock TX (joined 2023-11): 22 of 23 at **$235,634** charged a stay.
  - CarePoint Bayonne NJ: 89 of 154 at $223,858.
  - Cleveland Emergency Hospital, Humble TX: 13 of 18 at $225,815. Its HCRIS outpatient charges went from $583.0M (FY2021) to **$1,296.4M** (FY2023) on 16 beds, while its total costs fell from $54.9M to $49.4M.

**Hit means:** Medicare's outlier add-on is supposed to cover rare, costly cases. Here it pays out on most procedures at a handful of hospitals, and the trigger is their charges and CCRs, not their patients. The cleanest case is a brand-new hospital running a flagship's chargemaster through a startup CCR.

**Miss means:** if CMS reconciled these outliers at cost-report settlement, the money was clawed back. Then this is a timing quirk, not a leak.

**Boring:**
- **Tested, and it's not the general driver:** charges racing ahead of costs (the Tenet pattern).
  - Pooled O/E by FY2021→FY2023 charge-growth ÷ cost-growth: <0.9: 2.41; 0.9-1.1: 0.99; 1.1-1.3: 0.84; 1.3-1.6: 0.78; ≥1.6: 2.05.
  - It's U-shaped, so it only matters at specific hospitals.
- **Tested:** for-profit gaming. For-profits pool at 0.23 and physician-owned hospitals at 0.37. Outlier money leans nonprofit and public.
- **Partly tested:** sicker academic patients. Hershey runs 7x the academic pool. Not ruled out for specific services.
- **Not checked:**
  - The CCR Medicare actually used. The MAC's outpatient provider file isn't landed. I used HCRIS's overall CCR as a stand-in.
  - Whether CMS's outlier reconciliation ran for Lancaster or Hampden. That is the hinge.
- The file shows only the 72 comprehensive groups. Outliers on other services aren't in it.

**Traps**
- 45% of rows are all-blank stubs. A plain row count doubles the real number of hospital-service pairs.
- `RNDRNG_PRVDR_ST` is the street address, not the state.
- ⚠ HCRIS `COST_TO_CHARGE_RATIO` = **0.0 means missing**. In the 25,671 rows I pulled (these CCNs, FY2016+), 262 read 0.0 (171 FY2023, 91 FY2024) and 855 are null. Lancaster FY2024 reads 0.0, next to $199.0M of cost.
- There's no data-year column. Carbon-date it by POS participation dates.

## 2. PECOS Medicare enrollment: probed

**Checked**
- 2,978,925 rows, and **2,978,925 distinct enrollment IDs**.
  - 2,541,258 NPIs and 2,456,135 PAC IDs.
  - NPI is never blank and always 10 characters.
- The ID prefix: I = 2,546,692 (people), O = 432,233 (orgs). `ORG_NAME` is filled on exactly the 432,233 O rows.
- **The date inside `ENRLMT_ID` parses on 100% of rows:** 2002-08-01 to 2026-06-29. So the snapshot is about end of June 2026.
- **Probe 1: address stacking**, the known fraud pattern.
  - What I joined: high-risk org enrollments since 2023 → the NPPES practice address. The types were DME, labs, hospice, home health, ambulance, IDTF, pharmacy, home infusion, OTP, portable X-ray, mass immunizers, MDPP and cardiac rehab.
  - **Only 5 addresses hold 3 or more.** The top one is NYU's billing office (7, all "NEW YORK UNIVERSITY").
  - One small cluster: 3 independent labs (1420 Labs LLC, Newton Labs LLC, Quantum Molecular Labs Inc) at 314 Riley Fuzzel Rd Bldg 3, Spring TX. They enrolled 2025-07 to 2026-06, with 3 different officials and 3 different phones.
- **Probe 2: New York ambulance surge.**
  - NY added 22 ambulance suppliers in 2019-2021 and **204 in 2022-2024**. Its share of new US ambulance enrollments went from 4.1% to 21.2%.
  - **Join: NPPES + Part B by provider (DY2024).** NPPES lands 10,485 of 10,485 ambulance enrollments. Part B lands 8,945 (85%).
  - **Killed:** 184 of the 204 (90%) are fire districts, villages, towns and volunteer corps. Their median pay per patient is normal: $635 vs $631 for new suppliers elsewhere. Public EMS started billing Medicare. That's not fraud.

**Hit means:** a live address cluster of new high-risk suppliers would be a CMS enrollment-screening story.

**Miss means:** the snapshot only holds enrollments that are still active. Revoked fraud mills drop out, so a clean snapshot proves little about the past.

**Boring:** survivorship. Older enrollment years are small because ended enrollments vanish. Hospice: 530 in 2020, 182 in 2025. Year counts are survivors, not the flow of new enrollments.

**Traps**
- ⚠ Year counts are survivors only. Don't read them as enrollments per year.
- `14-E2` mental health counselor: 0 before 2023, then 4,591 in 2023 and 37,562 in 2024. That's the new 2024 Medicare benefit, not a surge.
- There is no `_SOURCE_RUN_ID` column on this mart (S09 failed on it).
- Already in memory: PECOS and the FFS enrollment table are the same file.

## 3. VA veteran suicide, national: probed

**Checked**
- 690 rows = 23 years (2001-2023) × 5 cohorts × 6 age groups. One load, no duplicate keys.
- **Male age-adjusted rate:**
  - 2001: veterans 25.7, non-veterans 26.0 (even).
  - 2023: veterans 43.5, non-veterans 29.0 (**1.50x**).
- Veteran deaths are flat: 6,021 (2001), 6,398 (2023). The veteran population fell from 25.90M to 18.18M.
- **Men 18-34:**
  - Veterans went from 25.8 to 54.8 per 100K (2.1x). Deaths went from 588 to 772 while that population fell from 2.28M to 1.41M.
  - Non-veteran men of the same age went from 20.1 to 26.7 (1.33x).
- Female veterans spiked in 2021: 353 deaths (age-adjusted 18.5), against 284 in 2020 and 278 in 2022.
- VHA users vs other veterans, ages 55-74: 36.0 vs 13.9 in 2001, then 32.2 vs 30.8 in 2023.
  - **Explained by who's in each group:** the VHA-user population grew from 1.56M to 2.43M, and the "other" population shrank from 8.18M to 4.52M.

**Hit means:** the young-veteran gap is real and holds within sex, with age bands and age adjustment.

**Miss means:** the VA prints this in its annual report, so it's context, not a scoop. The live veteran row is already on the menu from `VA_SUICIDE_STATE` (Oklahoma).

**Boring:** the rates rest on the VA's modeled veteran population. Deaths are what's actually counted, and they rose for young veterans. The composition shift explains the VHA split.

**Traps (ledger)**
- ⚠ **Summing `SUICIDE_DEATHS` gives 5,826,932.** Every death is in several rows:
  - The blank-age row and the "All" row carry the same number.
  - Veteran = VHA user + Other veteran.
  - US adult = Veteran + Non-veteran.
  - The true totals for 2001-2023: **897,624** US adults (the sum is 6.5x) and **147,180** veterans.
- Age-adjusted rates are only on the blank-age rows. The "All" rows are blank there.
- Male + female ≠ total. 2023: 6,091 + 286 = 6,377 vs 6,398.

## 4. CDC county drug-poisoning bands: probed

**Checked**
- 53,387 rows = 3,141 counties × 17 years (**1999-2015**, ends there).
  - FIPS is 5 characters on every row. No county-year duplicates.
  - 16 bands, despite the column name saying 11.
- Counties in the top band (>30): 0 in 1999-2000, 2 in 2001, **152 in 2015**. In 2015: KY 33, NM 17, WV 17, TN 15.
- **Join: CDC injury county file (Drug_OD, 2019-2024), FIPS = GEOID.** 3,135 of 3,140 counties land (99.8%). The misses are 5 renamed Alaska and South Dakota areas.
- **Time test, counties of 50K+ with unsuppressed counts:** the 2015 band predicts later rates only loosely.
  - Rank correlation: 0.50 (2019), 0.58 (2023), 0.54 (2024), across 811-919 counties.
- **Movers:**
  - Monroe County NY: 6.1-8 in 2015, then **58.1 per 100K and 435 deaths in 2023**. The series ran 208, 264, 189, 319, 435, then 261 in 2024.
  - Erie County NY: 6.1-8, then 41.2 (390 deaths).
  - Utah County UT: 22.1-24, then 11.1 (80 deaths).
  - By state, 2023 rate ÷ 2015 band: NY 2.83x, TN 2.82x, LA 2.77x; UT 0.73x, NE 0.88x.

**Hit means:** a county the model called low in 2015 became a hot spot. That's a local-map story.

**Miss means:** it's the fentanyl-moves-east story, already told.

**Boring:**
- Model bands vs counted rates are two different measures. The injury `RATE` may be crude while the band is age-adjusted. Not verified.
- The big 2023 numbers come from the other table.

**Traps**
- ⚠ Every county gets a modeled band, even Kalawao County HI (89 people): 24.1-26 in 2015. These are model output, not counts.
- 4 null populations (Bedford city VA 2010-2013). Broomfield CO and Denali AK start in 2003. Bedford city ends in 2013.

## 5. CDC WONDER national deaths: probed

**Checked**
- 880 rows = 22 years (1999-2020) × 20 ICD chapters × 2 sexes. One load.
- The crude rate recomputes from deaths ÷ population on every filled row (0 mismatches).
- The 2020 total is 3,383,729, the official US count.
- Crude rates 1999 → 2019:
  - Male endocrine deaths rose 55% (29.8 → 46.2). Female rose 7% (36.6 → 39.2).
  - Male external causes: 76.2 → 106.7, then 121.7 in 2020.

**Hit means:** nothing actor-level. It's a national backdrop.

**Miss means:** with no age column, every trend here is partly aging.

**Boring:** aging. It can't be age-adjusted from this table.

**Traps (ledger)**
- ⚠ **Maternal deaths break twice.** 379 (2002) → 545 (2003) as the pregnancy checkbox spread. 1,208 (2017) → 973 (2018). This fits NCHS's documented checkbox changes. I didn't re-check it against the NCHS notes.
- ⚠ **Dementia moves between chapters.**
  - Female "mental and behavioural" deaths: 25,365 (1999) → 101,776 (2013) → 82,180 (2019).
  - Female "symptoms" deaths dropped from 21,477 to 17,553 in 2014.
  - Female nervous-system deaths jumped by 15,956 in 2015.
  - Don't trend these chapters across 2013-2015.
- "Codes for special purposes" is 0 or blank except for 2001 = 2,926 (9/11) and 2020 = 350,921 (COVID).
- Deaths are 0 on 54 rows (the male maternal rows plus special-purpose codes) and null on 8.
