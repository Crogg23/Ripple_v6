## 1. FED_FJC_SERVICE: **live**

One row per judge per court seat. 4,766 rows, all unique on NID + SEQUENCE, covering 4,067 judges.
The data runs to June 2026: newest commission 2026-06-18, newest senior status 2026-06-21, newest end of service 2026-06-27.

**The mechanic**
- "Senior status" is semi-retirement. The judge keeps working part-time and the seat opens for the sitting president to fill.
- A judge qualifies under the "rule of 80": at least 65 years old, and age plus years on the bench adds up to 80 or more.
- Birth year comes from `FJC_JUDGE` (joined on NID). All 4,766 rows land. 2 judges have no birth year.

**Checked**
- Pool: every active district and appeals judge who qualified on inauguration day, for each of 8 presidencies.
- Outcome: took senior status within 517 days. 517 days is how far Trump's second term reaches into the data.
- Split by the party of the president who appointed the judge.

| Presidency | Same-party judges | Other-party judges |
|---|---|---|
| Reagan | 10 of 24 (41.7%) | 23 of 59 (39.0%) |
| Bush 41 | 10 of 34 (29.4%) | 12 of 38 (31.6%) |
| Clinton | 17 of 54 (31.5%) | 10 of 26 (38.5%) |
| Bush 43 | 31 of 63 (49.2%) | 8 of 28 (28.6%) |
| Obama | 34 of 82 (41.5%) | 14 of 78 (17.9%) |
| Trump 1 | 39 of 124 (31.5%) | 13 of 88 (14.8%) |
| Biden | 43 of 111 (38.7%) | 22 of 101 (21.8%) |
| **Trump 2** | **16 of 83 (19.3%)** | **6 of 70 (8.6%)** |

- **With a 365-day window instead:** Trump 2 same-party is 18.1% (next lowest: Bush 41, 23.5%). Other-party is 5.7% (next lowest: Trump 1, 12.5%). The result doesn't depend on the window.
- **Appeals courts:** 25 Republican-appointed circuit judges qualified and were active on 2025-01-20. 3 took senior status by the data edge:
  - Diane Sykes (7th Cir.), 2025-10-01
  - Sandra Ikuta (9th Cir.), 2025-11-07. She died 2025-12-07.
  - Duane Benton (8th Cir.), 2026-06-21, on the last day of the window
- 22 stayed, including 11 born before 1950: Newman, Lourie, Loken, Niemeyer, Milan Smith, Henderson, Wilkinson, Jerry Smith, Hartz, Easterbrook, Edith Jones.
- Trump 1, same point: 8 of 40 eligible Republican-appointed circuit judges went senior (20%).

**Dull explanations, tested**
- **The load is behind.** Tested: senior-status moves by quarter. 2025: 8, 3, 6, 7. 2026: 4, 5. The same six quarters of Trump 1: 12, 10, 13, 10, 11, 10. Recent quarters aren't empty, and the drop starts in spring 2025, so this isn't a lag at the edge of the data.
- **The pool is made of stayers.** Partly true. 58 of Trump 2's 83 had already qualified 4+ years earlier and sat out Trump 1. Trump 1's pool had 49 of 124.
  - Newly eligible (0-3 years): Trump 2 7 of 25 (28.0%), Trump 1 26 of 75 (34.7%).
  - Eligible 4+ years: Trump 2 9 of 58 (15.5%), Trump 1 13 of 49 (26.5%).
  - At Trump 1's rates, Trump 2's pool would have produced about 24 moves. It produced 16. Pool makeup explains part of the gap, not all of it.
- **The Senate is slow, so judges are waiting.** Weak: about 44 judges have been commissioned since 2025-01-20, against about 42 district and appeals judges by mid-2018 under Trump 1.
- **Not tested:** judges who have *announced* senior status "upon confirmation of a successor." Those don't show up here until they take effect. The courts' future-vacancies list isn't in the warehouse.
- **Not a partisan snub, exactly:** judges appointed by Democrats are also staying at a record rate (8.6%). The same-party vs other-party ratio (about 2.2x) is the same as under Obama and Trump 1. Judges of both parties are staying; Republican appointees are staying more than ever for a Republican president.

⚠ **Small numbers.** 16 of 83 against 39 of 124 is z ≈ 1.9, borderline at the usual 5% line. The ranking against 8 presidencies is sturdier than any single comparison.

**Hit means:** Trump is getting fewer seats opened for him by his own party's judges than any Republican president since at least 1981, measured against the judges who could leave.
**Miss would mean:** the courts' future-vacancies list shows a pile of announced-but-pending senior moves. Then the gap is timing, not a choice.
**Next:** the uscourts.gov future vacancies list (announced senior dates) for 2025-2026, set against the 22 circuit judges above. Whether the slowdown has already been reported: not checked. Some 2025 coverage is likely.

**Traps**
- ⚠ `POLITICS__FJC_APPOINTMENT` is a second copy of this table: 4,766 rows, all 4,766 NID + SEQUENCE keys match, and 4,731 have the same commission date. Its dates are text.
- Pauline Newman (born 1927) counts as active here. (Outside fact, not checked: her court has suspended her.)
- No far-future end-of-service placeholder dates in this table (0 rows at 2100 or later).

## 2. FEC_CANDIDATE_SUMMARY: **live**

One row per candidate ID per cycle. **Only two cycles: 2024 (3,856 rows) and 2026 (4,077).** Receipts, spending, cash and debt totals.

**Checked**
- Joined to `FEC_CANDIDATE` on CAND_ID + CYCLE for each candidate's principal campaign committee and declared election year. 7,922 of 7,933 rows land. 9 of the 11 misses are presidential filers, including the two in trap 2.
- Deduped to **one row per committee** (see trap 1).
- "Dormant" means all three:
  - a House or Senate campaign committee
  - $0 from individual donors all cycle, under every candidate ID it's filed under
  - the candidate's declared election year is before the cycle

## 3. TX_LOBBY_INDIVIDUAL_REPORTING: **probed**

One row per client named on a Texas lobbyist's activity report. 38,662 lines, 37,659 reports, 2000-2026.
The Las Vegas Sands media story (joins pass j6, deep2 pass 15) already came from this table. This pass looked for anything else.

**Checked**
- Join `REPORT_ID` = `TX_LOBBY_COVER.REPORT_INFO_IDENT`: **37,659 of 37,659 land.**
- Coverage: client lines are on 13.3% of cover reports, and on 15.9% of the reports with any non-media spending.
  - They carry 22.1% of non-media dollars ($19.11M of $86.56M) and 57.2% of media dollars ($57.27M of $100.08M; Sands is most of that).
- 37,011 of 37,659 reports (98.3%) name exactly one client, so the report's dollars belong to that client. Max 32 clients on one report.
- **Peer ranking, 2019-2026, single-client reports, non-media spending** (food, entertainment, gifts, travel, events, awards):
  - AGC OF TEXAS $824,779 (food $532,666, travel $252,943; 759 reports, 12 lobbyists)
  - CENTERPOINT ENERGY $778,550 (food $585,172, entertainment $168,161; 468 reports, 11 lobbyists)
  - TEXANS FOR LAWSUIT REFORM $408,392
  - Median client: $346, across 882 clients. The top two hold 27% of $5.93M.
- **Time:** CenterPoint by two-year window: $205.6K, $209.1K, $197.3K, then $166.5K for 2025-26 (partial). No jump.
- **Lateness:** the median report arrives 1-8 days early, every year except 2005, when 484 of 1,271 reports were late (38%). 2005 looks like a filing-system year. Not chased.

**Why it's not a story:** the utility peer check breaks on coverage. Since 2019, client lines name CenterPoint 478 times, Oncor 20, Entergy 13, AEP 0. That's a difference in how lobbyists fill in the form, not in what they spend.

**Traps**
- ⚠ A missing client line means nothing: 87% of reports have none.
- ⚠ The "client" is often the lobby shop itself. 2,196 of 13,195 lines since 2019 (16.6%) look like a firm name (LLC/LLP/"Strategies"/"Public Affairs"; rough pattern). 6 of the top 20 "clients" are lobby firms (5 firms; Hance Scarborough appears under two spellings): Hance Scarborough, Jerry Valdez Governmental Affairs, DTH Strategies, Pathfinder Public Affairs, Delisi Communications.
- ⚠ The client address is blank on **all 13,195** lines since 2019. The catalog says "about half"; that only holds for the older years.
- The due date runs to 2027-01-11 because that's when the 2026 annual report is due. Not an error.

## 4. CA_LOBBY_AMENDMENTS: **dead**

California Form 605: amendments to a lobbying firm's (F601) or lobbyist employer's (F603) registration, adding or dropping lobbyists, clients and firms.
25,249 rows = 25,249 distinct filing versions on about 4,800 filings (2,571 F601 + 2,229 F603). F601 filings average about 8 versions each.

**Checked**
- **Join:** `FILING_ID` lands **0 of 25,249** in `CA_LOBBY_COVER`, 0 in `CA_LOBBY_COVER2`, and 0 in `CA_LOBBY_FIRM_EMPLOYER`. Nothing names the filer.
  - A firm amendment says "dropped client X" without saying which firm did the dropping.
  - An employer amendment says "dropped firm Y" without saying which employer.
- **Time:** client adds rise from 298 (2005) to 764 (2025), and client drops from 133 to 335. That's growth, not an event.
- **The one spike:** firm drops hit 124 in 2020, against 21-31 in the two years on either side. 99 of those rows are two firms on 3 filings each: LANG, HANSEN, O'MALLEY & MILLER (60 rows) and JONES DAY (39 rows). Counted by filing, it's 3 employers apiece. Repeated versions inflate the row count.
- **Concentration:** the biggest "client dropped by many firms in one year" groups are **blank** or say "SEE ATTACHMENT A". The most for any named client is 5 filings. Median 1.

**Hit/miss:** the "firms that dropped clients before a scandal" idea can't be tested. The table doesn't carry who dropped whom.

**Traps**
- ⚠ Orphan filing IDs (above).
- ⚠ 27 signed dates are typos: years 0107, 0148, 0201, 1823, 2098, 3013, 5201 and others (21 F601, 6 F603).
- 459 rows put names in "SEE ATTACHMENT" instead of the name field. The attachment isn't in the data.
- 90 client-drop lines repeat across versions of the same filing (same filing, client and date). Count filings, not rows.

## 5. FED_FCC_LICENSING: **dead**

FCC amateur (ham) radio licenses: service HA (regular) 1,461,364 and HV (vanity call sign) 227,974. It sits in POLITICS but has nothing political in it.

**Checked**
- 1,689,338 rows = 1,689,338 distinct license IDs = 1,689,338 distinct full rows. **One row per license.**
- ⚠ The glance's "UNIQUE_SYSTEM_IDENTIFIER 3849791: 2.8K rows" doesn't reproduce. Every ID appears exactly once, so the glance's top-value counts for this table are wrong.
- 1,198,926 FCC registration numbers and 1,597,270 call signs. 97.9% of holders are individuals (applicant type I).
- **Time:** grants per year rise from 29K (2005) to a 98K peak (2017), dip to 69K in 2022, then reach 85K in 2024. `GRANT_DATE` is the latest grant, and renewals reset it, so this isn't a count of new hams.

**Traps**
- ⚠ `EIN` has one value, an empty string, on all 1.69M rows. The catalog lists EIN as this table's join key; it joins to nothing.
- `PHONE` holds only 'I' or blank.
- ⚠ Status A ("active") doesn't mean unexpired: 82,927 A licenses (granted 2005 on) carry an expiry date before the data edge, mostly 2014-2016 grants. Probably the 2-year renewal grace period. Not checked.
- `CANCELLATION_DATE` is filled on expired licenses too (for example 22,606 of 2005's grants aren't status C).
