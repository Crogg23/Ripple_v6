## 1. ENERGY__FED_EIA861_OPERATIONAL_DATA: **live**

**What it is:** 1,711 rows, one per utility per state. What each utility bought, made, sold, gave away and lost in 2024.
**Clean checks passed:** sources = disposition on every row. No negative losses. One load, one file [q01, q07].

### The lead: Holly Springs, MS bills for 57% of the power it buys

| | Holly Springs | Peer median | Peer group |
|---|---|---|---|
| Power bought (all wholesale) | 240,422 MWh | | |
| Power billed to customers | 137,809 MWh | | |
| **Lost** | **102,527 MWh = 42.6%** | **3.44%** | 425 city utilities that report losses. #2 is Chanute, KS at 17.9% |
| Lost, Mississippi only | 42.6% | 3.64% | 15 MS city utilities |
| Purchases ÷ peak × hours ("load factor") | 44.6% | 50.7% | 15 MS city utilities |
| **Billed** ÷ peak × hours | **25.6%** | 49.9% | 132 city utilities in MS, TN, AL, KY, GA. Holly Springs is last |
| Billed MWh per home | **9.27** | 12.93 / 13.38 | 15 MS city utilities (last) / 97 in MS, TN, AL (last) |
| Billed MWh per business | **17.3** | 46.0 | 15 MS city utilities (last) |

Sources: q07 (operational), q11 (sales by customer type), q14 (meters).

**What each check means:**
- **Purchases fit the town's peak.** 240,422 MWh against a 61.4 MW winter peak is a normal 44.6% load factor. So the "bought" number is not a typo.
- **Billing does not fit.** Billed power is 25.6% of peak-hours: dead last of 132 city utilities in five Southeast states.
- **Every customer has a smart meter.** 12,012 meters, 12,012 of them smart (AMI). The AMI-recorded energy is 137,809 MWh, the same as billed. The meters record what gets billed, and the gap sits between the wholesale meter and all the customer meters.
- **Homes and businesses both bill low.** Lowest per-customer use in its state in both classes. Customer mix alone doesn't explain that.

**What it's worth:** 102,527 MWh × its own average retail price (12.73¢/kWh) ≈ **$13.0M**. Its whole 2024 retail revenue was **$17.5M**.
(I didn't price it at TVA wholesale cost. That number isn't in the warehouse.)

**The dull explanations, and where they stand:**
1. **A typo on the EIA form.** Possible. Against it: sources = disposition, the sales schedule matches the operational schedule, and the meter schedule matches too. All three come from the same filer, though, so they aren't independent.
2. **A billing or meter-data failure** (unbilled, not stolen). Fits the data: the smart meters record only what gets billed. This would be a story in itself.
3. **Theft or broken meters.** Can't split this from #2 with this data.
4. **A regional reporting habit.** Four neighboring north Mississippi TVA co-ops also report high losses: Tallahatchie Valley 19.5%, Tippah 14.7%, Northcentral MS 13.4%, North East MS 12.7%. The Mississippi co-op median is 5.54% (n=23) [q19 counties].
   Tested: their billed use per home is **normal** (12.1-14.6 MWh vs 14.12 median). So their pattern is not Holly Springs' pattern. Holly Springs stands alone on billed use.
   Not everyone nearby is high: New Albany (also serves Marshall County) 4.9%, Oxford 4.6%.

**Hit means:** a city utility that couldn't bill for about $13M of power in one year. **Miss means:** a bad EIA filing, which is still worth one phone call.
**Can't test here:** whether this is new. EIA-861 for 2019-2023 isn't in the warehouse.
**Next join:** EIA-861 2022 and 2023 Operational Data files (EIA website, not loaded), then TVA wholesale invoices and the city's audited financials.

### Side thread: free power to city hall (probed, not a lead)
- City utilities that report free power give away a median **0.61%** of retail sales. Co-ops: 0.14%. Investor-owned: 0.09% [q07].
- **But the free-power line is dirty:**
  - 19 rows with free power report **no losses**. They're using "free" as the losses plug. That includes Lawrenceville GA (5.9%) and Altus OK (5.8%), whose sources equal retail + free exactly.
  - Wellsborough Electric (PA) shows 18,217 MWh "free". That's **exactly** its 18,217 MWh delivered for customers who buy from other suppliers [q11].
  - The MBTA shows 289,231 MWh free, **7.9x** its retail sales. That's its own trains.
  - ComEd's 394,273 MWh free fits its franchise deals with towns (outside knowledge, not checked). That's a known story.

### Traps found
- **Losses in retail-choice states are a plug.** United Illuminating 54.2%, NSTAR 21.0%, Narragansett 19.8%. They carry power for other suppliers. Two transmission companies show 100%.
- `UTILITY_NUMBER` **88888 "Withheld"** holds 3 Texas retail marketers. That's why there are 1,708 utility-state keys on 1,711 rows.

### Time: 2023 file vs 2024 file [q16, q17]
108 coal units (51.6 GW) had a retirement date in 2023 and still ran in 2024.
- **9 units, 5.1 GW: date pushed later.** Brandon Shores 1-2 (2025→2029), Baldwin 1-2 (2025→2027), Columbia 1-2 (2026→2029), South Oak Creek 7-8, Sherburne 1.

Naughton 1-2 and Edgewater 5 have an "other modification" flagged for 2026/2028: gas conversions by outside knowledge, since the flag doesn't name the fuel.
- **The 10 erased with no conversion flagged (4.2 GW):** Jim Bridger 3-4, Huntington 1-2, Wyodak, Dave Johnston 1, 2 and 4 (all PacifiCorp), Coleto Creek 1, Stanton 1.
- Net: **19 units, 9.4 GW = 18% of dated coal MW** delayed or erased in one year.
- Joint vs sole owner: 36.7% vs 20.6% raw, but **PacifiCorp's Bridger and Wyodak carry it**. That fails the "3-4 units carry it" test.

## 3. ENERGY__FED_EIA861_DEMAND_RESPONSE: **probed**

**What it is:** 339 rows. What utilities paid customers to cut power at peak, and what they got back. The key is utility + state + grid operator: Wabash Valley has two Indiana rows, one MISO and one PJM. TOTAL = sum of the parts on every filled row [q10].

### What was checked
- **Spending:** $1.59B in 2024 ($1.22B incentives + $0.36B other costs).
- **Paid, with no actual cut reported:** 54 programs, **$551M (35%)**. The top 4 hold 51% of that.
  - **36 left "actual" blank ($395M). 18 reported 0 ($156M).**
- **By program size:** 34% of programs with 200+ MW potential report no actual cut. For 50-200 MW programs it's 9%.
- **Florida:** FPL $119.8M (blank), Duke Energy Florida $91.9M (0), Tampa Electric $31.4M (blank). $243M for 2,830 MW potential and **0 MW reported actual.**
- **Dull test (reporting habit): all 7 Duke Energy rows**, in 5 states, report blank or 0. That's a company habit, not 7 separate programs that never ran.

### Best thread: Santee Cooper (SC state-owned utility)
- $94.1M in incentives to 1,446 customers. 731 MW potential (13.7% of its 5,325 MW winter peak). **0.814 MW actually cut.**
- **$128.7K per MW of potential**, against a **$40.7K median** across 50 industrial-heavy programs.
- Its neighbor TVA (TN row): 991 MW potential, 620 actual.
- Same price band: Arkansas Electric Coop $31.7M to **8 customers** ($3.96M each), Entergy Louisiana $31.0M to **11** ($2.82M each). Both left "actual" blank.

**Why it's not live:** these are interruptible-rate credits, paid for being on call. "Actual" counts only cuts at the annual peak hour. Around $100-130K per MW-year is roughly what a new peaker plant costs (outside knowledge, not checked). A hostile editor kills "paid for nothing" in one line. The table can't name who got the $94M.
