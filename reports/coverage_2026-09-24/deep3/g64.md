## 1. Net metering: SRP's customers went solar at a third to half the pace of their Arizona neighbors. **live**

### What the table is
- One row per utility, state, grid area (called a balancing authority) and AC/DC rating. 1,004 rows. Every source row landed (checked against the source sheet).
- The first block of columns is solar only.
- The labels were lost at load. I got them back from the source file: `_GRP2` = wind, `_GRP3` = other, `_GRP4` = all technologies.

### The check: same state, same sun, three years
Measure: net-metered home solar systems per 1,000 residential customers.
- The denominator is residential customers from SALES_ULT_CUST, counting only Bundled and Delivery rows.
- I joined on utility number + state. 919 of 920 utility-state pairs landed.

| Utility | 2014 | 2019 | 2024 | Added 2014-19 | Added 2019-24 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arizona Public Service | 28.8 | 90.6 | **145.4** | +61.7 | +54.8 |
| Tucson Electric Power | 16.1 | 63.6 | **124.3** | +47.4 | +60.7 |
| **Salt River Project** | 12.9 | 22.6 | **56.7** | **+9.7** | +34.1 |

- **SRP as a share of APS:** 0.45 in 2014, 0.25 in 2019, 0.39 in 2024.
- **SRP as a share of TEP:** 0.80, then 0.36, then 0.46.
- **2014-2019:** SRP added 10,264 home systems for about 900K homes. TEP added 18,594 for about 375K.
- **Median Arizona utility with 30K+ homes: 105.5.**
  - SRP is the lowest of the big three (400K+ homes each).
  - The three utilities below it are rural co-ops: Navopache, Sulphur Springs, Mohave.
- **Capacity says the same thing.** SRP has 373 W of home solar per customer and TEP has 843 W. Both report in AC.
  - APS reports in DC, so don't compare its megawatts (see traps).

### What a hit means, what a miss means
- **Hit:** SRP was already behind in 2014. But the gap doubled in exactly 2014-2019, then half-closed.
  - Public record: SRP put a demand charge on new solar customers in early 2015.
  - An antitrust suit followed (Ellis v. SRP). I didn't check it here.
  - The dates line up with the data.
- **What would kill it:** SRP counting solar homes differently from APS and TEP.
  - SRP's non-net row holds 4.7 MW, all of it backup-only internal combustion, and 0 solar. So SRP isn't hiding home solar in the other table.
- **Third-party-owned systems** (Sunrun, Tesla and so on) are on the unloaded TPO sheet.
  - Suppose none of them is already inside the utility counts. Adding them by grid area gives SRP 66.1, APS 209.0 and TEP 165.6.
  - The gap gets wider, not narrower.

### The dull explanation, tested
- "SRP's territory is just different" (housing, income): that explains a gap that stays the same size. It doesn't explain the pace of new installs changing between periods while the territory stayed put.
- The pace ratio moved from 0.16 of APS's (2014-19) to 0.62 of APS's (2019-24). It's the same utility and the same homes.
- Not tested: renters and apartments by service area. The next join is SERVICE_TERRITORY counties × census housing.

### Honest limit
- This is a known Arizona story. The new part is ten years of federal numbers side by side, not the policy itself.

### Other same-state gaps I saw (not chased)
- **Ohio:** Dayton P&L has 0.8 per 1,000. Ohio Power has 8.2 and Duke Ohio 7.7.
- **Florida:** FPL has 20.7. Duke Florida has 52.4 and Tampa Electric 38.1.
  - The dull explanation: FPL's condo-heavy southeast coast. Untested.

## 2. Dynamic pricing: regulators moved six utilities' customers onto time-of-use rates. **live**

### What the table is
- One row per utility and state: homes and businesses enrolled, plus Y-or-blank flags for 5 program types.
- 857 rows. All the States-sheet rows landed. The one Puerto Rico row is missing.

### The check: 2019 vs 2024, residential enrolled ÷ residential customers

| Utility | 2019 enrolled | Share | 2024 enrolled | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consumers Energy (MI) | 31,511 | 2.0% | **1,642,369** | **99.1%** |
| Xcel, Public Service Co of Colorado | 7,053 | 0.6% | **1,092,786** | **80.1%** |
| Ameren Missouri (Union Electric) | 120 | 0.0% | **891,431** | **81.4%** |
| Evergy Metro, **Missouri** | 232 | 0.1% | **245,547** | **89.7%** |
| Evergy Missouri West | 268 | 0.1% | **233,540** | **76.4%** |
| Southern California Edison | 498,196 | 11.1% | **2,654,452** | **57.8%** |
| *Evergy Metro, **Kansas*** | *196* | *0.1%* | *3,904* | *1.6%* |

- **The six together went from 537,380 to 6,760,125**, a gain of **6.22M homes**.
  - That's 42.7% of the table's national 15.83M enrolled homes.
- **The twin test:**
  - It's the same company, Evergy Metro, in the same Kansas City metro.
  - On the Missouri side, 89.7% of homes are enrolled. On the Kansas side, 1.6%.
  - Smart-meter coverage is near-total on both sides: 286,071 of 286,093 meters in MO, 256,449 of 256,454 in KS.
  - So the gap comes from the state regulator, not the hardware.
- **A handful carries it, and that is the point:** six utilities hold 42.7% of the national count. The median large utility (500K+ homes) is at 0.8%.
  - 26 of 56 large utilities have 95%+ smart meters and under 2% enrolled.

### What a hit means, what a miss means
- **Hit:** homes landed on time-of-use by default, not by choice. The count is each utility's own filing.
  - Public record: Missouri, Michigan and Colorado ordered default time-of-use in 2022-2023.
- **Miss:** other utilities that also moved everyone may simply not count it. So 6.22M is a floor, not a total. See the next section.
- **Next join:** residential revenue per MWh, 2019 vs 2024, at these six vs same-state peers (SALES_ULT_CUST, S07). Did bills move? Not run.

### ⚠ Traps in this table (for the ledger)
- **Four AEP rows report every customer as enrolled**, down to the unit. Together that's 972,363 homes, 6.1% of the national total:
  - PSO (OK): 501,093 of 501,093
  - SWEPCO AR: 108,102 of 108,102, with only **1,068 smart meters** among 109,029 home meters
  - SWEPCO TX: 156,271 of 156,271
  - SWEPCO LA: 206,897 of 207,011
  - PSO did the same in 2014 (100.9%) and 2019 (100.3%).
- **Series breaks from reporting choices:**
  - SDG&E: 664,476 (2019) to **8,125** (2024)
  - PG&E: 514,137 to 171,636
  - Ohio Power: 716,127 to **204**
  - Public record: SDG&E and PG&E moved homes to default time-of-use in 2019-2022, and DTE did in 2023.
  - DTE reports 23,062 (1.1%). If those defaults happened, these utilities don't count them.
- 74 rows have homes enrolled but no home program flag. The flags are Y or blank, never N.

## 3. Non-net-metered generators: Alabama Power's homes have almost no solar. **live**

### What the table is
- Small generators (under 1 MW) that are *not* on net metering. One row per utility, state, grid area and AC/DC. 507 rows.

### ⚠ First: the triage angle is wrong
- **The pitch was:** a Texas boom in gas backup generators after the Uri blackout.
- **What the data says:** Oncor's 1,106 MW is **956 MW solar + 146 MW batteries**. CenterPoint's 512 MW is 470 MW solar.
  - Internal-combustion generators (the gas kind) in all of Texas: **73.5 MW**.
  - Texas home solar lands here because the Texas wires companies don't run net metering.
- The backup-only generators are in **Florida**:
  - FPL: 207.8 MW backup-only, almost all internal combustion at businesses
  - City of Tallahassee: 108.9 MW
  - Duke Energy Florida: 98.8 MW
  - Florida holds 432.6 MW of the nation's 1,123 MW backup-only.

### The lead: home solar watts per home, net + non-net combined (S12)

| Utility | Homes | Home solar, watts per home |
|---|---|---|
| **Alabama Power** | 1,332,911 | **1.6** |
| City of Huntsville (AL, TVA) | 197,537 | 2.5 |
| Nashville Electric Service (TN, TVA) | 417,640 | 3.9 |
| Entergy Mississippi | 384,611 | 3.0 |
| Georgia Power | 2,452,488 | **30.1** |
| Mississippi Power | 157,437 | **31.5** |

- Alabama Power has **no net-metering row at all**. Its whole footprint here is 443 generators of any kind, with 2.1 MW at homes.
- **Time:**
  - Alabama Power had 62 net-metered homes in 2014, then 0 in 2019 and 0 in 2024.
  - Georgia Power went from 0 in 2019 to 11,208 in 2024.
- **Peer test inside the family:**
  - Alabama Power has 1/19th the home solar of its two Southern Co. sisters.
  - The sisters are in the same region, under the same parent company.
- **What breaks it:** the TVA cities are just as low, in Alabama too (Huntsville 2.5).
  - So the line isn't Alabama Power vs everyone. It's **no net metering vs net metering**.
  - Public record: Alabama Power charges solar homes a monthly capacity fee. TVA has no net metering. Not checked here.
- **Honest limit:**
  - The instructions let utilities send "best estimates" on this schedule.
  - No third-party owner reports any Alabama systems at all (the TPO sheet has zero AL rows). That's consistent, but it doesn't prove it.

### ⚠ Traps in this table (for the ledger)
- **`*_TECH_03` is battery energy in MWh, not MW.** The source header reads "Battery Energy (MWh)".
  - `TOTAL_CAPACITY_MW_ALL_TECH` leaves it out. I checked all 507 rows: the total equals the sum of the other nine groups.
  - Add TECH_03 in and you'd be 1,058 "MW" high.
- Tech group names, recovered from the source:
  - 01 solar
  - 02 battery MW
  - 03 battery MWh
  - 04 wind
  - 05 hydro
  - 06 fuel cells
  - 07 internal combustion
  - 08 combustion turbine
  - 09 steam
  - 10 other
- **1 row is all null.** It's the source file's footnote ("Note: Data includes distributed generators les…"), loaded as data. The warehouse has 507 rows; the source has 506 real ones.
- 43 adjustment rows (utility number 99999) sum to -15.0 MW.

## 4. Distribution systems: who reports voltage optimization. **probed**

### What I checked
- Joined to sales for customers per circuit. The median is 490 customers per circuit.
- Compared against EIA's 2019 file. National voltage-optimization share was 23.8% in 2019 and 28.7% in 2024.

### What's there
- **Zero voltage optimization on 1,000+ circuits:**
  - PG&E (3,422 circuits)
  - PSE&G (2,909)
  - Ameren Missouri (2,310)
  - Duke Energy Carolinas (2,186)
  - PECO (2,172)
  - Dominion Virginia (2,172)
  - We Energies
  - Interstate P&L
  - CL&P
  - SRP
  - NYSEG
  - LIPA
  - SDG&E
- **One parent, one state:** Duke Energy Carolinas reports 0 of 2,186. Duke Energy Progress reports 1,182 of 1,182 (100%). Both are in NC.

### Why not story-grade
- The field is self-reported and jumps between years:
  - SDG&E: 757 of 1,061 circuits (2019), then **0** of 1,058 (2024)
  - Alabama Power: 3 of 2,243, then **2,178 of 2,178**
  - PPL: 47, then 1,285 of 1,285
- Round 100% claims (Alabama Power, PPL, JCP&L, Duke Progress) probably mean "capable", not "in use".
- SDG&E also broke a series in dynamic pricing (664K to 8K). Its 2024 filing zeroed out two fields.

### ⚠ Traps
- **Lost row:** the first data row became the landing header, so City of Aberdeen (MS) is missing (8 circuits, 8 optimized). The key columns in the landing table are literally `C_2024`, `C_55`, `MS`.
- **Blank, not zero:** 298 rows (22%) leave voltage optimization blank, including Con Ed, APS, CenterPoint and Entergy Louisiana.
- **Circuit undercount:** NIPSCO reports 134 circuits for 492,700 customers. That's 3,677 per circuit, where its Indiana peers run about 700.
