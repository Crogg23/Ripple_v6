## 1. POLITICS__FED_SENATE_EFD_FILINGS — live

**What it is.** 799 periodic transaction reports (PTRs, the stock-trade forms) from 59 senators, Jan 2021 to Sep 2026.
Every row is a PTR; the title always starts "Periodic Transaction Report for".
FILING_KIND says whether it was filed online (`ptr`, 699) or on paper (`paper`, 100).

**The finding.**
- Blumenthal: **69 filings, 69 on paper.** Feb 2021 to Aug 2026.
  - He's the 2nd most frequent filer in the table. The median senator filed 3.
  - His paper count is rising: 9, 8, 9, 13, **21** (2025), 9 (2026 so far).
- Peers: 54 of 59 senators never filed on paper.
  - Boozman filed 27 on paper, then switched to online in 2023 and stayed there.
  - Feinstein (2), Burr (1), Fetterman (1) are the only others.
  - **Since 2024: 44 paper PTRs, 43 of them Blumenthal's.**
- What paper costs: a paper PTR is a scanned image. The trade-line table (FINANCE__FED_SENATE_EFD_PTR) gives each one a single blank line: no ticker, no date, no amount.
- The longer view: in FINANCE__SENATE_TRADES (Stock Watcher up to 2020, the Senate site from 2021), Blumenthal has **119 filings, 2012-2026. All 119 are "PDF Disclosed Filing" or paper. Zero parsed trades in 14 years.**
  - Senate Stock Watcher's own scrape (FINANCE__FED_SENATE_STOCK_WATCHER) also has only PDF rows for him, 50 of 50. So this isn't our loader.

**Checks run.**
- Peer: paper share per senator, all 59.
- Time: paper filings per senator per year, 2021-2026. Plus 2012-2020 from the Stock Watcher tables.
- Join: all 799 filings land in the trade-line table on FILING_ID (799/799). Checked that the paper ones have no trade detail.

**Dull explanation.** Paper filing is allowed, and the scans are public on the Senate site. So nothing is hidden, but nothing can be searched.
Blumenthal's wealth is mostly his family's and in managed accounts (general knowledge, not from the warehouse), which could explain paper statements.
Not tested: whether Senate rules still allow paper PTRs; whether reporters already wrote this; how many trades sit inside the 69 scans (the warehouse can't count them).

**Hit means.** One of the Senate's most frequent stock-trade reporters is invisible to every trade tracker built on parsed eFD data. That includes this warehouse and the F-009 committee angle.
**Miss would have meant.** Paper filing is spread across many senators and is just noise. It isn't: since 2024 it's one senator.

**Traps.**
- FILER_FULL reads just "Senator" on 98 of the 100 paper rows. The name is only in FILER_FIRST / FILER_LAST. That's the "Senator 98" top value in the glance.
- FILER_FIRST and FILER_LAST mix cases (RICHARD, John). Group on FILER_LAST_CLEAN.
- Any per-senator trade count built on the PTR line table silently drops Blumenthal and pre-2023 Boozman.

**Side check (the PTR table, not this one; late filing is already a round-2 lead).**
On online filings, the median senator with 20+ trade lines files 4% of lines more than 45 days late.
Fetterman: 35% late. 31 trades by a child's account, made Jan-Nov 2023, were filed 2024-08-13, up to 567 days late.

## 2. POLITICS__TX_LOBBY_GIFTS — live (moderate)

**What it is.** Itemized gifts from Texas lobby reports (Schedule E). 4,084 rows, 2005-2026. Each row names one recipient.
The cover sheet (TX_LOBBY_COVER) carries each report's gift **total**. Itemized rows exist only when a recipient gets named.

**The finding: gift money stays level, names disappear.**
- Every report ID in the gift table lands in the cover sheet: 1,134 of 1,134.
- Reports with $1,000+ in gifts, after deduping corrections and dropping repeated totals:

| Years | $1K+ gift reports | Name any recipient | Share | Gift $ on them |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2005-2011 | 228 | 61 | **26.8%** | $694K |
| 2012-2018 | 229 | 26 | 11.4% | $549K |
| 2019-2026 | 243 | 9 | **3.7%** | $627K |

- Session years alone: 2005 29%, 2009 30%, 2011 29%. Then 2021 0 of 40, 2023 2 of 51, 2025 2 of 60.
- In dollars: named gifts (bracket top) were 23% of cover-sheet gift dollars in 2005. They were 2% in 2025, when $282K of gifts was reported.
- The reports didn't shrink: 228, 229 and 243 $1K+ gift reports in the three eras.

**Peer comparison (other spending types, same reports).** Share of cover sheets with spending that itemize at least one row:

| Type | 2005 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Gifts | 16% (118/715) | 4% (23/598) |
| Food | 5.5% (221/4,002) | 2.3% (116/5,040) |
| Entertainment | 13% (99/738) | 3.4% (18/532) |
| Transportation | 44% (30/68) | 32% (15/47) |

(Food, entertainment and transportation counts are raw cover rows, not deduped for corrections.)
So the slide isn't gift-specific. Gifts, food and entertainment all fell. Transport fell less.
Food money almost doubled while this happened: $2.22M (2005) to $4.21M (2025) on cover sheets.

**"Who got the most gifts" is a small-money question.**
- A third of rows (1,344 of 4,084) sit on reports that gave the same item to 20+ people, like a gift bag to every member.
- Take those out and the top named official is Rep. Senfronia Thompson: 35 gifts from 18 lobbyists over 20 years, $1,242-$3,842 total. The median recipient got $143 (bracket top).

**Dull explanation.** Texas only makes lobbyists name a recipient above a dollar threshold, and the state raises its thresholds with inflation (general knowledge, not from the warehouse).
If gifts cluster just under a rising line, names vanish while totals hold. That fits the slide across food and entertainment too.
**Not tested:** TEC's threshold history isn't in the warehouse. Under-$100 gifts still make up 56% of the rows that do get itemized in 2021-26 (67% in 2004-08), so some lobbyists name small gifts anyway.

**Hit means.** The public can see a lobbyist spent $1,000+ on gifts but almost never who got them. Either a law story (thresholds) or a compliance story (thresholds not the cause).
**Miss would have meant.** Itemization tracks spending. It doesn't.

**Colour, not the story (2005-2015, all small).**
- Guns as lobby gifts. Henry AR-7 .22 rifles to four men on one 2009 report (Robert Saunders): Mark Homer, Edmund Kuempel, Jim McReynolds, Chuck Hopson. A Henry Golden Boy rifle ($385) and a Stoeger 12-gauge shotgun ($300) from Louis Sinclair in 2015. A $500 gift card "to purchase a gun at McBride's Guns" for Allen Fletcher in 2015 (Christopher W. Jones).
- Charles Stuart (Blue Cross Blue Shield of Texas on his 2007-2015 client lines) gave golf gift bags to 70 guests of the Caring For Children Foundation golf tournament, in both 2005 and 2006. Bracket tops average $177 and $296 each.

**Traps.**
- Repeated total: Toby Goodman's cover sheet shows $20,120 of gifts on 9 monthly reports in a row (Apr-Dec 2013), $181K in all. That's 39% of 2013's gift total. Christy Muse repeats $1,200 seven times. Drop these before any year trend.
- A $2,500 "Campaign contribution" to Sen. Bob Hall is filed as a gift (2019), with first and last name swapped (first = Hall).
- The $7,500 top row went to the Texas Association of Manufacturers, not an official. RECIPIENTNAMEORGANIZATION is empty, so the org sits in the last-name field.
- ACTIVITYEXACTAMOUNT is 41% filled. For bracket rows, RANGELOW is 0 for LT100. Use the bracket top as a ceiling, never a sum of true dollars.
- 65 itemized reports (6%) show $0 gifts on their own cover sheet.
- Corrections don't double-count here: no filer-period has both an original and a correction in this table.

## 3. POLITICS__TX_LOBBY_EVENTS — probed

**What it is.** Schedule G: lobbyist spending on fundraisers and charity events that honor an official. 3,107 rows, 2005-2026.
**No dollar column.** Dollars live only on the cover sheet (TOTAL_EXPEND_EVENT): $1.90M across 744 filers, 1991-2026.

**Checks run.**
- **The session ban.** Texas bars contributions to legislators and statewide officials from 30 days before a regular session until 20 days after it ends (general knowledge; session dates typed in by hand, not from the warehouse).
  - Dated fundraiser rows inside those windows: **7 of 929 (0.8%)**. 26.4% of calendar days fall inside them.
  - Control: dated charity events land inside 34.6% of the time. So the dates are real, and fundraisers avoid the window.
  - The 7: two are county officials (not covered). One is "Speaker's Reunion Day". The rest are edge dates: Garnet Coleman (2005-01-10), Mark Strama (2010-12-13, one day in), Sergio Munoz (2016-12-28), Railroad Commissioner Elizabeth Ames Jones (2009-06-11). ACTIVITYDATE can be the payment date, so these are thin.
- **Concentration.** 4 lobbyists file 39% of fundraiser rows.
  - Lionel Aguirre: 183 rows, 98 officials, 2009-2016. He lists Blue Cross Blue Shield of Texas as his client in 2014.
  - Lynton Allred and Chris Newton, both for the Texas Petroleum Marketers and Convenience Store Association: 210 rows, 2005-2008.
  - So the table shows who itemizes carefully, not who spends most.
- **Honorees.** 445 officials. The median has one lobbyist. Seven officials tie at 8 lobbyists; Greg Abbott has the most rows of those, 15 (2006-2026).
- **Top event spender on cover sheets:** Charles Stuart (Blue Cross Blue Shield of Texas on his 2007-2015 client lines), $119,459 over 36 reports, 2003-2015. Next: Jenny Perez Capitaine, $58,893.
- Join: every report ID lands in the cover sheet, 769 of 769. 47 show $0 events on their own cover.

**Dull explanation.** Paying for an official's fundraiser is legal and reported. The ban is being followed.
**Hit would have meant** a pattern of fundraisers during session. **Miss means** compliance, and it's a clean miss.

**Traps.**
- **Shadow rows.** The 562 rows with a blank event kind (2005-2014) are twins. 553 name only a beneficiary, with no date or description. 495 mirror a real row on the same report: the real row's recipient is the twin's beneficiary. Counting rows double-counts about 16% of the table before 2015.
- ACTIVITYDATE is blank on 861 rows (562 of them the twins).
- RECIPIENTNAMEORGANIZATION and BENEFICIARYNAMEORGANIZATION are 100% empty.

## 4. POLITICS__TX_LOBBY_AWARDS — probed

**What it is.** Schedule F: plaques, trophies and mementos. 1,589 rows, 2005-2026.

**Checks run.**
- Time vs the cover sheet:
  - Itemized bracket tops were 76% of cover award dollars in 2005 and 104-118% in 2008-09. They were 7% in 2025.
  - Reports that itemize: 46% (2005) to 18% (2025). Same slide as gifts.
- Size: cover-sheet awards run $4K-$35K a year. Small.
- Concentration: 312 rows sit on reports that gave 20+ people the same award, like group scorecard awards ("Law & Order Award", "Courageous Defender of Life").
- Top recipient: Sen. Jane Nelson, 21 awards from 19 lobbyists, 2005-2021, $3,741 bracket top.
- Over $500. Texas caps lobbyist awards and mementos at about $500 (general knowledge, not from the warehouse; the statute and any inflation index were not checked). Rows above it:
  - Texas Dental Association "Excellence in Patient Advocacy" awards, **$1,000 each**, to Fred Brown, Judith Zaffirini and Jodie Laubenberg (2006, Oliver Bond).
  - Texas REALTORS, $692.80 to Sen. Kelly Hancock (2019). Plaque and vase, $668.65, to Leticia Van de Putte (2007). IIAT award, $568.40, to Dan Patrick (2016). Remington statue, $525.01, to Tommy Williams (2007).

**Hit means.** A handful of over-cap awards, mostly 17-20 years old. A lead to check, not a story.
**Traps.** Same as gifts: exact amount is 11% filled; brackets are ceilings; 29 itemized reports show $0 awards on their cover sheet.

## 5. POLITICS__TX_LOBBY_DOCKETS — probed

**What it is.** Schedule A docket lines: the case, project or bill number a lobbyist wrote down. 1,207 lines, 167 lobbyists, 2001-2026.

**Checks run.**
- Reach: no single docket drew more than 7 lobbyists. The top real one is PUC Docket 51812 (2021), with 7.
  - Top filers are a few utility lobbyists (Harrelson 119 lines, Hekman 111, 2012-2015) and the Texas Municipal League.
- Agency field: "Other" on 843 of 1,207 (70%). Public Utility Commission 154.
- Junk text: blank, "N/A", "_", "Not applicable" and "See various" on 226 lines.
- **Join to CourtListener** (real key: the court case number):
  - Texas Supreme Court and Court of Criminal Appeals numbers: **42 of 42 land**. Most are city cases the Texas Municipal League listed, 2016-2025.
  - Intermediate appeals numbers (like 03-13-00084-CV): **0 of 11 land.**
  - Abortion cases showed up in 2024-25: State v. Zurawski (23-0629) and Weldon v. Lilith Fund (24-0250), listed by three lobbyists in all (Pojman, O'Donnell, Murillo-Zuniga).
- "Who won" isn't in the table, so the glance's question can't be answered from it.

**Hit means.** Usable to look up who listed a given PUC project or court case. **Miss:** no crowd around any docket, so no ranking story.

**Traps.**
- **DOCKET_ID is not a docket.** It's the state's line serial: 1,207 distinct on 1,207 rows. The catalog tags it as a DOCKET key. The real docket is DESIGNATIONTEXT, which is free text.
- LOBBYFORMTYPE is a constant (LA-A5).
