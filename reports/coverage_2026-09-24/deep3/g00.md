## 1. NPPES: hospice registrations follow a Los Angeles phone out of California. **live**

  - 7.3M persons, 1.9M organizations.
  - 346,179 rows have a blank type. Those are the deactivated NPIs.

  - Joined to Medicare hospice enrollments by NPI: 5,572 land.
  - 5,490 of those land in the certified-hospice file by CCN.
- The **time** pattern in Los Angeles-area ZIPs (900-918):

  - 53 in 2021 Q4
  - 3 in 2022 Q1
  - Almost none since

  - Next come tiny counts: Alaska 1 of 10, Idaho 3 of 42. Texas is 2.3% (23). Every other state is under 5%.

  - Everyone else: 81 of 246 (32.9%)
  - Fisher p = 4e-8
  - A name-only match to the enrollment or certified files adds 12 LA-phone and 30 others. They may have enrolled under a different NPI.
- **Second field:** 58 of 88 Arizona LA-phone NPIs (2020 on) have an authorized official who also signs for a California hospice NPI.

  - Other out-of-state NPIs: 453 of 3,419 (13%)

**Hit means:** after the LA hospice boom went bust, LA-linked operators opened hospice shells in Arizona and Nevada, and almost none reached Medicare billing. That fits license-and-NPI farming.

**Miss means:** if these are real multi-state operators with LA offices, or they enrolled and got revoked, it's churn, not fraud.

**Boring:**
- Enrollment lag. **Tested:** at Arizona's own same-year rates, 26.4 enrollments were expected and 4 happened.
- Legitimate chains with LA officers. Compassus's official has a 310 phone. Not tested one by one. The overall 10.6% enrollment rate (19 of 179) says most are not established chains.
- **Not tested:** hospices that enrolled and were later revoked drop out of the current enrollment file. That would also produce "never enrolled". It's the biggest hole.
- Known context (from memory, check it): California froze new hospice licenses starting 2022. In 2023 CMS put new hospices in AZ, CA, NV and TX under extra oversight. The state list is known. The phone trail and the enrollment gap are the new part.
- The phone number is self-reported.

**Traps**
- ⚠ **The 346,179 deactivated NPIs carry nothing but the NPI and the deactivation date.** No type, no name, no taxonomy, no address. **Checked: 0 of them have any name or taxonomy.**
  - So every count by specialty misses shells whose NPI was shut off.
  - The hospice counts above are floors.
- The hospice enrollment file sits in the wrong schema: `IMMIGRATION.IMMIGRATION__FED_CMS_HOSPICE_ENROLLMENTS`.
- 1,198 of 5,572 enrolled hospice CCNs aren't six digits (for example `A91649`, `B51545`). A digits-only CCN check drops 21% of them.
- 747 is the 818 overlay. Leave it out and the Arizona 2020-23 count drops from 83 to 48.

## 2. MDS answer frequencies: one Missouri chain and the schizophrenia count. **live**

- ⚠ **Complementary suppression** on schizophrenia (I6000):

  - 3,010 homes show only "No". That count matches their full resident count (median 0.88 of average daily residents), so it's zero or near it.
  - 9,560 homes have both hidden, which means 1-10 residents. Suppressed doesn't mean zero.
- National rate estimate: 7.8% of residents. The bounds are 3.9-11.8%, depending on the hidden homes.
- **Peer, by state:** 632 homes have 11+ residents coded schizophrenic at 3x or more their state's rate. That's 21,637 residents. Against the other 14,062 homes:

**Hit means:** Missouri concentrates mentally ill nursing-home residents in one operator's homes. That operator is worse than other homes serving the same population: stars, fines, harm-level abuse.

**Boring:**

  - Dementia is low (9-46% against a Missouri median of 36.6%).
  - These are psych-population homes. The national "phony diagnosis" angle can't be separated in this file: 0 homes passed the long-stay-only test.
- The star rating is inspection-heavy, and harder populations draw more citations. That's why the peer test uses only other 40%+ homes.
- The diagnosis is self-coded by the home. The harm claims rest on surveyor citations and CMS fines, not on the coding.

**Traps**
- ⚠ **Hidden "Yes" and "No" together** (complementary suppression). Treating a missing percent as zero undercounts; treating it as missing overstates the rate.
- `TOTAL_RESIDENTS` is the placeholder `_` on 27.1M of 31.4M rows.
- 73,475 duplicate (CCN, question, answer) rows: 31,403,215 rows against 31,329,740 keys.
- In the star file, `SPECIAL_FOCUS_STATUS` holds empty strings, not NULL. `IS NOT NULL` counts every home.

- **Severity, CA (S15 re-derived in SQL):**

  - Poverty 18.2% vs 16.0%.
  - 17 of the areas on the withdrawal list have zero full-time doctors.
- Florida: score 15 vs 15, poverty 16.8% vs 15.5%.

**Hit means:** millions of Californians in areas that are still short of doctors could lose shortage status and what comes with it: Medicare bonus pay, loan-repayment placements, visa waivers.

**Miss means:** if "proposed for withdrawal" in CA mostly means the state office hasn't resubmitted data, it's a paperwork backlog. That's still a story, but a different one.

**Boring:**
- This is HRSA's routine update cycle, and a proposed withdrawal can be reversed.
- The scores on the withdrawal list may be stale, not re-scored. **Tested:** severity and poverty are no better than in designated areas.
- **Not tested:** whether California's office submitted updates. That's the hinge.
- Some areas really did improve: Petaluma's score is 4.

**Traps**
- The score is zeroed on withdrawal (withdrawn median 0). You can't see how bad an area was before it came off the list.
- `DESIGNATION_POPULATION` repeats on every component row. Take the max per HPSA_ID, never the sum.
- One `DESIGNATION_DATE` of 1970-01-01 (an FQHC). `RECORD_CREATE_DATE` is constant.
- The September 2025 update date is not a withdrawal-only marker.
