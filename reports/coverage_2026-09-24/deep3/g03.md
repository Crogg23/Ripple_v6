## 1. Medicare inpatient by hospital: **live**

**The lead**
- Oroville Hospital, CCN 050030, Oroville CA. Its Medicare patients averaged **1.78 hospital stays each**: 2,213 patients, 3,938 stays.
- That ranks **#1 of 2,109** hospitals with 500+ Medicare patients.
  - National mean 1.34 (SD 0.08). Oroville sits 5.3 SD above it.
  - Next biggest hospital (2,000+ patients): Comanche County Memorial, OK, at 1.58.
  - The only hospital that comes close is North Vista, NV, at 1.77. It's smaller (787 patients) and 79% dual-eligible.
- **Peer groups:**
  - California median: 1.34. At that rate Oroville would have had ~2,961 stays, not 3,938.
  - Micropolitan hospitals (RUCA 4-6, n=289): median 1.35. Oroville is #1.
  - Its neighbor Enloe Health, in the same county (Chico): 1.50.

**Checked**
- **Sicker patients? No.** The risk score is 2.00 against a California median of 2.41 (173rd of 213). The dual-eligible share is 49%, right at the California median of 47%.
- **Adjusted:** I regressed stays per patient on risk score and dual share nationally. After that, and after the state's own residual, Oroville still has ~1,060 extra stays. Per patient, that excess is #1. In raw extra stays only Meritus (MD), a hospital twice its size, is higher at ~1,200.
- **The stays are shorter and cheaper.** Length of stay is 4.0 days against 5.1 (201st of 213). Medicare pays $15,089 per stay against $18,038. More, shorter, cheaper stays of less-sick patients is the low-threshold-admission shape.
- **Sepsis, from the DRG table (S06):**
  - DRGs 871/872 are **40%** of Oroville's listed Medicare stays. The California median is 20%, which puts Oroville 6th of 180. Enloe is at 11%.
  - Caveat: nationally, sepsis share and stays per patient don't move together (corr -0.07). Hospitals in the top 10% on both: 16, against ~21 by chance. Sepsis is a second observation, not confirmation.
- **Independent check, CMS Hospital Compare (S04):**
  - Oroville is "worse than national" on 4 of 10 readmission measures. Only 4.3% of hospitals have 4 or more.
  - Across all 2,109 hospitals, my stays-per-patient score tracks CMS's own readmission ratings. The top tenth averages 1.72 "worse" measures; the bottom tenth averages 0.95. So the score measures something real.
- **Time, from HCRIS cost reports (S19):**
  - Oroville's Medicare (Title XVIII) discharges went from 3,406 (FY2011) to 6,609 (FY2019), while beds only moved from 133 to 153.
  - Peak was 45.7 per bed in FY2018. That's 4.6x the California median of 9.9 and 1.5x Enloe's 30.2.
  - They then fell to 4,034 by FY2023 (26.4 per bed; Enloe 33.2).
- **The file carries no year column.** Oroville's 3,938 stays sit closest to its FY2023 cost report (4,034), so it's probably 2023 data.
- **DOJ check came back empty, and the miss means nothing.** The warehouse's DOJ link table (S21) holds 751 distinct link texts and zero hospital or False Claims links.
- My unverified memory is that DOJ announced a False Claims Act matter with Oroville Hospital over inpatient admissions. **Check justice.gov before pitching.** If it's real, the 2019-2023 drop is the before/after.

**Hit means:** one hospital admits Medicare patients again and again, well above every peer group, with healthier patients and heavy sepsis coding.
**Miss would have meant:** the rate is explained by sickness, poverty or region. The risk, dual-share and neighbor tests say it isn't.

**The dull explanation:** Butte County is poor, and the 2018 Camp Fire displaced a lot of people.
- Tested against the neighbor: Enloe serves the same county and runs 1.50, not 1.78.
- Not tested: whether Oroville counts same-day returns or transfers in ways others don't. The file doesn't show that.

**Traps**
- The file has no data year.
- `TOT_DAYS - TOT_CVRD_DAYS` (days Medicare didn't cover) runs 20-38% at Kaiser hospitals, Alaska Native Medical Center and Queen's Honolulu. The median is 2.8%. Its meaning isn't defined in the table. Don't use it until CMS's definition is read.
- Kaiser hospitals are 13 of the top 15 (all sizes) on "non-Medicare share of payment". That's a Kaiser billing artifact, not a finding.

## 2. FDA GUDID device catalog: probed

**Checked**
- 5,083,948 rows, 5,061,910 distinct barcodes (`PRIMARY_DI`), 11,918 labeler DUNS, one load run.
  - `RECORD_STATUS` is "Published" on every row.
  - `PUBLISH_DATE` runs 2013-02-20 to 2026-04-22.
- **Concentration (S12):** Cardinal Health has 370,346 barcodes (7.3%) and Medline 251,265 (5.0%). 97% and 94% of those are kits. The catalog's top end is custom surgical kits, one barcode per kit recipe.
- **Time (S13):** new barcodes spike in 2016 (762K) and 2022 (613K). Those are the FDA's Class II and Class I barcode deadlines.
  - FGX International (reading glasses) filed 74,463 barcodes between 2021-08 and 2022-10, right at the Class I deadline.
  - The share "not in commercial distribution" falls with age: 36% of 2014 records against 1% of 2024-25. The flag tracks age, not trouble.
- **Join (S02, S14): MAUDE adverse-event reports to GUDID on the barcode.**
  - MAUDE here is **2020-2021 only**: 2,743,561 reports. 65.9% carry a barcode.
  - Raw land rate is 72.3% of those, and 89.7% for death reports (6,702 of 7,472).
  - Of the 501,509 misses, ~237,617 report-rows match once leading zeros are stripped on both sides. MAUDE writes 13-digit and 18-digit zero-padded versions. That lifts the land rate to ~85%.

**Hit means:** GUDID is a usable bridge from harm reports to maker and product code.
**Miss means:** little. The misses are mostly formatting, and MAUDE's `MANUFACTURER_NAME` is blank on every missed row.

**Why no story:** device harm per maker needs units sold. GUDID counts models, not units. Ranking makers by deaths per model would just rank market share.

**Traps**
- 14,818 barcodes repeat across 36,856 rows. One Rmo Inc. barcode (00000008857972) sits on 3,539 separate device records. Don't assume `PRIMARY_DI` is unique.
- MAUDE→GUDID needs `LTRIM(di,'0')` on both sides.
- MAUDE covers only 2020-2021.

## 4. HRSA health-center sites: probed

**Checked**
- 19,038 sites under 1,526 grant numbers.
  - Every row is "Active", and `RECORD_CREATE_DATE` is 2026-08-07 on every row. Both are constants.
  - `OPERATING_SCHEDULE` and `OPERATING_CALENDAR` are "Unknown" on about 70% of rows.
- **Join 1, NPPES on site NPI (S08).** 6,096 sites carry an NPI (6,048 distinct):
  - 5,971 are organizations and 4 are people.
  - 45 aren't in NPPES at all. That includes 1234567890, which is on 7 sites.
  - 28 are deactivated.
  - Name agrees on 5,834 of 6,013 org NPIs (97%); state agrees on 99.7%.
  - **The triage's "fake NPI" lead dies here.** The 227 in the glance was 1234567890's summed weekly hours, not a site count.
- **Join 2, UDS 2025 patient counts on grant number (S07):** 1,342 of 1,356 land (99%).
- **Peer:** patients per service site against the state median.
  - The bottom is school-based programs. La Clinica de los Campesinos (WI) runs 63 of 74 sites at schools: 232 patients per site against a WI median of 2,274.
  - Also at the bottom: homeless programs (Boston HCH), Alaska village clinics and county health departments.
  - Schools are 46.9% of sites in the bottom tenth against 27.3% overall.
  - **Dull explanation, tested, holds.**
- **Join 3, CMS Provider of Services on the site's Medicare number (S22):**
  - 7,539 of 8,426 numbers land (89%).
  - 101 are terminated in CMS's file (96 voluntary merger or closure), yet sit on 102 "active" HRSA sites. That's stale paperwork, not a story.
- **Time:** added-to-scope dates run 1966-2026. This is a snapshot of *today's* sites, though, so the old years hold survivors only.
  - 611 dates fall on Jan 1.
  - Mobile vans: 1,032 of 1,385 (75%) were added since 2019.
- 128 sites claim 168 hours a week; 47 of them are the two Alaska tribal systems.

**Why no story:** every outlier had its dull explanation checked, and the explanation held.

## 5. Open Payments profile roster: probed

**Checked**
- **Shape (S09): a clean lookup.**
  - 1,697,025 rows = 1,697,025 profile IDs.
  - 1,681,790 NPIs, each on exactly one row. 15,235 rows have no NPI (true nulls, no empty strings).
  - The alternate last name differs from the last name on 230,115 profiles, which is useful for name matching.
- **Join 1, 2024 payments on profile ID (S10):**
  - 15,336,961 of 15,336,988 NPI-bearing rows land, and the NPI never disagrees.
  - The 13,668 blank-NPI rows ($9.87M) land too (13,592), but the roster has no NPI for any of them. **Zero recovered.**
- **Join 2, the LEIE name path the NPI path misses (S11).** LEIE people with no real NPI were matched on first name + last or alternate last name + city + state:
  - 434 matches. 173 people with 223 match rows were paid after their exclusion date in 2024: $215,028.
  - **But the specialty mostly disagrees.** The top 5 rows hold 58.5% of the dollars, and all 5 are mismatches: a nurse's aide in LEIE against an ophthalmologist in the roster, and the like. The top names are common names in Miami, Houston and LA.
  - About a dozen agree on specialty, all small money. The best is a Miami Beach psychiatrist excluded in 2005 (1128b4, license), with 114 payments, $2,979.62, from 16 makers.
  - The NPI path was done on 2026-09-05 (F-113 to F-118). This adds almost nothing.

**Hit means:** name joins need specialty as the second field, not just city.
**Miss means:** nothing new here that the NPI path didn't find.

## Traps for the ledger
- **Facility affiliation is capped at 5 facilities per clinician per facility type.** "5" means 5 or more, so never sum a clinician's rows as reach.
- **MAUDE→GUDID needs leading zeros stripped:** 72% land raw, ~85% after. MAUDE in the warehouse is 2020-2021 only.
- **GUDID `PRIMARY_DI` isn't unique.** 14,818 barcodes repeat, and one Rmo barcode is on 3,539 records. `RECORD_STATUS` is constant.
- **The UDS sites table's status and create date are constants.** Its "1234567890" is on 7 sites. Its added-to-scope dates are survivors only.
- **The DOJ Wayback link table** (`INVESTIGATIONS__XC_WAYBACK_REPLAY_DOJ_LISTING`) has 751 distinct link texts and no hospital or FCA links. It isn't a DOJ index, so a miss there means nothing.
- **Medicare inpatient by-provider has no year column.** Oroville's count matches its FY2023 cost report best.

parked: the covered-days gap (Queen's Honolulu 21%, UVM, Alaska Native 38%) may be a patients-stuck-waiting-for-placement story. It needs CMS's definition of covered days first.
parked: hospitals in the top 10% on stays per patient carry 1.8x the readmission "worse" marks of the bottom 10% (1.72 against 0.95). A national screen with this score and HRRP penalty dollars would test it at scale.
