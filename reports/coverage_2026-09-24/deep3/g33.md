## 1. ENVIRONMENT__FED_EPA_RCRA_FACILITIES — live

**What it is.** 1,613,224 hazardous-waste handler IDs, one row each, from one load.
The table only lists them. The story comes from joining it to RCRA_EVALUATIONS (inspections) and RCRA_NAICS (industry).

**Method.**
- Plants = today's large-quantity generators (LQGs) with a manufacturing industry code (31-33). That makes 8,414 plants across the 28 states with 100 or more.
- Clock = evaluations through 2026-07-31, the last date on file.
- Dead records are ruled out by the base: plants inspected at least once since July 2016.
  Then we ask what share were also inspected since July 2021.

**The finding.**
- Louisiana: 195 manufacturing LQGs had an evaluation since mid-2016. **Only 106 (54.4%) had one since mid-2021.**
- Counting only full on-site inspections (CEI): **90 of 182, 49.5%.**
- 28 states: the median is **92.4%** (any evaluation) and **91.8%** (full inspections only). Louisiana is last on both.
  - Next lowest on full inspections: IL 62.7%, TX 66.5%, CT 71.5%, NY 76.3%. Top: AL 99.4%, KY 99.3%, NC 98.5%.
- **Time.** Distinct Louisiana manufacturing LQGs evaluated per year:
  - 2010-2019: **58.2 a year** on average. 2023: 29. 2024: 33. 2025: 37.
  - That's **-43%.** NY -44%, NJ -37%, TX -31%. Over the same years OH rose 15%, MI 14%, PA 9%.
- The agency didn't shrink. All RCRA evaluations logged in Louisiana ran 289-379 a year in 2012-2019 and **392-535 a year in 2021-2025**. The work moved away from the big plants.

**Five rows, eyeballed.** No evaluation of any kind since mid-2021:
- Shell Chemical LP, Geismar. Last 2018-02-14.
- CF Industries Nitrogen, Donaldsonville. Last 2019-03-27.
- Valero Refining Meraux. Last 2019-12-12, with 58 evaluations on file before that.
- Chalmette Refining. Last 2020-06-29.
- Mosaic Fertilizer Uncle Sam plant. Last 2020-06-30. It has **5 violations with no return-to-compliance date.**
- Also: Innophos, Geismar (last 2017-09-26, 4 open violations). Galata Chemicals, Hahnville (2021-03-29, 3 open).

**The dull explanations, tested.**
- **Second IDs.** One plant can hold two EPA IDs. 83 Louisiana plants were seen in 2016-21 but not since. 10 of them share a ZIP and the first word of their name with an ID inspected since mid-2021. Two of those are false matches (Chalmette Medical Center, Gulf South Armature). Even counting all 10, Louisiana is at 60.8% (113 of 186). Still last.
- **Bad industry codes.** 17 of Louisiana's 233 "manufacturing" LQGs carry NAICS 322219 (paperboard boxes), among them Ochsner Clinic, Rapides Parish School Board and Music Mountain Water. Texas has none. Dropping them: 55.4% (103 of 186).
- **Record reviews counted as inspections.** Tested: full-inspection-only numbers above. Louisiana's mix is 59% full inspections, the same as IL (58%) and NC (53%).
- **Inspections filed somewhere else.** Not ruled out. But Louisiana logs 392-535 evaluations a year into this system (2021-2025), so the data is still flowing.
- **An approved alternative plan.** EPA lets states swap some LQG coverage for other targets under an alternative plan. **Not checked.** From memory: EPA's strategy is about 20% of LQGs a year, or all of them every 5 years. Not checked against the current text.

**Hit means.** In 5 years, Louisiana's hazardous-waste program fully inspected about half its chemical plants and refineries. Most states reach nearly all of theirs.
**Miss would have meant.** Dead records, second IDs or record-type coding explain the gap. They move it about 6 points. The gap to the median is 38.
**Next step.** Ask LDEQ whether it has an alternative compliance-monitoring plan, and pull its inspection list for 2021-2026.

**Traps (for the ledger).**
- `FULL_ENFORCEMENT` is `------` on 1,611,303 of 1,613,224 rows (99.88%). Only 1,921 rows carry a letter, so the glance's "worst actors by FULL_ENFORCEMENT" has nothing to rank.
- One name, thousands of IDs: RESIDENCE sits on 43,670 IDs, CON EDISON on 17,347 (one per job site), MI DEPT/TRANSPORTATION on 2,401.
- The LQG list is lopsided: CA holds 11,253 and NY 6,181 of 33,768 LQGs. Only 1,894 of California's are manufacturers. Count LQGs by state only after an industry filter.
- California: 739 of its 1,894 manufacturing LQGs have **no evaluation ever**. Likely county-level (CUPA) inspections that never reach RCRAInfo. Not checked. Don't read it as a finding.
- Some "LQG" records are dead plants: Moran Galvanizing, last evaluated 1986; Rhone Poulenc Springhill, 1994.
- RCRA_EVALUATIONS rows aren't all inspections. It also logs SNC calls (SNY/SNN), self-disclosures (FSD) and "no 3007 information request received" (NIR).
- `HANDLER_ID` is a scrambled code, one per row. It isn't the EPA ID; use `ID_NUMBER`.

## 2. ENVIRONMENT__FED_EPA_ICIS_AIR_ICIS_AIR_FACILITIES — live

**What it is.** 279,728 Clean Air Act facilities, one row per permit ID (PGM_SYS_ID, 0 repeats). 13,559 are operating major sources.
**The join.** Joined to ICIS_AIR_VIOLATION_HISTORY (102,037 rows, 44,787 of them high-priority violations, or HPVs) and ICIS_AIR_FORMAL_ACTIONS (106,009). **100% of both tables' IDs land** in this table.

**The finding.**
- **33 of 75 operating major portland cement plants (SIC 3241) drew at least one new HPV in 2020-2025: 44.0%.**
- That's the highest of the 35 industries with 40 or more major plants. The median industry is **12.3%**. All majors: 9.3%. Power plants: 7.0%. Refineries: 28.1%.
- **Time (two six-year windows).** Cement went up from 37.3% (2014-19) to 44.0% (2020-25). Every comparison group went down: refineries 40.6 to 28.1, power plants 7.7 to 7.0, all other majors 10.3 to 9.2.
  - Cement was also high in 2008-13 (52.0%). It's a lasting pattern, not one bad window.
- **A few plants don't carry it.** The 33 plants sit in 19 states. They belong to 11 of the 19 cement company groups we could name, plus 3 plants we couldn't.
  - Heidelberg Materials/Lehigh: 10 of 14 plants. Without Heidelberg: 23 of 61, **37.7%**.
  - Outside California (where local districts log everything): 32 of 65, **49.2%**.
- **HPVs open 2+ years:** 14 of 75 cement plants (18.7%). All majors: 3.8%.
- **Enforcement does follow.** 30 of the 33 got a formal action since 2020. Penalties since 2020 add up to $12.76M across the 75 plants. The median plant with an HPV paid $197,500.
- What was violated (92 HPV records, 2020-25): particulate matter, mercury, visible emissions, NOx, SO2, VOCs. States found 81; local agencies found 11.

**Examples.**
- Heidelberg Materials, Mitchell IN: 6 HPVs in 2020-25. Its oldest open HPV started 2009-09-01. 5 formal actions since 2020, $330K.
- Amrize (formerly Holcim), Morgan UT: 3 HPVs in 2020-25, all 3 still open, the oldest from 2023-10-30. Status "Unaddressed-State". Its last formal action on file is from **2006-03-20**.
- Lehigh Southwest Cement, Cupertino CA: 6 HPVs in 2020-25. 6 are still open, the oldest from 2022-11-03. 13 formal actions since 2020, $485K.

**The dull explanations.**
- **Cement kilns are watched harder.** They run continuous emission monitors under the 2015 cement air-toxics rule (MACT), so more exceedances get caught. Partly tested: power plants run continuous monitors too, and sit at 7.0%. Not ruled out.
- **State logging habits.** Outside California the rate goes up, not down.
- **Small group.** 75 plants.
- **The HPV label is coded by the agency.** On its own it can't carry the claim. Here 30 of 33 plants also have formal actions and penalties on file, which back it up.

**Hit means.** Cement plants break the most serious air rules more often than any other big industry. They've kept doing it through the fines, while other industries improved.
**Miss would have meant.** One company, one state or one bad year. The numbers say it isn't.

**Traps (for the ledger).**
- 105 permanently closed facilities still carry a violation status. 36 of them read "Violation-Unresolved".
- `CURRENT_HPV` is a 10-value status. 1,515 rows read "Violation w/in 1 Year", which isn't an HPV.
- "Unaddressed-State" can sit on a plant with 5 formal actions since 2020 (Heidelberg Mitchell). The status field lags, or points at one specific HPV.
- `HPV_DAYZERO_DATE` has a future date: 2026-09-28 on IN0000001814100146.
- One registry ID can cover a whole fleet: 110037604152 = 150 portable Powerscreen crushers in VA. 110070082225 = 144 Evergreen well pads in CO.
- `SIC_CODES` is blank on 64,993 rows. `NAICS_CODES` holds the 999999 placeholder on 26,485.
- The glance's "worst actors by AIR_POLLUTANT_CLASS_DESC" ranks size class, not harm.

## 3. FINANCE__FED_NCUA_CALL_REPORTS_FS220 — live (moderate)

**What it is.** One quarter, 2026-03-31, 4,336 credit unions, 242 money lines named only by account code (ACCT_xxx).

**Decoding the codes.** We matched columns against FEDERALLY_INSURED_CU_LIST, which has named columns:
- ACCT_010 = total assets, ACCT_025B = total loans, ACCT_018 = deposits, ACCT_083 = members. Each matches exactly on over 95% of rows.
- ACCT_041B read as **loans 60+ days late**. Across the industry it comes to 0.84% of loans, the scale NCUA publishes. It equals the sum of buckets 021B-024B within 1% at Greater Nevada and U.S. Eagle. At Civic the gap is 10%.
  **The label isn't confirmed.** NCUA's account list isn't in the warehouse.
- Undivided earnings (940) + 602 + 658, divided by assets, reproduces the list's net worth ratio within 0.05 points on 75% of credit unions.

**Join.** FS220 to the insured list on CU_NUMBER = CHARTER_NUMBER: **4,250 of 4,336 land (98.0%).** All 86 misses are CU_TYPE 3 (see traps).

**The finding.** Peer group 6 is credit unions over $500M: 758 of them.
Medians: late loans 0.62%, net worth ratio 10.55%, ROA 0.68%, **net worth +7.7% over the year.**
- **Only 3 of 758 lost more than 20% of their net worth in the year to March 2026.** We picked them on that. Then we tested them on a separate measure from a different table, late loans. They rank #1, #2 and #4 of 758.

| Credit union | Assets | Net worth, 1 yr | Net worth ratio | Loans 60+ days late | ROA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Civic FCU, Raleigh NC | $3.19B | **-39.5%** | 6.45% (under the 7% "well capitalized" line) | 5.04% (8.2x median, #2) | **-2.67% (worst of 758)** |
| Greater Nevada CU, Carson City NV | $1.69B | -30.3% | **3.33%** | 4.41% (7.1x, #4) | +0.86% |
| U.S. Eagle FCU, Albuquerque NM | $1.48B | -20.6% | 8.11% | **6.63% (10.7x, #1)** | -0.91% |

- Greater Nevada is **the only credit union over $100M with a net worth ratio under 4%.** FS220 backs it up independently: undivided earnings alone are 2.93% of assets.
- Civic also shrank: loans -17.7%, members -13.6%.
- At Greater Nevada and U.S. Eagle, most of the late money sits in one bucket (ACCT_023B): $41.8M and $55.7M, 3.3% and 5.3% of loans. At the median big credit union that bucket is 0.03%.
  That points at a few big loans or one pool, not members across the board falling behind. The bucket's label isn't confirmed.
- Greater Nevada's charge-offs run low (0.31% a year). The late money hasn't been written off yet.

**The dull explanations.**
- Size noise: no. All three hold over $1.4B.
- A merger: none of the three appears in the 27 NCUA merger records.
- Business model (indirect auto, subprime): not ruled out. The low-income flag doesn't split them out: 2,379 of 4,250 credit unions carry it.
- Already public or already under NCUA action: **not checked.** Press and NCUA enforcement orders are the next step.

**Hit means.** Three large credit unions whose capital is draining while their late loans run 7-11x their peers'. It's a supervisory question: is there a net worth restoration plan?
**Miss would have meant.** If NCUA defines 041B differently, the late-loan ranks go, but the net worth numbers stand. They come from the named list.

**Traps (for the ledger).**
- **191 of 242 ACCT columns are 0 on every row**, as zeros, not nulls. ACCT_009 was only the first one found. Zero here means "not loaded," not "$0."
- ACCT_521-524 hold small numbers (medians 990, 1,171, 544, 627). They aren't dollars; likely rates. Needs NCUA's account list.
- The table isn't only federally insured credit unions. 86 rows are CU_TYPE 3, none on the insured list (Interra IN $2.0B, Beacon IN $1.8B, Clark County NV $1.6B).
  Matching them to the list by name hits other credit unions (Beacon IN vs a Beacon in TX).
- This is one quarter. The only time dimension comes from the list's 4-quarter growth columns.

## 5. ENVIRONMENT__FED_USGS_WATER — dead

**What it is.** 6,456,952 gauge readings, 2026-06-25 04:00 to 2026-07-02 19:00. Four measures: gage height (3.13M), streamflow (2.55M), water temperature (655K), pH (127K).

**Checks run.**
- Site counts hold steady every day: 3,594-3,607 height gauges, 846-853 temperature gauges. No break mid-week. The first and last days are partial.
- No repeats on site + time within a measure.
- Extremes all week: Rio Grande at Mission Main Canal TX, median 36.1 °C. Klamath above Keno Dam OR, pH 9.4-10.1. Denverton Slough CA, pH 4.3-5.7.
  178 of 858 temperature gauges hit 30 °C at least once.
- The dull explanation: summer heat, algae on the Klamath, acid soils in Suisun Marsh. Known natural conditions. Not verified, but nothing points elsewhere.

**Why dead.** It's one week, with no baseline to compare against. No key reaches harm, money or enforcement (only SITE_NO and HUC). It's fine as a gauge lookup.

**Traps (for the ledger).**
- **The glance's VALUE sum of -60.1B is filler.** 62,227 readings hold -999999, which alone adds up to -62.2B.
- VALUE mixes feet, cubic feet per second, °C and pH. Summing it means nothing, and the "MONEY" tag is wrong.
- 38,892 "daily" rows are summaries of the real-time rows. Mixing both double counts.
- One site (Purgatoire River near Thatcher, CO) logs 177 water temperatures below -50 °C, down to -13,100.
- 39,616 real-time flows are negative, down to -339,000 cfs. Tidal gauges run backward; that's real, not an error.
