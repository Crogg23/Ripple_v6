## 1. NPDES_NPDES_INSPECTIONS: live

One row per inspection per inspection type, at holders of EPA water-discharge permits.
1,900,067 rows. 1,786,956 distinct inspections. 286,752 permits.

**Checked: traps first**
- **113,099 rows (6.0%) are repeats.** Some inspections are logged under 2-4 inspection types.
  All 112,313 repeated inspection+permit pairs differ only on type. Count distinct ACTIVITY_ID, never rows.
- ACTUAL_BEGIN_DATE is blank on 916,098 rows (48%). ACTUAL_END_DATE is blank on only 263. Use the end date.
- 80 end dates fall before 1970, back to year 0005.
- STATE_EPA_FLAG holds only S (1,789,658) and E (110,409). No L, despite the catalog.
- ACTIVITY_OUTCOME is filled on 1,088 rows (0.06%). Useless.
- ACTIVITY_TYPE_CODE is INS on every row.
- **A state's count jumps when it starts sending data, not when it starts inspecting.**
  Yearly average, 2015-17 vs 2022-24: NC 158 → 1,268, NJ 268 → 1,475, WV 1,615 → 6,227, VA 990 → 3,821.
  Those states started reporting electronically to EPA under the NPDES eRule. Don't compare one state across 2017-2019.

**Checked: time. A series that stops**
- Test: each state's inspections by half-year, 2023 to mid-2026.
  Ratio = (2025 second half + 2026 first half) ÷ (2023 second half + 2024 first half).
  52 states and territories had 60+ inspections. **Median ratio 0.9.**
- Only three fell below 0.25: **OR 0.05, IA 0.06, CA 0.11.** Vermont was next at 0.28.

- **Is Oregon's whole data feed down? No.** Its other data kept arriving:
  - Discharge-report data covered 1,388 permits in 2025 and 1,411 in 2026.
  - Warning letters and other informal actions: 113 in 2025, 78 in 2026.
  - Only the inspections went to zero.
- Iowa's informal actions fell too: 254 → 51 → none in 2026. That looks more like a broken feed.
- California's informal actions: 1,153 in 2025, none in 2026. Same pattern as Iowa.
- EPA's own inspections in those states kept arriving. In 2025: OR 12, IA 33, CA 53.

**Hit means:** anyone using EPA's ECHO data sees Oregon doing zero water-permit inspections for 19 months. Iowa and California look nearly the same.
**Miss would have meant:** a normal dip, like the controls.
**Boring explanation:** a state changed its data system and the feed to EPA broke.
**Not tested.** The warehouse can't tell a broken feed from inspections that stopped. A call to Oregon DEQ can.
Either answer is a story: the state stopped inspecting, or it stopped telling EPA.

**Checked: time. EPA's own inspections**
- Distinct EPA-flagged inspections, January-June of each year. July is left out because it's still filling in.

```

2015   894      2019   708      2023   653
```

- 2026 is the high of the 2014-2026 window. It's 26% above 2025 and 65% above 2019.
- Late data would pull 2026 down, not up, so lag can't produce this.
- These are real site visits, not paperwork.
  - Full compliance evaluations (CEI), Jan-Jun: 575 in 2024 → 670 in 2025 → 903 in 2026.
  - Evaluations of industrial users: 65 → 159 → 138.
- The rise is spread out.
  - Biggest gains, Jan-Jun 2024 → 2026: IL +73, AR +51, MN +40, MA +38.
  - Those four make up 202 of the 443 increase (46%).
- EPA covering for the three dark states adds only 31 of the 443: CA 28 → 43, IA 8 → 22, OR 3 → 5.
- Before 2014 the counts were higher: about 2,000-3,800 EPA rows a year in 1985-2012 (not deduped). **This is a rebound, not an all-time high.**

**Hit means:** in the first half of the year, EPA inspectors are doing more water-permit inspections than at any time in the past 12 years.
**Boring explanations, not tested:** a national targeting push, or a change in how inspections get logged.
Check it against EPA's annual enforcement results.

**Checked: one join. Chronic violators nobody visited**
- Join: NPDES_QNCR_HISTORY (quarterly violation counts) to this table on NPDES_ID. Aggregated per permit before joining.
- "Chronic" = effluent-limit violations in at least 6 of the 12 quarters of 2023-2025.
  That's 8,503 permits, and 6,681 of them are individual permits (third character 0).
- For each: any inspection on file since 2021-01-01? Any informal or formal enforcement action since 2021?

- Illinois had the most uninspected: 67 of 231 (29%), against a median state of 3.7%.
  But 56 of those 67 got letters or orders. Illinois acts by mail.
- ⚠ **Wisconsin is a data hole, not a finding.**
  - Only 8 of its 106 chronic violators have any enforcement action on file. Elsewhere it's 78%.
  - Its inspection feed was near zero in 2023: 1 inspection in January-June, 16 in July-December.
- **Massachusetts is the lead.** EPA itself writes the water permits there.
  - 17 of 41 chronic violators have nothing on file since 2021.
  - Other EPA-run states don't show this. New Hampshire: 0 of 15 uninspected. New Mexico: 3 of 34.
  - Named example: Boston & Maine Railroad, East Deerfield (MA0000272). It had violations in 12 of 12 quarters, 60 in all. Its last inspection was 2013-09-24.
- Worst in the country by quarters and violation count (individual permits, no inspection since 2021):
  - Stelle Community Association STP, IL: 12 of 12 quarters, 393 violations. Last inspection 2017-03-22.
  - Bloomville WWTP, OH: 12/12, 204. Last inspection 2020-10-29.
  - Livingston Parish Sewer District, Buddy Ellis Road POTW, LA: 12/12, 174. No inspection anywhere in the table.
  - Alexis STP, IL: 12/12, 129. Last inspection 2012-07-10.
  - Tapia WRF, Calabasas, CA: 12/12, 101. Last inspection 2016-02-23.

**Hit means:** a permit holder broke its limits in most quarters for three years, and no agency visited or wrote.
**Miss means:** someone visited or wrote. That happened for 98.6% of chronic violators outside the listed states.
**Boring explanations, none tested:**
- Small lagoon towns with ammonia limits violate all the time, and states steer them into compliance schedules.
- Self-reported monthly figures can carry unit errors that create fake violations.
- A permit reissued under a new number takes its inspection history with it.

**Next join:** pull the 17 Massachusetts names. Then look them up in NPDES_FORMAL_ENFORCEMENT_ACTIONS back to 2015 and on ECHO's facility pages.

## 2. SDWA_SDWA_FACILITIES: live

This is the parts list of every public water system: wells, intakes, treatment plants, tanks, and the points where one system buys water from another.
1,554,832 rows. 430,916 systems.

**Checked: traps**
- **No history.** SUBMISSIONYEARQUARTER is 2026Q2 on every row.
- 9,545 deactivation dates fall before 1950 (placeholders like 1900-02-01). 7 are in the future, up to 2027-10-31.
  260 inactive parts have no deactivation date.
- **IS_SOURCE_IND = 'Y' also flags purchase points** (type CC, "consecutive connection").
  Those rows carry the seller's water type and filtration code. Count "sources" with that flag and you count bought water.
- **FILTRATION_STATUS_CODE is blank for whole states.** Active surface-water sources with no code:
  CO 1,134 of 1,142, MD 116 of 121, NM 82 of 83, TX 247 of 813. A blank there means nothing.
- "Must install filtration" (MIF) covers 57 active sources in 50 systems. 25 of those are community systems.
  NY has 11 and WY 7. Too few and too patchy to rank.

**Checked: one join. Does a violation follow the water?**
- Join: this table's SELLER_PWSID (who a system buys from) → SDWA_VIOLATIONS_ENFORCEMENT → SDWA_PUB_WATER_SYSTEMS.
  - Violations counted: health-based MCL violations under the chemical rules, starting 2021-2025.
  - Water systems table: active status and population.
  - Aggregated per system before joining.
- 2,487 systems had such a violation. 143 of them appear as the seller on an active purchase connection.
- They sell to 235 active systems serving 747,124 people.
  **210 of those 235 buyers (89%) show no violation for the same contaminant.** 25 do.
- Strict version: count only buyers with no active source of their own and only one seller. They drink only the seller's water.
  - **99 systems, 45,518 people, with no matching violation on their own record.** 9 more do carry it.
  - 98 of the 99 have a permanent connection, not an emergency one.
- Contaminants behind the 99: arsenic 33, combined radium 20, nitrate-nitrite 19, nitrate 9, gross alpha 7, asbestos 6, DEHP 3, uranium 3.
  - Asbestos usually comes from a system's own old pipes, and DEHP is often a lab artifact.
  - Drop both and at least 90 buyers remain.
- **Biggest example:** Saline Valley Conservancy District, IL (IL1655300), arsenic violation starting 2023-01-01.
  - Ten towns buy only from it, 20,568 people in all: Harrisburg (8,524), Eldorado (4,715), Carrier Mills, Shawneetown, Ridgway and five more.
  - None of the ten shows an arsenic violation.
  - Nearby Golconda buys from Millstone PWD and does carry its seller's arsenic violation. So a buyer's record can show one.
- **Schools:**
  - Red Mesa Public Schools (Navajo Nation, 900 people) buys from Sweetwater NTUA, which had 6 arsenic violations.
  - Crazy Horse School (428) and American Horse School (400) buy from Wanblee East, a Region 8 tribal system with 2 arsenic violations.
  - Aneth Community School (192) buys from Aneth NTUA, which had 13.
  - All four schools have clean records of their own.

**Hit means:** EPA's public lookup shows these towns and schools with a clean chemical record while their only water supply broke the limit. So does every tap-water tool built on that lookup.
**Miss would have meant:** buyers carry the seller's violation, as 25 of 235 do.
**Boring explanation:** the rule is built this way.
- Buyers usually don't have to test for arsenic or radium. The violation is charged at the seller's entry point.
- The seller must tell the buyer, and the buyer must tell its customers. **Not tested: whether they did.**
- So the story isn't a hidden violation. It's that the database hides who drank the water, and it raises the question: was Harrisburg told?

**Next join:** SDWA_PN_VIOLATION_ASSOC and the buyers' own public-notice violations for 2023-2025, starting with the Saline Valley ten.

- ⚠ **Stacked map points.**
  - One point in southwest Houston, 29.67161, -95.54656, holds 2,137 Texas facilities. Their city is UNKNOWN, ZIP 00000, street UNKNOWN. It's a default location, not a real place.
  - Others: a West Virginia point with 637 mines, a Cary, N.C. address with 627 water-system parts, and a Flowery Branch, Ga. point with 518 subdivision lots.
  - 46 points hold 100+ facilities each, 11,008 in all.
- No story on its own. Use it for addresses after a hit in a program table.

## 4. FRS_FRS_PROGRAM_LINKS: skip

- Crosswalk: 4,406,498 links across 13 EPA program systems.
- Clean one way: every (program, program ID) pair maps to exactly one registry ID. No repeated rows.
- ⚠ **Lumpy the other way.** One registry ID can hold up to 371 program IDs:
  - 362 NPDES permits on "CITY OF ALEXANDER", N.D.
  - 347 on "ROCKING K RANCH, PARCEL F", Ariz.
  - 245 on "UNKNOWN", Kan.
  - Most are construction stormwater permits piled onto one ID. Counting by registry ID undercounts permits, and ranking by it puts a lump on top.
- 145,334 registry IDs here have no row in FRS_FRS_FACILITIES. That's 3,422,891 here vs 3,277,557 there, and every facility ID appears here.

## Traps for the ledger
- NPDES inspections: one inspection can take 1-4 rows (one per type), 113,099 extra rows in all. Count distinct ACTIVITY_ID.
- NPDES inspections: ACTUAL_BEGIN_DATE is 48% blank, so use ACTUAL_END_DATE. Outcome is filled on 0.06%. The type code is a constant. The flag has no L.
- NPDES inspections: state jumps around 2017-2019 are eRule onboarding (NC ×8, NJ ×5.5, WV ×3.9).
- NPDES inspections: Oregon (2025 on), Iowa (mid-2025 on) and California (2026) state inspections are missing. Any recent state comparison is broken for these three.
- NPDES enforcement: Wisconsin barely sends enforcement actions (8 of 106 chronic violators have one). A Wisconsin miss means nothing.
- NPDES_QNCR_HISTORY: 117 rows are dated 2027-2033.
- SDWA facilities: one snapshot. 9,545 deactivation dates before 1950 and 7 in the future. IS_SOURCE_IND counts purchase points. Filtration status is blank statewide in CO, MD and NM, and a third of TX is blank.
- SDWA violations: a seller's chemical violation doesn't appear on the buyer's record. That applies to 99 all-purchase buyers serving 45,518 people.
- FRS facilities: California is 20.6% of rows because of hazardous-waste IDs. Houston's default point holds 2,137 facilities. 19% of rows have no point.
- FRS program links: a registry ID can hold up to 371 program IDs.
- NOAA alerts: it's a 31-hour snapshot. Station, wind and grid columns are empty.
