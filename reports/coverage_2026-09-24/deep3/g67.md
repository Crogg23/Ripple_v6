## 1. GOOGLE_POLADS_ADVERTISER_STATS: House members' official Google ads. **live**

**The mechanism.** 114 Google advertiser accounts give Google the same tax ID: **EIN 53-6002523**.
- Nearly all are named "REP. X - U.S. HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES".
- So this looks like the House's own EIN. That means these ads are paid from office budgets (taxpayer money), not campaign money.
- Not verified. Confirm the EIN with the House Chief Administrative Officer.
- Two more accounts are House offices by name but give no ID. Total: **116 accounts, $5,996,500 since 2018.**

**Check 1: time. It's growing fast, and not because of one account.**

| Weeks starting Jan-Jul | All House-office accounts | Without the big pooled account | Active accounts (without it) | Median per active account |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | $433,700 | $204,200 | 22 | $7,900 |
| 2025 | $381,700 | $248,500 | 11 | $3,900 |
| **2026** | **$1,382,900** | **$582,200** | **27** | **$13,100** |

- Full years: $201,000 (2019). $549,000 (2022). $1,118,300 (2024). $1,405,500 (2025). 2026 is already $1,400,900 by Aug 2.
- 2024 and 2026 are both election years, so the comparison is same phase, same months.

**Check 2: it behaves like official money. The 60-day blackout shows up in the data.**
- House offices may not send mass communications close to an election they're on the ballot for.
- Spend in the weekly table, 60 days before each general election, against the 60 days before that:

| Year | Last 60 days | Prior 60 days |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | $2,300 (2 accounts) | $68,000 (28) |
| 2022 | $15,300 (2) | $206,400 (23) |
| 2024 | **$900** (1) | **$553,000** (20) |

- Odd years, same calendar window: $49,400 / $49,100 / $117,600 / $119,200. So it isn't a seasonal dip.
- Ads stop on the exact day: 24 ads end 2024-09-06 and 20 end 2022-09-09. Each date is 60 days before election day.
- Who spent inside the window:
  - Pete Olson (2020) and Trey Hollingsworth (2022) left Congress at the end of that term. A retiring member isn't on the ballot, which is the likely exemption. Check it.
  - Henry Cuellar: $1,500 in the week of 2020-10-18.
  - **Kevin McCarthy's account: $11,600 over 8 weeks, Sep 11 to Oct 30, 2022, aimed at the whole country.** He was on the 2022 ballot: he served on until Dec 2023. Whether a leadership office falls under the blackout is an open question.

**Check 3: peer comparison. Do official ads lean toward contested seats?**
- Yardstick: 2024 general-election outside money per district (FEC independent expenditures, superseded lines dropped, S14).
- Official ads placed in a district for 2025-26: named accounts mapped to the member (name plus state must agree), plus the generic account's district-targeted ads.

| Districts, ranked by 2024 general-election outside money | With official Google ads 2025-26 | Official $ per district |
|---|---|---|
| Top fifth (87) | 22 (**25.3%**) | $7,544 |
| The other 344 | 54 (15.7%) | $3,955 |
| Top tenth (44) | 14 (**31.8%**) | $8,086 |
| The other 387 | 62 (16.0%) | $4,292 |

- One-sided test p = 0.03 (fifth) and 0.01 (tenth). A real lean, but not a huge one.
- Take out the four biggest districts on each side and it holds: $3,698 vs $2,581 per district.
- ⚠ Not placed in a district: ZIP-, county- and city-targeted generic ads, plus $178,100 from 4 unplaced accounts.
- Biggest official spend in the top fifth, with the outside-money rank: MI-7 $124,250 (14th), MD-6 $98,600 (62nd), NV-3 $72,150 (45th), NY-15 $54,400 (66th), MI-10 $51,000 (25th), PA-8 $44,550 (20th).
- ⚠ The yardstick is rough: NY-15, a safe Bronx seat, still ranks 66th.

**Check 4: who paid? The names on the accounts don't tell you.**
- **6 accounts are named only "US House of Representatives" (or "Office of Finance"). They hold $2,846,200, 47.5% of all House-office spend.** In Jan-Jul 2026 it's 63.1%.
- The biggest one (AR11776064889791971329, $2,658,300, 1,962 ads) is a pool. The ad rows show each ad aimed at one district.
  - Ads live in 2026 aim at 35 districts in 24 states. Top targets: MI-7, NV-3, WI-8, PA-2, Orange County ZIPs, FL-17, UT-1.
- Two accounts named for one member ran ads in **other members' districts** (S15, S16, ad-level links saved in `g67/S16_*.pkl`):

| Account name | Ads aimed at | When | Spend range |
|---|---|---|---|
| "REP. STEEL" (Michelle Steel, CA-45, term ended 2025-01-03) | NV-3 | Dec 2023 - Jan 2024 | $30,000-35,000 |
| | MN-8 | Mar 2024, Aug-Sep 2024, Aug-Oct 2025 | $9,500-11,100 |
| | **Ohio ZIPs 44056, 44067, 44087, 44202, 44203** | **Jun 22 - Aug 5, 2026** | $11,400-14,000 |
| | CA-45, her own district | Aug - Sep 2023, Aug 2023 - Jan 2024 | up to $7,300 (incl. 6 small city-targeted ads) |
| "Rep. Goldman" (Daniel Goldman, NY-10) | **CA-34** (Jimmy Gomez's district) | Jul 17 - Sep 5, 2024 | $59,500-70,500 |
| | NY-10 | Dec 2023 - Jan 2024 | $11,500-13,000 |

- So Google's political ad library credits a member who left Congress in January 2025 with official ads that ran in Ohio in summer 2026.

**The dull explanation.**
- Growth: the House raised office budgets, and outreach is moving from mail to digital. Not testable in the warehouse.
- Pooled and misnamed accounts: one buying service or vendor runs many offices' ads through a few verified accounts. Old names stick. That makes this a disclosure flaw, not misuse by the named member.
- Contested lean: vulnerable incumbents have long sent more franked mail in election years. This is the same pattern on new data.

**Hit means.** A checkable story: taxpayer-funded Google ads by House offices tripled in the election year, lean to swing seats, and Google's library names the wrong office for some.
**Miss means.** If House disbursement records show each ad billed to the district's own member and allowed, the misnamed angle shrinks to how Google labels ads. The growth number stands either way.

**The one join that failed, recorded.**
- This one is table-wide, not the House accounts. The FEC IDs advertisers gave land in `FINANCE__FED_FEC_COMMITTEES_DIM`: 2,898 of 2,934 (99.9% of the dollars).
- Against `POLITICS__FED_FEC_PAC_SUMMARY`, Google spend beats FEC total spending for 31 committees. That's a coverage gap: the summary mostly holds the period ending 2024-12-31 (644 of 861 committees). Trump for President shows $81.8M on Google against $46.4M in its 2 loaded periods. **Dead as a mismatch test.**

**Traps.**
- ELECTIONS is null on every row (known).
- SPEND_USD is 0 on all 5,109 non-US rows. Their money sits only in local-currency columns.
- One committee can have several accounts. Biden for President and Harris for President share FEC ID C00703975 ($154.4M). Roll up by ID, not name.
- IDs like `C20220000` are Georgia state IDs that look like FEC IDs. Several of the 36 FEC IDs that don't land are these.
- In the per-state spend table, 3,647 of 5,064 rows for these accounts are exactly $100. That's a display floor, so a few hundred dollars "out of state" is noise.
- `POLITICS__FED_MEDSL_HOUSE_RETURNS` stops at 2018 (S10).

**Next join.** House quarterly Statements of Disbursements (office by office, vendor and amount). Not in the warehouse. They would show which office paid for each pooled ad.

## 2. CFTC_COT_FINANCIAL: hedge funds and Treasury futures. probed

**What it is.** Weekly positions in financial futures. 34,683 rows, 133 market codes, 709 Tuesdays, 2013-01-08 to 2026-08-04. Futures only.

**Checked.**
- No week missing: no gap over 8 days. The Oct-Nov 2025 shutdown weeks are all there. 310 rows fall on a Monday (holiday weeks).
- Hedge funds' ("leveraged money") net short, summed over six Treasury contracts (2Y, 5Y, 10Y, Ultra 10Y, Bond, Ultra Bond, S05 math in `g67/cot.py`):

| First Tuesday of August | Net short (contracts) | Gross short as % of open interest |
|---|---|---|
| 2019 | 4,574,211 | 39.7% |
| 2020 | 2,677,790 | 36.6% |
| 2021 | 781,490 | 23.4% |
| 2023 | 5,641,891 | 43.0% |
| 2024 | 8,458,939 | 47.1% |
| 2025 | 9,016,250 | 47.0% |
| **2026** | **7,423,067** | **39.6%** |

- Peak net short: 9,508,920 on 2025-09-02. Peak gross short: 11,226,810 (48.4% of open interest) on 2025-12-30.
- March 2020: 3.54M (Feb 25) down to 2.84M (Mar 31), -20%.
- April 2025 tariff shock: no unwind in futures. It went up, 8.11M to 8.60M (Apr 1 to Apr 22).

**Dull explanation.** This is the Treasury basis trade. Regulators and the financial press chart it every week. Also, contracts aren't equal risk: an Ultra Bond carries far more rate risk than a 2-year note.
**Hit means.** A usable chart: the 2025 peak and the 2026 step down.
**Miss means.** No original story here without dealer-level or fund-level data.

**Traps.**
- Same code, 2-3 spellings of the name (a trailing space, and renames like "UST 2Y NOTE"). Group by `CFTC_CONTRACT_MARKET_CODE`, trimmed.
- Markets per week grew from a median of 36 (2013) to 87 (2026), mostly crypto "perp style" contracts since 2024. Any all-market total isn't comparable across years.
- The three "Consolidated" rows (S&P 500, NASDAQ-100, DJIA) are sums of other rows (g32). Adding them double counts.
- The big S&P 500 contract (138741) stops 2021-09-14. Most likely CME's delisting of the big contract, not a load gap. The Consolidated row carries on.
- Numbers are text with spaces, and `.` means blank. Reported plus non-reported long equals open interest on every row except 476, which are off by exactly 1 contract.
- 2010-12 sits in `FINANCE__FED_CFTC_COT_FINANCIAL_HIST` (g32). So a time series on this table alone starts in 2013.

## 3. GOOGLE_POLADS_GEO_SPEND: political ad money by district. probed

**What it is.** 1,289 rows:
- 1,126 US rows: districts, territories, and one "United States" row ($53,605,600)
- 163 rows in 11 other countries
- **No state-level rows.** Every US row is a district.

**Checked.**
- Completeness: US rows sum to $2,496,508,800. The advertiser table's US total is $2,496,534,800. That's $26,000 apart.
- Each district is cut by map year:

| Map | Rows | Spend |
|---|---|---|
| 2012 | 436 | $778,175,300 |
| 2022 | 435 | $1,441,400,400 |
| 2024 | 67 | $165,487,400 |
| 2026 | 181 | $56,148,200 |

- 10 states have 2026-map rows: AL, CA, FL, LA, MO, NC, OH, TN, TX, UT.
- **Peer check inside each state.** District money mostly mirrors statewide ads spread by population. Example: on the 2024 Alabama map, the top 4 districts each hold 15.0-15.6% of the state.
- The standouts against their state's median district:
  - AL-2 (2026 map): 58.7% of Alabama's $218,500, 8.5x the median
  - NY-17 (2024 map): 4.0x
  - MO-1 (2026 map): 3.4x
  - CA-11 (2026 map): 3.2x

**Dull explanation.** Statewide and presidential ads get split across districts. Only a few districts carry real race-specific money, and the table can't separate the two.
**Hit means.** Good for a map, and for a "where the 2026 money is landing" chart.
**Miss means.** Not a lead by itself.

**Traps.**
- ⚠ **SPEND_USD is 0 on all 163 non-US rows.** Their money is only in local currency (INR, BRL, ARS...). Ranking countries by USD puts every non-US country at zero.
- The 8 EU-currency columns (EUR, BGN, CZK, DKK, HUF, PLN, RON, SEK) are 0 on every row. There are no EU country rows at all.
- District names come in two formats. For some districts in the 6 states that gained seats in 2020 (CO, FL, MT, NC, OR, TX) it's the long one: "Texas's 38th Congressional District 2026 redistricting". At-large seats are "XX-AT LARGE". Parse both.
- Don't add map years together as if they were places. Each map row covers the years that map was in use.
