## 1. HEALTH__FED_CMS_LTCH: **live**

**What it is:** 311 long-term care hospitals (LTCHs). One row each. Name, address, ownership, beds.
**The key:** CCN. Digits 3-6 run 2000-2299 for every one: the real LTCH range.
**The join:** CCN to Medicare cost reports (HCRIS). **304 of 311 land (97.7%).** The 7 misses are new.
Second join: CCN to the provider-of-services roster (POS), which keeps closed hospitals with a closure code and date. **311 of 311 land.**

### What was checked
1. Time: every LTCH cost report, FY2011-FY2024. 5,285 reports, 477 CCNs.
2. Deduped: 158 CCN-years had two reports (ownership changes, short periods). Kept the longest.
3. Same-hospital panel: 321 hospitals that filed both FY2015 and FY2023.
4. Concentration: who carries the industry's losses.
5. Peer comparison: Kindred vs other for-profit LTCHs, same year, same state, same occupancy band, same bed band.
6. Second field on the chain: Medicare's enrollment file gives the legal owner of each CCN.

### The shrink (known story, checked)
- LTCHs filing cost reports: **441 in FY2012, 339 in FY2023.**
- POS closure codes: 125 voluntary closures and 9 conversions since 2016. **28 closures in 2024-2025 alone.**
- Same 321 hospitals, FY2015 to FY2023:
  - Medicare-paid days: 2,915,088 to 1,514,443. **-48%.**
  - All other days: 2,564,504 to 3,056,091. **+19%.**
  - Medicare discharges: 107,570 to 59,035. **-45%.**
  - Median Medicare share of days: **64.7% to 39.3%.** 82% of hospitals dropped 10+ points.
- Dull explanation: Medicare's 2016 "site-neutral" pay cut, plus patients moving to Medicare Advantage (those days are not counted as Medicare here). MedPAC has written this up for years. Context, not the lead.

### The lead: Kindred
Chain = the brand in the hospital's name on today's list. 45 say Kindred; 42 are for-profit.

| Fiscal year | Kindred (42) median margin | Other for-profit median | Kindred patient-care total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2012 | +6.4% | +8.1% | +$147.4M |
| 2016 | +7.0% | (Select +6.8%, rest +4.6%) | +$117.6M |
| 2017 | -2.7% | (Select +7.9%, rest +0.1%) | -$19.8M |
| 2021 | +1.7% | (Select +13.0%, rest +10.4%) | -$1.0M |
| 2023 | **-6.7%** | **+2.7%** (171 hospitals) | **-$113.8M** |
| 2024 | **-8.9%** | +2.3% (119 filed so far) | **-$157.6M** |

Margin = patient-care income ÷ net patient revenue, from the cost report.
Kindred tracked Select until FY2016, then split off and never came back.

**Peer tests, FY2023**
- Same state: Kindred's median sits below the other for-profits' in **11 of 13 states** that have both.
  - Florida -11.4% vs +1.4%. Texas -5.8% vs +3.6%. California -4.7% vs -1.5%.
- Same occupancy band: 60-75% full: Kindred **-9.3%** vs **+4.8%**. 75%+ full: -1.6% vs +7.9%. Under 60%: -8.1% vs -0.3%.
- Same size: 41-70 beds: -12.1% vs +2.9%. 71+ beds: -5.2% vs +0.3%. (Kindred's 5 small hospitals, 40 beds or fewer, are +7.3%.)
- Not 3 hospitals: drop the worst 3 and Kindred is still **-$72.6M.**
- Per patient day: Kindred costs **$2,260** vs **$1,864**. Kindred takes in $2,129 vs $1,973. Cost outruns revenue.
- Kindred's Medicare share fell faster: 60.8% (FY2015) to 29.4% (FY2022). Select: 61.5% to 36.7%.

**Second field on the chain:** the 45 Kindred-named CCNs enroll under 27 legal entities.
18 say KINDRED. The rest are old Kindred-family names: THC, KND Development, KND Real Estate, SCCI, Triumph, Transitional Hospitals. Two are JV-style names (Dallas LTACH LLC, Northland LTACH LLC). No non-Kindred-named LTCH enrolls under a Kindred entity.

**Dull explanation, not tested:** cost reports carry costs the parent books to each hospital: home-office charges and rent to related or REIT landlords. KND Real Estate as an enrolled owner hints at that setup. The cost report here has no rent or home-office line, so this pull can't split it out. If rent and overhead make the loss, that is still a story, just a different one.
Also: press reports say Kindred's LTCHs were spun into ScionHealth in 2021. That is not checked here.

**What a hit means:** the brand, not the policy, explains the gap. Every LTCH faced the same Medicare cut.
**What a miss would have meant:** if Kindred matched its state peers, the loss would just be the industry shrink.

### Traps found (ledger, not story)
- **The industry-wide loss is state hospitals.** LTCHs lost $884.7M on patient care in FY2023. The top 5 losers carry 48% of it. They are state-run chronic hospitals holding LTCH numbers: Eleanor Slater RI -$135.1M, Lemuel Shattuck MA -$95.7M, Tewksbury MA -$87.3M. The 11 government LTCHs on the list: -$445.4M. For-profits in total: +$41.0M.
- HCRIS bed count typo: The Rehab Hospital at Raritan Bay (312018), FY2015, **6,092,016 beds** (bed-days available 27,740). It inflates the FY2015 bed total to 6.1M.
- HCRIS FY2011 (166 LTCHs) and FY2024 (242) are part-years of filings. Don't read them as a drop.
- HCRIS `HOSPITAL_NAME` is the legal name. Select's hospitals read "SSH - COLUMBUS INC.", so a name search finds 14 Select LTCHs when there are 84. Use the brand name on the LTCH list.
- `CMS_REGION` is a region code (1-10), not a metric. The battery's "outliers of CMS_REGION" means nothing.
- `ADDRESS_LINE_2` is "-" on all 311 rows.

## 2. HEALTH__FED_CMS_MARKETPLACE_PLAN_ATTRIBUTES_PUF: **probed**

**What it is:** every plan design on HealthCare.gov for 2026. 30 states, 161 insurer brands.
**No premiums** in this file, and no enrollment. Money questions need the rate file, which isn't loaded.

### What was checked
1. Legal cap: the 2026 out-of-pocket maximum is $10,600 per person, $21,200 per family.
2. Deductibles by metal level, on the standard on-exchange variant only (one row per plan).
3. Peer comparison: each state's median, each insurer against its state.

### What came back
- **Zero plans over the cap.** 1,654 rows sit exactly on $10,600.
- Median in-network deductible, one person:

| Metal | Plans | Median deductible | Median out-of-pocket max | Deductible = max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bronze | 144 | $10,600 | $10,600 | **62%** |
| Expanded Bronze | 1,156 | $7,500 | $10,000 | 12% |
| Silver | 1,453 | $6,000 | $8,900 | 3% |
| Gold | 1,172 | $2,000 | $8,200 | 1% |
| Platinum | 44 | $0 | $5,200 | 0% |

- "Deductible = max" means the plan pays nothing but preventive care until the member has paid $10,600.
- State silver medians sit in a tight band: $5,000 to $6,250. North Dakota is the low one, $3,525. No outlier state.
- Bronze at the full $10,600 deductible: Tennessee 27% of 63 plans; BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee 40% of its 40.
- The plan's own "having a baby" example, median patient cost: Silver $7,750, Bronze $10,560, Gold $4,370.

**Dull explanation:** every number here is the metal-level design rule working as written. Nothing breaks the law; no state stands apart.

### Traps found
- **A row is a plan variant, not a plan.** 22,059 rows, 5,144 plan IDs. A silver plan appears up to 7 times: standard on and off exchange, 73/87/94% cost-sharing versions, and two American Indian versions (3,969 rows each). Count `STANDARDCOMPONENTID`, or filter `CSRVARIATIONTYPE` to one standard variant.
- Dollar columns are text: "$10,600 " with a trailing space, "$21200 per group", "Not Applicable".
- `CHILDONLYPLANID` empty on all rows (known).
- 3 rows carry a 2025 start date in a 2026 file. 4,963 rows were imported 2/3/2026: a mid-year refresh.

## 3. HEALTH__XC_OWID_LIFE_EXPECTANCY: **probed**

**What it is:** life expectancy by place and year, 1543-2023. 265 entities. No duplicates.

### What was checked
Time + peers: the US against 23 rich countries (Western Europe, Canada, Japan, Korea, Australia, NZ, Israel), 1950-2023.

| Year | US | Peer median | Gap | US rank of 24 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1950 | 68.1 | 66.8 | +1.3 | 12 |
| 1980 | 73.7 | 74.1 | -0.3 | 13 |
| 2000 | 76.8 | 78.7 | -1.9 | 22 |
| 2010 | 78.7 | 81.0 | -2.4 | **24** |
| 2019 | 78.9 | 82.4 | -3.5 | 24 |
| 2021 | 76.4 | 82.3 | **-5.9** | 24 |
| 2023 | 79.3 | 82.4 | -3.1 | 24 |

- 2023: the US ranks 63rd of 236 countries and territories, between Anguilla and the Falkland Islands.
- **Dull explanation:** this is the well-worn "Americans die younger" story. Good context line, not a lead.

### Traps found
- **The battery's top values are wrong.** It said LIFE_EXPECTANCY 62.7748 shows up 159 times. It shows up once (Zimbabwe 2023). It said code SWE has 347 rows; it has 273. The battery used `APPROX_TOP_K`, an estimate, on a column with 20,442 distinct values. Any battery "top value" on a high-cardinality column needs an exact recount.
- 1,348 rows have a blank code: regions and income groups, plus England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland sitting beside United Kingdom. Summing "countries" without a code filter double counts the UK.
- `OWID_` codes are aggregates (World, continents, income groups), except OWID_KOS = Kosovo and OWID_USS = USSR.

## 4. HEALTH__FED_CDC_ANXIETY_DEPRESSION: **probed**

**What it is:** Household Pulse Survey estimates, Apr 2020 to Sep 2024. 3 measures × state or group × 72 survey waves. No duplicates.

### What was checked
1. Time: national series, wave by wave, on real start dates.
2. Peers: every state ranked inside each wave; count significant gaps using the state's own interval.
3. Groups: age, gender identity, orientation, disability over time.

### What came back
- **A break, not a recovery.** Anxiety or depression symptoms, adults nationally:
  - Oct 2023: **34.2%**. Jan 2024: **20.7%**. One wave.
  - All three measures fall ~40% at the same wave: depression 22.8 to 13.4, anxiety 29.5 to 17.0.
  - The drop lands on the first day of Phase 4 (Jan 9, 2024), when the survey's design and window changed. The insurance questions show no step at the same wave (9.5% to 9.6%). Treat 2024 as a separate series. Confirm against CDC's technical notes before quoting.
- Peak: 42.6% (Nov 2020). Pre-break yearly averages: 37.8% (2020), 33.4% (2021), 33.1% (2022), 32.4% (2023).
- **States, persistent:** Louisiana ranked top 5 in 42 of 72 waves. Its interval sat fully above the national rate in 34 waves, never below. Mississippi 34 top-5 waves, Oklahoma 29, Nevada 31.
  - Minnesota: fully below national in 51 of 72.
  - State intervals are ~10 points wide. One wave proves nothing; the 72-wave count is the finding.
- Age: 18-29 year olds 50.4% vs 60-69 27.3% (2020-21 average).
- **Dull explanation:** screener symptoms (a 4-question check), not diagnoses. The high states are the poor Southern states. Worth a sidebar, not a story.

### Traps found
- **TIME_PERIOD "1" is reused** for 9 no-survey gaps between phases (PHASE -1, blank values, 702 rows). Filter `TIME_PERIOD = '1'` and you get Apr 2020 plus nine empty windows.
- **PHASE "3.1" is two phases:** Apr-Jul 2021 and Aug-Oct 2023 (Phase 3.10 lost its zero).
- TIME_PERIOD is text; it sorts 1, 10, 11... Use the start date.

## 5. HEALTH__FED_CDC_HEALTH_INSURANCE: **probed**

**What it is:** same survey, same waves. Uninsured, public and private coverage, adults 18-64.

### What was checked
The triage question: did state uninsured rates jump after Medicaid unwinding (started April 2023)?
- Before: Jan-Mar 2023 (3 waves). After: Aug-Oct 2023 (3 waves).
- Placebo: the same months in 2022, before any unwinding.
- Stopped at Oct 2023 so the Phase 4 redesign can't leak in.

### What came back
- **National:** uninsured 8.8% to 9.5% (+0.7). Same months of 2022: 10.0% to 9.7% (-0.3). Net: about **+1.0 point**.
- Public coverage: flat in 2023 (24.1 to 24.2); it rose in the 2022 placebo (23.6 to 24.9).
- **States: too noisy to name.**
  - In the 2022 placebo, 3-wave averages swung from -4.7 (New Mexico) to +3.7 (Colorado, Delaware).
  - 10 states rose 2+ points in 2023; 6 did in the placebo year.
  - Texas, the biggest unwinding state by headcount, shows no rise: 16.8% to 16.3%.
  - Montana (+4.9) looks like a jump, but its "before" wave (6.4%) is its own low; 2022 ran 8-10%.
- **Dull explanation:** state samples are small. A real 1-point national shift can't be pinned state by state from this survey.

### Traps found
- **Suppression is not random.** The 299 flagged rows hide the lowest-uninsured places: DC 42 waves, Rhode Island 38, Hawaii 36, Vermont 33, Delaware 25. Average across states and you drop the best-covered ones.
- `QUARTILE_NUMBER` -1 = suppressed (276 rows). `QUARTILE_RANGE` says "Estimate not reliable" on 277.
- Same wave traps as the anxiety table: TIME_PERIOD "1" reused for gap windows; PHASE "3.1" twice.
- Phase labels differ between the two Pulse tables: "3 (Jan 6 - Mar 29)" here, "3.0 (Jan 6 - Mar 29)" there. Join on start date, not phase.
