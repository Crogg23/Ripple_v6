## 1. CA_LOBBY_COVER: **live**

Cover pages of California lobbying reports, 568,988 rows. One row per version of a filing.
Forms: F635 employer quarterly (349K rows), F615 lobbyist (170K), F625 lobbying firm (45K), F645 $5,000+ spenders (4K).
0 duplicate filing+version rows. One source URL.

**The lead: AI arrives in Sacramento lobbying**

`LOBBYING_ACTIVITY` is the employer's own note on what it lobbied. I used the latest version of each F635 filing and counted distinct employers per year.

| Period year | Employers with real activity text | Any AI match | Spelled out "artificial intelligence" |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 | 2,584 | 3 | 3 |
| 2020 | 2,753 | 4 | 1 |
| 2021 | 2,850 | 2 | 1 |
| 2022 | 3,053 | 5 | 1 |
| 2023 | 3,092 | 26 | 20 |
| 2024 | 3,134 | **94** | 33 |
| 2025 | 3,210 | 59 | 29 |
| 2026, about 2 quarters | 2,964 | 51 | 18 |

- "Any AI match" = the phrase, "SB 1047", "automated decision", or "AI" as a standalone word.
- "Real text" = over 30 characters and not "see attachment".
- 2024 share: 94 of 3,134 employers writing real text = **3.0%**. In 2022 it was 5 of 3,053 = 0.16%.

**Checked**
- **Time:** same measure every year, 2000-2026. Zero to five employers a year until 2023.
- **Method check against known waves.** The same text search picks up events we already know about:
  - Cannabis (Prop 64 passed Nov 2016): 11 employers in 2015, 83 in 2016, 101 in 2018.
  - Privacy (the CCPA, 2018): 14 in 2017, 42 in 2018, 78 in 2019.
  - Wildfire: 0 in 2016, 37 in 2018, 71 in 2019, 88 in 2025 (the year of the LA fires).
  - AI's 94 in 2024 is close to the cannabis peak (101 in 2018).
- **Eyeball:** I read all 112 rows that matched only on standalone "AI", in the local raw file. Nearly all are AI policy ("AI regulation", "AI safety", "privacy and AI policies").
  - 2 employers are unclear: Valor Management (pitching CalPERS on its "Valor Atreides AI" fund) and Curriculum Associates ("EL PROGRAMS AI DATA VERIFICATION").
- **Split of the 94 in 2024:** 84 matched on the phrase, SB 1047 or "automated decision". 10 matched on the bare word "AI" only.
- 2024 names include OpenAI OpCo, Meta Platforms, Microsoft, AH Capital Management (a16z), Center for AI Safety Action Fund, SAG-AFTRA, TikTok and Salesforce.

**The dull explanation, tested: it's all one bill**
- SB 1047 (the 2024 AI safety bill, vetoed) accounts for 54 of the 94 employers in 2024.
- But in 2025 only 1 employer named SB 1047, and **58-59 still named AI**. The wave outlived the bill.
- Second dull explanation: employers just started writing more. Tested: employers writing real text grew 2.7% from 2022 to 2024. AI mentions grew 19x.

**Hit means:** a new lobbying front opened in 2023-24 and stayed open. It can be charted next to cannabis, privacy and wildfire as one story: "what Sacramento's lobbyists wrote down, 2000-2026."
**Miss would mean:** the counts come from shorthand or noise. The eyeball says no.
**Limits:**
- ⚠ The text field is **cut at 400 characters** (the max length is exactly 400 in every year). Long bill lists get truncated, so every count is a floor.
- Bill numbers restart each session. "SB 1047" in 2020 (2 employers) and 2022 (4) is a different bill, so the pre-2023 baseline is slightly inflated.
- AI lobbying in Sacramento got press in 2024. The new part is the 26-year yardstick.

**Next:** price it. CAL-ACCESS payment tables (LPAY/LEXP) aren't in the warehouse, so dollars per AI employer can't be summed yet.

**Second angle, probed: who amends most**
- Firms (F625) with 20+ quarterly reports, 2015-2025: 379 firms, median **15%** of reports amended.
  - Capitol Advisors Group: 37 of 44 (84%). Capitol Strategies Group: 36/44 (82%). California Strategies & Advocacy: 35/44 (80%).
- Employers (F635), 3,271 of them: median **6.8%**.
  - Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association: 34/44 (77%). CalChamber: 32/48 (67%). Chevron U.S.A.: 30/44 (68%).
- Why not a lead: without the payment tables you can't tell whether an amendment changed dollars or fixed a typo.
- An amendment wave was filed in 2021-22: F635 amendments hit 2,383 (2021) and 2,142 (2022), against about 1,300-1,700 in 2013-2020 and 2023.
  - It's spread thin. In 2021 the top employer carries under 1% of F635 amendments, and the median filer filed 2.
  - Cause unknown, maybe a Secretary of State cleanup. Not tested.

**Traps**
- ⚠ `LOBBYING_ACTIVITY` is empty on **every** F615 and F625 row (215,600 rows). Only F635 and F645 carry it, and it's capped at 400 characters.
- The glance's "top firm, 35% of rows" is a **blank** `FIRM_NAME`. It's blank on 173K of 344K employer (F635) rows, so it's the form, not missing data.
- 169 `RPT_DATE`s fall outside 1999-2026 (years 0004 and 6465). 181 `FROM_DATE`s do too. Some `FROM_DATE`s are 2026-10-01, a quarter that hasn't started.
- F615 lobbyist filings jump from 4,194 (2010) to 6,089 (2011): lobbyists filing go 1,105 to 1,748 in one year. Employers rose only 7% that year. Probably a rule change, not a rush of new lobbyists. Not tested.

## 2. FED_FEC_PAC_SUMMARY: **live**, but check the filing first

One row per committee per cycle, 45,709 rows. Dated rows run from 2017-01 to 2025-01.
Cycles 2018-2024: 8,524 / 9,462 / 9,692 / 10,128 rows.

**Checked: does the cash math add up?**
Opening cash + receipts − spending should equal closing cash.
- For committees that actually hold cash, it balances to the penny on **88-97%** of PAC rows (types N, O, Q, V, W) and 77-88% of party rows (type Y), every cycle.
- Rows off by $100K or more in 2024: N 1, O 4, Q 0, V 1, W 0, **Y (party) 1**.
- **Time check (cycle to cycle):** one cycle's closing cash should equal the next cycle's opening cash.
  - 2018→2020: 6,313 of 6,509 match (97.0%). 2020→2022: 97.0%. 2022→2024: 97.9%.
  - Only 2-4 breaks of $100K+ per pair. The file chains cleanly.

**The lead: the NRCC's 2024 line doesn't balance**

| NRCC (C00075820) | Open | Receipts | Spending | Close | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | $11.44M | $205.78M | $200.65M | $16.57M | $0.00M |
| 2020 | $16.57M | $280.91M | $284.92M | $12.56M | $0.00M |
| 2022 | $12.56M | $289.31M | $285.47M | $16.41M | $0.00M |
| **2024** | $16.41M | $236.31M | $219.75M | **$11.08M** | **$21.88M** |

- The math says $32.97M should be left at the close. The line says $11.08M.
- **Peers:** DCCC, NRSC, DSCC, RNC and DNC. That's 20 committee-cycles, 2018-2024. Every one balances within $5,000.
- Its own past: the NRCC balanced in 2018, 2020 and 2022.
- The chain is fine. The 2024 opening ($16.41M) equals the 2022 closing.
- The gap is positive, so either spending is understated by $21.9M, or receipts or the closing cash are off.

**The dull explanation, NOT tested (no budget left):** the FEC's bulk summary built this line wrong, for example by mixing an amended report with an original. Then it's a trap in the FEC file, not NRCC books.
**Hit means:** the NRCC's own 2023-24 reports don't reconcile, and that's a reporting story. Or the FEC's bulk file is wrong for one of the six biggest party committees, and that's a warning for everyone who uses it.
**Miss means:** the NRCC's 2023-24 Form 3X summary pages add up (spending about $241.6M, or closing cash about $33M). Then it's only a bulk-file trap.
**Next:** FEC API `/committee/C00075820/totals/?cycle=2024` and the NRCC's 2024 Year-End Form 3X summary page. That's one lookup.

**Traps**
- ⚠ **7,897 rows (17.3%) have no date and no money.** Every money column is null. They're registered committees with no summary. That's the whole "83% filled."
  - 1,787 of them share an ID with a dated row from another cycle.
  - Names include "10 STACKS TO THE FIRST MFER WHO HITS DONALD TRUMP WITH A MILKSHAKE".
- ⚠ **One junk row adds $268.7M.** "THE COMMITTEE OF 99 FOR GAMBERT, JASON" (C90022021, type I, through 2023-03-31) claims $268.71M in receipts. That's 92% of all type-I receipts in the 2024 cycle ($291.8M).
- Types C and I (communication costs, outside-spending filers) carry zero cash columns, so the cash math fails for them by design: 1-12% balance.
  - 23 of the file's 25 biggest "gaps" are these types or inaugural committees. The other two are the NRCC and Americans for Progressive Action USA (type O, $4.81M in, $0 out, $0 closing cash, ends 2020-07-31).
  - Inaugural committees report receipts but no spending: $106.8M for the 58th Presidential Inaugural Committee, $123.7M for PIC 2021.
- ⚠ **Conduits double count.** ActBlue plus WinRed are 33.2% of all summed receipts in 2020, 26.5% in 2022 and 23.7% in 2024. ActBlue alone is 17.6% in 2018. The same dollars get counted again at the campaigns. Joint fundraising committees (like Harris Victory Fund, $1.31B) do the same thing.
- There's no cycle column. Deriving the cycle from `COVERAGE_END_DATE` puts 25 committees twice in one cycle, with ends like 2019-01-31 or 2021-01-31. The 6 "2026" rows end 2025-01-31, and all of them are $0.
- The chunk calls the key `FEC_CMTE_ID`. The real column is `CMTE_ID`.

## 3. FED_MEDSL_SENATE_RETURNS: probed

MIT Election Lab Senate results: 3,945 rows, 1976-2024 (even years), plus the Jan 2021 Georgia runoffs as "2021".
33-36 races a year. `VOTE_SHARE` equals candidate votes over total votes exactly on every row.

**Checked**
- **Flags:** `IS_SPECIAL_ELECTION` and `IS_WRITEIN` are **False on all 3,945 rows**.
  - Specials exist in the data, though, and 50 rows are named write-in or scattering.
- **Two races, one key:** 18 state-years hold a regular race plus a special under the same year+state+stage. Only `TOTAL_VOTES` tells them apart.
  - Examples: 2020 GA (Perdue's race and the 20-candidate special), 2018 MN and MS, 2024 CA and NE.
- **Peer and time: Senate vote vs presidential vote, same state, same year** (roll-off).
  - The median state casts 97-99% as many Senate votes as presidential votes, every year 1976-2016.
  - Every low outlier has a known cause:
    - An unopposed incumbent: MS and WV 1976, 72-75%.
    - Two Democrats and no Republican: CA 2016, 86%.
    - No Democrat: ID 2004, 84%.
    - The data quirks below.
  - Nothing story-grade.

**Traps**
- ⚠ **Georgia runoffs are coded three ways.** In 1992 and 2008 the `gen` rows hold the **runoff** (Coverdell 50.65%, Chambliss 57.44%), and the November general sits under stage `pre`. Jan 2021 is year 2021, stage `runoff`. 2022 is `GEN RUNOFF`.
- ⚠ **Louisiana 1984 and 1992:** the unopposed winners carry `CANDIDATE_VOTES` = 1 and `TOTAL_VOTES` = 1. LA 1980 holds the open-primary numbers (841,013 votes).
- The president table (`FED_MEDSL_PRESIDENT_RETURNS`) **stops at 2016**. Every 2020 and 2024 join misses.
- In the president table, NE 2000 has `TOTAL_VOTES` 967,019. The Senate race there totals 692,350. It looks like transposed digits (697,019 would fit), but that needs an outside check.
- `STATE_FIPS` drops leading zeros (min length 1).

## 4. CA_LOBBY_COVER2: probed

The people listed on California lobbying cover pages, 206,493 rows. One name per line per filing version.
Codes: EMP (lobbyist) 148.8K, OWN 24.7K, PTN (partner) 23.6K, OFF (officer) 9.5K. Only on F625 firm and F635 employer reports.

**Checked**
- **Join to CA_LOBBY_COVER on FILING_ID + AMEND_ID:** 206,387 of 206,493 land (**99.95%**). 106 rows on 49 filings don't.
  - Every year 1999-2026 is covered, and it tracks the cover table's growth: 2,835 rows (2000) up to 11,243 (2024).
- 0 duplicate lines. One load run.
- **People listed under many filers (original versions):**
  - "STUART, SUSAN L." appears under 25 different filers (2005-2018), and under **15 in one quarter**, always as owner or partner.
  - "ROSS, MICHAEL": 15 filers, 13 in one quarter (2005-2012).
  - The next name has 11 filers, and no one else tops 9 in one quarter.
  - Maybe placement agents, who have to register as lobbyists and get listed on each investment firm's report. Not tested.

**Traps**
- ⚠ `ENTITY_ID` is blank or 0 on **75,433 of 105,924** in-house lobbyist rows on employer reports (71%). It's 80% on F635 officers.
  - On firm reports it's only 7% blank.
  - Following an in-house lobbyist across years means matching on names.

**Next:** follow the two multi-filer names to the employers on those reports. Placement-agent disclosure on pension deals is a known beat.

## 5. TX_LOBBY_SUBJECT_MATTER: probed (don't use it for trends)

The subjects Texas lobbyists ticked on their reports: 209,957 lines, 84 subject codes.

**Checked**
- **Coverage (join on REPORT_ID to TX_LOBBY_COVER):** subject lines sit on a sliver of reports.
  - 2021: 400 of 11,257 reports (3.6%). 222 of 1,784 lobbyists (12.4%).
  - 2007: 11.4% of reports, 22.2% of lobbyists.
  - Before 2005: almost none. 1991-1998: zero.
- ⚠ **Box-tickers.** Since 2018, 25-38 reports a year tick 80+ of the 84 subjects.
  - Since 2018, reports with 40+ subjects hold **41-63%** of each year's lines (2020: 2,796 of 4,441).
  - The median report lists 3-9.
- **The triage question: did the power grid draw a surge after the Feb 2021 freeze?** I removed the box-tickers and counted lobbyists listing Utilities or Energy:

| Year | Lobbyists (no box-tickers) | Utilities or Energy | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 389 | 98 | 25.2% |
| 2013 | 390 | 100 | 25.6% |
| 2015 | 390 | 107 | 27.4% |
| 2019 | 224 | 47 | 21.0% |
| **2021** | 198 | 50 | **25.3%** |
| 2023 | 230 | 47 | 20.4% |
| 2025 | 233 | 50 | 21.5% |

- **Miss:** 2021 just returns to the 2011-2015 level. No grid surge shows up.
- The one real shift is **Law Enforcement**: 6 of 224 lobbyists (2.7%) in 2019, then 24 of 198 (12.1%) in 2021 and 33 of 233 (14.2%) in 2025. In 2011-2017 it was 2-6%.
  - The counts are small, and it's a 12% self-selected slice of Texas lobbyists. It's a pointer, not a lead.
- Disaster Preparedness: 4.0% (2019) to 11.6% (2021), 12.0% (2025).

**Why the miss doesn't clear the grid:** it only speaks for the ~12% of lobbyists who file subject lines at all. The table can't show a surge among the other 88%.

**Traps**
- `SUBJECTMATTERDESCR` (typed text) is filled on nearly 100% of lines 2005-2010, 25% in 2011, then about 1-2% from 2012 on. The form changed.
- Codes 98 "All" and 99 "All from Cover" appear only in 2015-2016 (45 lines).
- 2011 includes rows with period starts up to 2016-01-01.
- Since 2016, 5-53 lines a year repeat the same subject on the same report.
