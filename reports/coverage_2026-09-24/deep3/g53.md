## 1. FBI NICS: Vermont's private-sale check law barely shows in the FBI count. **live**

**What the table is**
- 16,445 rows = 55 states and territories × 299 months, 1998-11 to **2023-09**.
- 0 duplicate state-months. TOTALS equals the sum of the other columns on every row.
- Source: a Data Liberation Project mirror, per the mart model. ⚠ **It stops at 2023-09.**
- The glance's "165 rows per month" does not reproduce. Every month has 55.

**The mechanics**
- A "private sale" check is a licensed dealer running the FBI check for two private people.
- Vermont's Act 94 (13 V.S.A. § 4019) requires that on private transfers. Family and a few emergency cases are exempt.
- It took effect on passage, **April 11, 2018**. Checked on the Vermont legislature site.
- Vermont's checks go straight to the FBI. As far as I know, there's no state office in between. (From memory, check.)

**Checked**
- **Time, same 12-month windows:**

| | Apr 2017 – Mar 2018 | May 2018 – Apr 2019 |
|---|---|---|
| Vermont private-sale checks per month | 4.5 | 8.4 |
| Vermont, per 100 dealer checks | 0.14 | 0.27 |
| Maine (no such law), per 100 dealer checks | 0.30 | 0.26 |

- The Vermont count stayed flat after that: 77 (2019), 71 (2020), 127 (2021), 77 (2022), 75 (Jan–Sep 2023).
- **Peers, 2022, private-sale checks per 100 dealer checks:**

| State | Law | Per 100 dealer checks | Per 100K residents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nevada | since 2020 | 5.56 | 196 |
| Washington | since 2014 | 2.28 | 142 |
| New York | since 2013 | 2.23 | 39 |
| Delaware | since 2013 | 2.20 | 111 |
| New Mexico | since 2019 | 0.99 | 71 |
| **Vermont** | **since 2018** | **0.17** | **12** |
| 29 states with no such law, median | none | 0.10 (middle half 0.07–0.15) | 7.6 |

- 4 states with **no** private-sale law log more private checks per dealer check than Vermont: Missouri 0.22, Oklahoma 0.30, South Dakota 0.57, Florida 0.63.
- **Other new laws, first 12 months:** New Mexico jumped from 6 a month (early 2019) to 72, and it's about 130 a month by 2021. Nevada went from 12 a month to 368. Vermont went from 4.5 to 8.4.
- Denominator: 2020 population from `CORE.DIM_COUNTY` (S07). "Dealer checks" = handgun + long gun + other + multiple.

**Hit means:** the law exists on paper, but Vermont's private sellers mostly don't send buyers to a dealer for a check. Or the state has no way to see whether they do. Either way it's a story.

**Miss means:** Vermont dealers log private transfers as ordinary sales. Then the FBI's private-sale category undercounts, and compliance could be fine.

**Boring:**
- **Dealers coding private sales as regular sales.**
  - **Tested:** if they did, Vermont's dealer checks would rise against its neighbors.
  - Vermont / (New Hampshire + Maine) moved -1.5% over the 12-month windows and +4.0% over the 22-month windows. No jump.
  - ⚠ The same Act raised the buying age to 21 and banned large magazines. That could hide a few points of re-coding. Not ruled out.
- **Small private market.** Vermont is rural with high gun ownership. Nothing here says its private market is smaller than New Mexico's. Not tested.
- **Exempt family transfers.** Every one of these laws exempts them, so it doesn't explain the gap with peers.
- **Small counts.** It's about 80 checks a year. But every full year from 2020 to 2023, Vermont (0.12–0.25 per 100) sits below all five peers in the table.
- **Already reported?** Not checked. Search VTDigger before pitching.

**Next:** Vermont State Police or dealer logbooks for private-transfer counts, and any enforcement cases under § 4019.

**Traps in this table (for the ledger, not stories):**
- ⚠ **TOTALS is not gun sales.** In 2022, 46.8% of all U.S. checks were permit checks or permit rechecks.
  - Illinois and Kentucky together are 27.5% of the national total.
  - Illinois ranks #1 in total checks but #14 in dealer checks. Kentucky ranks #2 but #23.
- ⚠ **Kentucky moved its monthly recheck of every carry-permit holder from PERMIT to PERMIT_RECHECK in June 2019** (PERMIT went from 350K to 1.6K a month). Illinois moved the other way in mid-2021 (PERMIT_RECHECK from 832K a month to 0).
- ⚠ **Dropping a permit-to-buy law makes HANDGUN jump overnight.** Checks move from the sheriff's permit to the dealer counter.
  - Michigan, Dec 2012: from 1.2K to 21.5K a month.
  - Iowa, Jul 2021: from ~250 to 4.1K a month.
  - North Carolina, Mar 2023: from ~1.7K to 46K in April.
  - That's not a buying spree.
- ⚠ **A zero in the private-sale column is not "no checks."** Colorado, Illinois, Tennessee, Utah and Virginia show 0 private-sale checks from 2013 to 2023, and Pennsylvania shows 1. Some of them have private-sale laws.
  - Oregon reads 0 until 2023-04. Connecticut reads 0 until 2021-07. California reads 0 until 2020-12.
  - Those are reporting starts, not law changes. (These are the states that run their own checks, from memory.)
- Columns start late: private sale 2013-08, permit recheck 2016-02, returned-gun checks 2006-05. The blanks before those dates mean "not collected yet," not zero.
- Unexplained: Rhode Island's returned-gun checks were 1,160 in 2019, 6,709 in 2020, 2,770 in 2021, then 405 in 2022. They fell off a cliff between April and July 2021. Not chased.

parked: Nevada logged 0 private-sale checks in 28 of the 36 months from 2017 to 2019, while its 2016 ballot law was on the books. That's the well-reported "unenforceable" fight. The data confirms it but adds nothing new.
