## TIER 1 — the five headliners

### 1 · The lie ledger — same company, two agencies, two answers
**Verb 5 · same entity, two agencies. Verb 17 · self-contradiction.**

The move: one company reports an employee count to OSHA, a revenue
figure to SEC, a payroll to DOL, a facility list to EPA. Line them up.

```
CHAIN     OSHA ITA establishment  ->  EIN / name  ->  SEC filer -> CIK
          DOL OLMS payroll        ->  EIN
          EPA FRS facility list   ->  FRS_ID -> corp crosswalk -> LEI
KEYS      EIN 86-100% steel. CIK~EIN 2-16%, one hop, sample only.
```

- Hit: a leaderboard of firms whose own numbers disagree by 2x or more
- Miss: definitions differ by statute and the gap is legal, not deceptive
- Risk: the CIK~EIN hop is a sample forever. Report counts, never rates.
- Trap: OSHA EIN quality is poor, min length 1, 43,260 blank. Key on
  ESTABLISHMENT_ID and treat name-at-ZIP as corroborated tier.

Why it lands: nobody audits across agencies because nobody holds all
four files. Ripple does. This is the warehouse's best argument for
its own existence.

### 2 · Never once looked at — the absence map
**Posture: absence hunting. Verb 16 · where the government even looks.**

The move: every facility holding a live permit with zero inspections
on record, ever. Count them, map them, name the biggest.

```
SOURCES   EPA ECHO 93,808 facilities, 44% null last-inspection date
          MSHA 31,277 mines, violations 1994-2026
          CMS nursing homes 14,700, deficiency window 2023-2026
          OSHA ITA ~1.18M establishment-years
```

- Hit: a national number for licensed-and-never-visited, by industry
- Miss: null inspection date means not-loaded, not never-inspected
- Risk: that null is the whole finding. Prove it means absence first.
  Check a known-inspected facility reads non-null before believing it.
- Trap: CMS deficiency coverage is really 2023-2026. The 2017-2022 rows
  are a straggler tail, 273 rows in 2017 against 121,925 in 2024.

Why it lands: "44% of federal pollution facilities carry no inspection
date" is a headline before you do anything else with it.

### 3 · The address that is four hundred things
**Verb 8 · address collision. Posture: cross-domain collision.**

The move: one street address matched across kinds of entity that have
no business sharing a door — donor, Medicare supplier, federal
contractor, UK shell parent, registered agent.

```
SOURCES   FEC individual contributions 84.2M, donor ZIP 99.94% usable
          UK Companies House PSC ~7M rows, a floor, load stopped early
          Medicare DMEPOS suppliers 381,228 rows
          USASpending recipients, UEI/DUNS, one hop then stop
KEY       NAME@ZIP 8% match. Multi-word names only. Sample, not a rate.
```

- Hit: a single door that is a doctor, a donor, a contractor, a shell
- Miss: the top hits are all mail-drop services and registered agents
- Risk: single-word name matches clear at 8%, multi-word cleared 92%.
  Enforce multi-word or the whole thing is noise.
- Trap: NAME@ZIP is corroborated tier, never steel. It names candidates
  for a human to check. It does not produce a rate.

Why it lands: this is the shape every financial-crime story has, run
across five unrelated registries at once.

### 4 · Rename, and the record resets
**Verb 7 · rebirth, same building, new name.**

The move: a facility closes with a bad record, reopens at the same
address under a new name or owner, and starts clean.

```
SOURCES   MSHA, 9,500 mines changed hands, CONTROLLER_ID stamped on
          every violation across 26 years. This is the strong leg.
          CMS nursing homes, CHAIN_NAME blank on 4,221 of 14,700
          EPA FRS, FRS_ID persists across owner change
```

- Hit: enforcement history that does not follow the building
- Miss: regulators carry the record over and the rename buys nothing
- Risk: the nursing-home leg is dead. PROVIDER_CHANGED_OWNERSHIP reads
  'N' on all 14,700 rows, a load defect, and no ownership history table
  exists. Build this on MSHA, where the key is real.
- Trap: never report a CMS ownership-change rate. The column is a
  constant, already logged in traps.

Why it lands: MSHA gives 26 years and a hard controller key. It is the
one place in the warehouse where the ownership-change study is possible.

### 5 · Redlined in 1935, the branch left in 2020
**Posture: follow one entity, over ninety years. Verb 15 · staleness.**

The move: HOLC redlining polygons from the 1930s, laid under bank
branch closures, HMDA denials, and today's county health outcomes.

```
CHAIN     HOLC polygon -> ZCTA -> XWALK_ZCTA_COUNTY -> FIPS
          FDIC branches 76k, deposits and coordinates, latest survey
          HMDA 19.1M applications 2007-2017, race/income/outcome per row
          HMDA historic reload 2026-09-05, 45M rows, all-records
KEY       FIPS 73-100%. Terminal only, aggregate there and stop.
```

- Hit: a ninety-year line from a 1935 map to a 2020 branch closing
- Miss: the pattern is population density wearing a costume
- Risk: FIPS is a destination, never a doorway. One map, one arc.
- Trap: HOUSING__FED_MAPPING_INEQUALITY mart is 1,155 rows against
  10,154 in landing, an 89% loss. Use landing, not the mart.
- Trap: HMDA first-lien historic is originations only. Group by
  ACTION_TAKEN before counting anything.

Why it lands: the visual is the argument. Four layers, one map, ninety
years, and every layer is already landed.

## TIER 2 — the other ten

### 6 · The round-number sweep
Run the bunching detector across every dollar column in all 676 marts.
One leaderboard: which federal datasets hold numbers that were
negotiated, not measured.
Risk: needs a per-column sample floor or small tables dominate.

### 7 · The regulator is the variable, across five agencies
E2 found a 9.5x severity spread across state nursing-home inspectors.
Run the same test on OSHA, MSHA, EPA ECHO, and HMDA examiners.
Hit: one index showing who inspects you beats what you did.
Risk: each agency needs its own severity scale. No shared metric.

### 8 · The twin study
Match two facilities identical on ~50 features. One gets fined, the
other does not. Watch what happens next. Nursing homes carry the
features already, 14,700 rows, zero null lat/lon.
Risk: a 3-year penalty window means short pre and post arms.

### 9 · The complaint that predicted the recall
3.83M CFPB narratives and NHTSA complaints against later enforcement.
Hit: consumers described the specific harm months before the action.
Risk: 27,510 copies of one templated text contaminate any text model.
NHTSA is headerless, only C3/C4/C5/C6/C8/C12 are usable.

### 10 · The government's heartbeat
403 sources on one shared time axis, already built. Chart every
agency's filing cadence. Who files on the deadline eve, who never
files, whose rhythm broke and when.
Risk: SNAPSHOT_DATE reflects when read, not when the event happened.

### 11 · Transparency theater, counted
Turn the trap log outward. ~175 dated column-level traps: fields that
exist and are structurally empty. NPPES EIN reads 100% non-null
because the value is an empty string.
Hit: a ranked list of agencies publishing columns holding nothing.
Risk: some are our load bugs, not theirs. Each needs a source check.

### 12 · Who moved first
Verb 2 · precedence. For every paired event, who acted first: the
lobbying filing or the bill text, the stock trade or the hearing, the
PAC cheque or the vote. 54.2M contributions reach 695 sitting members.
Risk: committee membership has no dates. It is a current snapshot.

### 13 · Smokestack to Medicare bill
Chain B, extended one turn. FRS facility -> county -> Medicare
respiratory claims and county overdose rates.
Risk: FIPS terminal rule. Aggregate at the county and stop there.
Trap: TRI lat/lon are packed DDMMSS with the longitude sign flipped.

### 14 · The ships that wait
58.1M AIS positions against coastal NPDES discharge permits. Vessels
loitering near facilities, and whether the loitering has a calendar.
Risk: no key at all. Geography only, and IMO reaches just 9 tables.
This is the highest-variance idea on the list.

### 15 · Offshore names on the donor list
GLEIF 484k dated ownership edges and UK PSC against FEC donors.
Hit: a donor whose money traces to a foreign parent.
Risk: NAME@ZIP again. Candidates for a human, not a rate.
Trap: FEC Schedule E carries prank filings worth tens of billions.
