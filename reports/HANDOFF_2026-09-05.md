## 4. Vintages — the single biggest source of wrong answers

**Most CMS tables have no year column. Check the registry, then say the year out
loud in every finding.**

| Table | Vintage | How known |
|---|---|---|
| `FED_CMS_PARTD_PRESCRIBER_DRUG` | **DY2022** | source URL says `DY22` |
| `FED_CMS_PART_D_PRESCRIBERS` | **DY2024** | registry |
| `FED_CMS_OPEN_PAYMENTS_2022` | 2022 | name |
| `FED_CMS_OPEN_PAYMENTS_2023` | 2023 | name |
| `FED_CMS_OPEN_PAYMENTS` | **2024** | `PROGRAM_YEAR` field |
| Part B by provider and by service | **DY2023, unproven** | registry says DY2023, COVID codes prove only 2021+ |
| `FED_CMS_ORDER_AND_REFERRING` | unknown | landed 2026-08-05 |

**What a missed vintage costs.** Joining OIG exclusions to Part D without a date
test returned **554 ghost prescribers**. With `EXCLDATE < data year` it returned
**2**. The naive number was 277x too big and looked completely real.

Registry lives at `LIBRARY_META."REGISTRY".SOURCE_REGISTRY`, 2,789 rows.
Read `NAME`, `URL`, `NOTES`. **Registry notes record last-written intent, not
landed state — verify with a `count(*)`.**

## 5. Traps that will bite in the first hour

1. **Everything is `TEXT`.** Dollars, dates, counts. Cast before comparing.
2. **The lineage column has two names and three types.** `INGESTED_AT` on 1,787
   tables, `_INGESTED_AT` on 421. 483 hold epoch micros where `year()` throws.
   Only 122 are `TIMESTAMP_NTZ`. Check per table.
3. **Empty string is not NULL.** `FED_CMS_NPPES.EMPLOYER_IDENTIFICATION_NUMBER_EIN`
   reads 100% non-null and holds nothing across all 9.6M rows. Test fill with
   `regexp_like(trim(col),'^[0-9]{9}$')`, never `IS NOT NULL`.
4. **CMS devowels column names.** It is `ANTPSYCT`, not `ANTIPSYCH`. Also
   `PRSCRBR`, `MDCR`, `SBMTD`, `RNDRNG`, `SPRSN`. Searching the English word
   made me declare a whole DY2024 table missing.
5. **The Part B service table is a suppressed subset,** not a breakdown. Rows
   under 11 beneficiaries are deleted. 90% of one oncologist's drug money
   vanished. 89,266 providers have every line suppressed.
6. **Part D suppresses too.** `Tot_Benes` is null under 11, and whole drug rows
   vanish. This deflates peer medians far more than it deflates high-volume
   outliers. **Rank by percentile, never by "N times the median."**
7. **`FED_CMS_FACILITY_AFFILIATION` under-reports.** Median 2 physicians per
   nursing home; 35.5% list exactly one. A lone name is the modal case, not a
   finding.
8. **`FED_HHS_OIG_LEIE` carries an NPI on only 10.5% of rows.** 75,001 exclusions
   cannot be joined at all. `REINDATE` is `00000000` on every single row — it is
   a constant, not a filter, because OIG drops people on reinstatement.
9. **169 `PORTAL_CKA` tables sit at a 10,000-row API cap.** Counts are floors.
10. **Broken loads.** `FED_EOIR_CASE_DATA` is 12.6M rows in one unparsed column.
    `FED_NHTSA_COMPLAINTS` is headerless `C1..C54`. `FED_MSHA_VIOLATIONS` carries
    literal double quotes on every value. `FED_CFPB_HMDA_HISTORIC` is
    originations only, zero denials. `FED_FDA_FAERS_*` ends 2014q2.

## 9. How to work with Chris

Read `CLAUDE.md` and `.claude/corrections.md` first. The short version:

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Answer in chat** | numbers and the call go in the message; long tables in the report file |
| **One screen** | headline as a fact, fenced number block, 3–5 bullets, one bold arrow line |
| **12 words per line** | outside tables and fenced blocks. No parentheses. One dash per line |
| **Bar speak** | plain words, short lines, every fact survives. Compress by word choice |
| **Walk the chain** | what was checked, what a hit means, what a miss means. A label is not a finding |
| **"Done" needs a skeptic** | never say done, works or fine before running the thing that could disprove it |
| **Money and destruction** | say what and what it cost, then wait for "go" |

**Run a skeptic pass on every real claim.** It caught nine wrong numbers across
two reports this session, including two that would have shipped as findings.

Save only two things to memory: Chris's corrections, and data traps.
