Ripple · ten studies in connection & flow

Nothing in the record happens alone

How big the public record is — and the harder truth: it's all one thing. Money moves, contact spreads, removals cascade, clusters merge. Ten still pictures of it, each readable in one look. Synthetic data shaped like the real warehouse.

01

Follow the dollar

One tax dollar enters at the left and splits downstream — agency, contractor, subcontractor. The width of every stream is the money in it. The rust endings are fines assessed along the way — and never paid.

a river delta of spending · stream width = dollars

02

The contact trace

Epidemiology's picture, pointed at business. Start with one company, then everything it touched, then everything they touched. Read left to right — by the third panel the frame can't hold it.

the company · 1
one hop out · 14 touched
three hops out · 3,940 touched

one bad actor is never one bad actor

03

The food web

An ecosystem holds until you pull one piece out. Left: the system intact. Right: the major employer closes, and everything that fed on it — suppliers, workers, the town — goes grey in order.

before · everything connected
after · the cascade

connection shown by what breaks, not by lines on a page

04

Pipes of government

Money flows down the building. Complaints and violations flow up. A working system — except where the pipes leak. The gold drips are fines assessed but never collected.

teal = money down · rust = complaints up · gold = leakage

05

The percolation moment

Hundreds of little clusters — separate stories, separate scandals. Add connections and nothing much changes… until the third panel, where it snaps into one web. Physics calls it percolation. We call it the moment you realize these were never separate stories.

few connections · largest cluster 9%
more connections · largest cluster 24%
the snap · largest cluster 87%

teal = the largest connected cluster · same dots in all three panels

06

The switchboard

Every domain of the record is a terminal, and every arc is a real match — the same name appearing in both places. A doctor in the payments data who is also in the sanctions data. Thicker cable, more matches.

arc weight = number of cross-domain matches

07

Murmuration

No lines at all. Thousands of dots that cluster like a flock because related things pull on each other — companies gathered around their owners. The rust arc is one enforcement action, frozen mid-travel through the sector it hit.

connection as position · density is ownership, not chance

08

One person's gravity

Start with one worker at one plant and walk the real connections, one hop at a time — employer, parent company, lobbyist, rule change, the inspection that never came. Six degrees, but with receipts.

every hop is a real join key · the chain loops back to her shop floor

09

Where it pools

Pour everything — money, ownership, violations — onto a flat surface and it doesn't spread evenly. It drains to the same few places. Deep pools where it concentrates, thin channels between them.

depth = concentration · the biggest pool holds more than the smallest fifty combined

10

The heartbeat

Each line is one domain of the record over one year, beating as filings land. Mostly they beat alone. Then something real happens in the world — and every line spikes in the same week. That's how you know it's one organism.

five domains, one year · the shared spike is a hurricane making landfall

What this page is: ten templates for showing size, connection, and flow in one still frame — drawn from the Laboratory notes (contact tracing, food webs, percolation, flocking, flow maps). Synthetic data throughout; the real edges exist in the warehouse and wire in scene by scene.
