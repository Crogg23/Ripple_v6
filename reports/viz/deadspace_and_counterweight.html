Eight patterns

How a shape decides what goes next to it.

Every visual you drop on the page carves out the empty space around it. That leftover shape isn't random — it follows the geometry of what you placed. Eight patterns below, each one a shape, the gap it leaves, and what sits well in a gap like that.

For picking which chart type gets you a given shape in the first place — the interactive Layout Picker does that live. This page is the reference for the physical reasoning underneath it.

01 / CIRCLE IN A BOX

Circle in a Box

Round shapes never reach the corners of a rectangular slot — pure geometry, not opinion. A rectangle's corners are always left over.

Big shapearea-true proportional circle · bubble chart · radar profile · donut
Fits the pocketa testimony callout · a single stat · an error-bar estimate — narrow and short. Leave the OTHER corner empty on purpose — that's the rest beat.
02 / FULL-BLEED BAND

Full-Bleed Band

A full-width element leaves nothing beside it — the gap isn't beside it, it's a constraint on what comes next. Two full slabs back to back read as stacked, not flowing.

Big shapefunnel chart · Sankey diagram · choropleth map · 100%-stacked bar
Fits the pocketthe next row must be a genuinely different shape — or split into 2-3 narrower pieces: three small stat tiles, not one more full slab.
03 / RADIAL STARBURST

Radial Starburst

Spokes at uneven angles leave irregular wedge gaps between them, not clean corners — the wedge is exactly where a caption belongs, following the spoke.

Big shapeego network (hub-and-spoke) · node-link graph · a branching org chart
Fits the pocketleader-line labels threaded into the gaps between spokes — mono annotations, not full content blocks.
04 / TALL COLUMN

Tall Column

A tall narrow element leaves two gutters, left and right, running its full height — a side channel, not a corner pocket.

Big shapeinterval/duration timeline · a tall org-chart chain · a stacked column over many years
Fits the pocketa narrow vertical stat stack, a mono annotation column, a sparkline column — matched to its height, not its width.
05 / DENSE FIELD

Dense Field

Weight here isn't about footprint — it's about mark count. A hundred small marks reads heavy even if it's physically small, and needs a calm neighbor, not a spatial gap.

Big shapetickwall / icon-array · adjacency matrix · quote wall · calendar heatmap · hex-bin map
Fits the pocketone quiet big numeral, one plain sentence, nothing else competing — the density/rest rule stated in shape terms.
06 / OFF-CENTER ANCHOR

Off-Center Anchor

Push the dominant shape off dead-center and you get one larger pocket instead of two small symmetric ones — big enough to hold a whole secondary finding, not just one stat.

Big shapethe existing hero circle, or any dominant shape shifted left or right of center
Fits the pocketa caption cluster, a small supporting chart, a short multi-line finding — how the shipped hero scene already works: claim and proof living together.
07 / DIAGONAL SWEEP

Diagonal Sweep

A line cutting the frame at an angle splits it into two uneven zones, not corners. What the line passes through reads as the main path; what sits outside it reads as an aside.

Big shapethe page's own dotted spine · a slopegraph · a connected-scatterplot trajectory
Fits the pocketcommentary, a quote, a source note — true but secondary, placed outside the line's path on purpose.
08 / FLAT GRID

Flat Grid

A table or matrix is a solid rectangle with almost no internal gaps — the relief it needs isn't spatial, it's emotional. It needs a release valve right after it, not more structure.

Big shapeleaderboard table · adjacency matrix · small-multiples grid
Fits the pocket(what must follow) one big human-scale number, one plain sentence, one quote — the page stepping out of spreadsheet mode back into a person's story.
