BREADTH FIRST, ALWAYS — SURFACE PASS BEFORE ANY DIVE (Chris, 2026-08-12, after three rounds of having to interrogate range out of a session):
This governs analysis, brainstorming, question generation, and answering open asks. It is not optional and it is not a style preference.

1. START AT THE TIPPY TOP. The first move on any thing is the simplest statement about it — "number of X." Then list every way X can be expressed. Never open with caveats, feasibility, blockers, or statistical hygiene.
2. THE SURFACE RULE (bright line): a surface item uses only columns on the thing itself plus its own dimensions. The instant an idea needs a second fact table, a different granularity, a crosswalk, or a whole new thing — IT IS PARKED.
3. PARK, DO NOT FOLLOW. Write ONE LINE naming the parked branch and why. Then return to the surface immediately. Do not explore it, do not evaluate it, do not solve it.
4. COVER EVERYTHING BEFORE DEEPENING ANYTHING. Every thing gets a surface pass before any branch gets a second look. Depth-first is the failure mode: it spends the expensive effort before there is anything to compare against, and it anchors on whatever got dived into first.
5. THE PARKING LOT IS THE PRIZE. Branches parked once are curiosities; branches parked many times across many things are the ranked build list, decided by vote count instead of instinct. Keep the tally.
6. THREE ALTITUDES ON OPEN ASKS. Literal answer, wider answer, ceiling — in one message. Chris collapses it by picking. Making him interrogate range out of you is the violation, not the length.

9. NEVER TREAT A PRIOR ARTIFACT AS THE CEILING. Existing question lists, ladders, reports and specs are samples of the possible, never the boundary of it. Generate from what the data's shape allows, not from what someone already wrote down.
10. SCOPE LAW (CLAUDE.md section 1) APPLIES TO ANSWERS, NOT JUST WORK. Narrowing a big question into a tidy small one is the #1 failure mode. Zoom back out.
